# COMMEMORATING THE INTERNATIONAL DAY AGAINST NUCLEAR TESTS AND THE CLOSURE OF THE SEMIPALATINSK NUCLEAR TEST SITE

**Speaking:** Foster, Bill (F000454) [D-IL]

```
   COMMEMORATING THE INTERNATIONAL DAY AGAINST NUCLEAR TESTS AND THE 
             CLOSURE OF THE SEMIPALATINSK NUCLEAR TEST SITE

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. BILL FOSTER

                              of illinois

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, August 27, 2026

  Mr. FOSTER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to mark the International Day 
Against Nuclear Tests.
  August 29, 2026, marks 35 years since Kazakhstan's historic decision 
to close the Semipalatinsk nuclear test site. In tribute to that 
decision, the United Nations General Assembly unanimously designated 
August 29 as the International Day Against Nuclear Tests in 2009, 
drawing global attention to the devastating human and environmental 
consequences of nuclear testing.
  This milestone reflects Kazakhstan's sustained commitment to nuclear 
nonproliferation, international oversight, and practical security 
cooperation, including its close partnership with the United States.
  The consequences of nuclear testing are deeply understood in both our 
countries. 1,054 U.S. nuclear tests, over 200 of which were 
atmospheric, exposed servicemembers, workers, and downwind communities 
to radioactive fallout. At Semipalatinsk, 456 Soviet nuclear tests 
between 1949 and 1989 exposed over 1 million people and left a lasting 
human and environmental toll.
  The Nevada-Semipalatinsk Movement, which united citizens affected by 
nuclear testing in Kazakhstan and the United States, helped generate 
the public momentum necessary to end testing at the site.
  After inheriting the world's fourth-largest nuclear arsenal, 
Kazakhstan voluntarily relinquished those weapons and chose security 
through diplomacy, international cooperation, and nonproliferation.
  The United States was a close partner in this historic 
transformation. Through the Nunn-Lugar Cooperative Threat Reduction 
Program, our countries eliminated weapons and delivery systems, 
dismantled nuclear infrastructure, sealed test tunnels, and secured 
dangerous materials. Project Sapphire alone removed nearly 600 
kilograms of highly enriched uranium from Kazakhstan.
  Kazakhstan has continued to put these principles into practice. It 
hosted two rounds of P5+1 negotiations with Iran in 2013, supported 
implementation of the 2015 nuclear agreement, and now hosts the IAEA 
Low Enriched Uranium Bank at the Ulba Metallurgical Plant.
  Mr. Speaker, when a country takes responsible actions, we should 
ensure to highlight it as a good thing. The closure of the 
Semipalatinsk Test Site, Kazakhstan's efforts to denuclearize, and the 
creation of the International Day Against Nuclear Tests prove that 
nonproliferation is not an abstract aspiration, but a practical 
instrument of national security and diplomatic problem-solving.
```
