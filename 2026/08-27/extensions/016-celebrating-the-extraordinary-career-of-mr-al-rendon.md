# CELEBRATING THE EXTRAORDINARY CAREER OF MR. AL RENDON

**Speaking:** Castro, Joaquin (C001091) [D-TX]

```
         CELEBRATING THE EXTRAORDINARY CAREER OF MR. AL RENDON

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. JOAQUIN CASTRO

                                of texas

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, August 27, 2026

  Mr. CASTRO of Texas. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Mr. Al Rendon 
for receiving the Aguila Aztlan award from the Centro Cultural Aztlan 
for his instrumental work in documenting and photographing the soul of 
Tejano and Mexican American culture in South Texas.

[[Page E811]]

For over 50 years, Mr. Rendon captured reflections of the soul of a 
people whose music, food and daily activities connect the past with the 
present and future.
  A San Antonio native, Mr. Rendon developed an interest in photography 
from an early age. His mother shared her love for photography with him, 
and after she gave him her camera, the city quickly became his muse. As 
a student at Central Catholic High School, his passion grew, joining 
the school's yearbook staff and covering live concerts. Following 
graduation, Mr. Rendon pursued his passion and launched a career that 
would document iconic moments in the history of South Texas.
  Early in Mr. Rendon's career, he began making a name for himself in 
shooting rock concerts held at San Antonio's HemisFair Arena and 
Municipal Theater. After a decade in the rock scene, Mr. Rendon was 
recruited by the Guadalupe Cultural Arts Center to photograph several 
of their cultural events and Conjunto Festival. It was at these events 
that in 1986 Mr. Rendon pivoted his focus to capturing the Mexican 
American community of Texas.
  In doing so, Mr. Rendon reconnected with his culture and was placed 
on a path that has helped others do the same. He focused on documenting 
the legacy of the spirit of Mexican Americans in Texas. He has created 
exhibitions of Conjunto and Tejano artists, portraits of cultural 
leaders, representations of Our Lady of Guadalupe, Charreada photos, 
and more. Mr. Rendon's cultural focus has led to his work being held at 
the Smithsonian National Portrait Gallery, the Museum of Fine Arts in 
Houston, and the Mexican American Museum of Art in Chicago.
  Mr. Speaker, please join me in celebrating Mr. Rendon's extraordinary 
career. It is through his lens that stories of passion, determination, 
and community are preserved. He is leaving behind a powerful legacy for 
future generations to broaden their understanding of the world.
```
