# THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF VICTOR BACH

**Speaking:** Torres, Ritchie (T000486) [D-NY]

```
                   THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF VICTOR BACH

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. RITCHIE TORRES

                              of new york

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, August 27, 2026

  Mr. TORRES of New York. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life 
and legacy of Victor Bach, a pioneering housing advocate, researcher, 
educator, and tireless champion for New Yorkers who spent his life 
fighting for affordable housing, stronger public housing, and economic 
justice.
  Victor Bach passed away on June 4, 2026, at the age of 93. Born in 
Brooklyn, Mr. Bach dedicated his professional life to public service 
and housing advocacy. He earned a Ph.D. in Urban Planning from the 
Massachusetts Institute of Technology and served on the faculties of 
the University of Texas and The New School for Social Research before 
beginning his extraordinary tenure at the Community Service Society of 
New York.
  Beginning in 1983, Mr. Bach spent approximately four decades 
directing housing research and advocacy at the Community Service 
Society, where he became one of New York City's most respected and 
influential voices on affordable housing. For 35 years, he served as a 
senior housing analyst and Director of Housing Policy at CSS, the 
Community Service Society of New York.
  Throughout his career, Mr. Bach produced groundbreaking research on 
homelessness, housing affordability, rent burdens, and the challenges 
facing low-income New Yorkers. His work also documented the devastating 
consequences of government disinvestment in the New York City Housing 
Authority and helped bring greater attention to the urgent needs of 
public housing residents.
  But Victor Bach's legacy cannot be measured solely by the reports he 
authored or the policies he influenced. He understood that housing 
policy must ultimately serve the people who call our communities home. 
He provided technical assistance to resident and community 
organizations and helped empower public housing residents to organize, 
advocate, and demand the resources necessary to preserve their homes 
and communities.
  Mr. Bach was also a mentor to generations of housing researchers and 
advocates. His colleagues at the Community Service Society have 
recognized the profound influence he had on those who followed him, 
helping develop some of New York's leading housing policy experts. His 
commitment to mentoring others ensured that his work would continue 
long after his retirement.
  For decades, Victor Bach reminded policymakers that affordable 
housing is not simply a matter of bricks and mortar. It is about 
dignity. It is about stability. It is about giving families the 
opportunity to remain in the communities they call home. And it is 
about recognizing that every New Yorker deserves a safe and affordable 
place to live.
  This message carries particular significance in the Bronx and 
throughout New York City, where working families, seniors, and low-
income residents continue to confront extraordinary housing costs and 
persistent threats of displacement. The fight for affordable housing 
that Victor Bach championed remains as urgent today as ever.
  Victor Bach leaves behind a remarkable legacy of scholarship, 
advocacy, mentorship, and public service. His work strengthened the 
movement for affordable housing and gave a stronger voice to those too 
often left out of policy discussions.
  On behalf of the people of the 15th Congressional District of New 
York, I extend my deepest condolences to Mr. Bach's children, 
grandchildren, family, friends, colleagues, and the many New Yorkers 
whose lives and communities were touched by his extraordinary work.
  Victor Bach dedicated his life to the belief that housing is a 
fundamental pillar of a just society. We honor his memory by continuing 
the work to which he devoted his life: building a New York where every 
person, regardless of income, has access to a safe, stable, and 
affordable home.
  May his memory be a blessing, and may his life's work continue to 
inspire the fight for housing justice for generations to come.
```
