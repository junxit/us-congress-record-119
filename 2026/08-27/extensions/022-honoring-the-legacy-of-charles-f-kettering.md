# HONORING THE LEGACY OF CHARLES F. KETTERING

**Speaking:** Jordan, Jim (J000289) [R-OH]

```
              HONORING THE LEGACY OF CHARLES F. KETTERING

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. JIM JORDAN

                                of ohio

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, August 27, 2026

  Mr. JORDAN. Mr. Speaker, I rise to commend to the House the 
extraordinary life and contributions of Ohio's own Charles F. Kettering 
as we near the 150th anniversary of his birth.
  Charles Franklin Kettering was born on his family's farm in 
Loudonville on August 29, 1876. After high school, he took a teaching 
position at a nearby school, where his evening lectures and 
demonstrations on science drew sizable crowds.
  A 1904 graduate of The Ohio State University with a degree in 
electrical engineering, he was hired to lead the research laboratory at 
National Cash Register in Dayton. During his five years there, he 
secured 23 patents for his work on the electric cash register and other 
innovations.
  While at NCR, Kettering and a small group of colleagues (dubbed the 
Barn Gang) worked during evenings and weekends to develop new gadgetry 
and electronic equipment for automobiles. This group was incorporated 
in 1909 as the Dayton Engineering Laboratories Company, or Delco. By 
1911, Kettering and his fellow engineers had developed the first 
practical electric self-starter for autos.
  Following the acquisition of Delco by General Motors in 1918, 
Kettering joined the team at General Motors Research Corporation, where 
he held the position of vice president for more than a quarter century. 
His work on air-cooled automobile engines, two-stroke diesel engines, 
fuel additives, and quick-dry automotive paint contributed richly to 
the development of the modern passenger vehicle.
  Charles Kettering's philanthropy was equally effective and 
noteworthy: Believing that industrial research could have practical 
applications in the medical field, he helped found what ultimately 
became the Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center, one of the top-
ranked cancer care facilities in the Nation.
  Mr. Speaker, the people of Loudonville will gather this Saturday to 
celebrate the accomplishments of its favorite son. On behalf of the 
people of Ohio's 4th Congressional District, I am proud to join in the 
accolades to this inventor, engineer, and philanthropist whose work 
revolutionized nearly every aspect of modern life.
```
