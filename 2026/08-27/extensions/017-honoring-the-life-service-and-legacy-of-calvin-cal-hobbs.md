# HONORING THE LIFE, SERVICE AND LEGACY OF CALVIN "CAL" HOBBS

**Speaking:** Thompson, Bennie G. (T000193) [D-MS]

**Measures:** H.Con.Res. 78

```
     HONORING THE LIFE, SERVICE AND LEGACY OF CALVIN ``CAL'' HOBBS

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. BENNIE G. THOMPSON

                             of mississippi

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, August 27, 2026

  Mr. THOMPSON of Mississippi. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the 
life, legacy, military service and historic contributions of Calvin 
``Cal'' Hobbs, a native of Winona, Mississippi, a distinguished member 
of the Tuskegee Airmen, and a proud American whose life was devoted to 
service, excellence, and inspiring future generations.
  Calvin ``Cal'' Hobbs was born on August 26, 1923, in Winona, 
Mississippi. In 1944, he enlisted in the United States Army Air Corps 
aviation cadet program in Omaha, Nebraska, and was subsequently 
assigned to first African-American Aviation Mechanics class at Amarillo 
Army Air Base.
  Mr. Hobbs distinguished himself through determination, discipline, 
and an exceptional work ethic. He eventually earned an assignment to 
the 477th Bomber Squadron, which was attached to the 99th Fighter 
Squadron. His military career advanced rapidly, and he achieved the 
ranks of Sergeant, Technical Sergeant, Master Sergeant, and ultimately 
Chief Master Sergeant. The Mississippi Legislature recognized his 
advancement and accomplishments in House Concurrent Resolution No. 78, 
adopted in 2018.
  Chief Master Sergeant Hobbs' service included completing Aerial 
Gunnery School and serving in important maintenance and leadership 
positions. He served as Line Chief with the 99th Fighter Squadron and 
the 332nd Fighter Group and became Crew Chief of a KC-135 aerial 
refueling aircraft at Mather Air Force Base. He also served as Flight 
Chief, Line Chief, and NCOIC Maintenance Control at Mather Air Force 
Base.
  After 20 years of service in the United States Air Force, Chief 
Master Sergeant Hobbs retired in 1964. His commitment to service, 
however, did not end with his military retirement. His obituary records 
that he subsequently spent another 20 years with Pacific Bell, while 
also dedicating significant time to civic, youth, religious, and 
community organizations.
  Mr. Hobbs was a founding member of Tuskegee Airmen, Inc.'s 
Sacramento/George ``Spanky'' Roberts Chapter. He remained deeply 
committed to preserving the history and legacy of the Tuskegee Airmen 
and became an integral member of the Tuskegee Airmen Living History 
Team. Through that work, he traveled to civic organizations, churches, 
and schools across the Nation, sharing the story of the Tuskegee Airmen 
and helping educate young people about aviation and science.
  His commitment to young people was evident not only through his work 
preserving history, but also through his willingness to serve as a 
mentor. His message to young people was straightforward and enduring: 
``Always be prepared when an opportunity arises!'' That simple 
statement reflected the discipline and perseverance that characterized 
his own life.
  Mr. Hobbs' legacy is particularly significant because the Tuskegee 
Airmen represented far more than a group of military aviators. The 
Tuskegee experience included the pilots, mechanics, technicians, 
support personnel, and other servicemembers whose dedication helped 
establish an important chapter in American military and civil rights 
history. Mr. Hobbs contributed to that history through his technical 
expertise, military leadership, and lifelong commitment to telling the 
Tuskegee Airmen's story.
  Beyond his military career, Mr. Hobbs devoted himself to his family 
and community. His obituary notes his involvement with organizations 
including the Tuskegee Airmen, Inc., Knights of Columbus, California 
Civil Air Patrol, Loaves & Fishes, Boy Scouts of America, Little League 
Baseball, and the PTA in the Folsom-Cordova School District, where he 
placed particular emphasis on supporting school music programs.
  On August 2, 2011, Calvin ``Cal'' Hobbs passed away peacefully at his 
home in Rancho Cordova, California, surrounded by his family. He was 87 
years old. His passing marked the loss of a remarkable Mississippian 
and American, but his contributions to his country, his community, and 
the preservation of the Tuskegee Airmen legacy continue to be 
remembered.
  Calvin ``Cal'' Hobbs demonstrated throughout his life what can be 
accomplished through preparation, perseverance, service, and a 
commitment to helping others. From his service in the United States 
Army Air Corps and Air Force to his work as a member of the Tuskegee 
Airmen community and his efforts to educate young people, he dedicated 
himself to making a lasting difference.
  His story is a reminder that the preservation of history is also an 
investment in the future. By sharing the experiences and 
accomplishments of the Tuskegee Airmen, Mr. Hobbs helped ensure that 
future generations would understand the sacrifices, determination, and 
excellence that helped expand opportunities for African Americans in 
military service and throughout American society.
  I am proud to honor Chief Master Sergeant Calvin ``Cal'' Hobbs, a 
native son of Mississippi, a veteran of the United States Air Force, a 
Tuskegee Airman, a mentor, and a devoted servant of his community.
  May his life continue to inspire young Americans to prepare 
themselves for opportunity, pursue excellence, serve their communities, 
and never allow adversity to define what they can accomplish.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring the life and 
enduring legacy of Calvin ``Cal'' Hobbs and in expressing our gratitude 
for his honorable service to the United States of America.
```
