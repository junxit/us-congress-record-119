# HONORING AUGUSTUS "GUS" TART

**Speaking:** Patronis, Jimmy (P000622) [R-FL]

```
                     HONORING AUGUSTUS ``GUS'' TART

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. JIMMY PATRONIS

                               of florida

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, August 27, 2026

  Mr. PATRONIS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to pay tribute to early 
Northwest Florida pioneers who settled the land in the little spot of 
earth that would later become Shalimar, Florida. Augustus ``Gus'' 
Tart's story embodies a remarkable chapter of Florida history. His 
legacy from his humble beginnings to becoming a social and political 
force in the county will long be remembered.
  Gus was born into slavery in South Carolina in 1840. By way of 
traveling through Andalusia, Alabama, he later made his home along 
Garnier's Bayou in Walton County, Florida, before his property became 
part of the newly created Okaloosa County.
  Following the emancipation from slavery, he registered to vote in 
Walton County in 1867. He became a local farmer, was the chief cook for 
the T.C. Brooks family who founded Fort Walton Beach, and built a life 
as a respected outdoorsman, hunting and fishing guide, host, and 
community figure. In 1903 he made his land ownership a reality. After 
applying for a land grant under the Federal Homestead Act, he was 
awarded a 135.5-acre federal land patent by the United States 
Government. ``Gus' Place'' as it was the called, now includes important 
public and civic places in Shalimar, including areas associated with 
Meigs Middle School, the Shalimar Library, the Okaloosa courthouse 
annex and country administration building, and surrounding 
neighborhoods.
  Gus and his life on the bayou were remembered as an important part of 
the community's history. His reputation extended far beyond the local 
fishing grounds, and he became known as a trusted adviser to 
politicians, community leaders, and those seeking success in business. 
People from across the region sought out ``Old Gus'' for his counsel, 
friendship, and approval.
  Among those who spent time with Gus were governors, U.S. senators, 
congressmen, judges, and county and state leaders. Many considered it 
an honor to call him a friend. Visitors would often join Gus on his 
boat for a day of fishing, returning to shore to enjoy a meal as Gus 
prepared their catch. Through these gatherings, Gus became not only a 
respected figure in the community but also a beloved host whose 
knowledge, generosity, and character left a lasting impression. In the 
fall of 1915, Gus fell ill and had his last will and testament 
prepared. Although he raised two sons, he appreciated the Brooks family 
friendship so much, he left his entire property, all 135.5 acres, to 
T.C. and Emma Brooks.
  Mr. Speaker, please join me and my colleagues in honoring the life of 
the founding father of Okaloosa County and Shalimar, Florida. His 
legacy and good work will be long remembered. It is my pleasure to 
support and join in requesting the State of Florida, Department of 
State, in placing a state Historical Marker at his gravesite so future 
generations will learn of the life of ``Old Gus''.
```
