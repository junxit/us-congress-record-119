# HONORING CASSY DORNBUSCH ON HER RETIREMENT

**Speaking:** Kaptur, Marcy (K000009) [D-OH]

```
               HONORING CASSY DORNBUSCH ON HER RETIREMENT

  (Under the Speaker's announced policy of January 3, 2025, Ms. Kaptur 
of Ohio was recognized for 60 minutes as the designee of the minority 
leader.)
  Ms. KAPTUR. Mr. Speaker, this evening, I have real pleasure to send 
positive messages forward on so many of the phenomenal citizens that I 
represent here and their life stories.
  I rise today to celebrate an extraordinary Ohio educator, Cassy 
Dornbusch, from Ottawa County, Ohio, Congressional District 9. She was 
honored by the Coalition of Rural and Appalachian Schools as the 
Outstanding Elementary Teacher for the 2025-2026 School Year, this 
year.
  For 33 years, Cassy has been a reading specialist at Benton-Carroll-
Salem and a builder of futures. With patience, compassion, and 
unwavering belief in every child who walks through her door, she has 
helped students discover how to read, yes, but more importantly, how to 
believe in themselves.
  In every word of encouragement, Cassy shapes the confidence, 
curiosity, and resilience of young minds. Her impact lives on in the 
voices, dreams, and successes of her students.
  As she starts her well-earned retirement, we thank Cassy for her 
dedication, her heart, and the lasting difference she has made in each 
and every life. Our rocket future is brighter because of her.


                    Honoring Life of Paul Hollenbeck

  Ms. KAPTUR. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life of Paul 
Hollenbeck, an architect, mentor, and rare lifelong community builder. 
His precious insights to build progress forward in every corner of our 
city of Toledo is unparalleled.
  Paul was an astounding, creative, humble citizen, and he embodied the 
community's hopes in every nook and cranny, and he rarely got 
attention. He was very, very humble. He didn't look for notoriety. He 
believed deeply that places matter because people matter. He spent his 
life proving that belief brick by brick, neighborhood by neighborhood, 
project by project. He was unrelenting.
  I knew Paul as a friend and as a doer. He didn't just talk about 
civic responsibility. He lived it, and he set an example for others to 
follow.
  Through his leadership in creating the Toledo Design Collective, his 
vision for downtown Toledo, or his quiet mentorship of young architects 
and community leaders, he reimagined the redevelopment of urban spaces 
and neighborhoods, literally rebuilding America in this new century. He 
breathed new life into every corner of our community.
  Paul always demonstrated a focus on the future, and that good design 
for people is about dignity, access, opportunity, and respect for our 
built environment. He understood that cities are living places, shaped 
by generosity and care, and he gave Toledo both.

                              {time}  2020

  Paul brought people together, listened closely, and always asked how 
we could do better for the next generation.
  He leaves behind the Design Collective, The Collaborative building, 
and revitalized neighborhoods. More importantly, he leaves behind a 
grateful family, grateful friends, a grateful community, and a civic 
spirit we all can carry forward by innovating and caring for the 
precious legacy of our built environment in the largest fresh watershed 
in the entire Great Lakes. Paul Hollenbeck's legacy will live on in the 
voices, the dreams, and the successes that have happened across our 
region.
  I say to his family, to his friends, and to the architects with whom 
he spent so much of his life: Congratulations. Job well-done, Paul 
Hollenbeck.


                     Thanking Teacher Andre Cowell

  Ms. KAPTUR. Mr. Speaker, our northwest Ohio region is lucky to have 
countless educators who guide our young people and help them grow into 
strong leaders of tomorrow. One of these educators is Andre Cowell.
  A retired Toledo detective now teaching criminal justice at 
Washington Local School District's Whitmer High School, he brings 25 
years of field experience to the classroom.
  Mr. Cowell's deep compassion extends to every student he encounters. 
A former student at Whitmer, Alivia Garcia, who wasn't even a student 
of Mr. Cowell's, spoke so highly of the impact he made on her life, I 
thought it was worth sharing here in the Halls of Congress. There are 
such good people everywhere, and he showed her true kindness in the 
wake of a difficult time in her life following the passing of her 
boyfriend.
  Mr. Cowell welcomed her into his classroom when she needed an ear to 
listen. He let her know if you ever need anything, I am always here, 
and you know where to find me.
  He kept his word and stays in touch with Alivia to this day.
  Mr. Cowell is a shining example of someone worthy of congressional 
recognition.
  The Whitmer community in northwestern Ohio is blessed to have an 
educator like Andre Cowell. He is a model of a man for others, sharing 
empathy and integrity for the generations to come.

                           Canadian Wildfires

  Ms. KAPTUR. Mr. Speaker, a serious issue is facing our continent. 
Right now, wildfires are ravaging our neighbors in the Canadian 
Province of Ontario which Ohio adjoins through Lake Erie. Many First 
Nation communities have been forced to evacuate. Hundreds of thousands 
of hectares of forests have burned.
  However, our northern neighbors were not the only ones who suffered. 
Just last week, in my hometown of Toledo, Ohio, hazardous air quality 
ranked among the highest levels in our Nation. As winds blew the smoke 
into northwestern Ohio, our community was at risk for the grave health 
concerns that accompany wildfire smoke: asthma attacks, heart attacks, 
and weakened immune systems.
  Burning forests remind us that environmental challenges like air 
pollution and wildfires, which will continue to be exacerbated by 
climate change, do not

[[Page H5169]]

stop at national borders. We in the Great Lakes nation must continue to 
work with our continental neighbor and strongest trading partner to 
share expertise on forest management and wildfire response.
  Joint efforts and collaboration are necessary to make both of our 
countries safer, healthier and more resilient. America belongs to all 
of us, no matter what side of the border one lives on. As allies 
working together, we can heal our freshwater and forest systems in our 
precious, bountiful, and life-sustaining freshwater region.

   Compensating Veterans with Disability Pay and Retirement Benefits

  Ms. KAPTUR. Mr. Speaker, I rise tonight in strong support of the 
bipartisan Major Richard Star Act.
  Servicemembers injured in the line of duty have already given so much 
to our Nation of their substance. They should not be forced to 
sacrifice again when they return home to their families.
  Many of these disabled veterans are unable to work full-time. They 
often face insurmountable challenges for the rest of their lives. Many 
are denied the full compensation they deserve for all their service.
  The Major Richard Star Act will correct this injustice by rightfully 
compensating these veterans with disability pay and retirement 
benefits.
  That is why I am a cosponsor of the bill and have signed the 
discharge petition to bring this essential piece of bipartisan 
legislation to the House floor for a fair up-or-down vote.
  I urge my colleagues who have not signed on to this petition to 
support our veterans and stand with me on their behalf. We are just 
three signatures away from being able to vote on and pass this bill 
which already has 337 bipartisan cosponsors. Let's bring it forward, 
and do the job.
  Mr. Speaker, I implore you to bring this monumental legislation for a 
clean vote, so we can give something back to those who have given so 
much for all of us and the generations to come. I ask my colleagues to 
sponsor the Major Richard Star Act, just sign the discharge petition. 
We only need three signatures more, and we can move this bill.
  Mr. Speaker, I yield back the balance of my time.
```
