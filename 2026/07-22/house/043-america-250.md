               Largest Tax Increase in California History

  Mr. KILEY of California. Mr. Speaker, I thank my colleague from Utah 
for yielding.
  Many Californians have been shocked to learn that our State 
legislature and Governor just imposed what the California Tax 
Foundation calls ``the largest tax increase in State history.''
  For a State that already has the highest taxes in the country and the 
highest cost of living, that is really saying something.
  As part of this, the Governor and legislature in Sacramento have 
imposed a massive tax increase on healthcare that is going to cause 
California families to have to pay up to $400 more for healthcare 
premiums.
  This is something that Californians simply cannot afford, and it is 
all the worse given that California voters already said that this kind 
of tax increase is barred. It is a double blow to democracy and 
affordability.
  Fortunately, there is a way that we can fight back to stop it. I am 
writing a letter putting forth a formal request, joined by my 
colleagues in the California delegation, for the Department of Health 
and Human Services here at the Federal level to block this tax 
increase, which does require Federal approval.
  We will be sending this request out by the end of this week, and it 
is my hope that California taxpayers can be spared yet another increase 
in our cost of living that so many Californians simply cannot afford 
because of the extreme policies coming out of Sacramento.


                        Recognizing Child Action

  Mr. KILEY of California. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor and 
congratulate Child Action for celebrating 50 years of service to the 
Sacramento community.

  Child Action is a nonprofit organization that has been supporting 
children and families since 1976. Rooted in the qualities of 
compassion, community, and support, Child Action has provided 
affordable and quality childcare to over 19,000 children throughout 
Sacramento County.
  When childcare is affordable, educational, and accessible, it greatly 
benefits the whole regional economy. As chair of the Subcommittee on 
Early Childhood, Elementary, and Secondary Education, I know full well 
the impact that Child Action's subsidy program and eligibility lists 
provide to deserving families with the necessary financial support. 
These forward-looking initiatives allow parents to seek employment or 
educational opportunities while still being able to support their 
families.
  In addition to the programs for families, Child Action also provides 
support and training to other childcare providers in the Sacramento 
region, raising the quality of care in the field while growing local 
businesses in the process.
  Partnering with like-minded stakeholders is just another way that 
Child Action enhances the quality of life in our community. They are, 
indeed, a pillar of compassion and care by supporting other childcare 
providers and guiding children and families toward brighter futures.
  I was honored to attend Child Action's Golden Gala in the city of 
Rancho Cordova. The evening was filled with connection, care, and 
support as guests raised awareness for programs that continue to 
provide high-quality care to those who need it most in our community.
  Therefore, on behalf of the United States House of Representatives, I 
am honored to recognize Child Action for 50 years of service to the 
Sacramento region.


                     Recognizing Amy Van der Molen

  Mr. KILEY of California. Mr. Speaker, in the spirit of National 
Zookeeper Appreciation Week, I rise today to recognize an outstanding 
zookeeper in my district, Folsom City Zoo Sanctuary Zookeeper Amy Van 
der Molen.
  Amy has been a zookeeper at the Zoo Sanctuary since 1994. Prior to 
that, she worked as zookeeper at the Fairytale Town Park in Sacramento 
and is an educator at the Sacramento Zoo Interpretive Center. During 
her career, she has cared for birds, reptiles, omnivores, and 
carnivores.
  During her tenure at the Folsom City Zoo Sanctuary, Amy has overseen 
the care of many animals, including 13 bears, 8 mountain lions, 2 
tigers, 5 wolves, and many others.
  As part of her zookeeping job, Amy participates in vet procedures for 
the animals in her care, creates behavioral and environmental 
enrichment, performs positive enforcement training, participates in 
keeper chats for guests to educate them about animals, and shares the 
Zoo Sanctuary's mission of teaching responsible behavior toward all 
animals.
  Amy has created the Annual Art Program where animals use their feet, 
nose, or other body parts to paint with nontoxic washable paint on a 
canvas. These paintings are sold in the zoo gift shop and have even 
been part of art shows at a local art gallery in Folsom and have sold 
at various special events and fundraisers to benefit the Zoo Sanctuary.
  Amy's love for her work and for the animals under her care is evident 
as she has been quoted as saying: ``During my career, I have loved 
every single animal that I have worked with, but of course there are a 
few that I will never forget and will always be a part of me. I have 
been eligible,'' she said, ``for retirement since January of 2025, but 
I love my job and my animal family. I don't see myself leaving anytime 
soon.''
  Therefore, on behalf of the United States House of Representatives, I 
am honored to recognize zookeeper Amy Van der Molen for her service to 
the Folsom community.

                          Honoring Ken Cusano

  Mr. KILEY of California. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor and 
congratulate former City of Folsom Fire Chief Ken Cusano on the 
occasion of his retirement, following 28 years of service and 
dedication to protecting the Folsom community.
  Chief Cusano attended Folsom High School and received his bachelor's 
degree from Sacramento State.
  Since joining the fire department in 1998, Chief Cusano climbed the 
ranks, starting his career as a firefighter paramedic and eventually 
taking the title of chief in October 2020, which he held until his 
retirement.
  Throughout his time as chief, he led a team of over 90 professionals 
and was known for strengthening the department during a time of 
unprecedented growth in the city.
  During his tenure, Chief Cusano lent his expertise in the design and 
construction of Fire Stations 39 and 34 and the remodel of Station 38. 
In addition to the new fire stations, Chief Cusano also played a key 
role in creating a medic unit and hiring extra personnel to keep up 
with the rapid growth of Folsom.
  Under the chief's steady hand and leadership, the mental health of 
the men and women in the department became a priority. The forward-
thinking approach was evident when the department introduced the 
region's first peer support dog and other proactive approaches to 
support the resilience and mental well-being of all department 
employees.
  The chief's dedication to the quality of life in his community could 
also be found with new initiatives, such as helping to implement a 
social host ordinance to deter illegal fireworks, as well as the 
seasonal closure of certain open spaces which posed fire risks.
  Chief Cusano has played a vital role in helping to shape Folsom's 
future. He faced his job and its challenges with a spirit of doing 
everything he could to keep the community safe. He will undoubtedly be 
missed by the city's grateful residents, businesses, and the men and 
women in his department that he called home for almost three decades.
  On behalf of the United States House of Representatives and 
California's Third Congressional District, I am honored to join the 
community in recognizing Fire Chief Ken Cusano's retirement. I thank 
him for his 28 years of

exceptional service and leadership, and wish him well in retirement.


                          Honoring Brad Tatum

  Mr. KILEY of California. Mr. Speaker, I solemnly rise today to honor 
the life of Brad Tatum, who passed away earlier this year.
  Brad will be known not only as the publisher of the Orangevale View, 
but as someone who cared deeply for small businesses, youth athletics, 
and his family and friends.
  Known as the heart behind the Orangevale View, Brad took pride in 
sharing the good news that enhanced the quality of life throughout his 
beloved Orangevale. This free, biweekly newspaper's stories were not 
only insightful but brought people together with a common touch.
  Brad's gift for selecting stories that reflected his sense of pride 
for the community could be seen through the ways that he highlighted 
local heroes, businesses, and the Casa Rams athletic program. His prose 
and choice of which stories to feature positively impacted Orangevale 
and the surrounding communities.
  Brad will undoubtedly be missed by his children, grandchildren, 
siblings, friends, and the more than 37,000 readers who enjoyed 
learning about the latest FFA achievements, breakfasts at the Grange, 
and all the other happenings that make Orangevale a special place. His 
dedication to his community will not be forgotten. The impacts he made 
will live on in the lives he touched and the stories he wrote.
  Therefore, on behalf of the United States House of Representatives 
and California's Third Congressional District, I am honored to 
recognize Brad Tatum for his many contributions to the Orangevale 
community.

  Ms. MALOY. The Fourth of July fireworks are over, and the barbecue 
leftovers are long gone. Now we have the rest of the year to continue 
to reflect on America's founding 250 years ago.
  John Adams believed that independence should be celebrated with 
solemn reflection. In that spirit, I sponsored a speech competition for 
the high school students in Utah's Second Congressional District and 
asked teenagers to write about what America means to them.
  Tonight, I will read the two winning speeches as well as a little bit 
about the two students who wrote them. These speeches were selected out 
of a pile of speeches with blind reading, so I didn't know who had 
written them.
  Kambree Giles is a student at Millard High School in the Second 
District. She is entering her senior year. She lives in Meadow, Utah, a 
town with fewer than 350 people, where she is an active member of her 
school's FFA chapter. After graduating, she plans to serve an 18-month 
proselytizing mission for the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day 
Saints.
  This is her speech:
  George W. Bush said, ``For we are given power not to advance our own 
purposes, nor to make a great show in the world, nor a name. There is 
but one just use of power, and it is to serve people.'' I love this 
quote because it shows just how America should be. I ask you: What are 
you doing to help the people around you, and therefore to help this 
country?
  I believe that this country is a country of love and freedom. Each of 
us has the unique opportunity to help those around us. If we love those 
around us, we can make someone feel better. Love is the first thing I 
think of when I think of what this great country is. This country means 
being able and willing to be a helping hand. We all have the freedom to 
help those around us. We never know just how much of an influence that 
can be.
  When people think about this country, they should be inspired and 
feel proud to call it home. I want everyone who hears this to be 
inspired to show kindness and feel the sense of pride that this country 
brings. Every time I stand up to say the pledge or when I hear the 
national anthem, I feel a sense of pride to be able to call this 
country my home. I get teary-eyed because I know what this country 
stands for and the lengths kindness has gone to keep it free. The most 
important thing this country stands for, for me, is kindness.
  If anything is taken from my speech, I hope everyone that hears this 
leaves feeling a stronger sense of pride for our country. If we want 
America to stay great, we must choose to keep it great by treating all 
of its people with kindness, lifting others up instead of putting them 
down. This country immediately needs more kindness.
  Thank you, Kambree.
  The second one is from Rylee Moore. Rylee is the second winner of our 
America 250 speech contest. She is also from a small town, Delta, Utah, 
where she attends Delta High School and is entering the tenth grade. 
Rylee's words reflect love for our Nation's history and faith in its 
future.
  This is Rylee's speech:
  America means so much to me because it is a place where freedom meets 
opportunity, where people from all over the world, not just America, 
can come together to live, work, and dream. In my opinion, America 
represents being able to make a difference

[[Page H5168]]

and choose your own path. The country is so diverse with so many 
cultures, ideas, and traditions, it is amazing.
  To me, America is also about standing up for what is right, working 
hard to make things happen, and better the world around us.
  It is far from perfect, but with the freedom to speak our mind and 
fight for justice, great communities are created. I am proud to live in 
a country that supports freedom and opinion.
  In conclusion, America brings hope, opportunity, and a place where I 
can imagine and create a bright future for myself as well as others.
  Thank you, Rylee.
  Kambree and Rylee are young, and their words reflect well on them, 
their families, and Utah, where they were raised. Like them, I am 
grateful for our country and excited about the next 250 years of 
American goodness and freedom. I thank each of the high school students 
who rose to the challenge and wrote a speech. I sincerely hope that 
each of them learned something new and that their love for this country 
grew even just a little bit.
