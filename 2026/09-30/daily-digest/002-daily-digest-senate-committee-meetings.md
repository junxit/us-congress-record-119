BUSINESS MEETING
Committee on Energy and Natural Resources: Committee ordered favorably 
reported the following business items:
  S. 465, to require the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission to reform 
the interconnection queue process for the prioritization and approval 
of certain projects;
  S. 1254, to amend the Aquifer Recharge Flexibility Act to clarify a 
provision relating to conveyances for aquifer recharge purposes, with 
an amendment in the nature of a substitute;
  H.R. 331, to amend the Aquifer Recharge Flexibility Act to clarify a 
provision relating to conveyances for aquifer recharge purposes;
  S. 1568, to amend the Energy Policy and Conservation Act to modify 
standards for general service lamps;
  S. 2970, to authorize the use of off-highway vehicles in certain 
areas of the Capitol Reef National Park, Utah, with an amendment in the 
nature of a substitute;
  S. 3371, to designate the Museum of the Blind People's Movement in 
Baltimore, Maryland, as the ``National Museum of the Blind People's 
Movement'', with an amendment;
  S. 3524, to amend title 54, United States Code, to modify certain 
cost-sharing requirements for grant programs under the American 
Battlefield Protection Program, with an amendment in the nature of a 
substitute;
  H.R. 7618, to amend title 54, United States Code, to modify certain 
cost-sharing requirements for grant programs under the American 
Battlefield Protection Program;
  S. 4116, to advance next-generation geothermal electricity generation 
demonstration projects in new regions, with an amendment;
  S. 4166, to amend the Energy Policy and Conservation Act to require 
States to include supporting the physical security, cybersecurity, and 
resilience of local distribution systems in State energy security 
plans, with an amendment in the nature of a substitute;
  H.R. 7257, to amend the Energy Policy and Conservation Act to require 
States to include supporting the physical security, cybersecurity, and 
resilience of local distribution systems in State energy security 
plans;

NOMINATIONS
Committee on Foreign Relations: Committee concluded a hearing to 
examine the nominations of Justin Bytheway, of Utah, to be Ambassador 
to the Kingdom of Tonga, William Flens, of the District of Columbia, to 
be Ambassador to the Republic of Chad, Douglas Holder, of Florida, to 
be Ambassador to the Republic of Bulgaria, who was introduced by 
Senator Scott (SC), Marc Dillard, of Virginia, to be Ambassador to the 
Republic of Madagascar, and to serve concurrently and without 
additional compensation as Ambassador to the Union of the Comoros, and 
Jessica Long, of Virginia, to be Ambassador to the Islamic Republic of 
Mauritania, all of the Department of State, after the nominees 
testified and answered questions in their own behalf.
NICARAGUA
Committee on Foreign Relations: Subcommittee on Western Hemisphere, 
Transnational Crime, Civilian Security, Democracy, Human Rights, and 
Global Women's Issues concluded a hearing to examine Nicaragua's 
political crisis, focusing on Ortega's assault on democracy, after 
receiving testimony from Eddy Acevedo, National Endowment for 
Democracy, Gil Guerra, Hudson Institute, and Deborah Ullmer, National 
Democratic Institute, all of Washington D.C.; and Juan Sebastian 
Chamorro, Ciudadanos por la Libertad Exilio, South Bend, Indiana.
ROGUE AI
Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs: Subcommittee 
on Disaster Management, District of Columbia, and Census concluded a 
hearing to examine rogue AI, focusing on securing the homeland against 
AI agents, including S. 2938, to require the Secretary of Energy to 
establish the Advanced Artificial Intelligence Evaluation Program, 
after receiving testimony from Chris Painter, Model Evaluation and 
Threat Research (METR), Covina, California; Marius Hobbhahn, Apollo 
Research, London, United Kingdom; Paul Ohm, Georgetown University Law 
Center, Washington D.C.; Kurt Gaudette, Dragos, Inc, Hanover, Maryland; 
and Daniel Kokotajlo, AI Futures Project, Berkeley, California.
BUSINESS MEETING
Committee on Health, Education, Labor, and Pensions: Committee ordered 
favorably reported the nominations of Nicole Saphier, of New Jersey, to 
be Medical Director in the Regular Corps of the Public Health Service, 
subject to qualifications therefor as provided by law and regulations, 
and to be Surgeon General of the Public Health Service, Timothy 
Westlake, of Wisconsin, to be Assistant Secretary for Mental Health and 
Substance Use, and Mary Lazare, of Florida, to be Assistant Secretary 
for Aging, all of the Department of Health and Human Services, and 
Loren E. Sweatt, of Virginia, and Douglas Ralph, of Kentucky, both to 
be a Member of the National Mediation Board.

NOMINATIONS

receive a briefing on certain intelligence matters from officials of 
the intelligence community.
