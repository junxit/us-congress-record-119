# EXECUTIVE CALENDAR

**Speaking:** Shaheen, Jeanne (S001181) [D-NH]

```
                           EXECUTIVE CALENDAR

  Mrs. SHAHEEN. Mr. President, I come to the floor this evening to try 
to discharge 10 career ambassadorial nominees from the Senate Committee 
on Foreign Relations.
  In the 21 months the Trump administration has been charged with 
America's national security, we have seen a complete hallowing out of 
our diplomatic service. Even with wars in the Middle East, in Sudan, 
Ethiopia, in Ukraine, and a growing Ebola crisis and strikes against 
United States bases and diplomatic facilities, over 100 diplomatic 
posts remain empty. Meanwhile, China--who I think almost everybody in 
this Chamber would acknowledge is the United States' biggest 
competitor--has 170 Ambassadors in posts around the world.
  Despite continued commitments from Secretary Rubio to send more 
career Ambassadors to the Senate for consideration, only 13 career 
Ambassadors have been confirmed under this

[[Page S5237]]

administration. We need Ambassadors on the ground to keep Americans 
safe, to be held accountable by the Senate when things go wrong.
  We have a crisis of U.S. leadership across the world. Not only has 
the President failed to nominate career experts to represent Americans, 
but he has gone a step further: He has arbitrarily recalled more than 
30 career Ambassadors from countries across Africa, from the Middle 
East, from the Pacific, from the Western Hemisphere. These are all 
regions where the PRC is engaging our partners as we retreat.
  We need American leadership overseas because competition with China 
doesn't just happen in the Indo-Pacific, and it doesn't just happen 
here at home, and it doesn't just happen in Africa or Latin America; it 
happens in every corner of the globe.
  Ensuring American businesses can compete for market access, offering 
alternatives to China's debt-trap diplomacy tactics, shoring up our 
alliances, and attracting international talent are why we need 
Ambassadors to represent the United States on the ground across the 
globe.
  Further, we know that China and Iran are working together to target 
American citizens and American infrastructure in the Middle East. If 
the Trump administration is serious about confronting China, we cannot 
let the Chinese write the future of foreign policy. This would be a 
dereliction of duty.
  We don't know where the next crisis point may be, and without 
American leadership on the ground, we risk being caught flatfooted.
  Now that these posters are up, I want to point out that, as I was 
talking about, we have 100 vacancies across the world. What we see here 
is where the United States has not filled ambassadorial posts. All this 
white, we don't have Ambassadors in those places. Look at China. They 
have Ambassadors in all those places.
  This isn't a partisan issue. When our allies and adversaries alike 
see the United States represented abroad, we need to speak with one 
voice. Career Ambassadors are nonpartisan public servants, often with 
decades of experience in the regions where they serve.
  So today, I am asking for unanimous consent to discharge from the 
Senate Foreign Relations Committee the nominations of 10 career 
ambassadorial nominees, and I am here to urge the Trump administration 
to take seriously our national security agenda.
  Mr. President, I ask unanimous consent that notwithstanding rule 
XXII, the Senate Committee on Foreign Relations be discharged from 
further consideration of PN1255-12, Peter Lord, Ambassador to Benin; 
PN1255-9, Keith Heffern, Ambassador to Gabon; PN1022-36, Daniel Travis, 
Ambassador to Sierra Leone; PN1022-39, Henry Wooster, Ambassador to 
Kenya; PN1201-4, Joshua Harris, Ambassador to Malaysia; PN1255-15, 
Nutan Patel, Ambassador to Cabo Verde; PN1255-7, Steven Gillen, 
Ambassador to Kyrgyz Republic; PN1255-8, Charles Goodman, Ambassador to 
Togo; PN1255-13, John McIntyre, Ambassador to Malawi; and PN1255-6, 
Natasha Franceschi, Ambassador to Nicaragua; and that the Senate 
proceed to their en bloc consideration and vote without intervening 
action or debate; that if confirmed, the motion to reconsider be 
considered made and laid upon the table with no intervening action or 
debate and the President be immediately notified of the Senate's 
action.
  There being no objection, the committee was discharged, and the 
Senate proceeded to consider the nominations en bloc.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The question is, Will the Senate advise and 
consent to the nominations of Peter Lord, of Florida, a Career Member 
of the Senior Foreign Service, Class of Minister-Counselor, to be 
Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary of the United States of 
America to the Republic of Benin; Keith Heffern, of Virginia, a Career 
Member of the Senior Foreign Service, Class of Minister-Counselor, to 
be Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary of the United States of 
America to the Gabonese Republic; Daniel Travis, of California, a 
Career Member of the Senior Foreign Service, Class of Counselor, to be 
Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary of the United States of 
America to the Republic of Sierra Leone; Henry Wooster, of Virginia, a 
Career Member of the Senior Foreign Service, Class of Minister-
Counselor, to be Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary of the 
United States of America to the Republic of Kenya; Joshua Harris, of 
Maryland, a Career Member of the Senior Foreign Service, Class of 
Minister-Counselor, to be Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary 
of the United States of America to Malaysia; Nutan Patel, of 
California, to be Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary of the 
United States of America to the Republic of Cabo Verde; Steven Gillen, 
of Virginia, a Career Member of the Senior Foreign Service, Class of 
Counselor, to be Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary of the 
United States of America to the Kyrgyz Republic; Charles Goodman III, 
of Florida, a Career Member of the Senior Foreign Service, Class of 
Counselor, to be Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary of the 
United States of America to the Togolese Republic; John McIntyre, of 
Texas, a Career Member of the Senior Foreign Service, Class of 
Minister-Counselor, to be Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary 
of the United States of America to the Republic of Malawi; Natasha 
Franceschi, of California, a Career Member of the Senior Foreign 
Service, Class of Minister-Counselor, to be Ambassador Extraordinary 
and Plenipotentiary of the United States of America to the Republic of 
Nicaragua, en bloc?

  The nominations were confirmed en bloc.
  Mrs. SHAHEEN. Mr. President, just to clarify since I wasn't clear on 
one of the names, PN1255-15, Nutan Patel, N-U-T-A-N P-A-T-E-L, 
Ambassador to Cabo Verde.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Duly noted.
  Mrs. SHAHEEN. Mr. President, I just want to point out how important 
it is to get our career Ambassadors on the ground, and hopefully, the 
administration will offer more career Ambassadors as nominees to the 
Senate.
  I yield the floor.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from Texas.
```
