# REMEMBERING CHARLES ANDERSON

**Speaking:** Britt, Katie Boyd (B001319) [R-AL]

```
                      REMEMBERING CHARLES ANDERSON

● Mrs. BRITT. Mr. President, I rise today to honor the impactful 
and inspirational life of an Alabama legend, Charles Anderson. An 
entrepreneur, philanthropist, husband, grandfather, and great-
grandfather, Charles will be remembered by his relentless drive, 
extreme generosity, and unwavering humility.
  From a young age, Charles learned the values of faith, persistence, 
and hard work. Born in Florence, AL, Charles was the first of Clyde W. 
and Ruth Keenum's three children. He developed his entrepreneurial 
spirit by helping at his father's local newsstand, eventually picking 
up his own newspaper route. By age 11, Charles had started his own 
business, a roadside fireworks stand, and by age 14, he was

[[Page S5246]]

assisting at his dad's magazine and book business, which had grown from 
a roadside newsstand to an entire storefront.
  Charles didn't just learn discipline and determination from working 
alongside his father. He was a star athlete, leader, and student. 
During his time at Coffee High School, he earned various accolades, 
including recognition as the youngest football player ever selected to 
the First Team All Tri-Cities Football squad and the All-Tennessee 
Valley Conference squad, cocaptain of the football team, and, to no 
one's surprise, was even named a senior class favorite at Coffee High 
School. His whole class looked up to him, a trend that would follow 
Charles wherever he went.
  After earning his bachelor of science in marketing and minoring in 
economics from Florence State Teachers College, now the University of 
North Alabama, Charles joined the family business full-time. Just as he 
had done as a child, Charles dreamed big, worked tirelessly, and 
trusted his vision. Soon, that family business had turned into a wide-
ranging network of successful enterprises, including Anderson Media, 
Anderson Press, Books-A-Million, and TNT Fireworks. And in 1987, 
Charles became the chairman of ANCO Management Services, eventually 
becoming chairman emeritus and providing mentorship and oversight to 
his beloved family-founded enterprises.
  Even more impressive than Charles' professional career was his 
service to the community that helped fuel his success. He gave his time 
and expertise freely, serving in multiple board capacities, including 
the U.S. Space and Rocket Center, Heritage Trust Fund, the First 
National Bank of Florence (now Truist), the Alabama Kidney Foundation 
of Alabama, the Salvation Army of the Shoals, the Shoals Economic 
Development Authority Board, and for nearly a decade, he was the 
executive chairman of the board of First Southern Bank. In 2026, 
Charles received the prestigious Life Membership award from the 
Salvation Army for his unwavering commitment to the organization. He 
was a valued and dedicated member of Westminster Presbyterian Church, 
where his life was celebrated by the many who knew and loved him.
  Charles also gave generously to various causes and organizations, 
from educational institutions and libraries to hospitals and medical 
research facilities. He was the benefactor of several scholarships, 
including the Clyde W. Anderson Scholarship at the University of North 
Alabama, named for his father, and a business scholarship at the 
University of Alabama. Proof of his generosity is scattered throughout 
our State--whether at the University of Northern Alabama's Anderson 
College of Nursing and Health Professions and Hilda B. Anderson 
Softball Stadium, the University of Tennessee's Anderson Athletic 
Training Center and Anderson Center for Entrepreneurship and 
Innovation, or at the University of Alabama at Birmingham Medical 
Center's Hilda B. Anderson Endowed Chair in Nephrology. The Charles and 
Hilda Anderson Foundation and the Anderson Foundation both gave 
abundantly to the causes of literacy, education, healthcare, 
entrepreneurship, and expansion of opportunity. His philanthropy 
showcased his spirit of giving, but Charles never asked to be 
recognized for his generosity; he gave only because he believed that 
blessings ought to be shared. The Anderson family creed truly 
exemplifies Charles' ethos: ``There is no limit to what you can 
accomplish if you don't care who gets the credit.''
  The great admiration Charles earned from his community and our entire 
State can be seen through several honors in his namesake. He was 
inducted into multiple prestigious associations, including the Alabama 
Business Hall of Fame, the Alabama Academy of Honor, and the Horatio 
Alger Association. Charles was named the 1979 University of North 
Alabama Alumni of the Year, the 2016 Shoals Chamber of Commerce Citizen 
of the Year, listed as one of the 2021 Most Influential People in 
Numismatics by Coin World Magazine, named an honorary life fellow of 
the American Numismatic Society, and named the 2024 Numismatist of the 
Century by COINage Magazine. In 2016, he was a Walk of Honor inductee, 
selected for his national and international acclaim among individuals 
in Florence and Lauderdale Counties. In 2018, the University of North 
Alabama awarded him an honorary doctorate degree recognizing his 
extraordinary achievements and his commitment to excellence. In 2025, 
Charles was honored by Business Alabama as one of five recipients of 
the Business Alabama Lifetime Achievement Award.
  Even with all of these great honors, it is hard to capture the 
spirit, kindness, and leadership that Charles exhibited each and every 
day. His family, friends, and each person who was fortunate enough to 
know him were witnesses to his extraordinary life of philanthropy, 
leadership, lifelong knowledge, and a deep love for his loved ones. His 
legacy will last for generations to come and touch the lives of so 
many.
  Today, we honor the life of a man who exemplifies the American Dream 
and has been a pillar of Alabama for decades. From a simple roadside 
newspaper stand--built from piano boxes and manned by a father and his 
5-year-old son--to a global success story with tens of thousands 
impacted by his generosity and leadership, Charles Anderson lived a 
life worth sharing, which I am humbled to amplify in this body. Along 
with his loved ones, the Florence community, and our entire State, I am 
honored to celebrate him here today.●
```
