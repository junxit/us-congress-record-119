# RATEPAYER PROTECTION ACT

**Speaking:** Van Hollen, Chris (V000128) [D-MD]

```
                        RATEPAYER PROTECTION ACT

  Mr. VAN HOLLEN. Mr. President, I cannot vote for a bill that promises 
protection but lacks the enforcement mechanisms needed to deliver on 
lowering Americans' electric bills. To add insult to injury, 
Republicans pushed for a vote on this without even taking the 
opportunity to consider real solutions, like my Power for the People 
Act.
  The Ratepayer Protection Act relies solely on a process that would 
ask States to consider implementing data center-specific rate classes, 
without any guarantee that these rate classes would be implemented and 
without any

[[Page S5199]]

of the additional measures needed to ensure that consumers are 
protected from rising electric bills caused by skyrocketing energy 
demand from data centers.
  Americans are facing an affordability crisis from the grocery store 
to the gas pump, and they deserve real solutions, which is why I filed 
my bill, the Power for the People Act, as an amendment to this bill. 
The Power for the People Act includes meaningful reforms that would 
actually hold data centers accountable for driving up energy prices, 
something that the Ratepayer Protection Act purports to do but cannot 
achieve on its own. Unfortunately, Republicans have provided no 
guarantees that we would consider amendments to the Ratepayer 
Protection Act.
  This body has repeatedly failed to address the real concerns of 
Americans struggling to make ends meet, with Republicans prioritizing 
tax breaks for the wealthy and big corporations and Trump's golden age 
for billionaires. Now the majority is claiming to address a real 
problem with the bill before us, with a solution that falls far short.
  The energy demand of data centers is unlike anything we have dealt 
with in recent history and our solutions need to meet the moment. My 
Power for the People Act protects consumers, creates good-paying jobs, 
and mitigates harm to the environment, but we have no assurance that it 
could be considered. Without the opportunity to provide amendments, the 
bill before us today is an empty gesture, leaving Americans shouldering 
Big Tech's costs with no relief.

                          ____________________
```
