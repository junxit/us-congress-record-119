# RATEPAYER PROTECTION ACT

**Speaking:** Husted, Jon (H001104) [R-OH]

**Measures:** S. 5028

```
                        RATEPAYER PROTECTION ACT

  Mr. HUSTED. Mr. President, I rise today to speak about making life 
more affordable by lowering electricity prices through my bill, the 
Ratepayer Protection Act.
  Americans are increasingly realigned on the computing power of data 
centers in nearly every part of their daily lives--from medicine and 
manufacturing to agriculture, education, national defense, and the 
delivery of basic services.
  As our reliance on this technology grows, so does the demand for data 
centers across the country. But more computing power requires more 
electricity, and when larger data centers come online, the cost of 
providing that power cannot be pushed onto everyone else. We have got 
to protect the American ratepayer, the American customer.
  We have to make sure that the growth of these data centers does not 
mean higher electric bills for working families, for senior citizens, 
or for small businesses.

[[Page S5193]]

  The companies driving this growth should be held responsible for the 
power they need to operate, not the American ratepayer. That is what we 
are talking about today: making the data centers accountable, not the 
American ratepayer.
  We need to win the global race for artificial intelligence--I don't 
think that anyone is disputing that fact--and we should not do it, 
though, by forcing American families and businesses to subsidize Big 
Tech.
  That is why I have introduced the Ratepayer Protection Act. My bill 
ensures that large data centers pay their fair share of electricity 
costs, infrastructure costs that they require, requiring them to own 
the responsibility for those costs, rather than shifting them to other 
ratepayers.
  I have been focused on this issue for a long time, since my work in 
State government, where I pushed Big Tech companies to enter into fair 
agreements with communities where they were building.
  In March, I went to the White House to watch the President sign the 
Ratepayer Protection Pledge. This summer, I took it a step further by 
introducing S. 5028, which we are talking about today, to turn that 
commitment into law.
  We can support American innovation. America is an innovative country. 
We must remain an innovative country and lead the world in 
superintelligence and build the infrastructure we need for the future 
without asking families, seniors, and small businesses to pick up the 
tab.
  So, today, we are considering the House-passed version of my bill 
before this Chamber. It passed the House 417 to 3. That is why we are 
here--to focus on this bill--because no other bill exists, other than 
this one, that can allow us to get this job done today.
  The Ratepayer Protection Act outlines a standard for data centers to 
pay their own way for electricity and the grid and the infrastructure, 
and it requires State utility regulators to consider this standard to 
ensure that large-load customers pay the full incremental costs of the 
infrastructure needed to serve them--pay for it all. The States can't 
avoid this. They are going to have to confront the issue. Our bill will 
require them to do that.
  Those standards could also require financial assurances so that 
families, communities, and small businesses are not left paying for 
stranded infrastructure costs from a project that they say they are 
going to build, but it gets delayed or abandoned.
  A lot of times, they pass that cost along to everyone else. No, this 
legislation would help make sure that that doesn't happen.
  Last week, I brought the Ratepayer Protection Act to this very Senate 
floor and asked for unanimous passage. Why? Because it passed the House 
417 to 3. Why delay? Why not get this done immediately?
  Well, I can't answer that. You will have to ask those who blocked it 
because it is ready to go. It could be law today if we just decide that 
we are going to look out for the American ratepayer, and we are going 
to take action to do so.
  As I mentioned, it passed the House 417 to 3. That is almost 
unprecedented in this era of politics and governing here in Congress. 
That means that over 99 percent of the Representatives agreed my bill 
was worth passing. Let that sink in. Ninety-nine percent of the House 
of Representatives cast a vote in favor of the Ratepayer Protection Act 
becoming law.
  Among those who voted yes on the Democratic side were Representatives 
Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, Ilhan Omar, minority leader Hakeem Jeffries, 
former Speaker Nancy Pelosi, and every single Member of the House 
Democratic leadership. They all supported the bill in the House.
  This bill had unanimous support from House Republicans, from Speaker 
Johnson to the Freedom Caucus. All of them supported my bill.
  You can't get any broader support than that, folks, in Washington, 
DC, in this day and age.
  The Ratepayer Protection Act is such a no-brainer that the most 
conservative and the most liberal Representatives all agreed it was 
good policy. They all agreed. The House of Representatives passed it. 
It is here. It is ready to go. All we have to do is take yes for an 
answer, and we will begin today at lowering electricity rates for 
American ratepayers.
  The American people have spoken loud and clear about this issue. They 
want the data centers to be held accountable. We have a chance to do 
that, to lower their electric rates. Let's take advantage of this 
wonderful opportunity we have to build bipartisan support around this 
bill.
  It is clear that Big Tech should be paying their own way, not passing 
the cost onto local communities and States. This bill does that. It 
does that.
  In a few moments, my colleagues and I will vote on the Ratepayer 
Protection Act. Together, we can send this bill to the President's desk 
for his signature today and prove to the American people we heard them; 
that we are doing something to actually help lower their prices--the 
prices that they pay for electricity.
  The Ratepayer Protection Act represents the most meaningful 
bipartisan step Congress can take to protect the American people from 
paying higher prices for electricity. It demonstrates that in 
Washington, it is still possible to work in a bipartisan way to address 
the problems most pressing to the American people.
  And I am proud to have led this effort in the Senate. Let's rise 
above the politics that often drag this place down and deliver for the 
American people and do our jobs.
  But I want to be clear about something, this bill--this bill--is the 
only path that we can take today that will lower electricity prices. No 
other options remain. This is it. This is the bill that can get it 
done. It is ready to go.
  All we have to do in this Chamber is say yes and it will be on the 
President's desk and we can prove to the American people we actually 
care about what they are asking us to do to lower their electricity 
prices. Any obstruction to that is to say no to them, that you don't 
want to do it.

  I listened to the Democratic leader talk about this bill a few 
moments ago, and I implore him: Stop blocking this bill. Stop blocking 
this bill. Let it go. Give us a chance, on a bipartisan basis, to prove 
that we care about what the American people are asking us to do: 
lowering their prices; making life more affordable; in this case, 
lowering their electricity prices. Give the American people what they 
want: lower energy prices and holding data centers accountable.
  I will conclude with this because I would like to get this done on a 
bipartisan basis, and I would like to be able for everybody in this 
Chamber to take credit for lowering electricity prices and holding data 
centers accountable. But if Democrats block this bill, they will prove, 
beyond a shadow of a doubt, that they do not care about affordability 
and that they will not do anything to take action to help. That is what 
blocking this bill will prove to the American people.
  Let's listen. Let's get this done. Let's lower electricity prices. 
Let's hold data centers accountable. And let's stop blocking a path to 
affordability.
  I yield the floor.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from Tennessee.

                          ____________________
```
