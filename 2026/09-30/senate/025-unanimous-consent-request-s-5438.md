# UNANIMOUS CONSENT REQUEST--S. 5438

**Speaking:** Sullivan, Dan (S001198) [R-AK], Heinrich, Martin (H001046) [D-NM]

**Measures:** S. 5438

```
                   UNANIMOUS CONSENT REQUEST--S. 5438

  Mr. SULLIVAN. Mr. President, today, I urge my colleagues to vote for 
Alaska's Right to Produce Act 2.0, which would provide greater 
certainty for responsible resource development on the North Slope of 
Alaska. It would benefit Alaskans, and it would benefit our great 
working Alaskans, but it would benefit America.
  As a little bit of background here--and you have heard me talk about 
this--for years, Alaska, as a resource development powerhouse for 
America, has been recognized by Congress to be so important for our 
country because of our extraordinary abundance of natural resources 
that we have legislated many times about our State's getting

[[Page S5196]]

resources to market for Alaskans, for Americans. And it is strongly in 
our national interest and certainly good for my State and the people I 
am honored to represent, particularly hard-working union members and 
others, who develop these resources not just for Alaska but for 
America.
  However, time and time again, when my Democratic colleagues get 
control of the Senate, of the House, of the White House, they have a 
core principle. One of their core principles is to come up to the great 
State of Alaska and try to shut it down. They kill thousands of jobs 
and try to turn this great State--my State--into a national park, with 
no ability to develop our economy and resources.
  This is just the latest example of what I call the Last Frontier 
lockup.
  During the Biden 4 years and the Senator Schumer 4 years, when he was 
the majority leader, the far-left Democrats--heck, all of the 
Democrats--came after Alaska in a huge way: the Last Frontier lockup. 
In every part of the State--southeast Alaska, the North Slope of 
Alaska--everywhere--on resource development, they came after us. How 
did they do that? What was the Last Frontier lockup? It is pretty hard 
to imagine.
  It was 70 Executive orders and Executive actions--70--exclusively and 
singularly focused on crushing Alaska--seven, zero. As a matter of 
fact, on day one of the Biden administration, they issued 12. They were 
ready to go. The Senate Democrats got power, and the Democrats got 
power in the executive branch, and they were, like: Let's go. Let's do 
what we always do. Let's crush Alaska. Let's kill thousands of jobs in 
Alaska.
  When this was at, like, 50, I actually met with President Biden and 
his team.
  I said: Mr. President, what are you doing? What are you doing? You 
are sanctioning my State more than you sanction Iran's and Venezuela's 
oil and gas sectors. We are Americans, and they are terrorists, and you 
are putting thousands of hard-working Alaskans out of jobs. Why are you 
doing this, sir?
  Three years ago, I did that with President Biden. Don't even get me 
going on his answer, but he was clueless. As we all know, he was 
clueless on a lot of stuff, but he was clueless on that--70. The White 
House--the executive branch--I don't think, in the history of America, 
has ever used its power to so forcefully single out one State to try to 
damage it and crush it. That is what we went through with the senior 
Senator from New York as the majority leader, who was all in on it, by 
the way.
  We are going to see one of the top guys who is always against Alaska 
here in a minute, because I know he is going to object to my bill. So 
it is pretty crazy that they would do that. Imagine any other State--
New Mexico--if the Federal Government said: Well, we are just going to 
go crush New Mexico.
  But they did it to Alaska.
  And it wasn't just the 70 Executive orders. There is LNG. Remember 
John Kerry, the climate czar guy and former Senator? He went over to 
Asia--Korea, Japan--and told them: Don't buy Alaskan LNG. Don't buy 
American LNG.
  I mean, this is almost treasonous, but that is what they did. For 4 
years, with Schumer and Biden, that is what my State got. But we have 
taken action. When Republicans gained control of the Senate and gained 
control of the House and gained control of the White House, we said: We 
are going to put Alaska back on top.
  So what did we do? We attacked the Democrats' attacks on my State, 
which, again, hurt workers and hurt Alaska but hurt America in a couple 
of different ways.
  First, last year, in the Working Families Tax Cut Act, we got 
mandatory lease sales for the next 10 years for NPR-A, the National 
Petroleum Reserve in Alaska; for ANWR; for Cook Inlet. These are all 
Federal oil and gas basins.
  We said--Congress said--hey, regardless of who is President in the 
coming 10 years, they have to do lease sales in Alaska.
  It is good for the country. It is good for Alaska. It is good for our 
workers. It is good for our families. It is good in terms of reducing 
energy costs, for goodness sakes.
  So we got that in the bill. That is in the law. If you have a 
President like Biden, who is anti-Alaska, and they come into office 2 
years from now, they are not going to be able to lock it up like Joe 
Biden did.
  Secondly, we passed Congressional Review Act resolutions. I led the 
effort on some of them. The Biden NPR-A rule locked down this entire 
area, the National Petroleum Reserve of Alaska, set aside by Congress--
for what?--for oil and gas development. That is why it is the National 
Petroleum Reserve. We ripped that Biden rule out by the roots with a 
CRA here in the Senate, and we did the same with the Biden lockup of 
ANWR, and those got passed into law.
  So it is a two-pronged strategy to prevent, unfortunately, my 
colleagues, which they always do--my Democrat colleagues in the Senate, 
in the House, and in the White House, if they gain power, which they 
will eventually--from coming back and trying to shut my State down, 
which they always do.

  Today, the Alaska's Right to Produce Act 2.0 is the third prong of 
this strategy. First, we got mandatory lease sales from the Working 
Families Tax Cut Act. Then we got these Congressional Review Act 
resolutions passed and signed into law to prevent more lockups of 
Alaska. This is the third prong.
  Now, this bill has some history. In 2024, the House passed the 
Alaska's Right to Produce Act 1.0. The bill was sponsored by my good 
friend from Minnesota in the House, Representative Stauber, who has 
done so much to help my State. Five Democrat House Members voted for 
it, despite the original cosponsor of that bill--who represented Alaska 
at the time--changing her mind and urging her Democratic colleagues to 
vote against her own bill to help Alaska.
  Wow, you can't make that up. But this is nothing new, as I have said, 
for Democrats in DC.
  A lot of Democrats in Alaska don't agree with shutting down Alaska, 
but it is here in the Senate where they have written over 16 letters to 
administration officials--Democrats and Republicans--opposing ANWR 
development and NPR-A development. They wrote six separate letters to 
insurers and financial institutions--believe it or not--using the power 
of the Senate to write the CEOs of banks and insurance companies: Don't 
invest in Alaska.
  It is an abusive use of power. My colleague from New Mexico usually 
leads those efforts, by the way. They have tried to shut down our State 
not just legislatively but by pressuring the private sector to not 
invest in Alaska. Still, five House Democratic colleagues understood 
how important Alaska was, and they voted for the Alaska's Right to 
Produce Act and bucked the pressure from far-left Democrats in the 
House, including from the original cosponsor of the bill.
  To them, I say thank you. Alaska says thank you. Our workers say 
thank you, and the country applauds your bravery.
  So what does this bill do?
  It is simple. For the coastal plain of ANWR, it protects oil and gas 
development in the nonwilderness portion of the coastal plain from 
being shut down or being reversed by a future administration. In the 
National Petroleum Reserve of Alaska, it similarly protects oil and gas 
development in NPR-A from a shutdown by a future administration. The 
bill is designed to give companies greater certainty in terms of the 
leases that they already have in these areas in that they can't be 
canceled.
  The bottom line is that this is the third prong of our ability to get 
Alaska to be developed again, and it does something pretty simple: It 
protects Alaska's existing oil and gas development opportunities from 
being shut down by a future administration.
  Now, it is a little ironic and maybe fitting that the Senator from 
New Mexico is going to object to my bill. I am pretty sure he is not 
here to support it. He has been a leader in shutting down Alaska. He 
has led these efforts. He has led these letters.
  Can you imagine if every U.S. Senator said, ``I am going to go after 
New Mexico. I am going to go after their workers. I am going to go try 
and hurt the Native people of their State. I am going to write all of 
the insurance companies and the banks and say, `Don't invest in New 
Mexico' ''? Can you imagine what a disaster this place would be?
  I wouldn't do that, but he does it. He does it all the time.

[[Page S5197]]

  And there is a little irony here. When the Biden administration and 
Senator Schumer were locking up my State for 4 years, it was ``drill, 
baby, drill'' in New Mexico. They were shutting down my State, led by 
the Senator from New Mexico, and New Mexico, on Federal lands, went 
from 1 million barrels of oil a day to 2 million barrels a day. If that 
is not hypocrisy, I don't know what is. But that is what we are up 
against.
  By the way, if the Senate changes hands and the Senator from New 
Mexico is going to be chairman of the Energy and Natural Resources 
Committee--holy cow--my State will be crushed because that is what he 
does. So I am pretty sure he is going to object to this.
  But, you know, I get a little hot about this issue because I think it 
is unfair. And I think, when people treat their State way differently 
than my State--imagine what they were doing: Hey, Joe Biden, shut down 
Alaska, but, by the way, make sure you approve all of these Federal 
leases in New Mexico so we can ``drill, baby, drill.''
  That is exactly what the Senator from New Mexico did.
  But here is why I get really hot about this. It goes beyond resource 
development. It goes beyond jobs. It goes to this chart. I have brought 
this chart out many times. This is a chart that depicts the life 
expectancy in America from 1980 to 2014 and the parts of America where 
people live longer. Now, you will see here that it is a little 
complicated of a chart. The blue and the purple are where people are 
living longer. In the green here, that is 13 years over a 25-year 
period. Red is where we have actually lost life expectancy really, 
really bad.
  But look at this map from 1980 to 2014. Unfortunately, we had a 
couple of places in America where people weren't living very long or 
were losing life expectancy. That was kind of the opioid epidemic and 
other things. But the place where people lived longer for that 25-year 
period in more than any other State was in Alaska--Alaska. Right here 
in the North Slope, in the Aleutian Islands chain, in the southeast, 
and in the Northwest Arctic Borough, people were living 13 years 
longer.
  I have asked my colleagues many times to give me a policy indicator 
more important of success than that of the people you are representing 
living longer. I don't think there is one.
  So there is huge success up here in my State.
  Now, why did that happen? Why are the people of Alaska living longer?
  Well, in part, a sad story is that a lot of Native Alaskans, 
primarily the Inupiat people--great people, by the way, incredible 
patriots--have a very low life expectancy, some of the lowest life 
expectancy in the country by far.
  But when we started developing our resources--oil and gas on the 
North Slope--people got jobs; people got hospitals; people got flush 
toilets; people got health clinics and hospitals--things that the rest 
of the country assumes that you have but we didn't have here.
  So this issue, for me, is about the life and death of my 
constituents. So when I have colleagues come and they block all this 
stuff, and yet they do it for their own State, which is really 
problematic, it riles me up because I am talking about life and death 
of my constituents.
  By the way, the greater Inupiat people here, during the Biden 
administration, the head of the Tribe, the head of the Alaska Native 
Corporation, the head of the borough--all the leaders, all Native 
Alaskans--came down to Washington, DC, eight times to try to meet with 
the Secretary of the Interior to say: Don't lock up our land. You will 
hurt us.
  Do you know how many times she met with them? Zero. Zero. And then 
they locked up their land anyway, canceling Native voices.
  So I am really hopeful, because of life expectancy, because of jobs, 
because of lowering healthcare costs, because of lowering costs of 
energy, because of our national security, that my colleagues will do 
the right thing and pass my legislation, the Alaska's Right to Produce 
Act 2.0, right now.
  Mr. President, I ask unanimous consent that the Senate proceed to the 
immediate consideration of Calendar No. 678, S. 5438; further, that the 
bill be considered read a third time and passed and that the motion to 
reconsider be considered made and laid upon the table.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Is there objection?
  The Senator from New Mexico.
  Mr. HEINRICH. Mr. President, reserving the right to object, nestled 
in the far northeast corner of Alaska is the Arctic National Wildlife 
Refuge--not ANWR, which sounds like some country in the Middle East, 
but the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge, one of the wildest and most 
untouched places in all of America. It is our land. It belongs to every 
single American. It is public land set aside by President Eisenhower to 
protect our amazing native wildlife.
  I have been blessed to see this refuge with my own eyes. I have seen 
the critical habitat on the coastal plain where caribou drop their 
calves. I have met with Alaskans whose way of life is sustained by 
those same caribou. The Arctic Refuge is also home to hundreds of 
iconic wildlife species, including 200 species of birds that have 
called this landscape home since long before humans even stepped on the 
continent. But that is only part of the story. It is also deeply 
connected to the traditions and daily life of people who have depended 
on that wildlife for thousands of years.
  Now, that said, the Arctic Refuge's future is increasingly at risk. 
In the past year, we have seen relentless attacks on the Arctic Refuge 
from both the Trump administration and my Republican colleagues here in 
the Congress.
  Today, the Senator from Alaska is trying to force through another 
bill that would once again prioritize oil and gas over literally 
everything else, and he is doing so by using a procedural tactic to 
bypass the legislative process.
  Now, let me be clear. The Energy and Natural Resources Committee has 
not held a single hearing on this legislation despite being chaired by 
the Republicans; nor have we reported it out of the committee.
  But here is what I can tell you already. This bill is deeply 
unpopular. Even the oil and gas majors themselves don't want to drill 
in the Arctic Refuge. And that is because development in the refuge is 
complicated, it is expensive, and it carries enormous reputational risk 
for these corporations. So to incentivize drilling there, this bill 
waives revenue requirements, meaning that the oil and gas industry pays 
less and the taxpayer gets less.
  I have been around long enough to remember a time when this same 
Republican Party promised the American people that they would get $1 
billion--billion with a ``b''--from these lease sales in the Arctic 
Refuge. Right now, those lease sales have secured $9 million. They seem 
to be missing a few zeroes--especially when it is sacrificing priceless 
public lands.
  I came to the Senate floor back in December when Republicans first 
overturned the management plan for the Arctic Refuge to warn that in 
order for drilling in the Arctic to be economical, oil prices would 
have to be at least $75 a barrel, just to break even on oil from the 
refuge.
  Well, with full control of the House, the Senate, and the White 
House, my colleagues have managed to make that math finally work. 
Thanks to the war in Iran, today, oil prices are hovering near $100 a 
barrel. We all feel that at the pump. I guess the President finally got 
his energy emergency, and it is one of his own creation.
  The reality is that oil from the Arctic Refuge won't solve the global 
instability that was caused by waging an illegal war in Iran. What it 
will cause is devastating impacts on that landscape, forever changing 
this landscape and the wildlife that depend on it, which is why his 
bill also waives all the environmental laws and deems environmental 
studies complete.
  So there won't be an accounting of the impacts this development would 
have on the caribou, whose 1,500-mile migration ends there, or the 
hundreds of species that live there, like the polar bear and musk ox, 
or the people who rely on the area for subsistence, which is why this 
bill also prohibits future litigation on oil and gas authorization.
  The bill does not stop with the Arctic Refuge. It would also ensure 
that subsistence rights and conservation on some of the most unique and 
special public lands in the western Arctic could never be evaluated 
again. It paves the way for a management framework where automatic 
approvals

[[Page S5198]]

of hundreds of fossil fuel projects are not only possible, they are 
guaranteed.

  We could be discussing ways to lower electricity costs, how to bring 
down the price of diesel, but, instead, here we are again trying to 
drill in a national wildlife refuge.
  I know there are some corners of the universe that believe this is 
part of a secret plan to promote New Mexico energy at the expense of 
Alaska energy. I want to be clear. I support oil and gas development in 
the right places. As the Senator from Alaska has stated many times, New 
Mexico does its part. But I don't support drilling everywhere. I asked 
the Biden administration to set aside Chaco Canyon for that very 
reason, and I especially do not support drilling in the crown jewel of 
America's National Wildlife System. Therefore, I do object.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The objection is heard.
  The Senator from Alaska.

respond.

  Without objection, it is so ordered.
  Mr. SULLIVAN. Mr. President, there has been no place that has been 
studied more than the ANWR 1002 plain in terms of environmental laws 
and environmental impacts, probably in the entire United States. This 
is something strongly supported by the people I represent, including 
the people that actually live there, which is something the Senator 
from New Mexico constantly forgets. The people I represent actually 
support this and the other opportunities to develop our resources.
  One of the reasons there was very little lease activity when we 
passed the ANWR bill recently is because the Biden administration 
didn't execute on it. They canceled the leases. A Federal judge held 
that against the law, so it was lawless action once we passed the bill.
  But, again, one of the things my colleague never talks about is that 
during the 4 years of the Biden administration and the 4 years of the 
majority being led by Senator Schumer, the attacks on Alaska were 
constant from all of them. The Last Frontier lockup: 70 Executive 
orders--a lot led by the Senator from New Mexico. But it was ``drill, 
baby, drill'' in New Mexico. Over 50 percent of all Federal leases on 
all Federal lands during the Biden administration were to drill for oil 
and gas in New Mexico, and the Senator from New Mexico was fine with 
that. They go from 1 million barrels to 2 million barrels a day. But he 
comes and wants to shut down my State. If that is not the definition of 
a hypocrisy, I don't know what is.
  So I am disappointed, but I am not surprised. We will continue to do 
what Alaskans want but what Americans want, and that is responsibly 
develop the resources of my great State to help the people of the North 
Slope who want it.
  When the Senator from New Mexico talks about--
```
