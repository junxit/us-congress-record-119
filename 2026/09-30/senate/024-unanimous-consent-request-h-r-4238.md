# UNANIMOUS CONSENT REQUEST--H.R. 4238

**Speaking:** Budd, Ted (B001305) [R-NC], Markey, Edward J. (M000133) [D-MA]

**Measures:** H.R. 4238

```
                  UNANIMOUS CONSENT REQUEST--H.R. 4238

  Mr. BUDD. Mr. President, the most basic responsibility of Congress is 
to identify problems and deliver policy solutions that better serve the 
American people. That means cutting unnecessary redtape, eliminating 
confusion, and fixing gaps in the Federal Government that prevent it 
from doing its job effectively.
  And that responsibility is often put to the test when disaster 
strikes. In Western North Carolina, Hurricane Helene did more than 
devastate our communities--it exposed weaknesses in our Federal 
disaster response that we, here in Congress, have a responsibility to 
fix.
  Any Senator who has served their State in the wake of a natural 
disaster knows that bureaucratic failures are not just problems on 
paper--they have real consequences for real people. Every unnecessary 
delay, outdated process, or a gap in our response can stand between a 
devastated community and the Federal assistance that it needs to 
recover and to rebuild.
  I understand that reality all too well, having walked the road to 
recovery with the people of Western North Carolina who lost everything 
in a once-in-a-century--hopefully, more than a century--storm.
  In this rural part of our State, small businesses drive local 
economies, and many were destroyed and shuttered after Helene. For 
many, their only hope of getting back on their feet was by accessing 
disaster loan assistance through the SBA, or the Small Business 
Administration.
  But in 2024, when these small businesses were trying to secure a 
fighting chance, the Biden administration--they dropped the ball by 
gravely mismanaging the SBA's Disaster Loan Program.

[[Page S5195]]

  By issuing irresponsible rules, drastically increasing the costs of 
making and administering these disaster loans, and failing to notify 
Congress that funding to administer these loans was running low, the 
Biden administration allowed for a 66-day shortfall that impacted 97 
percent of North Carolina residents. We can't allow this to happen 
again.
  So I am bringing a commonsense solution to the table that has 
received bipartisan support in the Small Business Committee. My 
Disaster Loan Accountability and Reform Act will create more safeguards 
against a future shortfall by requiring annual appropriations to the 
SBA's Disaster Loan Program and informing Congress when these funds are 
running low.
  The bill has one clear goal, and that is to make sure our Nation's 
small businesses are never left without the disaster assistance that 
they were promised.

  The next major disaster should not expose the same failures as the 
last. It is our responsibility to mend broken systems and to coordinate 
the Federal Government and its agencies so that we are ready to manage 
the aftermath of the next major disaster.
  So I ask my colleagues to join me in taking a necessary step forward 
in improving our Federal disaster response by voting to pass this bill.
  I ask unanimous consent that the Senate proceed to the immediate 
consideration of Calendar No. 448, H.R. 4238; further, I ask that the 
bill be considered read a third time and passed; and that the motion to 
reconsider be considered made and laid upon the table.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Is there objection?
  The Senator from Massachusetts.
  Mr. MARKEY. Let me just begin by saying I agree with Senator Budd 
about the importance of transparency and of accountability in our 
disaster programs.
  But I cannot support this legislation while the Trump administration 
politicizes and withholds aid from disaster victims in blue States, 
including my own home State of Massachusetts.
  Recent reporting revealed that Trump overrode the recommendation of 
his own Agency and prevented four Democratic States--including 
Massachusetts--from receiving disaster assistance related to blizzards 
earlier this year. So parts of my own home State got nearly 41 inches 
of snow in 24 hours, costing our State over $45 million in blizzard-
related expenses.
  And according to this report, the States easily met FEMA's internal 
qualifications for disaster aid. But for Trump, the only qualification 
is blind loyalty.
  And this is not an isolated incident. While red States' FEMA disaster 
applications have been approved 89 percent of the time, those from blue 
States receive signoff from the Trump administration only 23 percent of 
the time.
  But don't think you are safe just because you are in a State that 
voted for the President. Earlier this month, at a rally in North 
Carolina for Michael Whatley--Trump's hand-picked MAGA Senate 
candidate--President Trump told a community devastated by Hurricane 
Helene--here's what the President said this month in North Carolina: 
You know you're waiting for some additional funding. Now, if Michael 
loses, I'm not sending it to North Carolina.
  What? The people of North Carolina should get the help. It shouldn't 
depend upon whether the Senator is a Democrat or Republican, whether it 
is blue or red. It is about Americans who have suffered a disaster.
  And the numbers are incredible. This is the history, since Ronald 
Reagan, of disaster relief in the United States. And you can see that 
it tracks pretty much red and blue the whole way--Reagan, George Bush 
1, Clinton, Bush 2, Obama, Trump 1--stayed the same. Money went out in 
equal amounts to red and blue States, Democrat and Republican States.
  But then Trump won again, and you can see what is happening. In the 
blue States, it looks like the 1929 stock market crash. We go from 
being equal with Republican States to falling off the table, and you 
can't really treat disasters that way. They are Americans. It could be 
a flood; it could be a hurricane, a tornado. It doesn't make any 
difference. They are all our American neighbors. They deserve the 
Federal assistance.
  The President, again as recently as this month, is saying in North 
Carolina: If you elect a Democratic Senator, you won't get the help.
  That is just not right, and we all know that is not right. So that 
has to get rectified because that is really not cooperation; that is 
just corruption, right? That is just saying: We're going to take this 
Federal program and turn it into something where he gets arbitrarily to 
determine whether or not he dishes out the money.
  And it doesn't really make any difference to me if it is Oklahoma or 
North Carolina or Massachusetts or California; everyone who suffers a 
disaster should get the Federal help they need.
  The President just cannot be allowed to use essential disaster aid as 
a tool for political extortion. Yet there he is, he is making it plain 
as day that if you don't vote for a Republican Senator, you don't get 
any money in your State for a disaster which has devastated your 
people.
  We just have to make sure that we stand up and, if we are serious 
about transparency and accountability in the disaster recovery process, 
we should hold hearings, demand answers from this administration about 
why blue States are denied help at such a disproportionate rate.
  And if the Trump administration insists on putting MAGA before the 
many, Congress must force the administration to treat disaster victims 
fairly, regardless of the State where they live or who they voted for.
  I want to work with the Senator from North Carolina to address the 
administration's unacceptable treatment of disaster victims in 
Massachusetts and other blue States. We just have to get it back to the 
historic norm. The Senator has identified a problem, and I have worked 
with him in the committee to help to move that legislation.
  But this is in many ways an even bigger problem. We cannot have this 
disparate treatment to continue any longer. So because of that, but 
with my request that I work with the gentleman as we head into the 
lameduck session, that we can work to talk to the Trump administration 
about rectifying this historic injustice.
  So, at this time, as a result, I must object to the Senator's 
request.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The objection is heard.
  The Senator from North Carolina.
  Mr. BUDD. Mr. President, I understand my colleague is having an issue 
with disaster relief reaching his State. On that, I can certainly 
relate.
  And by the way, this is a colleague who has worked to pass this 
unanimously, I believe, out of committee--so much appreciated there--
but now seeks to block the bill.
  But I worked with the Trump administration to cut through the 
bureaucratic hurdles that had slowed down aid. I held a number of the 
administration's nominees to put a stop to them, put a pause on them, 
to ensure that my message was heard.
  But what I did not do was hold my fellow Senator's legislation from 
potential passage or hinder efforts to make our disaster program run 
more smoothly.
  I hope my colleague can now see how this bill would positively impact 
both my State and his State and do what is right for the American 
people by passing this bill in which he voted for, again, out of 
committee.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from Alaska.
```
