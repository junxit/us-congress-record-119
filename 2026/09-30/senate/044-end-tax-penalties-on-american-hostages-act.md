# END TAX PENALTIES ON AMERICAN HOSTAGES ACT

**Speaking:** Coons, Christopher A. (C001088) [D-DE], Hoeven, John (H001061) [R-ND], Heinrich, Martin (H001046) [D-NM], Cortez Masto, Catherine (C001113) [D-NV], Moreno, Bernie (M001242) [R-OH], Barrasso, John (B001261) [R-WY], Schumer, Charles E. (S000148) [D-NY]

**Measures:** H.R. 1829, H.R. 2252, S. 5681, H.R. 9496

```
               END TAX PENALTIES ON AMERICAN HOSTAGES ACT

  Mr. COONS. Mr. President, I wonder if you have heard the story of 
Jason Rezaian. Jason was a Washington Post reporter--an Iranian 
American--who was stationed in Tehran and reporting for the Washington 
Post on the goings-on of the Islamic Republic of Iran.
  In 2014, he was arrested by the secret police and disappeared. Months 
later, it was revealed that he was a political prisoner being held at a 
notorious prison inside Iran. Jason was ultimately detained for 544 
days before he was released, which he has recounted in his harrowing 
tale, ``Prisoner: My 544 Days in an Iranian Prison.''
  When Jason returned to the United States at last, having been freed, 
returned to his family, what was the first thing he got from the U.S. 
Government? A tax bill, a tax bill for $20,000 for late filing and late 
payment.
  Mr. Rezaian is a resourceful guy. He went to the IRS, and he said: 
Hey, how can you charge me for late payment, penalties, and fees? I was 
in prison in Iran.
  They said: Well, how are we supposed to know that?

[[Page S5239]]

  It was on the front page of the Washington Post.
  They said: Well, we can't just forgive fees and fines for anybody who 
comes and talks to us.
  They went through months and months of arguing back and forth, and he 
managed to get most of the fines and fees reduced but not all. And so 
after I met with him, now years ago, I drafted and introduced the Stop 
Tax Penalties on American Hostages Act.
  I bet you can guess what this bill does. There is a corollary--a 
companion--in the House, the End Tax Penalties on American Hostages 
Act, and I am grateful to Congresswomen Titus and Tenney of Nevada and 
New York for their cosponsorship. It has passed the House unanimously.
  Tonight, Senator Rounds and I have an opportunity to ask for 
unanimous consent that we pass this legislation in the Senate and send 
it to President Trump's desk.
  Incredibly, when we asked for the score on this bill, it is positive 
because the IRS insists on litigating these cases, and they almost 
always lose them. They waste staff time and money suing people who were 
hostages or prisoners overseas.
  This is simple; it is common sense; it is bipartisan; and it is 
compassionate. I want to thank Senators Crapo and Wyden for allowing 
Senator Rounds and me to pull this bill out of their larger tax package 
and pass it by unanimous consent tonight.
  And I want to thank Senator Mike Rounds for being an early adopter. 
When I explained what happened to Jason Rezaian, he said: We should 
pass that and pass it right away.
  Well, for better or worse, it has taken years, but here we are 
tonight on the verge of sending it to the President's desk. I cannot 
say how grateful I am for the chance to get this simple, clear, 
commonsense bill passed to help Americans who might tragically be held 
hostage or imprisoned wrongfully overseas.

  Mr. President, as if in legislative session and notwithstanding rule 
XXII, I ask unanimous consent that the Committee on Finance be 
discharged from further consideration of H.R. 9496 and that the Senate 
proceed to its immediate consideration.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The clerk will report the bill by title.
  The senior assistant legislative clerk read as follows:

       A bill (H.R. 9496) to amend the Internal Revenue Code of 
     1986 to postpone tax deadlines and reimburse paid late fees 
     for United States nationals who are unlawfully or wrongfully 
     detained or held hostage abroad, and for other purposes.

  There being no objection, the committee was discharged, and the 
Senate proceeded to consider the bill.
  Mr. COONS. I ask unanimous consent that the bill be read a third time 
and passed and that the motion to reconsider be considered made and 
laid upon the table.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Without objection, it is so ordered.
  The bill (H.R. 9496) was ordered to a third reading, was read the 
third time, and passed.
  Mr. COONS. Mr. President, with that, I understand this bill has been 
adopted by unanimous consent, and I am delighted to say that the End 
Tax Penalties on American Hostages bill will at long last go to our 
President's desk for his signature.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from North Dakota.


                       Unanimous Consent Requests

  Mr. HOEVEN. Mr. President, I ask unanimous consent that the Committee 
on Energy and Natural Resources be discharged from further 
consideration of H.R. 2252 and the Senate proceed to the consideration 
of the following bill: Calendar No. 106, H.R. 1829; further, that the 
committee-reported substitute to H.R. 1829 be agreed to; the bills, as 
amended, if amended, be considered read a third time and passed, and 
the motions to reconsider be considered made and laid upon the table 
with no intervening action or debate, all en bloc.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Is there objection?
  The Senator from New Mexico.
  Mr. HEINRICH. Mr. President, reserving the right to object, I am 
actually pretty sympathetic to the land management challenges in the 
West that have come from a history of public land disposal and 
development, but I cannot support the approach this bill takes 
transferring public land to a State without a fair exchange for the 
public.
  Under the bill, if the State of North Dakota transferred State land 
to a Tribe, the State could then select Bureau of Land Management 
public land to replace them. The public would not receive any land or 
compensation in return for the land transferred to the State.
  What is more, the bill doesn't specify which public land would be 
transferred out of public hands. It would allow the State to select any 
BLM-managed public land in North Dakota that hasn't already been set 
aside.
  Land exchanges are an important tool for western public land 
management. I get that. I have supported many land exchanges in New 
Mexico and many other States, but land exchanges need to include a 
public process to determine which parcels are being exchanged. They 
must be in the public interest and be of equal value for the public and 
the other parties.
  I am willing to work with my colleague from North Dakota to find a 
solution to the State lands within Indian reservations in his State, 
but the solution can't be simply giving away our public land, 
especially when we don't know even which lands would be transferred 
away.
  I hope we can work together to find a different path forward on this 
issue but as the bill is currently drafted, I must object.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The objection is heard.
  The Senator from North Dakota.
  Mr. HOEVEN. Mr. President, I would like to ask the ranking member on 
the Energy and Natural Resources Committee who--we have worked together 
on many different issues and I appreciate that and I hope we can work 
on this one.
  But there are some questions that I have to ask because I sincerely 
would like to get to a solution on a bill that, first off, does not 
affect other States. It solely affects land within my State of North 
Dakota, and it is supported by all of the reservations.
  We have five reservations in my State, and the Native Americans on 
all of the reservations across the board have asked us to do this for 
them. There is no net loss of lands. This is simply exchanging State 
land within the boundaries of reservations for Federal land, which is 
outside the reservations, which then would be Federal lands within the 
reservations, which would be to the benefit of the Native American 
Tribes.
  The only objection that I have heard from anybody at any time is in 
regard to hunting and public access. I am a hunter. I truly enjoy 
hunting, but I must admit I am probably not a hunter on the scale of my 
colleague from New Mexico. He is an accomplished hunter. We talk about 
it all the time.
  He knows how important that is, obviously, to him and his citizens in 
New Mexico. It is very important to me and to my citizens in North 
Dakota. But here is a situation where there is no net loss of land, 
and, in fact, if anything, there will be more land open and available 
to hunters.
  I was Governor of North Dakota for 10 years, and those State lands we 
opened up to public hunting and tourism. So there will be a gain for 
hunters and fishermen. And on the reservation, there is no net loss of 
public land whatsoever. It is a simple exchange of State land for 
Federal land.
  These Federal lands will go from outside the borders of the 
reservation to within the borders of the reservation, and be managed 
for the benefit of those respective Tribes within the reservations.
  And it is something that every single Tribe within my State has been 
asking for and we have been waiting for a long time to get done.
  And here is how it worked out in the House: It passed unanimously. 
So, yes, I am willing to work on it, but I have been working on it for 
a long time. And I am simply asking that the House bill be passed and, 
along with it, a Democrat bill from the House also that would be of 
real benefit in the State of Arizona, the Apache County and Navajo 
County Conveyance Act, which is paired with this bill.
  So by objecting, my good colleague from New Mexico is also holding up 
the Apache County and Navajo County Conveyance Act, which is important 
to

[[Page S5240]]

Native Americans in the State of Arizona.
  And so, again, I would ask for consent on this bill or at least some 
commitment as to how this is going to get done.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from New Mexico.
  Mr. HEINRICH. Mr. President, this is like a three-legged stool where 
one of the legs is missing.
  I support cleaning up the parcels inside of the Tribal reservations 
that currently do not belong to them.
  But the public, who owns the Bureau of Land Management lands, also 
either need compensation or equal value. And this is simply a transfer 
of the public land of the United States of America that all of our 
constituents own, whether they are in North Dakota or New Mexico or 
Florida, without any compensation to the people of the United States.
  Whether transferring public land to counties, transferring public 
lands to States, or transferring public lands to private entities, 
without an equal value for the public, is simply something I cannot 
support.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from North Dakota.
  Mr. HOEVEN. Mr. President, there is no net loss of public lands. It 
is simply a straight exchange in terms of location. There is no net 
loss, none whatsoever.
  I still fail to understand why the good Senator from New Mexico 
continues to make the case when there is no net lands. It is just a 
simple exchange.
  The second thing I would, again, reiterate is: Okay. I know--I know 
that my colleague is sympathetic to this issue. I truly believe it. 
What I would like to hear is how we are going to resolve this. I would 
really appreciate some commitment for how this is going to be resolved.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from New Mexico.
  Mr. HEINRICH. Mr. President, I look forward to working with my 
colleague from North Dakota on the issue. I think we need to find a 
path forward here. Tribal trust land is not the same as public land. 
Transferring lands from the Federal Government to the status State is 
not equivalent, and we need something that is fair to the Tribal 
constituents, the State, and the public that owns those Federal public 
lands.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from North Dakota.
  Mr. HOEVEN. I understand. But I, again, emphasize that the Federal 
Government will have the same amount of Federal land before and after. 
It is simply an exchange where it will be located within the boundaries 
of the reservation and will benefit the Tribes. By not doing this, we 
are denying it, to the Tribe's detriment.
  And as far as the lands that the State would retain, then they would 
be outside the reservation.
  So there is no net loss there. I will emphasize that again.
  And the second thing is, by opposing this measure, my colleague is 
also holding up the Apache County and Navaho County Conveyance Act, 
which is paired with it to pass the House--well, both bills passed the 
House without objection, so I just find it interesting.
  Here is two bills that went through the House on a voice vote, 
without objection, and they are being held up. I don't understand.
  So again, I ask for either consent or some commitment beyond ``we 
will work together.''
  I mean, I would like something--and I am sure the folks at the Tribes 
in Arizona would like the same thing--of some commitment as to how we 
are going to get to resolution within some reasonable period of time. 
What is it going to take?
  With that, I ask for consent.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from New Mexico.
  Mr. HEINRICH. Mr. President, I object.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from North Dakota.
  Mr. HOEVEN. I yield the floor.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from Nevada.


                   Unanimous Consent Request--S. 5681

  Ms. CORTEZ MASTO. Mr. President, I rise today to pass my bill which 
would ensure the Federal Housing Finance Agency's inspector general can 
do its job to protect the integrity of our housing market.
  The inspector general is an independent official who investigates 
fraud, waste, and abuse in government Agencies. We can all agree that 
is a critical position.
  Within the FHFA, the inspector general goes after legitimate fraud 
and abuse. The IG makes sure government-sponsored entities like Fannie 
Mae, Freddie Mac, and the Federal Home Loan Bank meet the housing needs 
of our communities.
  And the IG ensures American consumers who need mortgages and loans 
for their homes aren't being taken advantage of.
  For example, during President Trump's first term, the FHFA's Office 
of the Inspector General caught the president and the chief information 
officer at the Federal Home Loan Bank of Dallas embezzling hundreds of 
thousands of dollars.
  There is another example from just this month when the OIG worked 
with Florida law enforcement to investigate and prosecute a former 
attorney for real estate fraud.
  There are so many more instances out there of this office going after 
fraudsters.
  I, also, personally know how critical this work is, as a former 
attorney general of Nevada. It was the responsibility of my office to 
go after fraud and win restitution for Nevadans who had been exploited.
  So trust me when I say: The work the OIG does is essential to making 
sure Americans are getting a fair deal.
  Unfortunately, President Trump's pick to head the FHFA, Bill Pulte, 
has weaponized the Agency. Director Pulte has used his position to go 
after the President's perceived political enemies. He has pushed out 
staff who are doing their job, including economists who refused to 
inflate market outlooks. He has also forced out ethics and civil rights 
staff who help hold the Agency and its leadership accountable.
  President Trump himself fired the acting inspector general last 
month.
  Clearly, there is cause for concern here, and that is why we need to 
pass this bill.
  The law already requires FHFA to provide its Office of Inspector 
General with the resources it needs to do its job. My bill would add 
safeguards to ensure that the FHFA must provide the funding that the 
OIG requests. This would protect the OIG from being shortchanged by any 
FHFA Director playing politics, and it would ensure the FHFA inspector 
general--and not Bill Pulte--determines the amount of funding it needs 
to do its job.
  This bill would protect the American people from waste, fraud, and 
abuse, and to deter bad actors from taking advantage of their power. I 
encourage my colleagues to join me in passing this bill.
  So, as if in legislative session and notwithstanding rule XXII, I ask 
unanimous consent that the Senate proceed to the immediate 
consideration of S. 5681 submitted earlier today; further, that the 
bill be considered read three times and passed; and that the motion to 
reconsider be considered made and laid upon the table.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Is there objection?
  The Senator from Ohio.
  Mr. MORENO. Mr. President, Reserving the right to object, this bill 
would amend the funding statute for the Federal Housing Finance Agency 
so that the expenses of the inspector general are collected through the 
annual assessments of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac and the Federal Home 
Loan Bank in an amount independently determined by the inspector 
general.
  I support independent oversight. The Inspector General Act already 
requires a separate budget estimate for that office, and it already 
lets the inspector general tell Congress if a proposed budget would 
impair that work.
  Current law already funds this office. It has more than 100 employees 
and a budget of more than $50 million.
  This bill goes further, though. It would make the inspector general's 
own determination the amount the director must collect. There is no 
ceiling, no reasonableness test, and no vote of Congress on this matter 
whatsoever.
  These assessments, by the way, aren't free. They are costs on the 
housing finance system, and they show up in the mortgage market. We 
would literally be costing working Americans more money for mortgages 
so that a bunch of overpriced attorneys would

[[Page S5241]]

charge whatever they want, to do whatever work they felt necessary.
  If the Senate wants to change how this office is funded, that change 
could include a cap, a reporting requirement, and, most importantly, a 
recorded vote. And for that reason, I object.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The objection is heard.
  The Senator from Ohio.
```
