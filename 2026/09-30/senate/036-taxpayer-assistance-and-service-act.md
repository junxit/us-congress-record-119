                  TAXPAYER ASSISTANCE AND SERVICE ACT

  Mr. WYDEN. Mr. President, I rise to express the strongest possible 
support for the Taxpayer Assistance and Service Act.
  Senate Democrats began working on this more than a decade ago when I 
first became chairman of the Senate Finance Committee.

[[Page S5208]]

  This bipartisan legislation contains more than 60 tax reforms to 
improve tax policy for the American people. It cracks down, in 
particular, on shady tax prep services that scam low-income Americans 
to make a quick buck. For too long, taxpayers have been victimized by 
these shady tax preparers. They file returns that overclaim refunds; 
they pocket excessive fees from their customers; and then they leave 
the taxpayers struggling to repay the IRS for the erroneous tax returns 
the scammer filed on their behalf.
  These updates will help ensure refunds get out the door on time. It 
will improve customer service at the IRS, and it will make it easier 
for taxpayers to get the help they need during filing season.
  The bill contains provisions championed by Members--and I want to 
emphasize this--on both sides of the aisle. I could stand here all day 
describing how these provisions are going to improve service for 
taxpayers, but I am going to name just a few in the interest of time.
  The bill includes provisions championed by Senators Warnock and 
Warner that will digitize tax returns, expand online services, and make 
it easier for taxpayers to know when they will be getting their 
refunds. A provision championed by Senator Bennet protects access to 
the Earned Income Tax Credit. A provision authored by Senator Welch 
would expand access to critical low-income taxpayer clinic services for 
taxpayers who have a dispute with the IRS. It includes an important 
provision by Senator Coons which reduces unnecessary tax burdens on 
Americans held hostage abroad, and a bill that I worked on with Senator 
Hassan that helps ensure that whistleblowers will receive the 
recognition they deserve for blowing the whistle on wealthy tax cheats.
  So there has been an extraordinary amount of hard work that goes into 
this. I wish to yield now to my Northwest partner, and as we say in 
Oregon: This bill is no small potatoes.
  I yield to my friend.

  Mr. CRAPO. Mr. President, Senator Wyden, thank you. You are right,

  In fact, all of the colleagues in the Finance Committee have worked

  Every year millions of Americans work diligently to comply with our 
tax law. When they do, they deserve a tax system that is competent and 
responsive. Too often, however, taxpayers encounter a system that falls 
short of those baseline requirements. The Taxpayer Assistance and 
Service Act, or TAS Act, is a bipartisan response to this taxpayer 
frustration. It reflects what the Finance Committee has collectively 
learned through years of casework and oversight and proposals from both 
sides of the aisle.
  It is designed to provide taxpayers with top-quality service and 
ensure taxpayers' rights are protected. For example, a taxpayer who 
files a return should be able to go online and know where he or she 
stands with the IRS. A taxpayer who calls the Agency should reach a 
representative promptly. A taxpayer who submits a document 
electronically should not lose legal rights because the Agency 
processes it late.
  These are basic expectations of tax administration. The TAS Act 
modernizes the IRS so that it can better meet these and other similar 
expectations. It expands electronic processing of returns and 
correspondence so filers receive their refunds faster. It also improves 
online accounts and callback technology and requires the IRS to provide 
clearer information about backlogs, wait times, refunds, and suspended 
returns. It also improves online accounts and callback technology and 
requires the IRS to provide clearer information about backlogs, wait 
times, refunds, and suspended returns.

  But modernization alone is not enough. A modernized bureaucracy can 
still be burdensome.
  To better protect taxpayer rights, the TAS Act increases access to 
the IRS Independent Office of Appeals and the Taxpayer Advocate 
Service. It modernizes U.S. Tax Court procedures. It improves review in 
innocent spouse cases and other disputes. It unlocks funding for low-
income taxpayer clinics. And it increases accountability for 
unauthorized disclosures of taxpayer information.
  These are just a few of the examples of the 65 taxpayer-focused 
improvements in the TAS Act, which the Finance Committee approved by an 
overwhelming bipartisan vote of 26 to 1.
  That resounding bipartisan agreement is bolstered by support from the 
nonpartisan National Taxpayer Advocate and many leading tax 
practitioners, who agree that this bill will help the IRS administer 
the law more predictably and more promptly.
  Once again, I thank Ranking Member Wyden and the other Members of the 
Finance Committee for their partnership in shaping and advancing this 
legislation.
  I urge all of my colleagues to support the Taxpayer Assistance and 
Service Act.
