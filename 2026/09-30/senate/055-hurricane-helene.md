# HURRICANE HELENE

**Speaking:** Budd, Ted (B001305) [R-NC]

```
                            HURRICANE HELENE

  Mr. BUDD. Mr. President, some storms pass. Others permanently change 
the communities they pass through. For the North Carolinians who lived 
through Hurricane Helene, September 27, 2024, is not simply a date in 
the past. It is a dividing line between life before the storm and 
everything that has come after.
  This week marks the second anniversary of Hurricane Helene.
  Only 2 years ago, this Category 4 storm grew to insurmountable levels 
in a matter of hours, dumping up to 30 inches of rain with winds up to 
106 miles per hour. The result: Family homes that stood for generations 
were washed away, small businesses that had long served as pillars of 
their communities were shuttered, and roadways connecting towns and 
neighbors were torn apart. But most devastating of all was the loss of 
108 lives across Western North Carolina.
  Like many North Carolinians across our State and many Americans 
across the country, I remember watching the storm carve its path 
through the Blue Ridge Mountains on the news that fateful Friday 
morning. As the storm picked up pace, I did everything I could to stay 
in constant contact with local municipal leaders on the ground to 
gather intel on the storm's growing severity and better gauge the 
damage it would inevitably leave behind. But when phone lines suddenly 
dropped, we lost all communication with one-third of the State. And 
nothing could have prepared us for what would come after.
  Just days after the storm broke, I surveyed the wreckage from the air 
with North Carolina Agriculture Commissioner Steve Troxler. From above, 
the damage done was horrifying: Rivers rushed where roads once were, 
trees were collapsed onto buildings, cars were overturned, and homes 
were caught in mudslides.
  When it was finally safe to reach the ground, the destruction Helene 
left behind was overwhelming. I will never forget touring an elementary 
school classroom in Watauga County that has been flooded by several 
feet of water during the storm. Walking over the debris and remnants of 
what once was a place of learning, growth, and opportunity for young 
children was chilling. That visit made one thing abundantly clear: The 
road to recovery was about more than rebuilding what Helene destroyed. 
It was about restoring our communities.
  Overnight, Hurricane Helene left North Carolina with nearly $60 
billion in damage and recovery costs, a price tag so large that it is 
hard to conceptualize. Now imagine that scale of destruction falling 
onto counties, small towns, and entities whose entire budgets are not 
measured in billions, but in millions, or less.
  In the immediate aftermath of Helene, local governments had no choice 
but to completely drain their cash reserves to clear debris, restore 
roads, repair water systems, and provide the basic infrastructure that 
their communities needed to survive.
  But Helene's severe economic impact extends far beyond the 
communities directly hit by the storm because the 39 disaster-
designated counties account for 45 percent of North Carolina's GDP. 
That means western North Carolina's recovery is not just a regional 
priority; it is essential to our State's overall economic strength.
  That is why my No. 1 priority in Washington over the past 2 years has 
been unlocking Federal aid for western North Carolina's recovery.
  Over the past 2 years, I have made nearly 50 visits to Helene-
impacted communities, and my staff has made many more. My casework team 
has worked on over 500 cases related to Hurricane Helene recovery, with 
positive outcomes leading to over $66 million in awards. My policy team 
has worked diligently to ensure western North Carolina receives the 
Federal disaster funding it needs by securing its inclusion in Federal 
disaster re supplemental legislation and ensuring the Disaster Relief 
Fund has the resources needed to support a full recovery.
  I have also worked closely with Federal Agencies to clear backlogs 
and get recovery funds flowing so projects can get off the ground and 
outstanding bills can be paid. In the past 2 years, Federal Agencies 
have identified over $13 billion in Hurricane Helene-related awards, 
grants, infrastructure commitments, and Federal loan assistance for 
North Carolina, significantly advancing recovery efforts.
  Since September of 2024, FEMA has assisted over 161,000 households 
with nearly $567 million in direct assistance. North Carolina was also 
awarded a $1.4 billion Community Development Block Grant from the 
Department of Housing and Urban Development to support recovery efforts 
and address housing needs.
  Additionally, FEMA has removed roughly 15 million cubic yards of 
debris from rights-of-way and waterways, restoring access and helping 
communities move forward with rebuilding.
  In a region where tourism drives local economies, fully reopening the 
Blue Ridge Parkway by the end of the year is a major milestone on the 
road to recovery. By the end of 2026, the National Park Service will 
have repaired more than 55 landslides along the parkway's 200-mile 
stretch through North Carolina. Although work will continue to repair 
visitor centers and historic and cultural landmarks in the coming 
years, the parkway's complete reopening will undoubtedly allow western 
North Carolina to enter a new stage of much-needed economic recovery by 
welcoming tourists back to the mountains.
  When I think back to those first few days after Hurricane Helene, the 
devastation will forever be scared into my memory. But what stands out 
most is what emerged from that destruction: an extraordinary sense of 
unity. In every one of western North Carolina's 39 impacted counties, 
neighbors were helping neighbors. And that level of unity, in the face 
of severe adversity, is something I am enormously proud of.
  I want to recognize and sincerely thank the faith-based and nonprofit 
organizations that were among the first to respond after Helene--and 
for their continued efforts in helping families across western North 
Carolina rebuild.
  To every family still grieving the loss of a loved one, know that you 
are not alone. We will never forget the lives Helene stole from us on 
September 27, 2024.
  Helene may have changed western North Carolina forever, but it will 
not define our future. That belongs to the resilient people who call 
the Blue Ridge Mountains home. As communities continue to rebuild, 
businesses reopen, and visitors return, western North Carolina will 
continue moving forward with the same strength and resilience that 
carried us through the storm--because, if the last 2 years have taught 
us anything, it is this: No storm is stronger than a community 
determined to rebuild together.

                          ____________________
```
