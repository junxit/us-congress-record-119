# RATEPAYER PROTECTION ACT

**Speaking:** Hagerty, Bill (H000601) [R-TN]

```
                        RATEPAYER PROTECTION ACT

  Mr. HAGERTY. Mr. President, I rise in support of my esteemed 
colleague from Ohio, and I want to join him and applaud his leadership 
on this critical legislation. Why? Because America is undergoing 
technological advancement at unprecedented speed.
  As we push new frontiers in this new era of superintelligence, 
innovation can have a tremendously positive impact on our society and 
on our Nation's competitiveness.
  At the same time, we find ourselves neck and neck with adversaries 
racing to harness this technology for their own purposes. It is crucial 
that we keep up the pace, particularly regarding the development of 
large language models and computing power heretofore at levels unseen.
  Our objective is clear: The United States must remain at the leading 
edge. To do so, we need more energy. Conversations are taking place 
across the United States right now about how we will meet this 
increased energy demand in order to power superintelligence. The 
overarching concern is that we do so responsibly and with local 
community needs in mind.

[[Page S5194]]

  Companies at the frontier of this emerging technology have the 
resources to pay for the infrastructure and the costs associated to run 
these facilities. Importantly, these costs should not be borne by 
American families nor by American businesses.
  My colleague from Ohio has worked incredibly hard to get this bill 
across the finish line and deliver basic protections for the American 
people.
  The public is overwhelmingly in favor of the protections it would 
provide. Many States have already embraced it, including my home State 
of Tennessee. Tennessee adopted President Trump's Ratepayer Protection 
Pledge while preserving the ability of local communities to make 
decisions about data center development.
  The communities that have chosen to welcome innovation have seen 
incredibly positive impacts: more jobs, new investment, and an increase 
in their tax base that can allow for a full range of options from lower 
property taxes to greater investment in local public resources.
  Importantly, we are doing so while ensuring that the cost for 
addressing the energy demands of data centers aren't passed along to 
Tennessee families nor Tennessee businesses.
  What is the only thing standing in the way of enacting these 
protections? What is standing in the way? It is my colleagues on the 
other side of the aisle.
  In the House, the Ratepayer Protection Pledge passed on an 
overwhelmingly bipartisan basis with a vote of 417 to 3. You almost 
never see that level of consensus in Washington, and yet, last week, 
Senate Democrats blocked it from passing right here on the Senate 
floor. Unbelievable. Unbelievable.
  Instead of comprehensive measures that put the decision-making power 
in the hands of State and local communities, my colleagues across the 
aisle want Washington to decide, making sweeping decisions for our 
Nation without input from the towns and the cities it would directly 
impact.
  I can't stress this enough: Removing local choice and imposing 
blanket data center moratoriums are not the answer to responsible 
innovation. In fact, it is precisely what will kill innovation and put 
America underwater in the technological race against our adversaries.
  When it comes to investing in this new future, communities should be 
in the driver's seat. Localities should be able to decide when, where, 
and whether they should permit these facilities, especially when they 
have so much to gain from the investment.
  Sadly, we have seen a massive amount of misinformation circulating 
about data centers: their costs, their energy consumption, their 
wastewater usage, their noise levels.
  The truth is, we can find solutions to the concerns of Tennesseans 
and Americans, while putting the United States in the best possible 
position to succeed.
  We need to speak out against these false claims, put a stop to the 
fearmongering that is jeopardizing America's opportunity to lead in 
this critical area for our economy and for our national security, and 
we must clearly communicate the advantages that communities can have 
when they embrace American energy and American technological 
ingenuity--all the while protecting, and even bettering, the lives of 
Americans. It is time to pass the Ratepayer Protection Act.
  But here we are in an election season. Fearmongering is taking 
precedence over responsible and thoughtful action. False claims abound 
while America's technology leadership hangs in the balance. We should 
move responsibly, protect American communities, and support our 
Nation's economic and national security.
  The House of Representatives has seized this opportunity. It is time 
for the Senate to set partisan politics aside and pass the Ratepayer 
Protection Act.
  I yield the floor.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from Nebraska.

                          ____________________
```
