# HONORING FIRST EMMANUEL MISSIONARY BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
           HONORING FIRST EMMANUEL MISSIONARY BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 5, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor First Emmanuel 
Missionary Baptist Church of Baton Rouge, Louisiana, a congregation 
that has been a steady and trusted presence in the community for more 
than a century and a vital part of the faith community of Baton Rouge. 
The church began in the early 1900s as St. Paul Missionary Church, 
originally located on Highland Road in Baton Rouge.
  In 1931, Pastor Adam H. Landry became the church's leader and 
relocated the congregation to Education Street. During this period, 
Sister Ida Landry gave the church its present name, First Emmanuel 
Missionary Baptist Church. Construction of the church building began in 
1934 and was completed in 1937, and the church's members played a major 
role in the work, using their skills as carpenters, bricklayers, 
electricians, plumbers, and cement finishers to build their own house 
of worship. That a congregation would pour its own labor and talent 
into building a church, in the midst of the hardships of the 1930s, 
speaks to the depth of its faith and the strength of the bonds among 
its members, and it set a pattern of shared commitment that has defined 
the church ever since.
  The congregation continued to grow, and under the leadership of 
Pastor George Rogers Clark, the church acquired additional property, 
achieved financial stability, and retired its mortgage in 1969. That 
achievement reflects a congregation that has been a faithful steward of 
its resources and has invested in its future so that it could keep 
serving the community for generations. In 2004, the church undertook 
additional renovations to improve and preserve its facilities, ensuring 
that its doors remain open as a place of worship, fellowship, and 
gathering for the people it serves. In 1996, Pastor Henry J. Brown 
became the church's fourth pastor, and he continues to lead the 
congregation today.
  For many families in Baton Rouge, First Emmanuel has been a spiritual 
home across generations, a place where members come together in 
worship, find fellowship in times of joy and sorrow, and are reminded 
that faith is lived out in service to one another. Churches like First 
Emmanuel are among the most enduring institutions in our neighborhoods, 
anchoring communities through changing times and offering stability, 
encouragement, and a sense of belonging to those who walk through their 
doors.
  The impact of First Emmanuel extends beyond its own membership. As 
one of the longstanding congregations of Baton Rouge, it contributes to 
the strength of the broader faith community, standing alongside other 
churches in the shared work of nurturing faith, supporting families, 
and building a community grounded in values of compassion and service. 
Its example, of members who built their church with their own hands and 
then sustained it faithfully for decades, continues to inspire the many 
who worship there and those who look to it as a model of what a 
committed congregation can accomplish.

[[Page E1022]]

  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in celebrating First 
Emmanuel Missionary Baptist Church, Pastor Henry J. Brown, and the 
dedicated congregation for more than a century of faithful worship, 
fellowship, and service to the Baton Rouge community. May First 
Emmanuel continue to be a source of strength and a light of faith in 
Baton Rouge for generations to come.
```
