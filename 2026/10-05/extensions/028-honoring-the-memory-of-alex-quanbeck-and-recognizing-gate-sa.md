# HONORING THE MEMORY OF ALEX QUANBECK AND RECOGNIZING GATE SAFETY AWARENESS MONTH

**Speaking:** Huffman, Jared (H001068) [D-CA]

```
   HONORING THE MEMORY OF ALEX QUANBECK AND RECOGNIZING GATE SAFETY 
                            AWARENESS MONTH

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. JARED HUFFMAN

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 5, 2026

  Mr. HUFFMAN. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Gate Safety 
Awareness Month and to honor the memory of my constituent, Alex 
Quanbeck.
  In 2019, Alex was just seven years old when a nearly 400-pound gate 
at his school fell on him during recess and took his life. It was a 
devastating tragedy for his family and our community--and one that 
should never have happened. Alex's death could have been prevented by a 
simple safety feature that would have kept the gate from falling.
  What makes this tragedy especially heartbreaking is that the very 
gates we rely on to protect our children and communities can pose 
serious risks when they are poorly designed, damaged, or not properly 
maintained. Most people never think twice about a gate. We expect it to 
be safe. We should be able to.
  I never had the opportunity to know Alex, but I have come to know his 
parents, Eric and Dayna Quanbeck, and I have watched them turn 
unimaginable grief into a determination to protect other families. 
Through their organization, Ready, Set, Safe!, they have worked 
tirelessly to raise awareness and improve gate safety standards so that 
no other parent has to receive the phone call they received.
  Their work is making a difference.
  Over the past several years, the Quanbecks have worked with the 
American Fence Association, the International Code Council, engineers, 
manufacturers, building officials, and other safety professionals to 
develop practical, consensus-based improvements to gate safety. That 
work has now led to important new

[[Page E1021]]

provisions being approved for the 2027 International Codes, 
strengthening requirements for manual sliding and swing gates, and 
improving safety and maintenance requirements for existing gates.
  This is exactly the kind of progress we should be pursuing: 
identifying a preventable hazard, listening to the families who have 
experienced it firsthand, bringing together the people who understand 
the technology and standards, and putting practical solutions in place.
  I want to recognize the American Fence Association and its members 
for their leadership and commitment to improving gate safety. I also 
want to thank ASTM International, the International Code Council, and 
the many engineers, manufacturers, installers, code officials, and 
safety professionals who worked through an open and consensus-based 
process to turn the lessons of Alex's death into stronger safety 
standards.
  As we recognize Gate Safety Awareness Month, I also want to recognize 
the Consumer Product Safety Commission for its efforts to raise 
awareness about gate hazards and encourage consumers, property owners, 
and installers to follow established safety practices.
  There is still more work to do. When a simple, affordable safety 
measure can mean the difference between life and death, we should do 
everything we can to make sure that protection is available. We should 
continue raising awareness, improving safety practices, and working 
with industry and safety professionals to prevent these tragedies 
before they happen.
  Alex should be remembered not only for the terrible circumstances of 
his death, but for the change his family has fought to bring about in 
his memory.
  We cannot undo what happened to Alex. But we can learn from it. We 
can make our schools, parks, workplaces, homes, and public spaces 
safer. And we can help make sure that another family does not have to 
endure this kind of preventable loss.
  I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring the memory of Alex 
Quanbeck, recognizing the extraordinary advocacy of Eric and Dayna 
Quanbeck and Ready, Set, Safe!, and thanking the many professionals and 
organizations working to make gate safety a national priority.
  Alex's legacy can be a safer future for other children and families. 
That is a legacy worth fighting for.

                          ____________________
```
