# TRIBUTE TO DWIGHT CARVER DEBRO

**Speaking:** Bishop, Sanford D. (B000490) [D-GA]

```
                     TRIBUTE TO DWIGHT CARVER DEBRO

                                 ______
                                 

                      HON. SANFORD D. BISHOP, JR.

                               of georgia

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 5, 2026

  Mr. BISHOP. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor a man whose hands have 
made Columbus, Georgia, sing for more than three decades: Mr. Dwight 
Carver Debro, who this past Sunday, October 4, 2026, was honored by 
Greater St. Mark A.M.E. Church for 32 years of faithful service as its 
Minister of Music.
  Dwight was raised in that church. As a boy, he sat in Sunday School, 
sang in the choir, looked forward to Vacation Bible School every 
summer, and served as an altar boy. He was the fifth child of the late 
Rev. James Debro Sr., who pastored St. Mark and later served as 
Presiding Elder of the Albany District of the African Methodist 
Episcopal Church, and Mrs. Anna M. Debro, a teacher in the Muscogee 
County School District and a devoted leader at Greater St. Mark, who 
lived to the remarkable age of 104. Faith, service, and music were the 
air he breathed.
  After graduating from Baker High School, Dwight attended Morris Brown 
College and Columbus College. Then an extraordinary opportunity came: a 
chance to travel and perform with the band Earthquake. He stepped out 
on faith and followed the music. Over the years, he shared stages with 
The Manhattans, The Dramatics, Brass Construction, The Bar-Kays, The 
O'Jays, Frankie Beverly & Maze, Parliament-Funkadelic, and The Brothers 
Johnson. He and his band moved to Atlanta chasing a recording contract, 
and they earned one with Capitol Records as Pure Gold. When management 
troubles kept the group from the heights it deserved, Dwight refused to 
let disappointment define him. He formed The Slash Band, which opened 
for Atlantic Starr, Starpoint, B.B. King, Bobby Womack, and Bobby 
``Blue'' Bland, won first place on the television talent competition 
Discovery, and took home Best Group Vocals in a national competition. 
He even produced his sister Lisa Dore's album, Totally Yours. Along the 
way, he met his lifelong idol, Stevie Wonder.
  Think about that for a moment. Stevie Wonder, who has never seen a 
piano key, gave the world `'Sir Duke,'' a joyful tribute to the 
musicians who shaped him and a reminder that music belongs to everyone 
who is moved by it. He gave us `'Overjoyed'' and ``As,'' songs that 
insist that love and beauty outlast whatever life takes from us. Dwight 
heard those songs as a young man and learned something from them that 
he would carry for the rest of his life: that a musician's gift does 
not live in the eyes. It lives in the heart.
  Yet Dwight will tell you the greatest turning point of his life did 
not happen on a stage. It happened in a church. One day, his pastor, 
Rev. B.A. Hart, said to him, ``It's time for you to give your talents 
back to the Lord.'' Dwight sat down at the piano at Greater St. Mark, 
and for more than 32 years he never really left it.
  Consider what those years held. He played for the baptisms and the 
funerals, for Easter mornings and long nights of grief, for weddings 
and for the weary. He played for the grandmother who needed to feel the 
Spirit and for the child just learning the hymns. He played when the 
congregation was full of joy and when it could barely lift its voice. 
Whatever people carried through the church doors, Dwight met it with 
music, and more than one broken heart was mended by the sound of his 
hands on those keys.
  His service reached well beyond the sanctuary. He worked with 
children with autism at the Woodall Psycho-Educational Center and 
helped young people in recovery at Alchemy, giving patience and hope to 
those who needed them most. He concluded his professional career with 
16 years in the Risk Management Department at Total Systems, retiring 
in 2013. Throughout, he has been blessed with the steadfast love of his 
wife, Gwendolyn, and together they have been a loving anchor to a host 
of nieces and nephews.
  This year, as his eyesight failed, Dwight made the hardest decision a 
musician can make. In April, he stepped away from the keys. I cannot 
imagine what it cost him. And yet there is something fitting in it. The 
man who grew up idolizing Stevie Wonder now understands, in the deepest 
way, that music was never something he saw. It was something he gave. 
And a church that has been lifted by his playing for 32 years does not 
forget the sound. It lives in the walls, in the pews, and in every 
heart that was ever carried on a hymn he played.
  Dwight lives by these words: ``Be blessed so you can be a blessing.'' 
He also reminds all who will listen, ``If God said it. believe it, have 
faith, and watch it happen!'' Mr. Speaker, Dwight Debro has been 
blessed, and he has been a blessing to everyone who ever heard him 
play.
  I ask my colleagues to join my wife Vivian and me, along with the 
more than 765,000 people of Georgia's Second Congressional District in 
honoring Dwight Carver Debro, a living legend, a faithful servant, and 
a gift to the people of Georgia's Second Congressional District, I 
thank Dwight for the music. The piano may be quiet now, but we will 
keep hearing it.

                          ____________________
```
