# HONORING ST. ALMA BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                    HONORING ST. ALMA BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 5, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor St. Alma Baptist 
Church, a historic congregation in the rural community of Lakeland in 
Pointe Coupee Parish, Louisiana. Slavery was abolished in 1865, and 9 
years later, in 1874, under the leadership of its founding pastor, Rev. 
Horace Jones, St. Alma Baptist Church was erected on the Alma 
Plantation. The original church was a white wooden building with three 
rows of benches and a wood heater set in the middle of the sanctuary. 
Its initial membership of 65 persons consisted mainly of field workers, 
with Jake Whitfield serving as president of the Board of Deacons and 
William Watkins as senior deacon. Within just 9 years of freedom, a 
congregation of field workers had built a house of worship of its own.
  From its earliest years, St. Alma was committed to educating the 
children of the community. In the early 1900s, the church served as the 
local school for elementary students, and it continued that mission 
through The Saint Alma Elementary School until a school was built on 
the grounds of the church under the administration of the Pointe Coupee 
Parish School Board. That school did not close until the late 1950s. 
Rev. Horace Jones served the congregation until his death on June 8, 
1920, and after a year without a pastor, the congregation elected Rev. 
Steven Johnson in 1921.
  Over the course of its long history, St. Alma has been blessed with 
seven pastors, the first four of whom served until their deaths. Rev. 
Steven Johnson served until his death in 1928, and Rev. Sidney Davis 
served faithfully until his death in 1950. Rev. Cenas Jones then led 
the congregation until his death in 1973. Rev. James Allen Terrance was 
installed as pastor on February 6, 1994, and served until February 28, 
2003, when he resigned to become pastor of Friendship Baptist Church in 
Kansas City, Missouri. Rev. Davis Thomas, Jr. was installed as the 
church's sixth pastor on March 28, 2004, and served until his 
resignation on March 8, 2007.
  On February 17, 2008, Rev. Dr. Mary Moss was installed as St. Alma's 
seventh pastor, a historic moment in the life of what was then a 134-
year-old church. She became the first woman elected to the position of 
pastor of this traditional Baptist church and the first woman ever 
installed by the Fourth District Baptist Association, then 133 years 
old.
  Many ministries have been born out of St. Alma Baptist Church, each 
designed to meet the present needs of its members and the local 
community. In this post-pandemic season, the church remains faithful to 
intergenerational ministry, producing strong young leaders and helping 
its members find their fit for ministry, and more than 15 new members 
have been added. Central to its outreach is a radio broadcast aired on 
WXOK in Baton Rouge and KCLF in New Roads, carrying the church's 
message well beyond Lakeland. Today, St. Alma looks toward the future 
with plans to build a new sanctuary at 3736 Rougon Road. As the 
congregation testifies along with the Psalmist in Psalm 118:23, ``This 
is the Lord's doing; it is marvelous in our eyes.''
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in celebrating St. Alma 
Baptist Church, Rev. Dr. Mary Moss, and the dedicated congregation for 
more than 150 years of faith, perseverance, and service to the people 
of Pointe Coupee Parish and the surrounding community.
```
