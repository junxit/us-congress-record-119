# HONORING PLEASANT GROVE BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                 HONORING PLEASANT GROVE BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 5, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Pleasant Grove Baptist 
Church, located at 1140 Pride Port Hudson Road in Zachary, Louisiana, 
directly off Old Scenic Highway near the area known as the Plains. 
Founded and organized in 1857 under the leadership of the late Rev. 
Caddo Simms, Pleasant Grove is among the oldest churches in the Zachary 
area and in East Baton Rouge Parish, and its story spans nearly 170 
years of faith, family, and community.
  The land for the church's first site was donated by Dr. T.L. Mills, 
Sr. and his wife, Mrs. Mary Louise Young Mills, who was the driving 
force behind the gift. It is worth remembering that this gift was made 
before the Civil War, at a time when slavery was a well-known practice. 
A small building was constructed, and Rev. Simms and the congregation 
worshiped there until 1904, when the church was enlarged to about twice 
its original size. Rev. Allen Governor followed Rev. Simms as pastor, 
and over the years the congregation elected several other pastors, 
including Rev. Sam Watkins, Rev. T.D. Smith, Rev. Dan Williams, and 
Rev. Rivers Johnson, with Rev. Watkins and Rev. Johnson each elected 
twice.
  Pleasant Grove's history is closely tied to the education of Black 
children in the community. Under Rev. Johnson's leadership, the old 
church was moved to the site of the former Mills Rosenwald School, 
which stood on land also donated by the Mills family. Rosenwald schools 
were established so that Black children could receive a free education, 
an effort that grew out of the vision of Booker T. Washington and the 
financial support of Julius Rosenwald. In the late 1940s, the Mills 
family

[[Page E1015]]

and the members of Pleasant Grove made an even exchange of property, 
and the congregation began worshiping in the old school building, which 
was in better condition than the church, as neighborhood children moved 
on to the newly built Northwestern High School. The current church 
remains on that property and was rebuilt in 1960 while Rev. Johnson was 
still pastor. He served until his failing eyesight forced his 
retirement, supported by associate pastors Rev. Joe Thomas and Rev. 
Arthur Brown, Sr., who served until their deaths.
  Rev. Lucious O'Connor, who united with the church in the late 1940s 
and later organized its first choir, along with many choirs throughout 
the Zachary, Port Hudson, and Ethel areas, became pastor in 1963. The 
church's cornerstone was laid in 1974 by the M.W. Prince Hall Grand 
Lodge, with Ulysses Morgan serving as chairperson of the Deacons and 
Sis. Louise Brown Thomas as chairman of the Deaconess Board. Under Rev. 
O'Connor, the church was renovated several times, began holding 
Vacation Bible School for all of the community's children each summer, 
and established weekly Bible study. An outdoor baptismal pool was built 
in the early 1970s, and renovations in the early 1980s added an inside 
baptismal pool, new pews, an improved rear of the church, and a paved 
parking lot. Rev. O'Connor resigned in 1996 after many years of 
service.
  In 1997, Rev. Norwood C. Booker, Jr. was elected pastor, and for the 
first time Pleasant Grove began holding worship services every Sunday. 
He served faithfully until his untimely death in March 2011, and under 
his leadership the church became more financially stable. In January 
2012, Rev. Dennis Lawrence was elected pastor. Guided by its mission 
statement, ``We are a spiritual team striving for excellence through 
the guidance of the Holy Spirit, realizing that no one is perfect, yet 
continuously striving,'' the congregation improved its annex and 
sanctuary during his tenure, including replacing all of the sanctuary's 
light fixtures. In 2019, the Mills family and the church renegotiated 
their land agreement, allowing the church to remain at its present site 
with an additional 80 square feet donated by the Mills family.
  The end of 2019 and the start of 2020 brought a season of deep grief, 
as the church lost several beloved and faithful servants: Sis. Marie 
Jones, a loyal deaconess; Sis. Rosemary Duncan, a longtime usher; Sis. 
Juanita Thomas Hill, song leader and president of the choir; and Sis. 
Beulah Rene Richardson Collins, also a devoted usher. Then the COVID-19 
pandemic arrived, and beginning in March 2020 the congregation 
worshipped together every Sunday by conference call, with Sunday school 
at 7 a.m. and worship at 8 a.m. The congregation persevered, and in 
2021 it undertook the remodeling and reconstruction of the church's 
walls and roof, with all services continuing by call during 
construction. The first official Sunday back in the church was held in 
the first week of August 2022.
  In January 2024, the church selected Rev. Chad Cooper as its pastor, 
and he was installed and ordained at Pleasant Grove on March 9, 2024. 
Pastor Cooper began his leadership by encouraging the congregation to 
reach one more person, a reminder of the church's purpose of 
discipleship. Today, Pleasant Grove holds Sunday school every Sunday, 
worship on first Sundays, Holy Communion on third Sundays, and Bible 
study every Tuesday at 6:30 p.m., and under Pastor Cooper's leadership 
the deacon board completed needed work on the inside baptismal pool and 
the handicap ramp. Known through the generations as a family-oriented 
church with close community ties, Pleasant Grove has seen its 
membership change over the years, but its faith has not. As the 
congregation sings, ``What a Great God We Serve,'' knowing that God is 
still in control.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in celebrating Pleasant 
Grove Baptist Church, Pastor Chad Cooper, and the dedicated 
congregation for more than 168 years of faith, perseverance, and 
service to the Zachary community. May Pleasant Grove continue to march 
on for generations to come.
```
