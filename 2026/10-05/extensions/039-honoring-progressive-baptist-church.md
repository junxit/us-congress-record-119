# HONORING PROGRESSIVE BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                  HONORING PROGRESSIVE BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 5, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize a great church in 
my district, Progressive Baptist Church of Baton Rouge, Louisiana. The 
church traces its beginnings to 1915, when a group of dedicated, God-
fearing men and women came together with a shared desire to establish a 
house of worship that would meet the spiritual and physical needs of 
their community. Among its early founders were Eli and Susie 
Washington, James and Rosie Debose, John and Rose Taylor, Samuel and 
Hattie Knox, Robert and Mrs. Mills. Samuel Rhodes, Ernest ``Pet'' 
Coakley, Rev. Spencer C. Heedly, and contractor Jefferson Monroe. Their 
vision became Progressive Baptist Church, established at the corner of 
Julia and Elvira Streets, now South 10th Street.
  Building the church was truly a labor of faith and love. After 
completing their regular jobs, the men worked late into the night on 
the building while the women held lights, served coffee, and supported 
the effort. Until the sanctuary was completed, the congregation 
worshiped at a nearby Presbyterian church under Rev. J.G. Lee. The 
early sanctuary was simple, furnished with folding wooden chairs and 
heated by a wood- or coal-burning stove, yet neither weather nor 
limited resources diminished the congregation's enthusiasm for worship, 
and a large bell in the church belfry became a familiar call to worship 
throughout the surrounding community.
  Rev. Spencer C. Heedly, Progressive's first pastor, led the 
congregation as its ministries and departments took shape, and the 
church's

[[Page E1024]]

first cornerstone dates to January 9, 1916. Members sacrificed their 
time and resources to sustain the growing church, including the women 
of the Deaconess Board, who prepared and sold Saturday suppers to raise 
funds. Rev. Heedly served for approximately 16 years until his death in 
1932. Rev. Matthew W. Rivers became the second pastor in 1932, and 
under his leadership the church was remodeled and a second cornerstone 
was laid on January 9, 1942; he served faithfully for 11 years until 
his death in 1943. On December 12, 1943, Rev. Robert Douglas Thomas 
became the third pastor and began a remarkable 45-year pastorate, with 
particular emphasis on developing children and young people through 
choirs, Sunday School, Baptist Training Union, Boy Scouts and Girl 
Scouts, Youth-in-Action, youth encampments, and mission ministry. His 
decades of leadership helped shape generations of Progressive members 
until his death on November 9, 1988.
  Rev. Joseph James Mitchell became the fourth pastor on April 19, 
1989. During his tenure of approximately 11 years, Progressive 
established a Children's Church, organized evangelistic teams that 
ministered throughout the neighborhood, acquired additional property, 
purchased a church van, and expanded opportunities for Bible study. 
Following his resignation in September 1999, Rev. Everett G. Parker, 
Sr., provided spiritual leadership through June 2000. In June 2000, 
Rev. Melvin Ray Rushing, Jr., was called to lead Progressive, bringing 
a renewed commitment to discipleship, fellowship, and evangelism, along 
with a new vision and mission statement. During his leadership, the 
Men's Ministry was organized, the sanctuary was remodeled, the 
parsonage was refurbished for outreach purposes, and the youth ministry 
was reorganized and strengthened. Rev. Rushing served faithfully until 
his transition from labor to reward on September 5, 2021, after which 
Rev. Kendall Fieling, a devoted friend of Pastor Rushing and the 
Progressive family, provided leadership from September 12, 2021, until 
September 18, 2022.
  A new chapter began on September 19, 2022, when Progressive welcomed 
its sixth pastor, Elder DeAndre M. White Sr., of Cleveland, Ohio, who 
was formally installed on January 15, 2023. Under Pastor White's 
leadership, the church has continued building on the foundation laid by 
generations before it while expanding its ministry for a changing 
world. Progressive has made significant improvements to its sanctuary 
and ministry infrastructure, including new media and livestreaming 
capabilities, an upgraded sound system, renovated ADA-accessible 
restrooms, improvements to its kitchen and fire-suppression systems, 
and electronic giving options, along with the development of additional 
ministries, including Impact Ministry, HBO Ministry, and Church 
Security Ministry. The church has also expanded its digital presence 
through its website and social media platforms, allowing its ministry 
to reach beyond the walls of the sanctuary, and Pastor White has 
appointed a new generation of deacons to serve and lead alongside him.
  From a small group of faithful believers willing to work late into 
the night to build a place of worship, to a congregation now using 
modern technology to carry the Gospel beyond its walls, Progressive 
Baptist Church's story is one of faith, sacrifice, perseverance, 
service, and progress. For more than a century, the church has remained 
rooted in the Gospel of Jesus Christ while continuing to grow with each 
generation.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in celebrating 
Progressive Baptist Church, Pastor DeAndre M. White, Sr., and the 
dedicated congregation for more than a century of faithful service and 
leadership in Baton Rouge.
```
