# TRIBUTE TO RICHARD "DICK" OLIPHANT

**Speaking:** Calvert, Ken (C000059) [R-CA]

```
                  TRIBUTE TO RICHARD ``DICK'' OLIPHANT

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. KEN CALVERT

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 5, 2026

  Mr. CALVERT. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor and pay tribute to 
Richard ``Dick'' Oliphant, an American patriot who made incredible 
contributions to the Coachella Valley who passed away on Monday, July 
13, 2026. Dick was a past mayor of the city of Indian Wells, a 
successful developer, and businessman who will be deeply missed.
  Dick moved from Iowa to the Coachella Valley in 1962 to build and 
manage Palm City, the community later known as Palm Desert Country

[[Page E1018]]

Club. He launched his dedication to public service as the city of 
Indian Wells' first Fire Chief and one of the Coachella Valley's 
earliest paramedics. In 1984, Dick was elected to serve as Mayor of 
Indian Wells, which was a small bedroom community that was struggling 
to survive. Working through the City's newly formed Redevelopment 
Agency, Dick's financial ingenuity and leadership made possible the 
Indian Wells Golf Resort, which was the catalyst for the development of 
the Renaissance Esmeralda Resort & Spa, the now Grand Hyatt Indian 
Wells Resort & Spa and the BNP Paribas Open's future home of the Indian 
Wells Tennis Gardens.
  Dick believed development should create complete communities--not 
only homes and businesses, but also schools, public services, 
recreation and opportimities for the people who lived there. He helped 
shape the golfing landscape in the Coachella Valley--developing the 
Palm Desert Country Club and Classic Club. Dick was instrumental in 
creating the Coachella Valley Economic Partnership and the Greater Palm 
Springs Convention and Visitors Bureau. He also helped create the Palm 
Desert Campus of California State University, San Bernardino, by 
raising more than $30 million to bring higher education to the 
Coachella Valley. A full history of the many ways Dick influenced and 
shaped the future of the Valley is far too long to list, but it is safe 
to say few people have improved their communities in meaningful ways as 
much as Dick Oliphant did throughout his life.
  Dick was preceded in death by his son, Richard Oliphant II. He is 
suivived by his wife of 73 years, Jan Oliphant; his children, Virginia 
Tinley, Deborah Dolan, Wes Oliphant and Mike Oliphant; 8 grandchildren; 
and 12 great-grandchildren. He is also survived by his sisters, 
Valletta Suepple and Carol Olinger, and his brothers-in-law, Steve 
McWilliams and King McWilliams. I extend my personal and heartfelt 
condolences to the Oliphant family, his friends, colleagues, and 
everyone fortunate enough to know Dick. Although he may be gone, the 
many contributions he made to the Coachella Valley and our community 
will make a long lasting impact.

                          ____________________
```
