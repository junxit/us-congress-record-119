# HONORING HOLLY GROVE BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                  HONORING HOLLY GROVE BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 5, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Holly Grove Baptist 
Church of Zachary, Louisiana, a congregation built on the foundation of 
the apostles and prophets, with Jesus Christ as the chief cornerstone. 
Each year, the church pays tribute to God the Father, the head of the 
church, and to the many men and women who laid its foundation, 
sustained it, and continue to ensure that it thrives as a community of 
believers.
  Holly Grove was founded in 1896 by brothers Isaac Square, Sr. and 
Jacob Franklin, who traveled from Alabama to Louisiana. The church was 
named Holly Grove by Josephine Square Burks, Martha Square, and George 
Youngblood because of the holly trees on the property, and the original 
building stood to the left of the current structure, where a parking 
area is today. Rev. Isaac Square, Sr. served as the first pastor and 
set the example for those who followed. The Square family legacy 
continued with Rev. Isaac Square, Jr.. followed by Rev. Mark Smith. 
Rev. Verge Hayes, Rev. Prince Williams, and Rev. Henry Odell, who 
together served the church from 1896 to 1932 ensuring its stability and 
growth during its formative years. Many members today are direct 
descendants of these founding leaders.
  In those early years, the church held services twice a month, with 
Communion on first Sundays. Fourth Sundays were set aside for

[[Page E1020]]

``Speak Meetings,'' where members shared their testimonies, offering 
opportunities for spiritual reflection and mutual encouragement. Summer 
revivals were central to church life, renewing faith and reaching out 
to the surrounding community, and baptisms were held in the nearby 
Comite River. Holly Grove also has the distinction of having once been 
a school: documents indicate that the property next to the present 
building was donated on January 4, 1913, for use as ``a public school 
for colored children.''
  From 1932 to 1971, Rev. Robert Tate, Jr. served as pastor for nearly 
40 years, and under his leadership, with the support of church leaders 
and congregants, funds were raised to build the current church. He was 
followed by Rev. Pompey Square, the great-great-grandson of Isaac 
Square, Sr., who served from 1972 to 1982, a testament to a seed 
planted by a founder generations earlier. Rev. Lorenzo Neff, 
affectionately known as ``Rev. Nute,'' served from 1984 to 1986, and 
his brother, Rev. Robert Groce, followed in January 1987 and served 
until his passing in March 2006. During his tenure, Rev. Groce baptized 
many middle-aged members, organized the ``Next Generation'' Youth 
Choir, formed the Male Choir, expanded services to the second and third 
Sundays, initiated twice-monthly Communion and Speak Meetings, and led 
the church's first building projects, including the original fellowship 
hall. He also recognized the need to train younger deacons, including 
Wesley Green III, Marque Square, Marcus Square, and Robert Brown, 
though he passed before they were installed. Rev. William Barrow then 
served as pastor from January 2007 to January 2008.
  On February 24, 2008, Rev. Sam Williams, Jr. was elected pastor, and 
he was installed on April 27, 2008, as the youngest pastor Holly Grove 
had ever had. Focused on the vision of ``Becoming a Healthy Church,'' 
with an emphasis on discipleship, evangelism, worship, fellowship, and 
ministry, he added a fifth Sunday service and the Women of Virtue Choir 
and renamed the Youth Choir ``Joyful Noise.'' The sanctuary was 
renovated twice, first in December 2013, with services resuming in 
March 2014. When the church flooded in 2016, the congregation continued 
to worship in the fellowship hall until returning to the sanctuary in 
April 2017.
  During the church's 120th anniversary, a successful banquet was 
coordinated by Sister Vikki Wicker and Sister Lawanna Green. Rev. 
Williams also introduced interactive youth services on third Sundays 
and baptized seven young members on March 20, 2022. Throughout the 
pandemic, the church expanded its fellowship hall and held Sunday 
school and worship virtually. Rev. Williams served his last Communion 
on August 7, 2022, and Rev. Perry Jones served from August 2022 to 
August 2023.
  On May 12, 2024, Holly Grove elected Eric Patterson as its pastor, 
and he was installed on June 29, 2024. During his first year, Angie 
Jones and Barbara Neff were installed as deaconesses, and he has 
expanded the church's engagement through social media and digital 
giving. Holly Grove has been blessed with congregants who understand 
the importance of serving, from the ushers who welcome worshipers and 
help maintain order, to the choir and musicians who lead the 
congregation in worship each Sunday.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in celebrating Holly 
Grove Baptist Church, Pastor Eric Patterson, and the dedicated 
congregation for 130 years of faith, perseverance, and service. May 
Holly Grove continue to stand on the cornerstone of Jesus Christ for 
generations to come.
```
