# HONORING NEW HOME MINISTRIES

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                      HONORING NEW HOME MINISTRIES

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 5, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor New Home Ministries, a 
congregation whose roots in New Orleans reach back more than a century 
and whose ministry today extends across Louisiana, including Baton 
Rouge. New Home Missionary Baptist Church was first established in 1918 
in New Orleans, on the corner of Howard Avenue and South Robertson 
Streets, by a small group of followers led by the church's senior 
deacon. Rev. G.W. White was elected as the first pastor and served for 
several years, and he was succeeded by Rev. R.H. Royal, who was elected 
in 1924 and served for a short period.
  In 1925, Rev. Marshall Lewis, Sr., was elected pastor. During his 
leadership, in 1927, the church was incorporated with the State of 
Louisiana under the official name of New Home Missionary Baptist Church 
of New Orleans, and the congregation grew to more than 200 members. 
With the need to expand, the church moved from its original site to a 
larger location at 3140 Jackson Avenue. Rev. Lewis served until his 
death in 1946. Rev. Murphy Smith was elected pastor in 1947 and served 
until 1952, and during his tenure a new church building was constructed 
at the Jackson Avenue location.
  After the death of Rev. Smith, Rev. Freddie H. Dunn was elected 
pastor in 1953, a dynamic and influential leader throughout the New 
Orleans area. During his leadership, membership continued to grow, and 
a new church building on Jackson Avenue was dedicated in July 1962. In 
October 1964, Pastor Dunn resigned to become pastor of New Hope Baptist 
Church, and many members followed him, leaving behind only a committed 
few. Under the direction of the Louisiana Freedmen Association, the 
search for a new pastor began, and Rev. Willie Hausey, president of the 
Association, officiated over the church's operations and provided 
guidance during that time.
  After much prayer and deliberation, a young man from Woodville, 
Mississippi, Robert C. Blakes, was chosen as pastor. He was ordained 
and installed in January 1965 and began his pastorate with only 30 
members. Facing many challenges, with only a handful of members and a 
lack of finances, he proved to be a man of faith who put his trust in 
God, and the church grew and prospered despite the many obstacles in 
its path. Under Prophet Robert C. Blakes, Sr., New Home Ministries has 
been a beacon of spiritual guidance in the greater New Orleans area for 
decades. The ministry has grown to more than 10,000 members and has 
expanded its reach to Baton Rouge, Hammond, Algiers, and Houston, 
Texas.
  After the passing of Prophet Blakes, Sr., his sons, Bishop R.C. 
Blakes, Jr., and Bishop Samuel R. Blakes, took up the mantle and have 
continued to lead New Home Ministries in its mission of spreading the 
gospel and empowering believers. Bishop Samuel Raymond Blakes, the 
youngest son of Prophet Robert Charles Blakes, Sr., and Lady Lois R. 
Blakes, was called by God at a very young age and has been preaching 
the Word of God ever since. He has served as Senior Pastor of New Home 
Family Worship Center in New Orleans since 1995 and in Baton Rouge 
since 2008, and under his leadership the church has grown from just 14 
members to a thriving congregation of thousands.
  From a small group of followers gathered on a New Orleans street 
corner in 1918, to a ministry of thousands that has carried its message 
across Louisiana and beyond, New Home's story is one of faith, 
resilience, and growth. Its presence in Baton Rouge since 2008 has 
added to the strength of our faith community.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in celebrating New Home 
Ministries. Bishop Samuel R. Blakes, Bishop R.C. Blakes, Jr., and the 
dedicated congregation for more than a century of faith and service to 
the people of Louisiana. May New Home Ministries continue to be a 
beacon of spiritual guidance for generations to come.
```
