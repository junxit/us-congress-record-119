# HONORING NEW ST. JOHN BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                  HONORING NEW ST. JOHN BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 5, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor New St. John 
Missionary Baptist Church of Baton Rouge, Louisiana, a congregation 
whose history is both inspiring and revealing, and whose significance 
today is as enriching as it was when the church began in 1919 in the 
Benevolent Hall on Peach Street under the leadership of the late Rev. 
Octave Mitchell of Addis, Louisiana. The early membership consisted of 
59 people, formerly members of Macedonia Baptist Church, and the site 
on which New St. John stands today was purchased by its members with 
$1,500 received from Macedonia Baptist Church.
  On October 31, 1921, Rev. Mitchell was officially elected pastor. 
That same year, a congregation of 54 members began erecting a concrete 
block church at a cost of $9,600. When funds ran out before the 
building was complete, two dedicated members, the late Brother Isom 
Smith, Chairman of the Deacon Board, and the late Sister Janie Spears, 
Chairman of the Deaconess Board, mortgaged their homes to secure the 
additional funds needed. On November 6, 1921, all debts had been paid 
and the church was dedicated to God. It was in this church that the 
Fourth District Missionary Baptist Sunday School and Baptist Training 
Union Association had its beginning. Among the early officers of the 
church were Brothers Isom Smith, Ike Cage, Henry Williams, Oscar 
Jackson, John Stafford, James Green, Robert Franklin, Otis Criss, and 
Willie Locks, and Sisters Janie Peterson, Malou Cage, Janie Spears, 
Lucinda Green, Millie Woods, Frozene John, Anna Johnson, Margaret 
Simms, Julia Lews, Alice Johnson, Rosa Smith Brown-Locks, Josephine 
Smith, and Willie Bell Monget.
  In August 1928, Rev. E.D. Billoups was called to the pastorate, and 
under his leadership the membership grew. In 1930, the church building 
was remodeled, with twenty feet added and a study built for the pastor 
at a cost of more than $5,000. Rev. Billoups later observed major 
defects in the walls of the building and influenced the membership to 
work toward a new church. After several years of raising funds, 
construction of the present two-story brick church began in 1950, and 
it was completed and dedicated to God in October 1951. By April 1954, 
the church was free of all debt. In 1959, the membership purchased four 
lots southeast of the church for parking at a cost of $20,000, and in 
May 1960, construction began on an Educational Building, The Billoups 
Center, which provided classrooms, an office, and restrooms and was 
completed the same year. An additional story was added a few years 
later to provide space for recreation, larger meetings, and other 
church activities.
  On March 7, 1982, Rev. W. Marshall Myles was called to the pastorate. 
Under his leadership, the church was renovated and redecorated, adding 
an elevator from the first floor to the sanctuary, more office space, 
and a commercial kitchen. When Rev. Myles became pastor, the church's 
ministries included the Deacon and Deaconess Boards, Young Adult and 
Senior Usher Boards, the Senior, Young People, and Cherubim Choirs, the 
Missionary Circle, Pulpit Circle, New St. John Laborers, Prayer Band, 
Pastor's Aid Circle, Trustee Board, Brotherhood, Flower Committee, 
Sunday School and Baptist Training Union, and the Married Women 
Fellowship and Service League. Over the years, the church has added 
many more, including the Department of Christian Education, the 
Cultural Development Support Guild, Boy Scouts, a Scholarship Fund, 
Usher Board #3, a Youth Department, the New St. John Boy's Academy, the 
Senior Wellness Ministry, Sister to Sister Ministry, Social Justice 
Ministry, Youth Coordinating Council, Vision 2000, Master's Men, Male 
Chorus, Voices of NSJ, Refreshing and Renewing Bible Study, Youth 
Ushers, and a Tutoring Program.
  New St. John has also looked beyond its own walls. Through the years, 
the church has held membership in, and given financial support to, the 
Baptist World Alliance, the National Council of Churches, the National 
Baptist Convention U.S.A., Inc., the Foreign Mission Board, the Home 
Mission Board, the American Baptist Theological Seminary, the Louisiana 
Baptist State Convention, the Fourth District Baptist Association, and 
Leland College, strengthening the broader Baptist and faith community 
of Baton Rouge and beyond.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in celebrating New St. 
John Missionary Baptist Church and its dedicated congregation for more 
than a century of sacrifice, faith, and service to the people of Baton 
Rouge. May New St. John continue to be a source of inspiration for 
generations to come.
```
