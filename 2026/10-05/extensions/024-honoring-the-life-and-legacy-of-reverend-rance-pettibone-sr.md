# HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF REVEREND RANCE PETTIBONE, SR.

**Speaking:** Bishop, Sanford D. (B000490) [D-GA]

```
     HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF REVEREND RANCE PETTIBONE, SR.

                                 ______
                                 

                      HON. SANFORD D. BISHOP, JR.

                               of georgia

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 5, 2026

  Mr. BISHOP. Mr. Speaker, I rise today with a heavy heart to pay 
tribute to the life and legacy of Reverend Rance Pettibone, Sr., a 
soldier, a shepherd, a broadcaster, a husband, a father, and a 
gentleman of the highest order, who has gone home to be with the Lord. 
Reverend Pettibone passed from labor to reward on September 26, 2026. A 
home-going service to celebrate his remarkable life was held on 
Saturday, October 3, 2026, at 2:00 p.m. at Second Bethesda Baptist 
Church in Albany, Georgia. Reverend Pettibone was a man who gave his 
life away in service: to his country, to his God, to his family, and to 
the city of Albany, Georgia, which he so dearly loved.
  Born in Foley, Alabama, Rance was the biological son of Mazie Bell 
Dinkins and James Barnett, Sr., and was raised by Ambus and Ruby 
Pettibone. He carried both families in his heart for all of his days, 
honoring the parents who gave him life and the parents who gave him a 
home. In that quiet act of gratitude, we can already see the man he 
would become: a man who never forgot the people who helped make him who 
he was.
  Reverend Pettibone answered his nation's call and gave more than 
twenty years of his life to the United States Army. A Vietnam veteran, 
he served as a paratrooper, an infantryman, a sniper, and a supply 
sergeant. He knew the weight of a soldier's pack, the roar of an open 
aircraft door, and the cost of the freedom we too often take for 
granted. Again and again, he stepped out into the open sky for the sake 
of a country he loved.
  When his uniform was finally folded away, his service did not end. It 
simply changed its form. For Rance Pettibone had heard another call, 
the call to serve God and God's people, and he answered that one with 
the same courage and discipline he had carried into battle.
  He served in ministry in Europe, in Savannah, and in Brunswick before 
making Albany his home. In 2001, he accepted the call to pastor Second 
Bethesda Missionary Baptist Church, and for more than two decades he 
led that congregation with dedication, wisdom, and a genuine love for 
God's people. He was not a pastor who stood apart from his flock. He 
was also a faithful member of that church family, praying with them, 
rejoicing with them, and weeping with them. He served alongside his 
fellow ministers in the Albany Baptist Minister's Conference, always 
ready to lend his hand and his heart.
  His ministry could not be contained by church walls. Every Saturday, 
his voice reached homes, cars, and kitchens across Southwest Georgia 
through his radio program, ``Faith, Family, & Common Sense,'' on WZBN 
105.5. And on the first Saturday of each month, he gathered the people 
of Albany to pray for their city, its schools, its leaders, and its 
first responders. Many a teacher, police officer, firefighter, and 
public servant went about their work never knowing that a soldier of 
the cross had lifted them up before the throne of God. WALB rightly 
honored him as one of its ``Heroes Among Us,'' for that is exactly what 
he was.
  On April 15, 2006, Rance married the love of his life, Sandra, and 
together they blended their families into seven children, four 
daughters and three sons. Seven was his favorite number, because he 
believed it represented completeness, and in Sandra and their children, 
his life was made complete. He believed that a man's first 
responsibility was to provide for and protect his family, and he lived 
that belief every single day.
  He also knew sorrow that no parent should ever have to bear. He laid 
to rest two of his sons, Jason and Sheldon. Yet even through that 
unspeakable grief, he did not let go of God, and he did not stop 
pouring love into the family that remained.
  Rance Pettibone was a true gentleman. He held doors. He offered his 
arm to help people down the steps. He was the first to notice when 
someone needed a seat or a helping hand. His favorite saying was 
simple: ``Always think of others before you think of yourself.'' He did 
not just say those words. He lived them.
  Reverend Pettibone was preceded in death by his sons, Jason and 
Sheldon; his biological parents, Mazie Bell Dinkins and James Barnett, 
Sr.; the parents who raised him, Ambus and Ruby Pettibone; his sister, 
Zola Prim Anderson; his brother, Prentice Jackson; his brother-in-law, 
Zeno Green; three great-granddaughters; and other beloved family 
members.
  He leaves to cherish his memory his devoted wife, Sandra; his 
children, Anissa, Charlet Lewis (Tony), Kendra, Shannon (Rashard), and 
Rance Pettibone, Jr. (Tina); his daughter-in-law, Sharon Pettibone; his 
sister, Janice Green; his brothers, James Barnett, Steve Barnett, 
Melton Barnett (Mary), Louis Dinkins (Jackie), Gary Jackson, and George 
Jackson, Sr.; 27 grandchildren; 21 great-grandchildren; his mother-in-
law, Elizabeth Jones; his sisters-in-law, Patricia Gillison and 
Jeraldine Jones; his brothers-in-law, Jesse (Vickie), Jimmy (Alfreda), 
John, and Darryll (Tammie) Jones; and a host of aunts, uncles, cousins, 
extended family, church family, and friends.
  Mr. Speaker, the airwaves are quiet now on Saturday mornings in 
Albany, and a familiar voice no longer leads the people in prayer on 
the first Saturday of the month. But the prayers he prayed are still 
rising. The wisdom he shared is still guiding. And the lives he touched 
are still bearing fruit.
  Rance believed that seven was the number of completeness. And when we 
look back on his life, we see seven things he gave us in full measure: 
faith, family, service, sacrifice, wisdom, kindness, and love. His was 
a life made complete.
  The soldier who once leapt from aircraft into the open sky has made 
his final jump. And this time, there was no uncertainty about where he 
would land, for the Lord Himself was waiting to receive him and to say, 
``Well done, thou good and faithful servant.'' Like the Apostle Paul, 
he could say. ``I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, 
I have kept the faith.''
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join my wife Vivian and me, along 
with more than 765,000 people of Georgia's Second Congressional 
District, extend our deepest condolences to Sandra, to his children, 
grandchildren, and great-grandchildren, to the Second Bethesda 
Missionary Baptist Church family, and to all who loved him. We pray 
that they will be comforted by the God he served so faithfully. I ask 
my colleagues to join me in honoring the life and legacy of Reverend 
Rance Pettibone, Sr., a true American hero and a faithful servant of 
God.
```
