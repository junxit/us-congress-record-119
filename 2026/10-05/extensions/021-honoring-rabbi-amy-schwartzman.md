# HONORING RABBI AMY SCHWARTZMAN

**Speaking:** Beyer, Donald S. (B001292) [D-VA]

```
                     HONORING RABBI AMY SCHWARTZMAN

                                 ______
                                 

                       HON. DONALD S. BEYER, JR.

                              of virginia

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 5, 2026

  Mr. BEYER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Rabbi Amy 
Schwartzman's distinguished service to Temple Rodef Shalom in Falls 
Church, Virginia. Rabbi Schwartzman served as senior rabbi for twenty-
eight years, becoming one of the first women in the nation to assume 
such a role and lead a large Jewish congregation.
  A graduate of Hebrew Union College--Jewish Institute of Religion, 
Rabbi Schwartzman received her Master of Hebrew Literature in 1988 and 
was ordained as a rabbi in 1990. In 2015, she was awarded Doctor of 
Divinity, honoris causa, from the Hebrew Union College--Jewish 
Institute of Religion to mark her 25th anniversary of ordination. 
Throughout her rabbinate, Rabbi Schwartzman has provided leadership to 
the broader Reform Jewish movement. She served on the Executive Board 
of the Central Conference of American Rabbis and as a co-coordinator of 
the Women's Rabbinic Network of Reform Judaism.
  Under Rabbi Schwartzman's leadership, Temple Rodef Shalom's 
congregation grew from 600 families to over 1,850 families, becoming 
one of the largest congregations in the Reform Jewish movement and a 
significant religious institution in Northern Virginia. More important 
than its growth, however, has been Rabbi Schwartzman's commitment to 
creating a congregation rooted in community, compassion, social 
responsibility, and meaningful Jewish life. Rabbi Schwanzman's work has 
centered on social justice, fighting antisemitism, and advocating for 
human dignity. She has been a strong voice for interfaith understanding 
and has cultivated strong relationships with religious and civic 
leaders throughout Northern Virginia.
  For more than three decades, Rabbi Schwartzman has been not only the 
spiritual leader of Temple Rodef Shalom, but also an important 
religious and civic presence in Northern Virginia. I ask my colleagues 
to join me in honoring her thirty-six years of commitment to one 
congregation and to the larger community it serves.
```
