# RECOGNIZING OCTOBER AS NATIONAL SPINE HEALTH AWARENESS MONTH

**Speaking:** Walkinshaw, James R. (W000831) [D-VA]

```
      RECOGNIZING OCTOBER AS NATIONAL SPINE HEALTH AWARENESS MONTH

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. JAMES R. WALKINSHAW

                              of virginia

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 1, 2026

  Mr. WALKINSHAW. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize October as 
National Spine Health Awareness Month and in raising awareness of the 
importance of spine health for Americans of every age.
  More than 100 million Americans experience neck or back conditions 
each year, and low back pain is the leading cause of disability 
worldwide. The impact of spine conditions reaches far beyond pain. They 
can limit mobility and independence, disrupt work and family life, 
affect military readiness, and contribute significantly to health care 
costs and lost productivity.
  I am proud that the National Spine Health Foundation, headquartered 
in Reston, Virginia, is located in my district. Founded in 2002, the 
Foundation is the Nation's only patient-centered nonprofit dedicated 
exclusively to helping people overcome debilitating spinal conditions. 
Through research, education, and advocacy, the Foundation provides 
patients and families with trusted, evidence-based information to help 
them make informed decisions and navigate their spine health journeys 
with confidence.
  This September, the Foundation brought the importance of spine health 
directly to Capitol Hill through the first Congressional Spine Health 
Fair. Patients, physicians, researchers, military leaders, advocates, 
and national spine organizations came together in the Rayburn House 
Office Building to help Members of Congress and congressional staff 
better understand the human, economic, and societal burden of spine 
disease.
  The Fair highlighted an important message: spine health is about far 
more than back pain. It includes conditions ranging from pediatric 
spinal deformity and scoliosis to degenerative and neurological 
disorders, traumatic injuries, osteoporosis, and fragility fractures. 
These conditions can affect people at every age and at every stage of 
life.
  The Foundation is also helping unite the spine community through the 
Coalition for Spine Health, a collaborative alliance of leading 
professional societies, research organizations, and nonprofit partners. 
By working together on patient education, research, and advocacy, these 
organizations are helping translate advances in spine care into better 
information and better outcomes for patients.
  That same collaborative approach guides the Foundation's work on some 
of the most important challenges facing spine patients today. Its Bone 
Health Initiative is expanding education about osteoporosis, bone 
quality, fracture prevention, and the role strong bones play in 
successful spine care. Its work on opioids, pain, and spine health 
provides patients with evidence-based information about chronic pain, 
opioid risks, and approaches to pain management.
  The Foundation is also advancing the Military Spine Health 
Initiative, bringing together leaders in spine care and military 
medicine to address the unique needs of active-duty service members and 
veterans. Spine conditions

[[Page E1012]]

can have profound consequences for military readiness, return to duty, 
long-term mobility, and quality of life. This initiative seeks to 
improve education and advance strategies that help those who serve our 
country receive the spine care and information they need.
  Research remains central to all of this work. By collaborating with 
physicians, researchers, and institutions, the Foundation helps 
generate evidence and translate new knowledge into information patients 
can understand and use. As innovation continues to transform the 
prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of spinal conditions, connecting 
research with patient education is more important than ever.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in recognizing October as 
National Spine Health Awareness Month and in thanking the National 
Spine Health Foundation, the patients and advocates who share their 
experiences, the researchers advancing our understanding of spinal 
conditions, and the health care professionals who care for spine 
patients every day.
```
