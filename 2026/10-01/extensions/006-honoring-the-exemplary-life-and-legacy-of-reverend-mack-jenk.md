# HONORING THE EXEMPLARY LIFE AND LEGACY OF REVEREND MACK JENKINS

**Speaking:** Sewell, Terri A. (S001185) [D-AL]

```
    HONORING THE EXEMPLARY LIFE AND LEGACY OF REVEREND MACK JENKINS

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. TERRI A. SEWELL

                               of alabama

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 1, 2026

  Ms. SEWELL. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the exemplary life and 
legacy of Reverend Mack Jenkins, who passed away on September 22, 2026, 
at the age of 85.
  Reverend Jenkins was born on January 10, 1941, to the late James, Sr. 
and Hannah Bracy Jenkins. The youngest of ten children, virtues such as 
kindness, humility, forgiveness, and unconditional love were 
cornerstones of his childhood.
  Reverend Jenkins began his educational journey at the historic 
Calhoun School. Following his graduation in 1958, he began his pursuit 
of a degree in Fashion-Clothing and Tailoring at Wenonah State 
Technical Institute, which is now known as Lawson State Community 
College. His pursuit of his degree was suspended when he was drafted 
into the United States Army, where he proudly served his country. He 
later matriculated at Trenholm State Technical College, where he earned 
his degree in Heating, Air Conditioning, and Refrigeration, which 
prepared him for his career in the United States Department of Veteran 
Affairs.
  A committed servant of God, Reverend Jenkins confessed Christ at an 
early age and became an active member of Rock Creek Missionary Baptist 
Church. While at Rock Creek, he faithfully served in several 
capacities, such as choir member, deacon, Sunday School teacher, and 
superintendent. He was also a member of the renowned Beulah Primitive 
Baptist Church Male Chorus.
  A strong believer in Christian education, Reverend Jenkins served as 
Congress President for the Old Alabama District Association for more 
than 30 years. Under his leadership, the first nationally certified 
Baptist Leadership School was established, and numerous Rock Creek 
members, along with other district associations members from Lowndes, 
Macon, Dallas, and Monroe counties earned certification. Additionally, 
he carried and provided funds for members to attend local, state, and 
national Baptist conventions to stay abreast of the latest trends in 
Christian education. He also attended the Southeast District State 
Convention in June and the Alabama Missionary Baptist Congress of 
Christian Education in July of 2026.
  In 1988 he sensed a deep God-given appointment to preach the gospel 
and accepted a call to the ministry. In February 1989, he was called to 
serve as pastor of the Rock Creek Missionary Baptist Church. For more 
than 37 years Reverend Jenkins shepherded the flock entrusted to him. 
Reverend Jenkins was relied upon to pray for, love, guide, visit, 
counsel, and help each and every member. He did not simply occupy the 
role of pastor; he carried the responsibility of shepherding in his 
heart.
  Reverend Mack Jenkins is proceeded in death by his parents, Mr. 
James, Sr. and Mrs. Hannah Bracy Jenkins; his siblings, James Jenkins, 
Jr., Willie Jenkins Humphries, Viola Jenkins Gatson, Reverend Leroy 
Jenkins, Joe Jenkins, Sam Jenkins, Leon Jenkins, Leon Jenkins, Isaiah 
Jenkins, and Novella Humphries. He is survived by his loving and 
devoted wife of 62 years, Rubye Osborne Jenkins; his sons, Frederick 
Jose Jenkins and Mack Aldrich (Kecia) Jenkins; one daughter, Gwendolyn 
(Lewis) Daniel; grandchildren, Fredria (Chris) Jordan, Atravius Smith, 
Kaylin Jenkins, Brandon (Marquetta) Daniel, Morgan (Gavin) Scott, 
Deidra (Kamontee) Sigler, Kaitlyn Patterson, and Aerrien Tarrance; 22 
great grandchildren; one brother, Willis Jenkins; brothers-in-law, Tom 
Osborne, Sr. and Benjamin Osborne; sisters-in-law, Juliette Jenkins, 
Dorothy Gardner Jenkins Sanders, Vanessa Jenkins, Willie Frances 
Rudolph, Maggie (Eddie) Walker, Johnie Watson, Bonnie Johnson; 
godchildren, Jackie (Clemmon, Jr.) Barlow, Yvette Patterson, Diane 
Richardson, Addie (Leon) Bennett, Willie Mack, Jr. (Catha) Patterson, 
Michelle Jenkins, Barbara (Don) Hill; and a host of nieces, nephews, 
cousins and friends.
  On a personal note, Reverend Mack Jenkins was not only a beloved 
pastor, but my cherished relative as well. A pillar of the Lowndes 
County community, Reverend Jenkins was devoted to his family and 
congregation and was a beloved husband, father, grandfather, brother, 
cousin, uncle, pastor, neighbor, and friend. May we find comfort in 
knowing that Reverend Jenkins lived a full and blessed life and that 
his legacy will live on in the countless lives he touched.
  On behalf of Alabama's 7th Congressional District, I ask my 
colleagues to join me in celebrating the exemplary life and legacy of 
Reverend Mack Jenkins.

                          ____________________
```
