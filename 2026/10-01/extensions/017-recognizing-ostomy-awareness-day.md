# RECOGNIZING OSTOMY AWARENESS DAY

**Speaking:** Dingell, Debbie (D000624) [D-MI]

```
                    RECOGNIZING OSTOMY AWARENESS DAY

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. DEBBIE DINGELL

                              of michigan

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 1, 2026

  Mrs. DINGELL. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Ostomy Awareness 
Day, which will be observed on Saturday, October 3, 2026.
  An ostomy can be a difficult, frightening, and deeply personal 
journey--not just for the individual, but for their entire family. Yet 
for so many, this procedure is nothing short of a second chance at 
life. It provides a path forward for men and women facing serious, 
often devastating medical challenges--including colorectal cancer, 
inflammatory bowel disease, traumatic injuries, and severe illnesses. 
These surgeries are truly lifesaving and life-restoring. They give 
people their lives back, allowing them to return to their jobs, care 
for their children, stay involved in their communities, and get back to 
doing what they love.
  But too often, people living with an ostomy suffer in silence due to 
misunderstanding, stigma, and unnecessary barriers to care. Ostomy 
Awareness Day gives us a critical opportunity to educate the public, 
break down those taboos, and remind everyone that an ostomy does not 
define a person's worth or limit what they can achieve. Back home in 
Michigan, approximately 25,000 of our neighbors, friends, family 
members, coworkers, veterans, and community leaders are living with an 
ostomy. They represent the very best of Michigan--showing incredible 
resilience, strength, and determination in overcoming daunting health 
battles to live full, active lives.
  We also owe a deep debt of gratitude to the dedicated support systems 
around them--the families, caregivers, physicians, nurses, certified 
ostomy nurse specialists, and healthcare professionals who walk 
alongside these patients every single day. Their compassion, expertise, 
and tireless work make a world of difference in helping patients 
adjust, heal, and thrive.
  This year's theme, ``One Community--Stronger Together,'' hits home. 
It reminds us that no one should ever have to navigate a major health 
challenge alone. Access to quality care, peer support, and public 
education are essential to ensuring every individual living with an 
ostomy has the resources and dignity they deserve.
  An ostomy is not the end of someone's story--it is the start of a 
healthier, more hopeful chapter. I am proud to stand with the ostomy 
community in Michigan and across our entire Nation, and I urge my 
colleagues to join me in raising awareness, ending the stigma, and 
celebrating these remarkable survivors.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in recognizing October 3, 
2026, as Ostomy Awareness Day.
```
