# RECOGNIZING OSTOMY AWARENESS DAY

**Speaking:** Smith, Christopher H. (S000522) [R-NJ]

```
                    RECOGNIZING OSTOMY AWARENESS DAY

                                 ______
                                 

                       HON. CHRISTOPHER H. SMITH

                             of new jersey

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 1, 2026

  Mr. SMITH of New Jersey. Mr. Speaker, I rise to recognize Ostomy Day, 
which will be observed on Saturday, October 3, 2026. For the last 
several years the first Saturday of every October has been recognized 
as Ostomy Awareness Day by many who have undergone ostomy surgery as 
well as their caregivers and families.
  Ostomy surgery can provide a path forward for people facing serious 
illnesses and conditions, including Spina Bifida, colorectal cancer, 
inflammatory bowel disease, traumatic injuries because of domestic 
abuse or military engagement, and any other number of medical 
challenges. While a drastic and often disruptive surgery, the procedure 
can be lifesaving and life-enhancing, allowing individuals to return to 
their families, their workplaces, their communities, and the activities 
they love. Yet too often, people living with an ostomy face 
misunderstanding and unnecessary barriers to treatment.
  As far back as 1984, President Ronald Reagan recognized the benefits 
of increasing awareness when he designated August 1984 as national 
Ostomy Awareness Month. The awareness campaign is now carried forward 
annually with organizations such as the United Ostomy Associations of 
America and the International Ostomy Association, as well as members of 
the Spina Bifida community, highlighting the day as an important 
opportunity to educate the public, dispel misconceptions, and remind us 
that an ostomy does not define a person or diminish the quality of a 
life.
  In New Jersey, approximately 23,000 people are living with an ostomy. 
They are our neighbors, friends, family members, coworkers, veterans, 
caregivers, students, and community leaders. Their experiences 
demonstrate the strength and resilience of people who have overcome 
significant medical challenges and continue to live full and meaningful 
lives.
  Ostomy Awareness Day also recognizes the families, caregivers, 
physicians, nurses and certified ostomy nurse specialists, and other 
health care professionals who provide essential care and support to 
those living with an ostomy. Their compassion and expertise make an 
enormous difference in helping patients adjust to life after surgery 
and thrive.
  This year's Ostomy Awareness Day theme, ``One Community--Stronger 
Together,'' is a fitting reminder that no one should have to navigate 
life with an ostomy alone. Awareness, education, peer support, and 
access to quality care can help ensure that every person who needs an 
ostomy has the resources and support necessary to live with confidence 
and dignity.
  On Ostomy Awareness Day, we note the resilience of the ostomy 
community and recognize the dedicated professionals and medical 
advances that have made these life-saving surgeries possible. Most 
importantly, we affirm that an ostomy is not the end of a person's 
story--but, with education, awareness, and proper continued care, can 
be the beginning of a healthier and more hopeful chapter.
```
