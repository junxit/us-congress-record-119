# HONORING SALVADOR "SAL" LICCARDO

**Speaking:** Lofgren, Zoe (L000397) [D-CA]

```
                   HONORING SALVADOR ``SAL'' LICCARDO

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. ZOE LOFGREN

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 1, 2026

  Ms. LOFGREN. Mr. Speaker. I rise today, as Chair of and on behalf of 
the California Democratic delegation, to give our condolences to our 
colleague Representative Sam T. Liccardo on the passing of his father, 
Salvador ``Sal'' Liccardo.
  Mr. Liccardo was born in 1935 and grew up on San Jose's North Fifth 
Street. After attending Santa Clara University, he served in the United 
States Army for two years, where he handled top-secret military 
communications in the Signal Corps. Following his military service, Mr. 
Liccardo returned home and earned his law degree from Santa Clara 
University School of Law in 1960. After graduating law school, he 
opened his own law practice, and within 30 days after passing the 
California Bar exam, Salvador was working on his first felony criminal 
case. As a lawyer, Mr. Liccardo took

[[Page E1006]]

cases from the most vulnerable in his community, including injured farm 
workers, student activists, and indigent criminal defendants.
  He then transitioned to personal injury cases, which became his 
calling. To hone his craft and better advocate for his clients, he took 
medicine classes at night. It was this intellectual curiosity, 
dedication, and fidelity to his clients that made Mr. Liccardo rise 
above the rest and enabled him to be a standout leader in the legal 
field.
  In the 1970s, Mr. Liccardo earned national distinction for winning a 
million-dollar verdict for women's reproductive health. Later, Mr. 
Liccardo was inducted into the Inner Circle of Advocates, where he and 
his colleagues founded Trial Lawyers for Public Justice (known now as 
Public Justice), a nonprofit national law firm for consumer rights. 
Over a remarkable 40-plus-year legal career, Mr. Liccardo was committed 
to defending civil rights, upholding the rule of law, and defending 
access to justice.
  Additionally, he served on the board of his alma maters, Bellarmine 
College Preparatory and Santa Clara University. Mr. Liccardo 
established an endowment at Bellarmine, ensuring that no child would be 
denied admission based on their family's inability to afford tuition.
  Despite his long and grueling hours in the office and the courtroom, 
Mr. Liccardo never forgot about what he loved most: his family. With 
his wife Laura, Sal's proudest legacy was their five children and 
spouses Laura, Kathleen, Paul and Toni, Rosalie and Joe, and Sam and 
Jessica; their grandchildren and spouses Jason and Jessica, Gabriella, 
and Brian; and great-grandchildren Thatcher and Sutton. Mr. Salvador 
Liccardo was a person we can all look up to, admire, and emulate. He 
was admired and loved by his community and his family.
```
