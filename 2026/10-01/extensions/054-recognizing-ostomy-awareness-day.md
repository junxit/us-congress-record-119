# RECOGNIZING OSTOMY AWARENESS DAY

**Speaking:** Fitzpatrick, Brian K. (F000466) [R-PA]

```
                    RECOGNIZING OSTOMY AWARENESS DAY

                                 ______
                                 

                       HON. BRIAN K. FITZPATRICK

                            of pennsylvania

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 1, 2026

  Mr. FITZPATRICK. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Ostomy 
Awareness Day, which will be observed on Saturday, October 3, 2026.
  An ostomy can be a difficult and deeply personal journey, but for 
many individuals, it is also a second chance at life. Ostomy surgery 
can provide a path forward for people facing serious illnesses and 
conditions, including colorectal cancer, inflammatory bowel disease, 
traumatic injuries because of domestic abuse or military engagement, 
and any other number of medical challenges. These procedures can be 
lifesaving and life-restoring, allowing individuals to return to their 
families, their workplaces, their communities, and the activities they 
love.
  Yet too often, people living with an ostomy face misunderstanding, 
stigma, and unnecessary barriers. Ostomy Awareness Day provides an 
important opportunity to educate the public, dispel misconceptions, and 
remind us that an ostomy does not define a person or diminish the 
quality of a life.
  In Pennsylvania, approximately 32,500 people are living with an 
ostomy. They are our neighbors, friends, family members, coworkers, 
veterans, caregivers, students, and community leaders. Their 
experiences demonstrate the strength and resilience of people who have 
overcome significant medical challenges and continue to live full and 
meaningful lives.
  I also want to recognize the families, caregivers, physicians, 
nurses, certified ostomy nurse specialists, and other health care 
professionals who provide essential care and support to those living 
with an ostomy. Their compassion and expertise make an enormous 
difference in helping patients adjust to life after surgery and thrive.
  This year's Ostomy Awareness Day theme, ``One Community--Stronger 
Together,'' is a fitting reminder that no one should have to navigate 
life with an ostomy alone. Awareness, education, peer support, and 
access to quality care can help ensure that every person who needs an 
ostomy has the resources and support necessary to live with confidence 
and dignity.
  On Ostomy Awareness Day, we celebrate the resilience of the ostomy 
community and recognize the medical advances and dedicated 
professionals that have made these life-saving surgeries possible. Most 
importantly, we affirm that an ostomy is not the end of a person's 
story--it can be the beginning of a healthier and more hopeful chapter.
  I am proud to stand with the Pennsylvania ostomy community and with 
ostomy communities across our Nation. I encourage my colleagues and all 
Americans to join me in raising awareness, breaking down stigma, and 
celebrating those whose lives have been saved and restored through 
ostomy surgery.
  I ask my colleagues to join me in recognizing October 3, 2026, as 
Ostomy Awareness Day.
```
