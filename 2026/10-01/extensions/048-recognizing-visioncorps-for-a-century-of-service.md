# RECOGNIZING VISIONCORPS FOR A CENTURY OF SERVICE

**Speaking:** Smucker, Lloyd (S001199) [R-PA]

```
            RECOGNIZING VISIONCORPS FOR A CENTURY OF SERVICE

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. LLOYD SMUCKER

                            of pennsylvania

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 1, 2026

  Mr. SMUCKER. Mr. Speaker, I rise to recognize VisionCorps on a 
century of service.
  VisionCorps was founded in 1926 as the Lancaster County Branch of the 
Pennsylvania Association of the Blind. Located in downtown Lancaster, 
the agency's mission was to help blind veterans from World War I, and 
others who were blind, learn a trade. In the following years, the 
agency grew in its manufacturing capabilities, ranging from leather 
work and basket weaving to subcontracts for toy manufacturing.
  What has defined VisionCorps throughout the past century is its 
collaboration with other organizations. It had an early partnership 
with the Lancaster Lions Club, which promoted fundraising and 
educational efforts. For the first fifty years, every president of its 
Board of Directors was a member of the Lions. Through its partnership 
with local hospitals, VisionCorps held free vision clinics for children 
who lived in low-income households. In 1980, the organization began a 
partnership with the military through the National Industries for the 
Blind to make military products, and in 2009, it began providing 
contract closeout support for military bases.
  After numerous name changes over its history, the agency became 
VisionCorps in 2017. Since then, the organization began its signature 
rappel fundraiser, the Eye Drop, and in 2023 expanded its footprint to 
include a dry foods packaging facility in Little Rock, Arkansas. Today, 
VisionCorps serves more than 2,000 individuals across five Pennsylvania 
counties through rehabilitation services and screens more than 11,000 
children for vision loss annually.
  I thank VisionCorps for its tremendous impact on Lancaster County and 
Pennsylvania's 11th Congressional District. Through its advocacy and 
partnership with the community, VisionCorps has changed the lives of 
thousands and made independence and autonomy for people who are blind 
possible.
```
