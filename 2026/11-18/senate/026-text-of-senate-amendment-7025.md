# Text of Senate Amendment 7025

**Speaking:** Ossoff, Jon (O000174) [D-GA]

```
  SA 7025. Mr. OSSOFF (for himself and Mr. Warnock) submitted an 
amendment intended to be proposed to amendment SA 7005 submitted by Mr. 
Ossoff (for himself and Mr. Warnock) and intended to be proposed to the 
amendment SA 6883 proposed by Mr. Thune (for Mrs. Capito (for herself, 
Mr. Lee, Mr. Whitehouse, and Mr. Heinrich)) to the bill H.R. 2347, to 
amend the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 to

[[Page S5776]]

exclude from gross income any damages, other than punitive damages, 
received on account of any sexual acts or sexual contact; which was 
ordered to lie on the table; as follows:

       On page 1 of the amendment, strike lines 13 and 14 and 
     insert the following:
       (f) Accelerated Permitting for Projects Using Domestic 
     Content.--
       (1) Priority treatment.--In processing applications, 
     assigning agency personnel and resources, and scheduling 
     environmental reviews and other required agency actions for 
     eligible projects that produce electricity from solar 
     (referred to in this subsection as ``eligible solar 
     projects'') pursuant to this section, the Secretary of the 
     Interior or the Secretary of Agriculture, as applicable, 
     shall give priority to such eligible solar projects that 
     qualify under paragraph (2) over eligible solar projects that 
     do not qualify under that paragraph.
       (2) Domestic content certification.--
       (A) In general.--An eligible solar project shall qualify 
     for priority treatment under paragraph (1) if an applicant 
     submits to the Secretary of the Interior or the Secretary of 
     Agriculture, as applicable, a certification that a project is 
     expected, when placed into service, to satisfy the domestic 
     content requirements described in section 48E(a)(3)(B) of the 
     Internal Revenue Code of 1986 as of the date of enactment of 
     this Act (irrespective of whether the credit is claimed or 
     available to be claimed), including applicable regulations 
     and guidance.
       (B) Application.--
       (i) In general.--An applicant seeking priority treatment 
     under paragraph (1) for an eligible solar project shall 
     submit to the Secretary of the Interior or the Secretary of 
     Agriculture, as applicable, a certification at such time, in 
     such manner, and containing such information as the Secretary 
     of the Interior or the Secretary of Agriculture, as 
     applicable, may require, including--

       (I) a good faith commitment, explaining that the applicant 
     shall commit to ensuring that, once placed into service, the 
     eligible solar project will satisfy the requirements 
     described in subparagraph (A);
       (II) at the discretion of the applicant, a demonstration of 
     expected compliance with the requirements described in 
     subparagraph (A) using the domestic content requirements, 
     methodologies, or elective safe harbors established by the 
     Secretary of the Treasury, including those applicable to 
     repowered facilities; and
       (III) any other information the Secretary of the Interior 
     or the Secretary of Agriculture, as applicable, may require.

       (ii) Limitation.--Except for the requirements described in 
     clause (i), no Federal agency may impose any additional 
     domestic content, documentation, review, or other approval 
     requirement as a condition to receive priority treatment 
     under paragraph (1).
       (C) Completion and records.--
       (i) Completion.--Not later than 90 days after an eligible 
     solar project is placed into service, an applicant that 
     received priority treatment under paragraph (1) shall submit 
     to the Secretary of the Interior or the Secretary of 
     Agriculture, as applicable, a certification explaining that, 
     as applicable--

       (I) the eligible solar project has satisfied all the 
     requirements of subparagraph (A); or
       (II) the eligible solar project has not satisfied all the 
     requirements of subparagraph (A), including the level of 
     noncompliance.

       (ii) Records.--An applicant that received priority 
     treatment under paragraph (1) shall--

       (I) retain records sufficient to substantiate any 
     certification required under this paragraph for a period of 
     not less than 3 years after the eligible solar project is 
     placed into service; and
       (II) make the records required under subclause (I) 
     available, on request, to the Secretary of the Interior or 
     the Secretary of Agriculture, as applicable.

       (3) Verification.--
       (A) In general.--The Secretary of the Interior or the 
     Secretary of Agriculture, as applicable, shall consider a 
     certification submitted under paragraph (2)(A) as sufficient 
     to establish eligibility for priority without an independent 
     determination of domestic content compliance under that 
     paragraph, subject to the condition that the Secretary of the 
     Interior or the Secretary of Agriculture, as applicable, 
     shall subsequently verify the accuracy of the certification 
     using only the domestic content methodologies established by 
     the Secretary of the Treasury.
       (B) Inclusions.--Verification under subparagraph (A) may 
     include a review of the completion certification and 
     supporting records required under paragraph (2)(B), including 
     after placement into service.
       (C) Delay.--Verification under subparagraph (A) shall not 
     delay an environmental review or other Federal agency action.
       (4) Loss of priority.--
       (A) In general.--An eligible solar project shall cease to 
     qualify for priority treatment under paragraph (1) if, before 
     issuance of the final Federal authorization or permit 
     required for the eligible solar project--
       (i) the applicant withdraws the certification submitted 
     under paragraph (2)(A); or
       (ii) the Secretary of the Interior or the Secretary of 
     Agriculture, as applicable, determines, in writing, through 
     verification under paragraph (3), that--

       (I) the certification materially misrepresented the 
     expected compliance of the eligible solar project at the time 
     the certification was submitted; or
       (II) the eligible solar project is no longer reasonably 
     expected to satisfy the applicable domestic content 
     requirements described in paragraph (2).

       (B) Determination.--A determination under subparagraph 
     (A)(ii) shall describe the basis for its findings.
       (C) Effect.--
       (i) In general.--A loss of priority treatment under 
     subparagraph (A) shall apply--

       (I) prospectively only; and
       (II) from the date--

       (aa) of withdrawal by the applicant under subparagraph 
     (A)(i); or
       (bb) a determination is made under subparagraph (A)(ii).
       (ii) Impact.--Loss of priority treatment under subparagraph 
     (A) shall not--

       (I) invalidate any prior Federal agency action or issued 
     Federal authorization or permit;
       (II) restart any review period;
       (III) by itself constitute grounds for denying, suspending, 
     modifying, or revoking an issued Federal authorization or 
     permit; or
       (IV) relieve an applicant that received any priority 
     treatment of the completion certification and record keeping 
     requirements under paragraph (2)(C).

       (5) Rule of construction.--Nothing in this subsection 
     shall--
       (A) shorten any period for public comment or participation, 
     alter any deadline applicable to judicial proceedings, or 
     waive or alter any substantive requirement of the National 
     Environmental Policy Act of 1969 (42 U.S.C. 4321 et seq.) or 
     any other applicable Federal law;
       (B) require approval of any Federal authorization or 
     permit;
       (C) extend any deadline otherwise applicable to an eligible 
     solar project;
       (D) make an eligible solar project ineligible for 
     consideration because the applicant does not seek priority 
     treatment under paragraph (1); or
       (E) limit any right, protection, procedure, cause of 
     action, or remedy otherwise available to an eligible solar 
     project, applicant, or project sponsor under this Act or any 
     other applicable Federal law.
       (6) Civil penalty for material misrepresentation.--
       (A) In general.--An applicant that knowingly or recklessly 
     makes a material misrepresentation in a certification 
     submitted under paragraph (2)(A) shall be liable to the 
     United States for a civil penalty in an amount equal to 2 
     percent of the total costs of construction, subject to 
     subparagraph (C), for the applicable eligible solar project.
       (B) Inclusion.--In subparagraph (A), a material 
     misrepresentation includes a certification made without a 
     reasonable factual basis if the applicant knowingly or 
     recklessly disregarded facts material to the expected 
     satisfaction of the applicable domestic content requirements 
     described in paragraph (2).
       (C) Incomplete projects.--
       (i) In general.--If construction of the eligible solar 
     project has not been completed on the date on which an action 
     under subparagraph (E) is commenced, the total construction 
     costs under subparagraph (A) shall be determined from costs 
     incurred and reasonably documented projected costs of 
     completing the eligible solar project, excluding land 
     acquisition, financing, and operation costs.
       (ii) Abandonment.--If the eligible solar project has been 
     permanently abandoned, as determined by the Secretary of the 
     Interior or the Secretary of Agriculture, as applicable, the 
     total construction costs under subparagraph (A) shall consist 
     only of costs actually incurred through abandonment by the 
     applicant.
       (D) Responsible entities.--
       (i) In general.--An entity that knowingly directed or 
     participated in conduct described in subparagraph (A) may be 
     held jointly and severally liable with the applicant for the 
     applicable civil penalty required under that subparagraph.
       (ii) Establishment.--For the purposes of clause (i), common 
     ownership or affiliation, by itself, shall not establish 
     liability under that clause.
       (E) Enforcement.--
       (i) Referral.--The Secretary of the Interior or the 
     Secretary of Agriculture, as applicable, may refer a matter 
     under this paragraph to the Attorney General to bring in an 
     appropriate district court of the United States a civil 
     action to collect a civil penalty under this paragraph.
       (ii) Civil action.--In a civil action brought under clause 
     (i)--

       (I) the Attorney General shall establish a violation 
     described in subparagraph (A) and the amount of the penalty 
     by a preponderance of the evidence;
       (II) the applicant shall provide, to the extent reasonably 
     available, a statement of total construction costs; and
       (III) if the applicant fails to provide a statement of 
     total construction costs under subclause (II), the court may 
     determine costs from other available and reliable evidence.

       (iii) Limitations period.--An action brought under this 
     subparagraph shall be commenced not later than 3 years after 
     the date a certification is submitted by the applicant under 
     paragraph (2)(A).
       (F) Effect on authorizations.--A civil penalty or finding 
     of misrepresentation arising solely under this paragraph 
     shall not--
       (i) invalidate a previously issued Federal authorization or 
     permit;
       (ii) require the suspension of construction or operation of 
     an eligible solar project;

[[Page S5777]]

       (iii) be treated as fraud, intentional concealment, or 
     material misrepresentation in obtaining a Federal 
     authorization or permit for the purposes of section 
     1401(b)(1)(C); or
       (iv) be treated as fraud or material misrepresentation for 
     the purposes of--

       (I) paragraph (4)(C)(iv) of section 107(g) of the National 
     Environmental Policy Act of 1969 (42 U.S.C. 4336a(g)); or
       (II) section 1403(d)(3).

       (G) Effect on other law.--Subject to subparagraph (F), 
     nothing in this paragraph limits any other applicable Federal 
     law.
       (7) Report.--Not later than 30 days after the end of each 
     quarter beginning after the date of enactment of this Act, 
     the Secretary of the Interior and the Secretary of 
     Agriculture shall each submit to the Committees on Energy and 
     Natural Resources and Agriculture, Nutrition, and Forestry of 
     the Senate, and the Committees on Natural Resources and 
     Agriculture of the House of Representatives a report, that 
     includes--
       (A) the number of eligible solar projects for which 
     priority treatment was requested, granted, and terminated 
     under this subsection;
       (B) the processing times for eligible solar projects that 
     received priority treatment compared to other eligible solar 
     projects that did not receive priority treatment, including 
     compliance with the reduced deadlines required under 
     paragraph (1);
       (C) the results of any verification conducted under 
     paragraph (3); and
       (D) a qualitative assessment of whether priority treatment 
     provided under this subsection is supporting domestic 
     manufacturing, which shall include a recommendation on 
     whether the Secretary of the Interior or the Secretary of 
     Agriculture, as applicable, should continue to provide 
     priority treatment under this subsection.
```
