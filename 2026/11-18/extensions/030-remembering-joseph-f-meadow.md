# REMEMBERING JOSEPH F. MEADOW

**Speaking:** Hoyer, Steny H. (H000874) [D-MD]

```
                      REMEMBERING JOSEPH F. MEADOW

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. STENY H. HOYER

                              of maryland

                    in the house of representatives

                      Wednesday, November 18, 2026

  Mr. HOYER. Mr. Speaker, I rise to remember the life of Joseph F. 
Meadow, who passed away on April 6, 2026. A native Marylander, Joseph 
lived a life of service and contribution to his community and country.
  Joseph grew up as the son of immigrants in Baltimore during the Great 
Depression, shining shoes to help his family of seven. After graduating 
Mount St. Joseph High School, he enlisted in the United States Navy, 
and he served in the Korean War. Later, he attended the University of 
Maryland under the G.I. Bill, where he was active in campus life and 
president of the Delta Tau Delta fraternity.
  After receiving his degree in psychology in 1958, he worked on the 
staff of Texas Senator Ralph Yarborough. At night, he went to law 
school at the University of Maryland. Joseph was on Senator 
Yarborough's staff for a decade, during which time he was involved in 
the passage of the Civil Rights Act and Voting Rights Act. While on 
Capitol Hill, Joseph became president of the Burro Club, an 
organization of Senate Democratic chiefs of staff and legislative 
directors. Later, he went to work for United Action for Animals, 
advocating before Congress against animal cruelty and abuse, and then 
opened his own law practice in College Park, Maryland.
  Joseph was active in local, state, and national politics and was 
involved in many charitable ventures. He was president of his Kiwanis 
Club chapter, a board member of the Salvation Army Prince George's 
Advisory Council and deeply involved in supporting his community 
through the Catholic Church.
  Joseph and his wife Cynthia, who passed away in 2019, were also 
devoted parents to their son Michael--whom I have had the pleasure of 
knowing for many years. Michael worked as a mechanical engineer for the 
Architect of the Capitol, helping to advance Congress's goal of 
reducing its carbon footprint. I was saddened to learn of Joseph's 
passing earlier this year, and I hope the House will join me in 
remembering this public servant and offering condolences to Michael, 
his wife Paige, and their son John.
  May Joseph's legacy of service continue to inspire Marylanders and 
all who serve on Capitol Hill.
```
