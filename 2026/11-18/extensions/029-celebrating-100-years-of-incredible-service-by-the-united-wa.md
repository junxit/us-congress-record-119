# CELEBRATING 100 YEARS OF INCREDIBLE SERVICE BY THE UNITED WAY OF SOUTHEAST LOUISIANA

**Speaking:** Carter, Troy A. (C001125) [D-LA]

```
   CELEBRATING 100 YEARS OF INCREDIBLE SERVICE BY THE UNITED WAY OF 
                          SOUTHEAST LOUISIANA

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. TROY A. CARTER

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                      Wednesday, November 18, 2026

  Mr. CARTER of Louisiana. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize the 
incredible impact the United Way of Southeast Louisiana has had serving 
the constituents of Louisiana's 2nd Congressional District, the 
district I proudly serve as Congressman, and to celebrate the 100 years 
of service that organization has provided Southeast Louisiana.
  The United Way began in 1887 in Denver when a priest, a rabbi, and a 
minister recognized the need for cooperative action to address their 
city's welfare problems, a result of the thousands of families who came 
to Colorado during the Gold Rush.
  The local organization began in 1924, as the Community Chest began in 
New Orleans to meet the needs of a growing city. Raising $902,000 for 
local agencies, it established itself as one of the oldest United Way 
traditions in America. In 1952, New Orleans Community Chest evolved 
into the United Fund and raised $2.5 million. In 1974, the New Orleans 
United Fund joined United Way of America. As a member, the organization 
would benefit from national brand recognition, training, national 
campaign structure and other resources, while maintaining local 
autonomy and retaining 98.5 cents of every dollar raised. This health 
and human service organization was known as the United Way for the 
Greater New Orleans Area.
  In 1994, the Tangipahoa Area United Way merged with the United Way 
for the Greater New Orleans Area to provide funding to agency programs 
serving the residents in Jefferson, Orleans, Plaquemines, St. Bernard, 
St. Tammany, and Tangipahoa Parishes. In 2005, Hurricane Katrina struck 
the Gulf Coast. Despite suffering major damage from extreme flooding at 
its own corporate headquarters and being unable to return to the 
facility for over 5 months, with 90 percent of the homes of its 
employees severely damaged or destroyed, United Way used its fiscal 
reserves to keep all 60 of its Community Impact partner social and 
human service agencies fully funded after the immediate aftermath of 
the hurricanes.

[[Page E1245]]

  In 2009, the Washington Area United Way merged with the United Way 
for the Greater New Orleans Area to join in funding to agency programs 
expanding the service area. In 2010, United Way answered the call 
following the oil spill in the Gulf of Mexico, distributing mini grants 
to local organizations to assist those impacted by the oil spill. In 
2011, United Way for the Greater New Orleans Area changed its name to 
United Way of Southeast Louisiana to reflect the region the 
organization is now serving. In 2015, United Way of Southeast Louisiana 
partnered with United Ways throughout Louisiana on a groundbreaking 
study of financial hardship known as the ALICE Report. In 2016, United 
Way introduced its Blueprint for Prosperity, a road map to eradicating 
poverty in Southeast Louisiana.
  For a century, United Way of Southeast Louisiana has been at the 
forefront of addressing critical issues, creating opportunities, and 
fostering positive change in our community. No other nonprofit 
organization in the area has taken such a broad, objective view of the 
community like United Way of Southeast Louisiana. The Core Values of 
United Way of Southeast Louisiana represent some of the highest 
priorities and deeply held beliefs. Respect for individuals and the 
value that each team member brings to the organization is an inherent 
part of our Core Values, recognizing that having a diverse organization 
allows benefits from a variety of perspectives, strengthening the 
ability to achieve their mission.
  The organization has built lasting partnerships with donors, 
advocates, volunteers, corporate partners, government officials, and 
nonprofits, contributing to the well-being of individuals and families 
in need. United Way's internal initiatives, including financial 
capability programs, Volunteer Income Tax Assistance (VITA), advocacy 
efforts, and disaster response and recovery, have positively impacted 
our community by empowering individuals, addressing systemic issues, 
and providing crucial support during times of crisis.
  United Way of Southeast Louisiana continues to play a vital role in 
building a stronger and more resilient community, exemplifying the 
spirit of compassion, collaboration, and service. It continues to be 
committed to demonstrating respect and appreciation of the differences 
in age, race, gender, nationality, sexual orientation, physical 
ability, and cultural backgrounds within the organization, continuing 
to bring together unique individual backgrounds to collectively and 
more effectively address the issues facing our community. Our community 
understands its impact, raising $13,362,245 in 2022 to 2023 to support 
the collective impact model, generating $190,105,612 in community 
impact during 2023 to 2024.
  Most notably, the Prosperity Center's namesake and longest-serving 
head of Entergy Corporation was a remarkable man I called my friend and 
neighbor, J. Wayne Leonard. The J. Wayne Leonard Prosperity Center, a 
one-stop financial stability center, offers an array of programming to 
the citizens of Greater New Orleans, including financial education and 
coaching, credit building and counseling, benefits screening, and 
income tax assistance. As part of United Way's footprint located at a 
newly acquired site at 2901 Canal Street, the new facility will also 
house United Way's IDA Project, a matched savings account program 
helping low-income individuals purchase long-term assets through a 4:1 
match of their own savings. The program is addressing the challenges of 
intergenerational poverty by providing opportunities for participants 
to purchase a new home or vehicle, start or expand a small business, or 
pay for postsecondary education--all through their IDA accounts.
  These in-house financial stability resources help to empower low-to-
moderate income families to achieve financial stability today and 
prosperity tomorrow, leading to more vibrant communities for us all. In 
its first year of operation, the J. Wayne Leonard Prosperity Center 
served more than 2,000 people referred from the City of New Orleans' 
Network for Economic Opportunity's Workforce Partnership Centers. These 
opportunity centers include Strive New Orleans, JOB1 Business and 
Career Solutions Center, Total Community Action, Urban League of 
Greater New Orleans, and Goodwill Industries. Because of the center's 
successes, United Way has expanded the center's offerings to each 
parish of United Way's Southeast Region.
  I am happy to work alongside the United Way of Southeast Louisiana 
supporting Louisiana's constituents working across the River Parish. I 
congratulate United Way of Southeast Louisiana. I am honored to 
celebrate United Way of Southeast Louisiana's Centennial Anniversary 
serving Louisiana's 2nd Congressional District. May they continue to 
change the lives of thousands in the years to come.
```
