# CELEBRATING THE 90TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE NEW ORLEANS ALUMNAE CHAPTER OF DELTA SIGMA THETA SORORITY, INC.

**Speaking:** Carter, Troy A. (C001125) [D-LA]

```
CELEBRATING THE 90TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE NEW ORLEANS ALUMNAE CHAPTER OF 
                    DELTA SIGMA THETA SORORITY, INC.

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. TROY A. CARTER

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                      Wednesday, November 18, 2026

  Mr. CARTER of Louisiana. Mr. Speaker, today, I rise with great pride 
to recognize my Beloved Sisters as they celebrate 90 years of the New 
Orleans Alumnae Chapter of Delta Sigma Theta Sorority, Incorporated. 
The organization was chartered in 1936, formally establishing the Alpha 
Zeta Sigma Chapter of Delta Sigma Theta Sorority, Incorporated, a 
legacy of service and sisterhood.
  The chapter was established by the following Charter Members whose 
professional leadership and commitment to the sorority laid the 
foundation for Delta Sigma Theta's alumni presence in the Gulf South: 
Emma Joyce Adams Bailey, Julia Washington Bond, Melva Dier Borris, 
Sadie N. Boyce, Elizabeth Stubbs Davis, Jessie Covington Dent, Irene 
Colbert Edmonds, Oralee Baranco Mitchell, Sylvia A. Labat Randall, 
Estelle V. Baranco Turpin, and Naomi E. Evans Washington. These women 
were committed to the sorority's guiding principle of achievement in 
every field of human endeavor. Their request was granted on June 20, 
1936.
  In November 1936 the chapter was renamed Alpha Eta Sigma Chapter at 
the request of some of the younger members. As the sorority's naming 
evolved, the chapter became known as the New Orleans Alumnae Chapter--a 
name now associated with excellence, leadership, and meaningful 
community impact. The membership has grown from its unpretentious and 
dynamic eleven to over 700 educated and socially responsible members.
  As the chapter marks 90 years of sisterhood, scholarship, service, 
and social action, it stands as a tribute to the vision, courage, and 
perseverance of its eleven Founders. Throughout its history, the New 
Orleans Alumnae (NOA) Chapter has earned an outstanding reputation for 
its stellar programming and transformative community imprint through 
generation-friendly, creative, and award-winning programs implemented 
through Delta Sigma Theta's Five-Point Programmatic Thrust: Economic 
Development, Educational Development, International Awareness & 
Involvement, Physical and Mental Health and Political Awareness & 
Involvement.
  During the administration of Past President Evangeline Jones (1965 to 
1967), the New Orleans Alumnae Chapter hosted a special Christmas party 
honoring the four courageous six-year-old little girls who desegregated 
New Orleans public schools, and eighty other first graders. The girls, 
famously known as the ``New Orleans Four'', and attendees listened to a 
taped message from Delta Sigma Theta Sorority Incorporated's 12th 
National President, Dr. Jeanne L. Noble, and a recording from Delta 
Sigma Theta Sorority, Incorporated's Founder, Osceola Macarthy Adams. 
At a time of social unrest and resistance to integration, this 
heartfelt event brought joy, encouragement, and dignity to these 
trailblazing children and their families; reflecting the chapter's 
commitment to social action, justice, and community uplift.
  The renowned Youth Initiatives, ``formerly known as Delta Teen 
Lift'', are hallmarks of the Sorority's National Educational 
Development Programmatic Thrust. These impactful programs include the 
Dr. Betty Shabazz Delta Academy for girls ages 11-14, and the Dr. 
Jeanne L. Noble Delta G.E.M.S. (Growing and Empowering Myself 
Successfully) Institute for young ladies aged 14 to 18, both designed 
to promote academic excellence, foster interest in science, technology, 
engineering, the arts, and math disciplines, and inspire participants 
to realize and achieve their fullest potential. The E.M.B.O.D.I. 
(Empowering Mates to Build Opportunities for Developing Independence) 
program, conceptualized by my beloved mentor, Cynthia Butler-McIntyre, 
Delta Sigma Theta Sorority's 24th National President, addresses the 
educational, social, and emotional challenges facing African American 
males. A youth favorite and signature component is the celebrated Black 
College Tour, which empowers, educates, and elevates the aspirations of 
countless young people throughout the community by exposing them to 
higher education opportunities and encouraging the pursuit of academic 
and personal excellence. Additionally, since its inaugural presentation 
in 1964, NOA's annual Cotillion Presentation Ball has remained a 
treasured tradition of the chapter and the community, promoting 
scholarship, leadership, poise, and service among generations of young 
women.
  Aligning with the Sorority's Political Awareness and Involvement 
Thrust, through intense advocacy efforts spanning decades, the 
chapter's participation in Delta Days at the Nation's Capital and 
Louisiana Red & White Days in Baton Rouge has inspired generations of 
Sorors to exercise the power of their voices through voter engagement, 
advocacy, social and political action.
  Since its inception, NOA has cultivated strong partnerships 
throughout Orleans, Jefferson, and St. Bernard Parishes supporting and 
advancing the Sorority's Mental and Physical Health Programmatic 
Thrust. The chapter has maintained a distinguished record of public 
service, awarded millions of dollars in scholarships to high school 
seniors, and worked alongside a broad network of civic and community 
organizations, including but not limited to: United Negro College Fund, 
St. Jude Children's Research Hospital, March of Dimes,

National Council of Negro Women-New Orleans Section, Edna B. & Joyce 
Fay Washington Breast Cancer Foundation, New Orleans Delta Foundation, 
Split Second Foundation, Alzheimer's Association, Crescent Care, Second 
Harvest Food Bank, American Red Cross, National Association for the 
Advancement of Colored People, American Cancer Society, Metropolitan 
Human Service District, Total Community Action, Thurgood Marshall 
College Fund, State Farm, Girl Scouts of Louisiana, Urban League of 
Louisiana, and countless other local and national affiliations and 
associations. It has sought to heighten the public's awareness 
regarding political and social issues that affect our community.
  The New Orleans Alumnae Chapter is widely recognized as a leader 
within Delta Sigma Theta Sorority, Inc. Its members have served in 
national, regional, and state leadership roles, reflecting the 
chapter's long-standing commitment to excellence and influence across 
every level of the organization. New Orleans Alumnae is the home of the 
first National President from the Southwest Region, Cynthia Butler-
McIntyre, 24th National President; she also wears the title of past 
Southwest Regional Director. Additionally, the chapter is honored to be 
the home of the late Oralee Baranco Mitchell who served as National 
First Vice President during the late Dorothy Height's administration; 
the late Mattie Clarkson Braden, served as the Grand Second Vice 
President (currently known as National Second Vice President); Gloria 
Bryant-Banks, past National Secretary, past Southwest Regional Director 
and former National Ritual & Ceremonies Commissioner; Carolyn E. Lewis, 
past National Treasurer; Pamela M. Rogers past Southwest Regional 
Director; Michelle Easton past Regional Representative and the late 
Aromenta Evans, who was elected to serve on the National Scholarship 
and Standards Committee; Cheryl A. Gray Evans, National Social Action 
Commissioner; Diana Bajoie, Ellenese Brooks-Simms, and Renee G. Pratt, 
past National Social Action Commissioners; Sharon Barnett-Starks, past 
Louisiana State Coordinator; Rochelle Jones Moses, Louisiana State 
Chaplain and numerous chapter members appointed to National, Regional 
and State committees.
  Grounded in sisterhood, scholarship, service, and social action, the 
chapter continues to honor its history while building on its legacy. It 
remains committed to leading with purpose, serving with excellence, and 
making a lasting impact locally, nationally, and globally. For nine 
decades, the New Orleans Alumnae Chapter has remained resilient through 
extraordinary challenges, including severe weather events and the 
coronavirus pandemic. Throughout it all, its members have stayed 
engaged and steadfast, continuing the chapter's tradition of public 
service in the greater New Orleans community with integrity, strength, 
and purpose.
  Today, I am humbled to support and call the members of the New 
Orleans Alumnae Chapter of Delta Sigma Theta Sorority, Incorporated, my 
sisters, as they remain one of the most historic and influential 
chapters within the National Organization. For 90 years, this chapter 
has upheld the sorority's commitment to achievement, sisterhood, and 
service. Through its continued work in mentorship, civic engagement, 
and leadership development, the chapter remains dedicated to advancing 
the mission of Delta Sigma Theta, Incorporated, while strengthening the 
communities it serves throughout the Greater New Orleans area.
```
