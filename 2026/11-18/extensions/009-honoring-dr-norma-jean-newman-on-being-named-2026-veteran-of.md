# HONORING DR. NORMA JEAN NEWMAN ON BEING NAMED 2026 VETERAN OF THE YEAR FOR VIRGINIA'S 4TH CONGRESSIONAL DISTRICT

**Speaking:** McClellan, Jennifer L. (M001227) [D-VA]

```
HONORING DR. NORMA JEAN NEWMAN ON BEING NAMED 2026 VETERAN OF THE YEAR 
               FOR VIRGINIA'S 4TH CONGRESSIONAL DISTRICT

                                 ______
                                 

                       HON. JENNIFER L. McCLELLAN

                              of virginia

                    in the house of representatives

                      Wednesday, November 18, 2026

  Ms. McCLELLAN. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to celebrate the 2026 
Veteran of the Year for Virginia's 4th District, Dr. Norma Jean Newman 
of North Dinwiddie, Virginia. Norma Jean is a distinguished Army 
veteran, tireless advocate, and dedicated public servant. Through 
nearly a quarter-century of honorable military service and decades of 
unwavering commitment to fellow veterans, Norma Jean has devoted her 
life to serving others and ensuring that those who served our military 
receive the care, benefits, and dignity they have earned.
  Dr. Newman served in the United States Army from 1984 to 2008, 
retiring with the rank of Sergeant First Class after 24 years of 
honorable service. A Gulf War veteran, she deployed in support of 
Operations Desert Shield and Desert Storm and was injured while 
conducting air-quality testing related to chemical exposure. During her 
military career, she earned numerous awards and decorations, including 
the Army Meritorious Service Medal, Army Commendation Medal, Army 
Achievement Medals, National Defense Service Medal and additional 
awards recognizing her exemplary leadership and service to our Nation.
  While her military accomplishments are impressive, Dr. Newman's 
impact did not end when she took off the uniform. Instead, she began a 
new mission serving veterans in communities across Virginia and beyond. 
Drawing upon her expertise in Veterans Affairs disability claims 
documentation and medical board proceedings, she has dedicated herself 
to helping veterans navigate complex benefits systems and access the 
support they earned through their military service.
  For nearly three decades, Dr. Newman has trained veteran service 
officers and provided hands-on assistance to thousands of veterans at 
no cost. She strongly believes that no veteran should have to pay to 
receive the benefits they've earned. Through her work with The American 
Legion, Disabled American Veterans, AMVETS and numerous other 
organizations, as well as her recent completion of the National 
Veterans Legal Services Program in Washington, D.C., she has become a 
highly respected and effective advocate for veterans in the 
Commonwealth. Her expertise and mentorship have benefited veterans and 
service officers not only across Virginia but throughout the region.
  Dr. Newman's commitment to service extends beyond veterans advocacy. 
She is an active member of the National Buffalo Soldiers Association's 
Mark Matthews Chapter in Petersburg, serves as Chair of Trustees at 
First Baptist Church West Petersburg and is widely respected throughout 
her community for her faith, leadership and generosity. Whether helping 
a veteran secure benefits, find housing for a family in crisis or 
simply keeping her word to someone in need, she has built a reputation 
for showing up when others cannot. Her actions reflect a lifelong 
belief that service is measured not by recognition, but by the lives we 
improve along the way.
  Dr. Newman has also served on both the Washington, D.C., and Roanoke 
Veterans Appeals Boards, where she fought tirelessly on behalf of 
veterans seeking fair consideration of their VA benefits claims. As 
Women's Coordinator for Military Sexual Trauma and Post-Traumatic 
Stress Disorder programs, she has provided critical support and 
advocacy for veterans confronting some of the most difficult challenges 
associated with military service. Those who know Dr. Newman 
consistently describe her as compassionate, dependable, and deeply 
committed to the veterans she serves. Despite her extraordinary impact, 
she remains humble and focused not on recognition, but on results. Her 
advocacy has helped countless veterans secure benefits, find stability, 
overcome adversity, and improve their quality of life.
  Mr. Speaker, Dr. Norma Jean Newman's life exemplifies the highest 
ideals of military service, citizenship and community leadership. For 
her, service did not end when she retired from the Army. It continues 
every day through her tireless advocacy, her faith, and her unwavering 
commitment to the veterans and families she serves. Her life's work 
reminds us that honoring our veterans means more than thanking them for 
their service. It means ensuring they never have to face life's 
challenges alone.
  I ask my colleagues to join me in congratulating Dr. Norma Jean 
Newman on being named the 2026 Veteran of the Year for Virginia's 4th 
District and in thanking her for her extraordinary service to our 
Nation and to the countless veterans whose lives she has changed 
through her compassion, dedication and selfless leadership.
```
