# CELEBRATING GEORGE "BITTY" BENN ON HIS 65TH BIRTHDAY

**Speaking:** Carter, Troy A. (C001125) [D-LA]

```
         CELEBRATING GEORGE ``BITTY'' BENN ON HIS 65TH BIRTHDAY

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. TROY A. CARTER

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                      Wednesday, November 18, 2026

  Mr. CARTER of Louisiana. Mr. Speaker, I rise today with great pride 
to recognize the 65th birthday of a very dear friend and, I dare say 
affectionately, a member of Cut Off royalty, Mr. George ``Bitty'' Benn.
  Bitty Benn is the proud generational leader and proprietor of 
historic Benn's Grocery, a treasured institution in the Cut Off 
community of Lower Algiers. He is a devoted husband to Deon, a loving 
father to Nia, Desmond, and Devon, a respected community leader, and to 
me, a lifelong family friend.
  To know Bitty is to know a man who carries history with humility, 
strength, and grace. He represents the continuation of a family legacy 
that reaches back nearly 150 years and stands as a living reminder of 
the pride, independence, and endurance of our beloved Cut Off 
community.
  Local oral history holds that Benn's Grocery was established by the 
Benn family in the years following emancipation. Many accounts point to 
Isaac Benn, Sr. as the patriarch connected to the founding of the store 
and the first generation of the family to operate the neighborhood 
grocery.
  After the passing of the founder, the store remained in the family 
and passed to the second generation, when Isaac Benn, Jr. continued the 
work his father had begun, maintaining the store as a vital gathering 
place for residents of the community.
  The legacy was carried forward into the third generation by Kermit 
``Cun-Nay'' Benn, who operated the store for decades and became one of 
the most recognizable and beloved figures in the Cut Off. Kermit Benn 
was known not only as a businessman, but also as a lifelong resident of 
Algiers, a gospel singer, and a generous supporter of churches and 
schools throughout the community. His life reflected a deep commitment 
to faith, service, and neighborhood pride. He passed away in 2013 at 
the age of 77.
  Today, that proud tradition continues with the fourth generation, as 
George ``Bitty'' Benn carries forward the family's historic connection 
to Benn's Grocery and to the community that has supported it for well 
over a century.
  For generations, residents have recalled a marker associated with the 
store noting that it was ``paid in full since 1887.'' If that date is 
correct, Benn's Grocery has been a part of the Cut Off story for well 
over 135 years, making it one of the oldest continuously recognized 
neighborhood businesses in Lower Algiers. That matters.
  It matters because Benn's Grocery represents something deeply 
important in the history of our people and our place. It reflects the 
story of a Black family building something lasting in the 
Reconstruction era. It reflects the story of a self-sustaining Black 
community where people owned homes, built churches, supported local 
businesses, and created a life rooted in dignity and self-
determination.
  The Cut Off itself is rich with that history. In the years after the 
Civil War, it became one of the earliest self-sustaining Black 
communities in New Orleans. Institutions such as Second Baptist Church, 
founded in 1868, stand as enduring reminders of the faith and resolve 
that shaped the neighborhood. Benn's Grocery stands firmly in that same 
tradition. Today, Bitty Benn carries that legacy forward.
  He does so not with fanfare, but with steadiness, authenticity, and 
deep love for community. He is part of the fabric of the Cut Off. He is 
one of those people whose name and presence mean something to 
generations of families because he is tied not only to a place, but to 
a legacy of service, pride, and belonging.
  For me, this recognition is personal. Bitty has been a lifelong 
friend and family friend, and his story is woven into the story of the 
community that helped shape me. So today, as we celebrate his 65th 
birthday, we also celebrate a life of meaning, a family legacy of 
consequence, and a business that remains part of the living history of 
Lower Algiers.
  On behalf of Louisiana's 2nd Congressional District, and with deep 
personal pride as a son of the Cut Off, I ask my colleagues to join me 
in honoring George ``Bitty'' Benn on this milestone birthday.
  May God continue to bless him, his family, Benn's Grocery, and the 
beloved Cut Off community for generations to come.
```
