                         Honoring Vincent Hall

  Mr. BEAN of Florida. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life and 
legacy of Principal Vincent Hall, the beloved principal of William 
Raines High School in Jacksonville, Florida.
  A proud Raines graduate, Mr. Hall returned to lead the school that 
helped to shape his own life. He devoted nearly three decades to Duval 
County Public Schools, investing in young people and helping them see 
the possibilities ahead.
  Under his leadership, Raines earned its first-ever B grade in 2024, 
and he subsequently was named the district's 2025 Principal of the 
Year.
  His greatest legacy lives in the students he encouraged, the 
educators he supported, and the community he brought together.
  Mr. Hall believed every student deserved the opportunity to succeed. 
He honored the proud tradition of Raines while preparing a new 
generation of Vikings to build their own futures.
  Abby and I extend our heartfelt condolences to his family and the 
entire Raines community. We know it hurts.
  May God comfort them, and may his example continue to inspire the 
students whose lives he shaped.
  Principal Hall's legacy will live on.

                 Gratitude has a Place in Every Season

  Mr. BEAN of Florida. Mr. Speaker, as Thanksgiving approaches, we 
remember a tradition rooted in gratitude. In 1789, then-President 
George Washington proclaimed a national day of thanksgiving. During the 
Civil War, President Abraham Lincoln called upon Americans to give 
thanks even amid tremendous hardship. Their example reminds us that 
gratitude has a place in every season of our Nation's life.
  This year, Abby and I have an especially joyful reason to give 
thanks, a new grandbaby. I know what you are thinking, Mr. Speaker, I 
am too young to be a grandpa, but, yes, we are blessed. Holding a new 
life reminds us of God's goodness, the blessing of family, and our 
responsibility to leave the next generation a better place than we 
inherited from our parents.
  We are thankful for the men and women who defend our freedom, the 
neighbors who care for one another, and the opportunity to serve the 
people of northeast Florida in these very Halls of Congress.
  May we make room at our table for someone who might otherwise be 
alone, and let's remember those who are facing an empty chair.
  From my family to yours, Happy Thanksgiving, and may God continue to 
bless America.

                        Honoring Melania Escobar

  Mr. CARTER of Georgia. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize an 
extraordinary young girl, Melania Escobar, who is showing the world 
that a diagnosis does not have to define your future.

[[Page H6276]]

  Diagnosed with type 1 diabetes at just 4 years old, Melania has spent 
the last 10 years overcoming challenges most children never have to 
think about, but she has never let her diagnosis slow her down. A 
straight-A student, Melania has been a ballerina since the age of 2, 
practicing more than 10 hours a week with dreams of becoming a 
professional dancer.
  Melania is Dexcom's first and only child-sponsored representative, 
appearing in commercials in both English and Spanish alongside a roster 
of professional athletes and chefs.
  Recently, Melania and her family joined Dexcom at the NASDAQ closing 
bell ceremony in recognition of National Diabetes Awareness Month, 
where she proudly represented the type 1 diabetes community.
  Mr. Speaker, Melania's story is a powerful reminder of why medical 
innovation matters. Behind every breakthrough is a child who gets to 
spend more time simply being a kid, chasing dreams without being 
defined by a diagnosis.
  I congratulate Melania on this tremendous honor and thank her for 
inspiring others living with type 1 diabetes.

        Honoring Joe Higgins, Chatham County Veteran of the Year

  Mr. CARTER of Georgia. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize my good

continued service.


                        Recognizing Mike Pharis

  Mr. CARTER of Georgia. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to congratulate Mike 
Pharis of Brunswick, Georgia, on receiving the Federal Aviation 
Administration's prestigious Wright Brothers Master Pilot Award for 
more than 50 years of safe flying.
  Mike's love for aviation began as a young boy when he would sit in a 
cardboard box with homemade wings and toilet plunger for a control 
stick, dreaming of taking to the skies.
  As he grew older, Mike pumped gas to save money for flying lessons, 
eventually taking his first solo flight out of Brunswick Air Park in 
1970. After serving in the United States Army, Mike turned his 
childhood passion into a lifelong career in aviation and now joins the 
prestigious group of roughly 10,000 pilots to receive this honor.
  Mike's story is a testament to what dedication, hard work, and a 
lifelong passion can accomplish. I congratulate Mike on 50 years of 
taking to the skies.
