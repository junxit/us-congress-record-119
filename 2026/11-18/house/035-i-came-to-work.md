# I CAME TO WORK

**Speaking:** Blair, Everton (B001328) [D-GA]

```
                              {time}  1710
                             I CAME TO WORK

  (Under the Speaker's announced policy of January 3, 2025, Mr. Blair 
of Georgia was recognized for 30 minutes.)


                             General Leave

  Mr. BLAIR. Mr. Speaker, I ask unanimous consent that all Members may 
have 5 legislative days in which to revise and extend their remarks and 
include extraneous material on the subject of my Special Order today.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Is there objection to the request of the 
gentleman from Georgia?
  There was no objection.
  Mr. BLAIR. Mr. Speaker, I have been a Member of this body for 78 
days, and I may be the only Member who can count his or her whole 
congressional career on a calendar page or two.
  Earlier today, my Democratic colleagues made history by naming Hakeem 
Jeffries Speaker-designate. He is likely to be confirmed and to become 
the Speaker this January and will make history as the first Black 
Speaker of the House.
  That was not without some fanfare that we saw online and in phone 
calls to our office. In some sort of dissonance, I found the morning to 
be a little disconnected from what I had been experiencing online and 
over the phones, but there were a small contingent of us that did 
dissent. I just want to draw a little bit of attention to that because 
there is power in our unity here.
  The honorable  Jim Clyburn this morning actually reminded us that 
unity does not require unanimity, and I found that to be incredibly 
compelling, especially in a party that is as broad a tent as ours.
  I encourage us to really reflect on how we are going to bring people 
into the fold, how we are going to expand our coalition and tap into 
that. I think a lot of our new Members are looking for bravery and 
boldness. People sent us here with a mandate that the systems as they 
are currently designed are not working for us. What are we going to do 
to change them?

[[Page H6287]]

  Tapping into that pocket of our community, that growing coalition 
across many of our constituencies, will enable us to be stronger, to be 
better, and to approach things not in an adversarial or contentious way 
but in a stronger, more unified, more balanced approach. I am very 
eager to see how that portends for the 120th Congress, and I am excited 
for it.
  But a historic gavel is not the same as a historic Congress. Before 
we congratulate our soon-to-be-leaders, before they take the helm, 
let's talk about everything that the current leaders failed to do.
  I was sworn in on September 1, and the people of Georgia's 13th, many 
of whom voted in four consecutive elections, month after month, from 
May to August, over three of which I was on the ballot, they did not 
send me here as an honorific. They sent me here to do work. They sent 
me here to change things for them for the better. They sent me here to 
add fresh perspectives and new ideas to the work that has been done for 
decades, centuries, in this country that might need a fresh set of 
eyes.
  Within days, this House went home. It didn't adjourn after the work 
was finished. It just canceled the work. Honestly, it was a slap in the 
face to so many hardworking Americans who are doing everything right 
and struggling to get by.
  Just imagine leading an organization or an institution, and you do 
everything in your power to cut the lines, to skirt the issues, to skip 
asking the hard questions, and you just send everybody home. How could 
that organization be successful? That is not why people send us here.
  The leadership canceled the last 2 weeks of September and sent 
Members home for a 7-week campaign break after we had just had an 
August recess. The House was in session for less than 2 weeks, and I 
was immediately sent home, frankly, for an election that I was not even 
on the ballot for.
  So what did I do? I knew I couldn't sit on these laurels and do 
nothing. We got to work. We expanded our constituent services in 
Georgia's 13th Congressional District. We opened satellite offices. I 
held numerous townhalls. I showed up at every townhall that every 
elected official had in the district, or I had someone from my team 
there. I availed myself virtually for office hours. We hosted meet and 
greets. I sat down with my constituents to hear their take on the 
issues like artificial intelligence, worries about data centers, 
cracking down on the predatory practices of housing in their 
communities, talking about the cost of living, veteran affairs, and so 
many important issues that we need a Representative who was willing to 
pay that time and listen to them.
  Unfortunately, at every turn, I had to basically tell them this 
Congress probably isn't going to do much of it, but I am going to try 
to do something anyway.
  So in the absence of people who were willing to do the work, in a 
very negligent and malfeasant Congress, we just chose to do good work 
anyway, by introducing pieces of legislation, even if we knew that they 
weren't likely to go to a committee vote or to go to Rules or 
suspension, and by making sure that people at least heard the voice of 
the issues that they had not yet heard reflected from a powerful body 
and from the dais like this. That is what people sent us here to do.
  During our telephone townhall, we had the greatest turnout in the 
history of any townhall in Georgia's 13th. Over 15,000 people stayed on 
the phone to ask me questions, to engage with me on the issues. I was 
honored to be able to do that.
  In our meet and greets, we had dozens, hundreds of folks at many of 
those. Just this past weekend, we held passport day and had hundreds of 
people come in for expedited services in renewing or acquiring their 
first passport, things that I am honored to do, because we know these 
systems are really, really hard, and people just don't have the time to 
figure it out on their own.
  Then, for these midterm elections, recognizing that I wasn't on the 
ballot, I was not subject to the same blackout period rules that many 
of our frontliners or colleagues who were seeking reelection were 
subject to. I sought to make sure that everyone in the community had 
every piece of information so that they could exercise their civic 
duty.

  I used the resources, the talent, and the capacity that we had in our 
office to reach out, using innovative means like texting, digital ads 
and every available mechanism to make sure everyone knew that there was 
an election coming up and that they had early voting options in their 
county where they could go to any of the available locations over a 3-
week period. The results spoke for themselves. That is the power of 
just working hard and doing things differently.
  Out of all 14 congressional districts in Georgia, Georgia's 13th 
ranked first in young voter turnout, first in Black voter turnout, 
first in first-time midterm voter participation, and third in number of 
votes cast overall. These are significant records, never before seen in 
the history of Georgia's 13th Congressional District, just because I 
wanted to do the work.
  Imagine what we could be doing if every single one of us had that 
type of temerity and sought to do everything in their capacity to take 
every available opportunity to deliver for the people who send them 
here.
  There is a sentiment here that Republicans have taken on, somewhat of 
a retrospective. It feels like a reflection on ways that they abdicated 
their responsibility, an acknowledgment that the cost of living was top 
of mind for folks and that they should have been doing something about 
it. Don't let that fool you. They could be doing something about it 
right now. Their insistence not to is all the more reason why I am 
excited for what the 120th Congress can do when Democrats get these 
gavels.
  Congress is broken, and people back home see it. But from a kitchen 
table in Georgia's 13th, an empty House Chamber doesn't look like a 
scheduling decision. It frankly looks like nobody cares. That is why so 
many people have lost faith in this institution. So many people don't 
see their lives changing with the pendulum swings of who has power. Let 
us recognize that and let us meet the moment in the 120th Congress. It 
is up to us now.
  I say to the American people: Don't let it discourage you. If you are 
not happy about what is happening, use your voice and use your advocacy 
to continue to do something about it.
  It will take bravery to fix this place. As somebody who has only been 
here for a short period of time, I am starting to see all of the ways 
in which many of the rules must change if we are going to be best 
served.
  This election, this wave election, wasn't necessarily an endorsement. 
It was resistance. It was a refusal to accept what is. As a consequence 
of that, we have to treat it as a mandate for what can be.

  I am excited because we have the opportunity to hold and stay true to 
everything we believe while delivering.
  There is a constant tension that I am hearing among community 
members: Are you going to prioritize affordability or accountability? 
How can we chew gum and walk at the same time? How can we deliver both?
  The reality is I know far too well that the snippy sound bites are 
going to come from the Oversight Committee, from a judiciary hearing, 
from anything that really requires us to do the accountability thing. 
Let's prioritize affordability. Let's prioritize the hard work that has 
gone unaddressed in this Chamber for too long because we keep sending 
ourselves home.
  There is a lot of work to do immediately here: increasing the minimum 
wage; reversing all of the cuts that the Trump administration underwent 
at the top of last year. It is not enough just to go back to the day 
before this administration took office because those systems weren't 
working for us either.
  We have got to be bold and brave in extending our capacity to make 
sure that the American condition is improved; to root out corruption 
from this body; to end gerrymandering once and for all; to get the 
money out of our politics and to mean it when we say that we are going 
to do something about it; finally, expanding the middle class by making 
sure that everybody has access to purchase a home if they choose to do 
so.

[[Page H6288]]

  Fighting for universal single-payer healthcare because in the richest 
country in the history of the world it makes no sense why people are 
picking between going to see a doctor and paying a car note or a 
mortgage.
  We earn the support of the people who send us here every other year, 
and it is up to us now to make sure that we achieve what they have 
invested and entrusted within us. Nobody shows up to vote just for a 
rewind. They want to see progress. They want things to be better. Let's 
make sure we deliver on that.
  The next Congress must simply do better. We have got to show up. 
Everybody has to show up. It is the bare minimum. Just imagine getting 
a new job and missing days that are already on the schedule that you 
are supposed to be here. You couldn't get away with that in too many 
different instances without getting fired. Here, you can basically do 
it for 2 years, and the chance to be replaced doesn't occur until the 
next election. We have got to do better than that, especially when 
deadlines are pending.
  This next vote on the budget, we only have a few weeks to pass. I 
commend my colleagues who have been really, really vocal on making sure 
that we have, at a minimum, a CR that doesn't shut the government down. 
We should also be fighting for the things we want to see in the next 
budget right now because we have the power to do it.
  If Congress is going to send us home, expose them. Expose the 
Republicans who refuse to take up the hard questions and do the hard 
work, so that we can retain power. When we have the gavel, we will show 
them what good leadership looks like.
  I have been here for just a few short weeks. Nobody should hand me a 
gavel. I understand why experience matters, maybe better than anyone in 
the room right now. I honor seniority. For communities that were shut 
out of power for generations, it means so much to have finally gotten 
to the place where they can lead in the most powerful Chambers in the 
richest country in the history of the world, and we do not take that 
lightly.
  It is also time for us to contend with how we balance that with 
innovation and inclusion, how we ensure that there is a generation of 
fresh ideas that continue to have their voices and their needs 
reflected and seats at the table. If we are going to keep relics of an 
old system that was never designed for us to achieve and ascend, we 
have to figure out ways to change and adapt those systems so we can do 
better at creating a face that is representative of the multitude of 
coalitions that we represent in this country.
  That is my charge to the next Congress, to our leadership, to those 
who, frankly, might have stayed too long because their predecessor 
stayed too long and they are just waiting their turn because they have 
been trying to lead a really important issue to them.
  At the end of the day, it is not about us. It is not about us in 
these fancy suits and these fancy chairs. It is about the people who 
need their voices better reflected. The longer we stay here, frankly, 
the more we get out of touch from the conditions of the American 
people.
  Let's make sure we are doing everything we can to maintain that 
connection and that we don't lose sight of what really matters: 
fighting for the people that send us here.
  We have this question of seniority, right? What do we really make of 
it? How can we ensure that there are some healthy term limits or some 
shifting of the status quo, movement of the condition, mentorship, 
pipelines that we can build from? It shouldn't just be that every time 
we have a wave election, we have this new class of freshmen. We could 
have new freshmen all the time, increasing our fold, increasing the new 
ideas.
  Who writes the rules for artificial intelligence or for the economy 
of 2040 if those very people who will be here are not at the table 
right now? That is on us. I don't have the answer to it, but I can tell 
you that we need to do some things differently if we are going to 
collectively have that answer.
  To the incoming freshmen, I won't get to serve with many of you, at 
least not now, but I have had a pretty unique perspective as one of the 
youngest people here, as the literal newest Member here, and as 
somebody who won't be returning in the 120th Congress. I think about 
how warm my reception was, from Democrats and Republicans, frankly, but 
I also think about the ways that these systems and these places and 
this institution at large is not really designed to acknowledge or 
embrace the talent that you possess.
  I charge you, when you feel that way, when you feel excluded or 
overlooked, name it. Do something any way. I speak up mostly because I 
ain't got nothing to lose, and my community didn't send me here to just 
collect a check and sit on the sidelines. I go above and beyond to make 
sure that my voice is heard. If there is not time in the agenda in the 
big room, I will find the person after the meeting to just share some 
ideas and ask questions. I encourage all of you to do that as well.

  This place kind of feels like high school at times. I was pretty good 
at high school. I also taught high school. You look into certain spaces 
where we convene as a body, and it kind of feels like a pep rally in 
the high school commons or in the gymnasium: cliques forming, a lot of 
drama that seems manufactured, some gossip around who likes who, who is 
going to get what title, who is going to vote for whom. At the end of 
the day, what really matters is what we achieve for the people that 
send us here.
  You will have more time in this building than I did, so don't waste a 
day of it. Do as much as you can. Your community will reward you for 
it. Go there, push the bounds, and get into good trouble. Having a 
constructive critique is probably what needs to be said or heard, and 
you can do so while still being a team player, still showing up to 
broaden our coalition, and still doing it in a way that honors the 
integrity of the institution and the integrity of your character.
  I believe in all of you in the 120th Congress. I am excited for what 
this shift can really bring to a body that needs some change.
  As long as your North Star stays focused on the people who sent you 
here, you will be fine. I came to work; this House didn't. Next 
Congress, let's all come to work.
  Congress is broken, but fixing it is possible. It just requires 
bravery. Don't let that discourage you. Let it recruit you.
  Mr. Speaker, I yield back the balance of my time.
```
