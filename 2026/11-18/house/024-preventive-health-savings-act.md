  Mr. ARRINGTON. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and pass the 
bill (H.R. 4464) to amend the Congressional Budget Act of 1974 
respecting the scoring of preventive health savings.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 4464

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Preventive Health Savings 
     Act''.

     SEC. 2. SCORING OF PREVENTIVE HEALTH SAVINGS.

       Section 202 of the Congressional Budget and Impoundment 
     Control Act of 1974 (2 U.S.C. 602) is amended by adding at 
     the end the following:
       ``(h) Scoring of Preventive Health Savings.--
       ``(1) Determination by the director.--Upon a request by the 
     chairman and ranking minority member of the Committee on the 
     Budget of the Senate and chairman and ranking minority member 
     of the committee of primary jurisdiction of the Senate or by 
     the chairman and ranking minority member of the Committee on 
     the Budget of the House of Representatives and the chairman 
     and ranking minority member of the committee of primary 
     jurisdiction of the House of Representatives, the Director 
     shall determine if proposed legislation would result in net 
     reductions in budget outlays in budgetary outyears through 
     the use of preventive health care.
       ``(2) Projections.--If the Director determines that 
     proposed legislation would result in net reductions in budget 
     outlays as described in paragraph (1), the Director--
       ``(A) shall include, in any projection prepared by the 
     Director on such proposed legislation, a description and 
     estimate of the reductions in budget outlays in the budgetary 
     outyears and a description of the basis for such conclusions; 
     and
       ``(B) may prepare a budget projection that includes some or 
     all of the budgetary outyears, notwithstanding the time 
     periods for projections described in subsection (e) and 
     sections 308, 402, and 424.

[[Page H6267]]

       ``(3) Limitation.--Any estimate provided by the Director 
     pursuant to paragraph (1) shall be used as a supplementary 
     estimate and may not be used to determine compliance with the 
     Congressional Budget Act of 1974 or any other budgetary 
     enforcement controls.
       ``(4) Definitions.--As used in this subsection--
       ``(A) the term `budgetary outyears' means the 2 consecutive 
     10-year periods beginning with the first fiscal year that is 
     10 years after the current fiscal year; and
       ``(B) the term `preventive health care' means an action 
     that focuses on the health of the public, individuals, and 
     defined populations in order to protect, promote, and 
     maintain health and wellness and prevent disease, disability, 
     and premature death, including through the promotion and use 
     of effective, innovative health care interventions that are 
     demonstrated by credible and publicly available evidence from 
     epidemiological projection models, clinical trials, 
     observational studies in humans, longitudinal studies, and 
     meta-analysis.''.

and Scott Peters, for joining me on this bill. I also would like to

  Mr. Speaker, healthcare costs are continuing to rise increasing 
financial burdens on American families and threatening the long-term 
sustainability of our Nation's Federal healthcare programs. Congress 
should be looking for every opportunity to address both of these 
challenges by keeping Americans healthier and thereby reducing 
unnecessary spending.
  Preventive healthcare has the potential to do exactly that. It is a 
long-term investment in a healthier America, stronger communities, and 
a more sustainable healthcare system. By identifying and treating 
diseases earlier, we can help Americans avoid serious health 
complications, costly hospitalizations, and expensive medical 
treatments later in life.
  This is especially important in rural communities like the ones that 
I represent where access to healthcare is often limited and many 
patients have to travel hours to receive necessary medical treatment. 
That is why early detection and intervention can be of the most help to 
these patients to avoid more serious health complications and avoid 
costly specialized treatment far from home.
  However, unfortunately, under current law, the Congressional Budget 
Office evaluates the fiscal impact of health-related legislation within 
a 10-year budget window. While this provides Congress with important 
information about near-term costs, it often fails to capture potential 
savings for preventive healthcare that might not materialize until much 
later.
  This legislation addresses this limitation by allowing the 
Congressional Budget Office, upon bipartisan request by a committee of 
jurisdiction, to instead evaluate potential Federal savings from 
healthcare legislation over two additional 10-year periods beyond the 
traditional budget window.
  This will help Congress better understand whether preventive health 
policies could improve health outcomes while reducing long-term costs 
to both taxpayers and our Federal healthcare programs.
  Let me be clear. This is not merely a budgetary gimmick. This bill 
does not change existing budget enforcement rules. It does not create 
new spending authority, and it does not allow projected future savings 
to be used to offset spending today. Any long-term savings estimates 
would be supplementary to CBO's traditional budget projections.
  Nor does this legislation assume that every preventive intervention 
will generate savings. It simply gives Congress better information to 
identify policies that have the potential to deliver meaningful 
benefits to both patients and taxpayers alike.
  If a preventive healthcare bill has the potential to keep Americans 
healthier, prevent costly illnesses, and save taxpayers money over the 
long term, then Congress should have the information necessary to 
evaluate those savings. That is exactly what this legislation will 
accomplish.
