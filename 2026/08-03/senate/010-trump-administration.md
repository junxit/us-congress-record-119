# TRUMP ADMINISTRATION

**Speaking:** Thune, John (T000250) [R-SD]

```
                          TRUMP ADMINISTRATION

  Mr. THUNE. Mr. President, this week, we will move our fifth block of 
civilian nominees, which includes 74 well-qualified individuals. By the 
end of the week, we will have cleared 95 percent of the civilian 
nominees who are eligible for floor consideration--95 percent.
  At this same point in President Trump's first term, there were 155 
unconfirmed nominees on the calendar. This year, that number is on 
track to be about 10. It is the best confirmation track record in a 
quarter century.
  As I said, we will have confirmed 95 percent of the civilian nominees 
available for confirmation, meaning that we are doing better at this 
point than we were during President Obama's first term, President 
Trump's first term, or President Biden's.
  It didn't always look like we would be able to achieve this. From the 
very beginning of his second term, Democrats met President Trump's 
nominees with a historic level of obstruction. And when I say historic, 
I do mean historic.
  Up until extremely recently, President Trump was the only President 
on record--the only President on record--not to have had any civilian 
nominees approved by unanimous consent or voice vote. That used to be 
the standard way of approving these nominations.
  During President George H. W. Bush's 4 years in office and President 
Clinton's first 4 years, both Presidents had 98 percent of their 
civilian nominees--98 percent--confirmed through unanimous consent or 
voice vote. President George W. Bush and President Obama both had 90 
percent.
  And then we get to President Trump's first administration. Democrats 
introduced an unprecedented new level of partisanship into the 
historically bipartisan confirmation process, forcing time-consuming 
votes on civilian nominees who would once have gone by unanimous 
consent or by voice vote.
  But despite this--despite this new level of partisanship--both 
President Trump in his first term and President Biden had more than 
half of their nominees confirmed by unanimous consent or voice vote.
  And then we get to President Trump's current administration. As I 
said, until recently, he had not had one single nominee confirmed by 
unanimous consent or voice vote--zero. So the fact that we are standing 
here today with a civilian nominations calendar about to be essentially 
cleared is pretty impressive.
  But we have been on a mission to ensure that the President has his 
nominees in place so that he can do the job the American people elected 
him to do. That included taking action to restore the Senate tradition 
of en bloc confirmation of largely noncontroversial nominees. And, most 
of all, it included putting in the work--lots of work in committees, 
lots of work on the floor, and lots of votes--lots and lots of votes.
  That has paid off. We have confirmed 52 article III judges, 91 
percent of the U.S. attorneys that have made it out of committee this 
Congress, 23 Cabinet members, and the list goes on.
  And we are going to continue to ensure that nominees are confirmed in 
a timely fashion and that the President is able to have his team in 
place.
  I yield the floor.
  I suggest the absence of a quorum.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The clerk will call the roll.
  The senior assistant executive clerk proceeded to call the roll.
  Mr. THUNE. Mr. President, I ask unanimous consent that the order for 
the quorum call be rescinded.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Without objection, it is so ordered.

                          ____________________
```
