# Daily Digest/Senate Committee Meetings

**Measures:** S. 239, S. 1055, S. 1514, S. 1748, H.R. 2400, S. 3219, S. 4199, S. 4407, S. 5171

```
Committee Meetings
(Committees not listed did not meet)
BUSINESS MEETING
Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation: Committee ordered 
favorably reported the following business items:
  S. 1748, to protect the safety of children on the internet, with an 
amendment in the nature of a substitute;
  S. 4199, to require entities that make artificial intelligence 
chatbots available to minors to implement certain safe design features, 
with an amendment in the nature of a substitute;
  S. 4407, to require the creation of family accounts for children to 
be able to use artificial intelligence chatbots, to require verifiable 
parental consent for teens using artificial intelligence chatbots, with 
an amendment in the nature of a substitute;

[[Page D813]]


  S. 5171, to require a study of AI-enabled toys and development of a 
joint action plan regarding the marketing and sale of AI-enabled toys, 
with an amendment in the nature of a substitute; and
  A promotion list in the Coast Guard.
SOCIAL SECURITY
Committee on Finance: Committee concluded a hearing to examine 
exploring process approaches for addressing Social Security solvency, 
after receiving testimony from Charles P. Blahous, George Mason 
University, Fairfax, Virginia; and Marc Goldwein, Committee for a 
Responsible Federal Budget, Nancy A. LeaMond, AARP, and Rebecca D. 
Vallas, National Academy of Social Insurance, all of Washington, D.C.
NOMINATIONS
Committee on Foreign Relations: Committee concluded a hearing to 
examine the nominations of Peter McCoy, of South Carolina, to be 
Ambassador to Montenegro, Daniel Travis, of California, a Career Member 
of the Senior Foreign Service, Class of Counselor, to be Ambassador to 
the Republic of Sierra Leone, and Nathaniel Morris, of Kentucky, to be 
Ambassador to the Republic of Colombia, all of the Department of State, 
after the nominees testified and answered questions in their own 
behalf.
WAR IN SUDAN
Committee on Foreign Relations: Committee received a closed briefing 
update on the war in Sudan from Steven Galpern, Acting Deputy Assistant 
Secretary of Analysis, Bureau of Intelligence and Research, Department 
of State.
PREDICTION MARKETS
Committee on Indian Affairs: On Tuesday, August 4, 2026, Committee 
concluded a hearing to examine tracking prediction markets' exponential 
growth, focusing on tribal implications and beyond, after receiving 
testimony from Mathura Sridharan, Ohio Solicitor General, Columbus; 
Mark Macarro, National Congress of American Indians, and Tehassi Hill, 
Indian Gaming Association, both of Washington, D.C.; Jamie Hummingbird, 
National Tribal Gaming Commissioners and Regulators, Oneida, Wisconsin; 
and Harry Levant, Public Health Advocacy Institute, Boston, 
Massachusetts.
BUSINESS MEETING
Committee on Indian Affairs: Committee ordered favorably reported the 
following business items:
  S. 239, to take certain mineral interests into trust for the benefit 
of the Crow Tribe of Montana, with an amendment in the nature of a 
substitute;
  S. 1055, to amend the Indian Health Care Improvement Act to modify 
the notification requirement for emergency contract health services for 
certain beneficiaries;
  S. 1514, to take certain land in the State of Washington into trust 
for the benefit of the Quinault Indian Nation, with an amendment;
  S. 3219, to transfer certain Federal land into trust for certain 
Indian Pueblos in the State of New Mexico, with an amendment in the 
nature of a substitute; and
  H.R. 2400, to take certain Federal land in the State of California 
into trust for the benefit of the Pit River Tribe with an amendment in 
the nature of a substitute.
NOMINATIONS
Committee on the Judiciary: Committee concluded a hearing to examine 
the nominations of Anna St. John, of Louisiana, to be United States 
Circuit Judge for the Fifth Circuit, Gregory Carl Cook, to be United 
States District Judge for the Northern District of Alabama, who was 
introduced by Senator Husted, D. Adam Candeub, of the District of 
Columbia, to be an Assistant Attorney General, Department of Justice, 
and John Peter Cronan, of New York, and Jason Manion, of Ohio, each to 
be a Member of the United States Sentencing Commission, after the 
nominees testified and answered questions in their own behalf.
MUSLIM BROTHERHOOD NETWORK
Committee on the Judiciary: Subcommittee on Federal Courts, Oversight, 
Agency Action, and Federal Rights concluded a hearing to examine the 
Muslim brotherhood network in America, after receiving testimony from 
Lara L. Burns, The George Washington University Program on Extremism, 
Washington, D.C.; Arielle F. Klepach, National Jewish Advocacy Center, 
Miami, Florida; and Kyle Shideler, Center for Security Policy, 
Nashville, Tennessee.
THE NEXT 250
Committee on Small Business and Entrepreneurship: Committee concluded a 
hearing to examine the next 250, focusing on small businesses leading 
the way, after receiving testimony from Ajay P. Malshe, Arkwright 
Space, West Lafayette, Indiana; Lorena Cantarovici, Maria Empanada, 
Denver, Colorado; ValJay Rigby, Utah Farm Bureau Federation, Newton; 
and Nikki High, Octavia's Bookshelf, Pasadena, California.
BUSINESS MEETING
Select Committee on Intelligence: Committee met in closed session to 
consider pending intelligence matters.
  Committee recessed subject to the call.

[[Page D814]]


INTELLIGENCE
Select Committee on Intelligence: Committee met in closed session to 
receive a briefing on certain intelligence matters from officials of 
the intelligence community.
```
