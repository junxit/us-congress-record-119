# RECOGNIZING NATIONAL BOYS & GIRLS CLUB WEEK

**Speaking:** Yakym, Rudy (Y000067) [R-IN]

```
                              {time}  1045
              RECOGNIZING NATIONAL BOYS & GIRLS CLUB WEEK

  (Mr. Yakym of Indiana was recognized to address the House for 5 
minutes.)
  Mr. YAKYM. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize National Boys & 
Girls Club Week and celebrate the incredible impact the Boys & Girls 
Clubs have on young people across our country.
  Before coming to Congress, I had the privilege of serving as board 
chair of the Boys & Girls Clubs of Northern Indiana. Through that 
experience, I saw firsthand how these clubs do things like taking care 
of young people, how they change lives, how they create opportunities, 
and how they mentor our young people and the future generations.
  When I came to Congress, I knew I wanted to continue to support their 
mission. That is why I was proud to introduce a bipartisan resolution 
last night honoring the National Boys & Girls Club Week with my 
friends, Representatives Scholten and Fitzpatrick.
  Each year Boys & Girls Clubs serve more than 4 million young people 
nationwide, including over 180,000 Hoosiers. Through career readiness 
programs, these clubs help prepare the next generation for success in 
school, work, and life.
  Mr. Speaker, I encourage all Americans to recognize the dedicated 
staff, volunteers, and supporters who work tirelessly to build great 
futures for millions of young people. I also urge my colleagues to join 
me in supporting this resolution and celebrating National Boys & Girls 
Club Week.


  Honoring Deputy Jon Samuelson of LaPorte County Sheriff's Department

  Mr. YAKYM. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor a hero from Indiana's 
Second Congressional District, Deputy Jon Samuelson of the LaPorte 
County Sheriff's Office.
  On May 22, Deputy Samuelson did what good people do: He stopped to 
help a stranger stranded on the side of the road and drove him to the 
hospital. That act of decency nearly cost him his life, but before it 
did, he saved someone else's life.
  What began as kindness quickly escalated into a violent 
confrontation. Before the suspect opened fire, Deputy Samuelson 
intervened. He deflected the weapon away from a nurse who was directly 
in the line of fire. That nurse told investigators that Jon saved his 
life.
  That is the definition of sacrifice and service.
  There is a thin blue line, and it is a dangerous line, that separates 
chaos from civilization. Most of us will never see that line. However, 
men and women like Deputy Samuelson stand on it every single day so the 
rest of us don't have to.
  Jon comes from four generations of law enforcement in LaPorte County. 
He knew what the badge demanded, and he chose it anyway.
  To his wife, Quinn: We stand with you as Jon recovers.
  May God continue to have His healing hand on Jon.
```
