# HONORING THE LIFE AND PUBLIC SERVICE OF DR. CHARLES ROBERT KING

**Speaking:** Griffith, H. Morgan (G000568) [R-VA]

```
    HONORING THE LIFE AND PUBLIC SERVICE OF DR. CHARLES ROBERT KING

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. H. MORGAN GRIFFITH

                              of virginia

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 19, 2026

  Mr. GRIFFITH. Mr. Speaker, I rise to honor Dr. Charles King, former 
president of Southwest Virginia Community College (SVCC),

[[Page E1098]]

who passed away on October 10, 2026, at the age of 92.
  Dr. King was born on March 22, 1934, in Fort Pierce, Florida. From an 
early age, Dr. King and his family faced hardship and adversity. But 
despite personal challenges, Dr. King worked hard in school. Not only 
did he succeed as a student, but also as a student-athlete. These 
experiences helped shape Mr. King's professional interests in pursuing 
education as a career and, more specifically, supporting community 
colleges. Following his graduation from the University of Florida, he 
became a dean at Southeastern Community College.
  However, in 1967, Dr. King was selected as the first-ever president 
for a new community college. Located in Cedar Bluff, Virginia, SVCC was 
created to serve educational opportunities for students in Southwest 
Virginia. Dr. King believed in the school's mission to ensure ``no 
student was left behind.''
  Over the course of four decades under Dr. King's leadership, SVCC 
expanded its academic programs, improved facilities, established 
community partnerships, and made lasting impacts in the region. 
Starting with roughly more than 700 students in 1968, SVCC has helped 
nearly 100,000 students. This growth and the school's continued 
operations highlight the success Dr. King helped foster during his 
tenure as president.
  In 2007, Dr. King retired from his post, making him the longest-
serving president in the Virginia Community College System. The current 
SVCC president credits Dr. King for his vision that changed countless 
lives across Southwest Virginia, Of the numerous honors he has 
received, Dr. King is a recipient of the Virginia Community College 
System Chancellor's Award for Leadership in Philanthropy.
  Dr. King is survived by his four children, Charles R. King, Jr. (Dee) 
of Belfast, Virginia, Cheryl Lynn King (Doug Minner) of Fort Pierce, 
Florida, William Chandler King, and Lloyd Chadwick King; grandchildren, 
Jessica Nicole Bailey, Derek Joel King, Andrew James Minner, Corbin 
Ryan King, Victoria Lynn Minner, Mary Mattlyn King, Ella Kate King, and 
Laura King; his brother, Dr. Maxwell C. King; five great, 
grandchildren; and several cousins, nieces, nephews, and so many dear 
friends.
  Thanks to Dr. King's service, thousands of local students in 
Southwest Virginia obtained access to fruitful educational programs, 
received accreditations in professional fields, and supported local 
communities in the region. His legacy will continue to live on in 
school programs, regional education initiatives, and Southwest Virginia 
communities.
```
