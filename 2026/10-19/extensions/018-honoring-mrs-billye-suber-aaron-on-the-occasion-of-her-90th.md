# HONORING MRS. BILLYE SUBER AARON ON THE OCCASION OF HER 90TH BIRTHDAY

**Speaking:** Bishop, Sanford D. (B000490) [D-GA]

```
 HONORING MRS. BILLYE SUBER AARON ON THE OCCASION OF HER 90TH BIRTHDAY

                                 ______
                                 

                       HON. SANFORD D. BISHOP JR.

                               of georgia

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 19, 2026

  Mr. BISHOP. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor a woman of grace, 
grit, and uncommon purpose, Mrs. Billye Suber Aaron, who celebrated her 
90th birthday on October 16, 2026, surrounded by family and friends at 
the Hyatt Regency Hotel in Atlanta, Georgia.
  Mr. Speaker, some lives are measured in years. Billye Aaron's life is 
measured in the lives she has lifted. For nine decades she has carried 
herself with a quiet dignity that commands a room without ever 
demanding it.
  Billye Jewel Suber was born on October 16, 1936, in Anderson County, 
Texas, to Nathan Suber and Annie Mae Smith Suber. She came up in the 
segregated South, where the doors of opportunity were too often closed 
to a young Black girl with big dreams. She did not wait for those doors 
to open. She studied her way through them. She attended Clemons School 
in Neches, Texas, and graduated from Lincoln High School in Dallas in 
1954. In 1958, she earned her Bachelor of Arts degree in English from 
Texas College in Tyler, Texas. Her promise earned her a fellowship to 
Atlanta University, where she received her Master of Arts degree in 
1960. And she continued her post-graduate studies at the University of 
California at Berkeley.
  She gave her gifts to the classroom first, teaching English in the 
Atlanta Public Schools and at Spelman College, Morehouse College, South 
Carolina State College, and Morris Brown College. Generations of 
students learned from her that words have power, and that a well-
prepared mind is something no one can ever take away.
  Then, in 1968, she broke a barrier many said could not be broken. 
Hired as co-host of WSB-TV's ``Today in Georgia,'' Billye became the 
first African American woman in the Southeast to co-host a daily, hour-
long talk show. Mr. Speaker, consider the year. In April 1968, the 
Reverend Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr. was struck down in Memphis, and 
Atlanta laid her favorite son to rest. Just two months later, Senator 
Robert F. Kennedy was assassinated in Los Angeles. A grieving Nation 
wondered whether the dream had died with them. Yet in that very year, 
little Black girls across the South turned on their televisions and, 
for the first time, saw a face like their own looking back at them with 
intelligence, poise, and authority. In a season of mourning, Billye 
Aaron was living proof that the dream was still alive. She later hosted 
her own weekly program, ``Billye,'' on WTMJ-TV.
  The late Congresswoman Shirley Chisholm, herself a trailblazer, once 
said she wanted to be remembered as a woman ``who dared to be a 
catalyst for change.'' Mr. Speaker, Billye Aaron is that woman. She did 
not simply occupy a seat that had been denied to so many. She used it 
to change what an entire region believed was possible.

[[Page E1100]]

  In 1973, Billye married Henry Louis ``Hank'' Aaron. With great pride 
I note that Hank and I grew up in the same community in the historic 
neighborhood of Toulminville, in Mobile, Alabama. I knew his family as 
friends, neighbors, and schoolmates. When Hank chased the most hallowed 
record in American sports, he did not chase it alone. As hate mail 
poured in by the bagful and threats were made against his very life, 
Billye stood beside him. On April 8, 1974, when he hit home run number 
715 and broke Babe Ruth's record, the whole world saw the triumph. 
Billye knew what it had cost. She was his shelter in the storm, his 
partner in purpose, and the keeper of his peace.
  In 1980, Billye turned her talents to opening doors for others, as 
she became Development Director for the Atlanta branch of the United 
Negro College Fund. For over fourteen years, she co-hosted the annual 
``Lou Rawls Parade of Stars'' telethon, cofounded the Mayor's Masked 
Ball, and became only the second woman in the organization's history to 
serve as Vice President of its Southern Region. Every dollar she raised 
was a dream kept alive and a young person who would not have to choose 
between hunger and higher learning.
  When, she retired in 1994, she did not rest. She and Hank founded the 
Hank Aaron Chasing the Dream Foundation, awarding scholarships to low-
income children so that they too might develop their talents and pursue 
their dreams. A longtime member of the NAACP, she chaired its premier 
fundraiser, the annual Freedom Fund Dinner, for five years, and she was 
named Director emeritus of the NAACP Legal Defense Fund. Her service 
has been recognized with the 2003 Martin Luther King, Jr. ``Salute to 
Greatness'' Award, the YWCA Woman of Achievement Award, numerous 
honorary doctorates, and the Billye Suber Aaron Pavilion at the 
Morehouse School of Medicine bears her name.
  On January 22, 2021, our Nation lost Hank Aaron. Billye lost her 
husband of 47 years. She could have stepped back into private grief, 
and no one would have blamed her. But Billye Aaron did what she has 
always done. She stood up and has carried his legacy and their shared 
mission forward with the same steady hand she lent him through every 
storm. Every scholarship awarded and every young life turned toward 
possibility is a home run they are still hitting together.
  But Mr. Speaker, the title Billye Aaron treasures most is not 
``pioneer'' or ``philanthropist.'' It is ``Mother'' and 
``Grandmother.'' She is the mother of Ceci Haydel, and she embraced 
Hank's children, Gaile, Hank, Jr., Lary, and Dorinda, with a love that 
made one family. She is the proud grandmother of Emily Jewel and Victor 
Aaron Haydel. They know what the world has only glimpsed: a woman whose 
strength was never loud, whose love was never conditional, and whose 
faith has never wavered.
  This 90th year milestone also carries an empty chair. Last year, 
Billye lost her favorite nephew, Nathan Suber, III, who passed away on 
June 16, 2025, at the age of 74. Nathan served the people of Columbus, 
Georgia, in my district, with the same devotion to service that runs 
through the Suber family. He wore the badge as a patrolman and 
detective with the Columbus Police Department, and he represented 
District 1 on the Columbus City Council for three terms, from 1994 to 
2006, including service as the Council's Budget Chairman. Mr. Speaker, 
Nathan was also one of my closest friends in public life. He was a man 
I could call on, a man whose counsel I trusted, and a man whose loyalty 
never wavered. I miss him dearly. Nathan was not in the room at the 
Hyatt Regency, but he was not absent. His love for his Aunt Billye, and 
her love for him were nevertheless felt in every toast and every 
embrace.
  Mr. Speaker, ninety years is a gift from God. But the greater gift 
has been Billye Aaron herself: to her family, to Atlanta, to the State 
of Georgia, and to every child who was told ``you can't'' and then met 
a woman who showed them ``you can.'' She has taught us that true 
greatness is not found in the spotlight, but in what we do for others 
after the lights go down.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues in the House of Representatives to 
join my wife, Vivian, and me; the people of the 2nd Congressional 
District of Georgia and of this Nation in extending our heartfelt 
congratulations to Mrs. Billye Suber Aaron on the occasion of her 90th 
birthday. Moreover, we thank her for a lifetime of extraordinary 
service to humanity and wish her joy, health, and God's richest 
blessings in the days, weeks, months, and years ahead.
```
