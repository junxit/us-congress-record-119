# RECOGNIZING THE LEGACY OF MR. BOBBY PLAIR

**Speaking:** Norman, Ralph (N000190) [R-SC]

```
               RECOGNIZING THE LEGACY OF MR. BOBBY PLAIR

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. RALPH NORMAN

                           of south carolina

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 19, 2026

  Mr. NORMAN. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Mr. Bobby Plair, a 
South Carolinian whose legacy reflects unwavering leadership, 
patriotism, and excellence.
  Born in Rock Hill, South Carolina on March 22, 1927, Bobby grew up in 
the Crawford Road community where he spent most of his life. Shortly 
after graduating Friendship High School in 1945, Bobby served in the 
United States Marine Corps and trained at Montford Point, North 
Carolina. The Montford Point Marines were the first African Americans 
to serve in the United States Marine Corps. At a time when African 
American recruits were segregated and excluded from job opportunities 
given to their fellow servicemen, these recruits demonstrated 
excellence and helped further the integration of our Armed Forces. 
Bobby was assigned to Saipan, an island in the Pacific, where he served 
until the end of World War II. In 2012, Bobby was recognized for that 
service and was awarded the Congressional Gold Medal, the Nation's 
highest civilian honor.
  After being honorably discharged in 1946, Bobby earned his degree 
from North Carolina A&T and began his career as an educator, band 
director, and musician. Bobby's first pursuit was sparking a passion 
for music in students. After starting school bands in Foil Mill and 
Great Falls, Bobby served as chorale director at Clinton Junior College 
in his home community. Bobby later went to Finley High School as the 
band director and taught in Chester, South Carolina.
  While teaching, Bobby was an entrepreneur and ran several businesses. 
One of those businesses was a Plairs grocery store located on Simrill 
Street in Rock Hill. The store became a staple of the community and has 
been in business for over five decades. Bobby was also very successful 
in real estate, purchasing and renting property across Rock Hill. His 
most notable purchase was the Old Herman Church on Dave Lyle Boulevard, 
where he attended church. The church is also the site of the first 
school for African Americans in Rock Hill, an important place during 
the civil rights movement.
  Through all of his professional achievements, Bobby was a devoted 
family man. He and his wife Juanita had been married for over fifty 
years and raised three children, Sarah, Victor, and Bobby, Jr., who 
shared his love of music. Bobby was truly a man of strong character, 
whose life's work has made a meaningful impact on countless 
individuals.
  Mr. Speaker, on behalf of the people of South Carolina, I am proud to 
recognize Bobby's unmatched commitment to our Nation, state, and 
community. His servant leadership leaves a legacy that will continue to 
inspire generatIons to come.
```
