# CELEBRATING BELMONT'S 100TH ANNIVERSARY

**Speaking:** Mullin, Kevin (M001225) [D-CA]

```
                CELEBRATING BELMONT'S 100TH ANNIVERSARY

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. KEVIN MULLIN

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 19, 2026

  Mr. MULLIN. Mr. Speaker, I rise to recognize the City of Belmont in 
my Congressional District, halfway between the City of San Francisco 
and the City of San Jose. With stunning views of the San Francisco Bay, 
its rolling green hills slope down to become part of the economic 
engine of San Mateo County. Belmont, home to over 25,000, is a hidden 
gem with a small-town charm. Belmont officially incorporated on October 
29, 1926, and will celebrate its 100th anniversary on October 29, 2026.
  The land was originally inhabited by local Ohlone tribes who lived on 
the Peninsula for roughly 4,000 years. Belmont evolved from a 35,240-
acre Spanish land grant known as Rancho de las Pulgas, granted in 1795 
to the Arguello family, into a vital stagecoach stop between San 
Francisco and San Jose in the 19th Century. In 1850, Englishman Charles 
Aubrey Angelo established ``Angelo's Roadhouse'' (or Angelo's Corners) 
at the junction of what is now Old County Road and Ralston Avenue. This 
convenient location shaped the traveler's stop into a community known 
as ``the Corners'' before officially adopting the name Belmont. Elias 
Beard, a San Francisco realtor and property promotor, named Belmont in 
1853. It is believed to be derived from the French belle montagne, or 
Italian bel monte, meaning ``beautiful mountain.''
  Between 1850 and 1900, Belmont would then transform from a rural 
stagecoach stop into a residential community. Key developments included 
the establishment of a United States post office in 1854, the third in 
then-San Francisco County. Foreshadowing the city's commitment to 
nature and open space, Twin Pines Park was founded by Carl and Dorothea 
Janke in 1864 as a German-style beer garden and picnic ground inspired 
by beer gardens in Hamburg, Germany. Today it has evolved into a civic 
center. It hosts Belmont Historical Society Museum, Twin Pines Senior & 
Community Center, Manor House (which serves as an art center), Belmont 
City Hall, and more. By 1868, the San Francisco and San Jose Railroad, 
running through Belmont since 1864, had transformed the area from an 
isolated rural hamlet into an accessible commuter suburb and popular 
recreation destination. The 1867 installation of a passenger depot 
facilitated rapid transit to San Francisco, enabling tourism 
particularly to Belmont Park picnic grounds and increased its appeal 
for wealthy individuals, such as William Chapman Ralston, who were 
building country estates.
  Dubbed ``The man who built San Francisco,'' William C. Ralston was a 
successful banker and investor in San Francisco who had chosen Belmont 
for his country estate. He purchased an Italian villa built by Count 
Leonetto Cipriano, then dismantled, boxed, and shipped it to Belmont, 
where it was reconstructed. Renowned for entertaining dignitaries, 
elected officials, and celebrities, Ralston's home became known as 
``The White House of the West,'' and in 1922, became home to Notre Dame 
de Namur University.
  In the early 20th Century, Belmont's primary economic and social 
driver became sanitariums, providing an influx of jobs, supporting 
local businesses, and stabilizing the community. Many Victorian 
mansions previously functioning as hotels or private residencies were 
converted, and some are still present within Belmont today. During the 
first half of the 20th Century, Belmont was home to five major 
sanitariums: Gardner Nerve, California, Alexander, Twin Pines, and The 
Howard Foundation's Children's Tuberculosis Sanitarium. Belmont earned 
the reputation as a ``city of sanitariums.'' Today Belmont is known for 
having a strong network of high-quality senior care facilities.
  Businesses began to flourish near Old County Road, and the ``Old 
County Road 4 Corners Area'' was the commercial hub. Popular 
establishments included the Ellet Hotel, Belmont Hotel, American Hotel, 
a soda works, livery stables, and a blacksmith shop. The community was 
served by a one-room schoolhouse on Old County Road, which was shared 
with the City of San Carlos.
  At the same time, the Japanese American community in Belmont became 
major producers of chrysanthemums, particularly after 1907. Japanese 
immigrants had begun growing flowers in the Bay Area including Belmont 
in the late 19th Century; however, commercial, large-scale production 
accelerated early in the 1900s due to soaring local demand for 
luxuries. Belmont specialized in cultivating large, high-quality 
chrysanthemum blooms. Featuring large, layered petals like the 
Chrysanthemum ``King George,'' they set high standards for the 
industry. The driving force, Japanese Americans, became crucial to the 
local economy. Belmont became the leading chrysanthemum center in the 
United States until World War II.

  Belmont centers the preservation of its history and heritage through 
the Belmont Historical Society, founded in 1987. It is a volunteer-run 
non-profit organization, dedicated to preserving and sharing the city's 
history. The Historical Society holds recordings that contain first-
hand accounts from long-time community members. These are available 
through the Belmont Historical Society archives, or more recently, the 
city's social media platforms.
  As with other communities, Belmont experienced a rapid post-WWII 
growth with its population increasing from roughly 5,000 to nearly 
25,000 people. As orchards and farms were replaced by housing for the 
influx of residents, the area saw continuous residential construction. 
During the 1980s and 1990s, Belmont began focusing on expanding 
community spaces, like Waterdog Lake, to build the city's character and 
serve the community. The proximity to Silicon Valley brought more 
residents and community projects and continued the transition to a 
largely residential bedroom community.
  Since the early 2000s, Belmont has focused on improving the quality 
of life for its residents by introducing development plans that 
emphasize environmental sustainability, enhanced public spaces, and 
mixed development. The city works to transform the city center and 
other resources to foster a more vibrant and connected community. The 
general resident satisfaction in Belmont is high, with residents 
consistently expressing appreciation for the city's quiet, small-town 
feel, and highly rated schools. The peaceful and beautiful atmosphere 
is an enjoyable retreat from the bustle of nearby large cities.
  Mr. Speaker, as Belmont proudly celebrates 100 years as an 
incorporated city, I ask my colleagues to join me in recognizing 
Belmont's heritage and the unique history that has shaped the city into 
a vibrant community, evolving from a once rural retreat to a modem 
residential hub, housing a close community of outstanding residents.
```
