# HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF PROFESSOR EMERITUS JAMES E. "JIM" JERNBERG

**Speaking:** Larsen, Rick (L000560) [D-WA]

```
  HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF PROFESSOR EMERITUS JAMES E. ``JIM'' 
                                JERNBERG

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. RICK LARSEN

                             of washington

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 19, 2026

  Mr. LARSEN of Washington. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life 
and legacy of Professor Emeritus James E. ``Jim'' Jernberg of the 
Humphrey School of Public Affairs at the University of Minnesota.
  Born and raised in Duluth, Minnesota, Professor Jernberg built a 
career devoted to education, public service, and mentorship that 
spanned generations. He earned his bachelor`s degree at the University 
of Minnesota Duluth, master's degree in public affairs at the 
University's Twin Cities campus, and PhD at the University of 
Wisconsin-Madison. He joined the University of Minnesota faculty in 
1964 and served at the University for 31 years before retiring in 1995.
  Throughout his career, Professor Jernberg was known for his passion 
for teaching and his commitment to preparing students for careers in 
public service. He helped shape generations of public servants who went 
on to influence policy and serve in every level of government. 
Professor Jernberg was my professor at the Humphrey School, and he left 
a lasting impact on my approach to public service and the 
responsibilities of government. He taught me to appreciate that ``in 
government, nothing ever ends,'' which has served as a reminder that 
the work of public service requires persistence, and that progress must 
be sustained long after a law is passed or a policy is adopted.
  Professor Jernberg was a kind and generous mentor who valued his 
relationships with students and alumni. He and his wife, Sally, 
welcomed students into their home, creating a place where they could 
share meals, discuss public policy, and talk about their lives long 
after graduation. That spirit of hospitality and friendship continued 
for decades through regular gatherings known as ``Lunch with Jim.'' 
Even 30 years after his retirement, Jim remained an active member of 
the Humphrey School community.
  Mr. Speaker, Professor Jernberg's legacy lives on in the students he 
taught, the public servants he mentored, and the communities they 
serve. I extend my condolences to his family, friends, and the entire 
Humphrey School community. I am grateful to have been his student.
```
