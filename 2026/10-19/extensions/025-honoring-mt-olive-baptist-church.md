# HONORING MT. OLIVE BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                   HONORING MT. OLIVE BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 19, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Mt. Olive Baptist 
Church of Opelousas, Louisiana, a congregation with more than 130 years 
of faith, education, and service. In 1890,

[[Page E1102]]

a group of Christians came out from Little Zion Baptist Church and 
began to discuss plans for what would become Mt. Olive. The church was 
organized in 1891 in the home of Brother Alex Sanders, with Brother 
Felix Green as leader. On January 5, 1891, the congregation purchased 
land from Mrs. Louis Prudhomme for the purpose of erecting a church 
building, and that plan was accomplished when the first church was 
built. From its earliest days, Mt. Olive grew out of the faith and 
initiative of a small group of believers who were determined to have a 
church home of their own.
  The church's service to its community reached well beyond worship. In 
1897, when there were few schools for Black children in Opelousas and 
across Louisiana, the 7th District Baptist School was founded at Mt. 
Olive. The school was housed in the original church building on Market 
Street and remained active until 1918, for about two decades. It was 
one of only two private schools for area Blacks, the other being St. 
Joseph's Convent. In providing a place to learn at a time when 
educational opportunities were scarce, Mt. Olive helped shape the lives 
of generations in the Opelousas community and showed that the church's 
mission included the education of its young people.
  Mt. Olive's role in the faith community of Opelousas has been shaped 
by this history of service. A church that opens its building to a 
school understands that faith and the welfare of the community go 
together, and that investing in education is an investment in the 
future of families and neighborhoods. That legacy continues to inform 
the church's identity and its place among the congregations of 
Opelousas.
  Today, a historic marker in front of the church designates the site 
as a historic site in Louisiana, preserving for future generations the 
story of the church and of the school it housed. Mt. Olive's history is 
part of the story of Opelousas, and of the determination of its people 
to build institutions that served their families.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring Mt. Olive 
Baptist Church and its congregation for more than 130 years of faith, 
education, and service to the people of Opelousas. May Mt. Olive 
continue to strengthen the faith community of Opelousas and to serve 
its community for generations to come.
```
