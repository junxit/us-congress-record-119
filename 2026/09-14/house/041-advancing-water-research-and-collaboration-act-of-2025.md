# ADVANCING WATER RESEARCH AND COLLABORATION ACT OF 2025

**Speaking:** Westerman, Bruce (W000821) [R-AR], Elfreth, Sarah (E000301) [D-MD], Wittman, Robert J. (W000804) [R-VA]

**Measures:** H.R. 7889

```
         ADVANCING WATER RESEARCH AND COLLABORATION ACT OF 2025

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and pass the 
bill (H.R. 7889) to amend the Water Resources Research Act of 1984 to 
reauthorize the water resources research and technology institutes 
program, and for other purposes, as amended.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 7889

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Advancing Water Research and 
     Collaboration Act of 2025'' or the ``AWRC Act of 2025''.

     SEC. 2. CONGRESSIONAL DECLARATION OF PURPOSE.

       Section 103(4) of the Water Resources Research Act of 1984 
     (42 U.S.C. 10302(4)) is amended by inserting ``, including 
     the growing artificial intelligence industry,'' after 
     ``private industry''.

     SEC. 3. WATER RESOURCES RESEARCH AND TECHNOLOGY INSTITUTES.

       Section 104 of the Water Resources Research Act of 1984 (42 
     U.S.C. 10303) is amended--
       (1) in subsection (f)--
       (A) in paragraph (2), by striking ``subsection 104(g) of 
     this Act'' and inserting ``subsection (g)''; and
       (B) by striking the subsection designation and all that 
     follows through ``Any sums'' in paragraph (2) and inserting 
     the following:
       ``(f) General Authorization of Appropriations.--
       ``(1) In general.--Except as provided in paragraph (2), 
     there is authorized to be appropriated to carry out this 
     section $16,000,000 for each of fiscal years 2026 through 
     2029.
       ``(2) Failure to obligate.--Any amounts''; and
       (2) in subsection (g)--
       (A) in paragraph (2), by striking ``(2) Research funds'' 
     and inserting the following:
       ``(4) Competitive grants.--Research funds'';
       (B) in paragraph (1)--
       (i) in the third sentence, by striking ``Funds made'' and 
     inserting the following:
       ``(3) Availability of funds.--Funds made''; and
       (ii) by striking ``by institutes which focuses'' in the 
     first sentence and all that follows through ``Such funds when 
     appropriated'' in the second sentence and inserting the 
     following: ``by institutes with respect to any of the 
     following:
       ``(A) Research that focuses on water problems and issues of 
     a regional or interstate nature beyond those of concern only 
     to a single State.
       ``(B) Research that relates to specific program priorities 
     identified jointly by the Secretary and the institutes.
       ``(C) Research that relates to water problems identified by 
     Congress as being of an interstate nature.
       ``(2) Federal cost-share.--Funds made available under this 
     subsection''; and
       (C) by striking the subsection designation and all that 
     follows through ``only'' in the first sentence of paragraph 
     (1) and inserting the following:
       ``(g) Additional Funds for Research Focused on Water 
     Problems of Interstate Nature.--
       ``(1) In general.--Of the amounts authorized under 
     subsection (f)(1) for each of fiscal years 2026 through 2029, 
     not more than 20 percent is authorized to be used''.

  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Pursuant to the rule, the gentleman from 
Arkansas (Mr. Westerman) and the gentlewoman from Maryland (Ms. 
Elfreth) each will control 20 minutes.
  The Chair recognizes the gentleman from Arkansas.


                             General Leave

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I ask unanimous consent that all Members 
may have 5 legislative days to revise and extend their remarks and 
include extraneous material on H.R. 7889, the bill now under 
consideration.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Is there objection to the request of the 
gentleman from Arkansas?
  There was no objection.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise in support of H.R. 7889, the Advancing Water 
Research and Collaboration Act of 2025, sponsored by Representative 
Wittman from Virginia.
  Water is one of our Nation's most important resources, and the 
challenges facing our water systems are becoming more complex. This 
legislation takes a practical approach to strengthening the science and 
partnerships we need to manage those challenges.
  The bill reauthorizes the Water Resources Research Act Program, which

[[Page H5576]]

supports water research and education through a network of university-
based institutions. It extends the program through fiscal year 2029 and 
increases funding from $15 million to $16 million annually, consistent 
with currently enacted levels.
  By investing in research at the universities across the country and 
encouraging collaboration on State and regional water issues, the bill 
helps ensure that policymakers, communities, and water managers have 
the science and tools needed to find solutions before water challenges 
become crises.
  Mr. Speaker, I support this bill, and I reserve the balance of my 
time.

                              {time}  1700

  Ms. ELFRETH. Mr. Speaker, I rise in support of H.R. 7889, the 
Advancing Water Research and Collaboration Act, which would reauthorize 
the Water Resources Research Act and continue support for national 
water research institutes.
  Across the country, our water supplies are facing growing challenges, 
from increased demand and pollution to drought and other natural 
disasters. As these pressures strain our water resources, we need to 
make sustained investments in research, conservation, education, and 
new technologies to overcome these challenges and strengthen our long-
term water security.
  Water research institutes play a critical role in developing and 
advancing solutions to our Nation's water challenges by supporting 
water research and education while also building the workforce needed 
to address pressing challenges.
  This work grows in importance as drought, wildfire, and other natural 
disasters become all the more frequent, straining water resources for 
communities across the Nation.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge support for this bill, and I reserve the balance 
of my time.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, as we consider water in our country, I 
want to point out a couple of trips that I have made recently. One was 
to the little town of Beaver, Utah, where they had a 100,000-acre 
forest fire, and a few weeks later, they had an inch to an inch and a 
half of rain, which normally would be very welcome there, but because 
this forest fire basically clear-cut this whole canyon that provides 
the water for the town of Beaver.
  Instead of replenishing their water supply, what it did was wash vast 
amounts of soil and debris into the watershed. It took out three 
hydropower plants. It washed mud and debris across bridges and took 
them out. It also washed all this debris into the town and had their 
water system offline for an extended period of time.
  We often don't associate forest health and water as much as we 
should. This is a crisis that is facing us across the country. That is 
why it is important to have legislation like Representative Wittman has 
proposed here.
  I was in southwest Colorado at the end of last week, and we flew over 
another 100,000-acre fire. We saw, again, how when the land is denuded, 
to use a word from Teddy Roosevelt, and soil is no longer held in place 
and we get rain, it causes catastrophes.
  It doesn't have to be this way. We can have safe, healthy drinking 
water all across this country. Actually, about two-thirds of our water 
comes from forested landscapes.
  We have seen how important this is, even in places like New York 
City, where they have a tremendous water system that people, putting a 
lot of forethought into this many, many decades ago, protected the 
watershed and protected the water going into New York.
  We see it in Boston. We typically think of these forest issues out 
West, but the Quabbin Reservoir in Boston is another forested area that 
is protected and managed strictly for water.
  It is because of research and efforts that have happened for many 
years that give us the science to understand how to manage the 
landscape to produce the water that we all need. When we talk about an 
ounce of prevention being worth a pound of cure, we know that managing 
our forests gives us that ounce of prevention.
  It saves a lot of money in the long run and saves a lot of headaches 
and hard work to restore these systems in the long run, and it is 
important that we continue to do the research and fund these programs 
so that we can provide safe, clean water to all Americans.
  Mr. Speaker, I reserve the balance of my time.
  Ms. ELFRETH. Mr. Speaker, I concur with everything the chairman just 
said, and I reserve the balance of my time.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield such time as he may consume to 
the gentleman from Virginia (Mr. Wittman), the lead sponsor of this 
bill.
  Mr. WITTMAN. Mr. Speaker, I rise in strong support today of H.R. 
7889, the Advancing Water Research and Collaboration Act.
  This legislation would reauthorize the Water Resources Research Act, 
better known as the WRRA, and it will strengthen Federal investment in 
the water research that our communities depend upon. It supports a 
nationwide network of research institutes that address State and 
regional water challenges.
  This is locally driven, nationally significant research. It helps 
safeguard drinking water supplies, reduces nutrient pollution, and 
addresses emerging contaminants that we may find in critical water 
resources.
  With growing water demands from advanced manufacturing, data centers, 
and artificial intelligence infrastructure, the need for sound water 
science has never been greater.
  In Virginia, the WRRA supports research that helps to address water 
challenges that matter most to our communities, including efforts to 
improve the health and resilience of our great natural resource, the 
Chesapeake Bay.
  Reauthorizing this program will help ensure that every State has the 
science-backed information it needs to address its own unique water 
challenges. By investing in scientific research today, we help ensure 
future generations have access to safe, reliable water resources.
  Mr. Speaker, I am proud to lead this bipartisan effort, and I 
encourage all of my colleagues to vote in support of this bill.
  Ms. ELFRETH. Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support this bill, 
and I yield back the balance of my time.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I support H.R. 7889, and I yield back the 
balance of my time.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore (Mr. Fong). The question is on the motion 
offered by the gentleman from Arkansas (Mr. Westerman) that the House 
suspend the rules and pass the bill, H.R. 7889, as amended.
  The question was taken; and (two-thirds being in the affirmative) the 
rules were suspended and the bill, as amended, was passed.
  A motion to reconsider was laid on the table.
```
