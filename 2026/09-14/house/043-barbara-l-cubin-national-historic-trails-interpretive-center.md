# BARBARA L. CUBIN NATIONAL HISTORIC TRAILS INTERPRETIVE CENTER

**Speaking:** Westerman, Bruce (W000821) [R-AR], Elfreth, Sarah (E000301) [D-MD]

**Measures:** S. 790

```
     BARBARA L. CUBIN NATIONAL HISTORIC TRAILS INTERPRETIVE CENTER

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and pass the 
bill (S. 790) to redesignate the National Historic Trails Interpretive 
Center in Casper, Wyoming, as the ``Barbara L. Cubin National Historic 
Trails Interpretive Center.''
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                                 S. 790

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. DESIGNATION OF BARBARA L. CUBIN NATIONAL HISTORIC 
                   TRAILS INTERPRETIVE CENTER.

       (a) In General.--The National Historic Trails Interpretive 
     Center established under section 2(a) of Public Law 105-290 
     (112 Stat. 2783) in Casper, Wyoming, shall be known and 
     designated as the ``Barbara L. Cubin National Historic Trails 
     Interpretive Center''.
       (b) References.--Any reference in a law, map, regulation, 
     document, paper, or other record of the United States to the 
     National Historic Trails Interpretive Center referred to in 
     subsection (a) shall be deemed to be a reference to the 
     ``Barbara L. Cubin National Historic Trails Interpretive 
     Center''.
       (c) Conforming Amendment.--Section 2(a) of Public Law 105-
     290 (112 Stat. 2783) is amended, in the second sentence, by 
     striking ``the National Historic Trails Interpretive Center'' 
     and inserting ``the `Barbara L. Cubin National Historic 
     Trails Interpretive Center' ''.

  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Pursuant to the rule, the gentleman from 
Arkansas (Mr. Westerman) and the gentlewoman from Maryland (Ms. 
Elfreth) each will control 20 minutes.
  The Chair recognizes the gentleman from Arkansas.


                             General Leave

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I ask unanimous consent that all Members 
may have 5 legislative days to revise and extend their remarks and 
include extraneous material on S. 790, the bill now under 
consideration.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Is there objection to the request of the 
gentleman from Arkansas?
  There was no objection.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  I rise in support of S. 790, a bill to redesignate the National 
Historic Trails Interpretive Center in Casper, Wyoming, as the 
``Barbara L. Cubin National Historic Trails Interpretive Center,'' 
sponsored by Senator Cynthia Lummis in the Senate and led by 
Representative Harriet Hageman in the House.
  This bill honors former Representative Barbara Cubin, Wyoming's first 
woman elected to Federal office. During her 14 years in Congress, 
Representative Cubin strongly advocated for Wyoming and the West. As a 
founding member of the Congressional Western Caucus and chair of the 
House Committee on Natural Resources Subcommittee on Energy and Mineral 
Resources, she worked to advance responsible development of America's 
natural resources and improve the management of our Federal lands.
  Representative Cubin also played an important role in shaping and 
telling the history of the American West. In 1998, her legislation 
authorizing the National Historic Trails Interpretive Center in Casper 
was signed into law.
  The center is managed by the Bureau of Land Management, in 
partnership with local and private organizations, and offers free 
interactive exhibits and educational programming about America's 
westward expansion.
  Unfortunately, last year, Representative Cubin publicly announced she 
had been diagnosed with Alzheimer's disease. We continue to hold her, 
her family, and all who know and love her in our prayers.
  It is a fitting tribute to her legacy that the center Representative 
Cubin helped establish will now bear her name. S. 790 recognizes her 
years of service to Wyoming and ensures that her contributions to the 
history and heritage of the American West are remembered for 
generations to come.
  I thank Representative Hageman and the entire Wyoming delegation for 
their leadership on this legislation and their commitment to honor 
Representative Cubin's life and legacy.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support S. 790, and I reserve 
the balance of my time.
  Ms. ELFRETH. Mr. Speaker, S. 790 would redesignate the National 
Historic Trails Interpretive Center in Casper, Wyoming, as the 
``Barbara L. Cubin National Historic Trails Interpretive Center.''
  Former Representative Barbara Cubin was the first woman to represent 
Wyoming in Congress. Mr. Speaker, I am going to deviate from my notes 
here to remind all the folks watching at home that there have been less 
than 400 women ever elected to the Congress of the United States. It is 
a privilege to stand here and defend this bill.
  She was a founding member of the Congressional Western Caucus and the 
original sponsor of the legislation establishing the center. Renaming 
the center will honor her hard work and lasting legacy in Wyoming and 
the West.
  Casper, Wyoming, is known as the city of the crossroads of history 
because it is the only geographic location in the western United States 
where four congressionally recognized historic trails converge.
  The historic trails that pass through the Casper area are a 
distinctive part of our national character, holding important 
historical and cultural values that reflect the migration, settlement, 
transportation, and commerce that all shaped the West.
  The National Historic Trails Interpretive Center in Casper, owned and 
operated by the Bureau of Land Management, helps keep the story and 
impact of the trails alive today.
  Honoring the legacy and impact of Representative Cubin is a fitting 
tribute to a woman who fought for these trails as a trailblazer in her 
own right.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge support of S. 790, and I reserve the balance of 
my time.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I have no further requests for time. I am 
prepared to close, and I reserve the balance of my time.
  Ms. ELFRETH. Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support this 
legislation, and I yield back the balance of my time.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, again, I thank Representative Hageman and 
Senators Lummis and Barrasso for their leadership on S. 790, and I urge 
my colleagues to support the bill. Mr. Speaker, I yield back the 
balance of my time.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore. The question is on the motion offered by the 
gentleman from Arkansas (Mr. Westerman) that the House suspend the 
rules and pass the bill, S. 790.
  The question was taken; and (two-thirds being in the affirmative) the 
rules were suspended and the bill was passed.
  A motion to reconsider was laid on the table.
```
