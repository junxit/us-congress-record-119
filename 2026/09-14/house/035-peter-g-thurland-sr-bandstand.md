# PETER G. THURLAND, SR., BANDSTAND

**Speaking:** Westerman, Bruce (W000821) [R-AR], Hoyle, Val T. (H001094) [D-OR], Plaskett, Stacey E. (P000610) [D-VI]

**Measures:** H.R. 8121

```
                              {time}  1610
                   PETER G. THURLAND, SR., BANDSTAND

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and pass the 
bill (H.R. 8121) to designate the Christiansted Bandstand at the 
Christiansted National Historic Site, St. Croix, Virgin Islands, as the 
``Peter G. Thurland, Sr., Bandstand'', as amended.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 8121

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. DESIGNATION.

       The bandstand commonly referred to as the ``Christiansted 
     Bandstand'', located at the Christiansted National Historic 
     Site, St. Croix, Virgin Islands, is designated as the ``Peter 
     G. Thurland, Sr., Bandstand''.

     SEC. 2. LEGAL REFERENCES.

       Any reference in any law, regulation, document, record, 
     map, or other paper of the United States to the bandstand 
     referred to in section 2 is deemed to be a reference to the 
     ``Peter G. Thurland, Sr., Bandstand''.

  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Pursuant to the rule, the gentleman from 
Arkansas (Mr. Westerman) and the gentlewoman from Oregon (Ms. Hoyle) 
each will control 20 minutes.
  The Chair recognizes the gentleman from Arkansas.


                             General Leave

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I ask unanimous consent that all Members 
may have 5 legislative days to revise and extend their remarks and 
include extraneous material on H.R. 8121, the bill now under 
consideration.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Is there objection to the request of the 
gentleman from Arkansas?
  There was no objection.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise in support of H.R. 8121, which would officially 
name a historic bandstand located within the Christiansted National 
Historic Site on the Island of St. Croix in the U.S. Virgin Islands as 
the Peter G. Thurland, Sr., Bandstand.
  For generations, the Christiansted bandstand has served as a 
gathering place for music and community in the heart of St. Croix. H.R. 
8121 honors a man whose life and legacy are closely tied to that 
history.
  Peter Gregory Thurland, Sr., was born in Christiansted in 1892. While 
serving in the United States Navy, Mr. Thurland helped design and 
construct the Christiansted Bandstand and performed there as a member 
of the Christiansted Naval Band until his honorable discharge in 1921.
  His service to his community did not end there. Mr. Thurland founded 
the St. Croix Community Band and spent decades training generations of 
young musicians and bringing music to the community through free 
monthly concerts.
  A master craftsman, talented musician, and dedicated mentor, Mr. 
Thurland made a lasting contribution to the cultural and civic life of 
St. Croix. It is particularly fitting that the bandstand he helped 
build, which became a center of community life in St. Croix, bears his 
name.
  Mr. Speaker, I support H.R. 8121, and I reserve the balance of my 
time.
  Ms. HOYLE of Oregon. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, H.R. 8121, introduced by my colleague Delegate Plaskett, 
will honor the legacy of Peter G. Thurland by naming Christiansted 
Bandstand after him.
  This bandstand is a historic landmark at the Christiansted National 
Historic Site in St. Croix, U.S. Virgin Islands. It symbolizes the 
rich, cultural heritage of St. Croix and has served as a community 
gathering place since its construction.
  Peter G. Thurland was a central figure in the St. Croix community. 
Mr. Thurland was known as a master craftsman, musician, and proud 
veteran who served in the Navy Band. Not only did he perform there 
during his military service until his honorable discharge but he 
personally helped design and construct the bandstand.
  He continued to advocate for the arts and founded the St. Croix 
Community Band where he personally trained generations of young 
musicians. Efforts to recognize Mr. Thurland go back to the 103rd 
Congress with the legislation first introduced by Delegate Ron de Lugo.
  It is time to honor Mr. Thurland's legacy and ensure that his impact 
on the cultural and civic life of St. Croix is remembered.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge all of my colleagues to support this legislation, 
and I reserve the balance of my time.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I support H.R. 8121, and I reserve the 
balance of my time.
  Ms. HOYLE of Oregon. Mr. Speaker, I yield such time as she may 
consume to the gentlewoman from the Virgin Islands (Ms. Plaskett), the 
sponsor of this bill.
  Ms. PLASKETT. Mr. Speaker, I rise today in support of H.R. 8121, 
legislation I introduced to designate the Christiansted Bandstand in 
Christiansted, St. Croix, in the Virgin Islands of the United States as 
the Peter G. Thurland, Sr., Bandstand.
  Mr. Speaker, I thank Chairman Westerman, Ranking Member Huffman, 
along with the members and staff of the House Committee on Natural 
Resources, for their consideration of this bill and for advancing it to 
the floor. I appreciate their thoughtful attention to a matter that 
means a great deal to the people of St. Croix, and I look forward to 
working with Members on both sides of the aisle to advance this 
legislation to the President's desk.
  The Christiansted Bandstand is one of the most significant cultural 
landmarks in the U.S. Virgin Islands. Located in the heart of 
Christiansted, it has served for over a century as a gathering place 
for residents and visitors and as the center of St. Croix's musical and 
civic life.
  H.R. 8121 would designate that landmark in honor of the man most 
responsible for its character and continuity, Peter G. Thurland, Sr.
  Mr. Thurland was born on February 19, 1892, in Christiansted, St. 
Croix, then the Danish West Indies. On March 31, 1917, he performed 
with the Christiansted Industrial Brass Band at the formal transfer 
ceremony on the Christiansted Wharf, present at the very moment the 
Virgin Islands became part of the United States.
  Following the transfer of the Danish West Indies to the United 
States, he enlisted in the United States Navy on June 12 of that same 
year and served as a member of the Navy Band.
  During his service, he was instrumental in the physical construction 
and design of the Christiansted Bandstand. He performed there with the 
Christiansted Navy Band until his honorable discharge in September 
1921.

[[Page H5567]]

  In 1938, Mr. Thurland founded the St. Croix Community Band, which 
performed free monthly concerts for three consecutive decades. He 
trained generations of young musicians and led the band until 1968. He 
was also a master craftsman and cabinetmaker. His work can still be 
seen throughout our island.
  Mr. Speaker, my own parents talked about the many activities the 
bandstand represented for children growing up in the town in the 1930s 
and 40s. One of our most iconic period painters, Leo Carty, used the 
bandstand in some of his most memorable paintings. My own children have 
played around the bandstand without a marker to explain who built the 
gathering spot. A naming with a marker sharing the creator and the 
history of the bandstand can only bring our island closer together.

  This legislation was first introduced, as you have heard, from 
Congressman Ron de Lugo in the 103rd Congress. I am proud to bring it 
again in the 119th Congress. The Thurland family has long advocated for 
this recognition, and their persistence reflects the esteem in which 
their grandfather, their patriarch, is held across St. Croix.
  Peter G. Thurland, Sr., helped construct the Christiansted Bandstand, 
served his country with distinction, and dedicated decades to the 
cultural life of St. Croix. His name belongs on that landmark.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support H.R. 8121.
  Ms. HOYLE of Oregon. Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support 
this legislation, and I yield back the balance of my time.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I support H.R. 8121, and I yield back the 
balance of my time.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore. The question is on the motion offered by the 
gentleman from Arkansas (Mr. Westerman) that the House suspend the 
rules and pass the bill, H.R. 8121, as amended.
  The question was taken; and (two-thirds being in the affirmative) the 
rules were suspended and the bill, as amended, was passed.
  A motion to reconsider was laid on the table.
```
