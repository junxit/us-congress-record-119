# ARCHIE CAVANAUGH MIGRATORY BIRD TREATY CLARIFICATION ACT

**Speaking:** Westerman, Bruce (W000821) [R-AR], Hoyle, Val T. (H001094) [D-OR], Begich, Nicholas J. (B001323) [R-AK]

**Measures:** H.R. 6021

```
        ARCHIE CAVANAUGH MIGRATORY BIRD TREATY CLARIFICATION ACT

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and pass the 
bill (H.R. 6021) to amend the Migratory Bird Treaty Act to clarify the 
treatment of authentic Alaska Native articles of handicraft containing 
nonedible migratory bird parts, and for other purposes, as amended.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 6021

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Archie Cavanaugh Migratory 
     Bird Treaty Clarification Act''.

     SEC. 2. PURPOSE.

       The purpose of this Act is to clarify the treatment of 
     authentic Alaska Native articles of handicraft offered for 
     sale under the Convention between the United States and Japan 
     for

[[Page H5565]]

     the Protection of Migratory Birds and Birds in Danger of 
     Extinction and Their Environment, signed at Tokyo March 4, 
     1974 (TIAS 7990).

     SEC. 3. CLARIFICATION FOR AUTHENTIC ALASKA NATIVE ARTICLES OF 
                   HANDICRAFT CONTAINING MIGRATORY BIRD PARTS.

       (a) In General.--Not later than 180 days after the date of 
     the enactment of this Act--
       (1) the Secretary of State shall work with the Secretary of 
     the Interior to enter into appropriate bilateral procedures, 
     as necessary, with the foreign country that is party to the 
     treaty described in section 2 to clarify the treatment of 
     authentic Alaska Native articles of handicraft to be offered 
     for sale containing nonedible migratory bird parts from the 
     species of migratory birds listed in the treaty; and
       (2) the Secretary of the Interior may modify any 
     regulations implementing the Migratory Bird Treaty Act (16 
     U.S.C. 703 et seq.), as appropriate, consistent with the 
     treaty described in section 2, in consultation with the 
     foreign country that is party to the treaty, the Alaska 
     Migratory Bird Co-Management Council, and the Pacific Flyway 
     Council, and in coordination with the Law Enforcement Office 
     of the United States Fish and Wildlife Service, to implement 
     any necessary clarifications resulting from the procedures 
     described in paragraph (1), including conditions for 
     consignment and re-sale, certification of authenticity, and 
     provisions pertaining to species of conservation concern.
       (b) Modification of Regulations.--If the Secretary of the 
     Interior determines that any of the species used under this 
     section show significant population declines or is determined 
     to be a Bird of Conservation Concern pursuant to section 13 
     of the Fish and Wildlife Conservation Act of 1980 (16 U.S.C. 
     2912), the Secretary may modify regulations to remove the 
     species from application under this section.
       (c) Outreach.--The Secretary of the Interior shall work in 
     collaboration with the Alaska Migratory Bird Co-Management 
     Council and other appropriate stakeholders to develop 
     outreach and communication resources and means to communicate 
     rules pertaining to the sale of authentic Alaska Native 
     articles of handicraft including parts of migratory birds.
       (d) Technical Amendment.--Section 2(a) of the Migratory 
     Bird Treaty Act (16 U.S.C. 703) is amended by inserting a 
     comma after ``March 4, 1972''.
       (e) Definitions.--In this Act:
       (1) Alaska native.--The term ``Alaska Native'' means a 
     member of any Indian Tribe that is based in the State of 
     Alaska, as verified through issuance of--
       (A) a Tribal enrollment card;
       (B) a Certificate of Degree of Indian Blood (commonly known 
     as a ``CDIB'') by the Bureau of Indian Affairs; or
       (C) a permit under the Silver Hand program of the Alaska 
     State Council on the Arts.
       (2) Authentic alaska native article of handicraft.--The 
     term ``authentic Alaska Native article of handicraft''--
       (A) means any item that is--
       (i) composed, wholly or in a significant respect, of 
     natural materials that were found, foraged, or gifted; and
       (ii) produced, decorated, or fashioned in significant 
     part--

       (I) by an Alaska Native;
       (II) in the exercise of traditional Alaska Native 
     handicrafts; and
       (III) without the use of any mass copying device;

       (B) includes--
       (i) any weaving, carving, stitching, sewing, lacing, 
     beading, drawing, or painting that meets the criteria 
     described in clause (i); and
       (ii) any item, including clothing, described in subclause 
     (I) that combines the techniques described in that subclause; 
     and
       (C) does not include any item that contains a part of a 
     migratory bird that was taken in a wasteful or illegal 
     manner.
       (3) Indian tribe.--The term ``Indian Tribe'' has the 
     meaning given the term in section 4 of the Indian Self-
     Determination and Education Assistance Act (25 U.S.C. 5304).

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise in support of H.R. 6021, the Archie Cavanaugh 
Migratory Bird Treaty Clarification Act, sponsored by Representative 
Begich of Alaska.
  The Migratory Bird Treaty Act protects more than 900 species of 
migratory birds and plays an important role in conserving these 
species. However, the law also created challenges for Alaska Native 
communities whose longstanding cultural traditions include using 
migratory bird feathers and other nonedible bird parts in traditional 
handicrafts and artwork.
  In 2012, Alaska Native artist Archie Cavanaugh faced Federal charges 
after attempting to sell traditional artwork containing flicker and 
raven feathers. He faced up to 10 years in prison and a $100,000 fine, 
but a settlement was reached in which he agreed to pay a $2,000 fine.
  H.R. 6021 addresses this unfairness by directing appropriate updates 
to the Migratory Bird Treaty with Japan and any subsequent revisions to 
domestic regulations that may be necessary to clarify that authentic 
Alaska Native handicrafts containing nonedible bird parts may be sold 
and shipped, provided those parts were not taken in a wasteful or 
illegal manner.
  I commend Representative Begich for his work with the Alaska Native 
communities to preserve their cultural traditions while maintaining 
important protections for birds.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support H.R. 6021, and I reserve 
the balance of my time.
  Ms. HOYLE of Oregon. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise in strong support of H.R. 6021, the Archie 
Cavanaugh Migratory Bird Treaty Clarification Act.
  For over a century, the Migratory Bird Treaty Act of 1918 has played 
an important role in protecting migratory birds across North America, 
but due to the rigid way the law is interpreted, a few Alaska Native 
artisans who use bird parts in their traditional arts and regalia have 
been criminalized for their craft. This legislation addresses the 
conflict.
  H.R. 6021 would direct the Department of the Interior and the State 
Department to work with Japan to clarify their Migratory Bird Treaty 
with the United States. It would make clear that authentic Alaska 
Native handicrafts made or sold with nonedible migratory bird parts are 
exempt from Federal law.
  This language ensures that while we will continue to have strong 
protections against wasteful harvesting and illegal poaching of birds, 
we are not doing so at the expense of Alaska Native traditions.
  This bill respects Tribal sovereignty, protects traditional 
knowledge, and maintains conservation integrity.
  I thank Representative Begich and Chair Westerman for working 
constructively across the aisle to come to an agreement we can all 
support.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge a ``yes'' vote, and I reserve the balance of my 
time.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield 3 minutes to the gentleman from 
Alaska (Mr. Begich), the lead sponsor of this bill.
  Mr. BEGICH. Mr. Speaker, H.R. 6021, the Archie Cavanaugh Migratory 
Bird Treaty Clarification Act, addresses a narrow but consequential gap 
in Federal law that has criminalized longstanding Alaska Native 
artistic and cultural traditions for more than a century.
  The Migratory Bird Treaty Act implements four separate international 
agreements with Canada, Mexico, Japan, and Russia. These treaties, 
however, do not always line up with each other.
  The treaties with Japan and Russia recognize the subsistence needs of 
indigenous peoples. The earlier treaties with Canada and Mexico did not 
provide the same protections. These gaps have had real consequences for 
Alaska Natives.
  Because the courts have required the Fish and Wildlife Service to 
apply the most restrictive of the four treaties across the board--in 
this case, our treaty with Japan--an Alaska Native artist who sells a 
traditional handicraft containing a raven feather or woodpecker feather 
can face up to 5 years in prison and a $10,000 fine.
  That is not conservation. That is not what these treaties were meant 
to do. It is not something the Alaska Native community should have to 
live with.
  Archie Cavanaugh was a Tlingit artist from Juneau. In 2012, he was 
prosecuted under the Migratory Bird Treaty Act for selling a headdress 
and a hat containing feathers consistent with cultural practices going 
back generations. His case became a symbol of how badly the current 
framework fits Alaska.
  This bill clarifies that the sale and shipment of authentic Alaska 
Native handicrafts containing nonedible migratory bird parts is not a 
Federal

[[Page H5566]]

crime, provided the parts were not taken in a wasteful or illegal 
manner. It contains species protection safeguards. It respects the 
existing co-management structure within the Alaska Migratory Bird Co-
Management Council.
  The bill directs the Department of State and the Department of the 
Interior to work with our treaty partner to update the international 
framework so that Federal regulation and treaty text finally line up.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge the House to support this bill.
  Ms. HOYLE of Oregon. Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support the 
legislation, and I yield back the balance of my time.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I once again thank Representative Begich 
for his hard work in representing his constituents and advancing this 
important legislation, like he has done on so many pieces of 
legislation this Congress.
  I urge my colleagues to support H.R. 6021, and I yield back the 
balance of my time.

the treatment of authentic Alaska Native articles of handicraft

  A motion to reconsider was laid on the table.
```
