# ADVISORY COMMITTEE ON THE RECORDS OF CONGRESS SUNSET ACT

**Speaking:** Griffith, H. Morgan (G000568) [R-VA], Morelle, Joseph D. (M001206) [D-NY], Miller, Mary E. (M001211) [R-IL]

**Measures:** H.R. 9360

```
                              {time}  1500
        ADVISORY COMMITTEE ON THE RECORDS OF CONGRESS SUNSET ACT

  Mr. GRIFFITH. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and pass the 
bill (H.R. 9360) to sunset the Advisory Committee on the Records of 
Congress, and for other purposes, as amended.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 9360

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Advisory Committee on the 
     Records of Congress Sunset Act''.

     SEC. 2. MANAGEMENT AND PRESERVATION OF THE RECORDS OF 
                   CONGRESS.

       (a) Sunset of Advisory Committee on the Records of 
     Congress.--Effective on the date that is 60 days after the 
     date of the enactment of this Act, title 44, United States 
     Code, is amended--
       (1) by striking chapter 27; and
       (2) in the table of chapters, by striking the item relating 
     to chapter 27.
       (b) Report.--Not later than February 1 of the year 
     beginning after the date of enactment of this Act, and 
     annually thereafter, the Director shall submit a report on 
     the management and preservation of the records of Congress 
     during the previous year to--
       (1) the Archivist;
       (2) the Secretary;
       (3) the Clerk;
       (4) the Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental 
     Affairs and the Committee on Rules and Administration of the 
     Senate; and
       (5) the Committee on Oversight and Government Reform and 
     the Committee on House Administration of the House of 
     Representatives.
       (c) Review.--The Archivist, the Secretary, and the Clerk 
     shall meet to review the management and preservation of the 
     records of Congress--
       (1) not later than 60 days after the date on which the 
     Director submits a report under subsection (b); and
       (2) not later than 180 days after the date on which an 
     individual begins to carry out the duties and 
     responsibilities of the Archivist, Secretary, or Clerk.
       (d) Definitions.--In this section:
       (1) Archivist.--The term ``Archivist'' means the Archivist 
     of the United States, or any successor thereto.
       (2) Clerk.--The term ``Clerk'' means the Clerk of the House 
     of Representatives.
       (3) Director.--The term ``Director'' means the Director of 
     the Center for Legislative Archives of the National Archives, 
     or any successor thereto.
       (4) Secretary.--The term ``Secretary'' means the Secretary 
     of the Senate.

  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Pursuant to the rule, the gentleman from 
Virginia (Mr. Griffith) and the gentleman from New York (Mr. Morelle) 
each will control 20 minutes.
  The Chair recognizes the gentleman from Virginia.


                             General Leave

  Mr. GRIFFITH. Mr. Speaker, I ask unanimous consent that all Members 
may have 5 legislative days in which to revise and extend their remarks 
and include extraneous material on this bill.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Is there objection to the request of the 
gentleman from Virginia?

[[Page H5546]]

  There was no objection.
  Mr. GRIFFITH. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may consume.
  Mr. Speaker, H.R. 9360, the Advisory Committee on the Records of 
Congress Sunset Act, led by Representative Mary Miller of Illinois, is 
an important measure that will modernize how Congress manages its 
records.
  H.R. 9360 sunsets the Advisory Committee on the Records of Congress, 
an outdated committee, and replaces it with a more efficient process 
for reviewing congressional records.
  The bill instructs the Director of the Center for Legislative 
Archives of the National Archives to submit an annual report to the 
Archivist of the United States, the Clerk of the House, the Secretary 
of the Senate, and requisite congressional committees on the management 
and preservation of the records of Congress.
  By eliminating this obsolete committee, H.R. 9360 provides a more 
efficient process for the management and preservation of congressional 
records through annual reports and cuts burdensome administrative 
hurdles.
  Mr. Speaker, I reserve the balance of my time.
  Mr. MORELLE. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may consume.
  Mr. Speaker, the Advisory Committee on the Records of Congress was 
created in 1990 to review and advise Congress and the National Archives 
on the management and preservation of congressional records.
  The House Clerk, who is one of the leaders of the advisory committee, 
approached us with this proposal because the committee accomplished its 
key goals and is no longer useful in addressing current challenges.
  This makes sense because the ways we manage and preserve records have 
changed significantly over the last three-and-a-half decades. 
Sunsetting this committee will save taxpayer dollars and allow those 
resources to be used to improve contemporary archiving practices.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge all of my colleagues to support the bill, and I 
reserve the balance of my time.
  Mr. GRIFFITH. Mr. Speaker, I yield such time as she may consume to 
the gentlewoman from Illinois (Mrs. Miller).
  Mrs. MILLER of Illinois. Mr. Speaker, I rise today in support of my 
bill, H.R. 9360, which will sunset the Advisory Committee on the 
Records of Congress.
  This committee was created in 1990 to advise Congress and the 
National Archives on the management and retention of congressional 
records.
  My bill, however, would eliminate this outdated committee. Due to 
advancements in technology over the last several decades and changes in 
the way Congress operates, sunsetting this committee is necessary and 
will streamline the archival process.
  This bill will also maintain oversight of the Center for Legislative 
Archives by requiring the center to publish an annual report on the 
management and preservation of records. Sunsetting this advisory 
committee will furthermore save taxpayers approximately $38,000 
annually in operating costs without sacrificing the transparency of 
congressional records.
  As this advisory committee hits its 36th year, we can agree that the 
committee has accomplished its original goals and has met its intended 
purpose, thereby outliving its usefulness.
  Mr. Speaker, as we continue to improve the efficiency of our 
government, I urge all of my colleagues to support this bill.
  Mr. MORELLE. Mr. Speaker, I appreciate the opportunity to have the 
bill before the House, and I urge all my colleagues to vote in support 
of it.
  Mr. Speaker, I yield back the balance of my time.
  Mr. GRIFFITH. Mr. Speaker, I, too, urge my colleagues to support H.R. 
9360, and I yield back the balance of my time.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore. The question is on the motion offered by the 
gentleman from Virginia (Mr. Griffith) that the House suspend the rules 
and pass the bill, H.R. 9360, as amended.
  The question was taken; and (two-thirds being in the affirmative) the 
rules were suspended and the bill, as amended, was passed.
  A motion to reconsider was laid on the table.
```
