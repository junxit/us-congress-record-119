# SAFE BEACHES, SAFE SWIMMERS ACT

**Speaking:** Westerman, Bruce (W000821) [R-AR], Hoyle, Val T. (H001094) [D-OR], Kiggans, Jennifer A. (K000399) [R-VA]

**Measures:** H.R. 5063

```
                    SAFE BEACHES, SAFE SWIMMERS ACT

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and pass the 
bill (H.R. 5063) to ensure continuity of staffing at Federal beaches, 
and for other purposes, as amended.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 5063

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Safe Beaches, Safe Swimmers 
     Act''.

     SEC. 2. ADEQUATE LIFEGUARD STAFFING AT FEDERAL SWIMMING AREAS 
                   AND BEACHES.

       (a) In General.--If the Secretary determines there is a 
     staffing shortage with respect to a designated swim location, 
     the Secretary may seek to enter into an agreement with one or 
     more local government agencies to enable lifeguards employed 
     by those local government agencies to--
       (1) adequately staff such designated swim location during 
     normal seasonal operating hours; and
       (2) ensure visitor safety and provide rescue and first aid 
     services at the designated swim location as necessary.
       (b) Reimbursement.--In entering into an agreement under 
     subsection (a), the Secretary shall ensure that the local 
     government agency providing the lifeguard services is 
     reimbursed for all reasonable costs incurred by the local 
     government agency in carrying out the agreement.
       (c) Definitions.--In this section:
       (1) Designated swim location.--The term ``designated swim 
     location'' means a swimming area or swimming beach--
       (A) on lands and waters managed by the National Park 
     Service, the United States Fish and Wildlife Service, the 
     Bureau of Land Management, or the Bureau of Reclamation; and
       (B) that is typically monitored by federally employed 
     lifeguards during normal seasonal operating hours.
       (2) Staffing shortage.--
       (A) In general.--The term ``staffing shortage'' means a 
     level of staffing that negatively impacts the ability to 
     adequately staff a designated swim location with federally 
     employed lifeguards and will likely result or has resulted in 
     a designated swim location being left unmonitored by 
     federally employed lifeguards during normal seasonal 
     operating hours.
       (B) Exclusion.--The term ``staffing shortage'' does not 
     refer to short-term staffing shortages caused by temporary 
     absences, such as an employee taking annual or sick leave.
       (3) Secretary.--The term ``Secretary'' means the Secretary 
     of the Interior.

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise in support of H.R. 5063, the Safe Beaches, Safe 
Swimmers Act, which is championed by Representative Jen Kiggans of 
Virginia.
  Each year, millions of Americans visit our Federal lands to spend 
time outdoors with their families. Whether they are hiking a trail, 
camping, fishing, or spending a day at the beach, visitors should be 
able to safely enjoy the incredible recreation opportunities our public 
lands provide.

[[Page H5564]]

  At federally managed beaches and swimming areas, lifeguards play an 
important role in keeping visitors safe, but when Federal agencies 
experience lifeguard staffing shortages, they do not always have the 
flexibility to quickly fill those gaps, even when trained local 
lifeguards may be available to help.
  Representative Kiggans' bill provides a commonsense solution. Her 
legislation allows the National Park Service, U.S. Fish and Wildlife 
Service, Bureau of Land Management, and Bureau of Reclamation to 
partner with local government agencies to provide lifeguard services 
when Federal lifeguards are in short supply at designated swimming 
areas.
  Under these agreements, local lifeguards can provide services 
visitors depend on, including water rescue and first aid, during the 
peak summer season.
  Representative Kiggans' legislation recognizes that the Federal 
Government cannot always solve staffing challenges quickly. When 
qualified local partners are willing and able to help keep visitors 
safe, Federal land managers should have the flexibility to work with 
them.
  These partnerships can help maintain lifeguard coverage, support 
continued public access, and make sure families can enjoy some of our 
country's most popular outdoor recreational activities.
  Mr. Speaker, I again thank Representative Kiggans for her leadership 
on this legislation introduced after her constituents highlighted 
lifeguard shortages that limited access and impacted safety at 
Assateague Island National Seashore. She worked diligently with her 
local community and National Park Service leadership to find a solution 
that will not only help keep her constituents and visitors safe but 
also ensure millions of visitors can continue to enjoy Virginia's 
pristine and world-class beaches.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support H.R. 5063, and I reserve 
the balance of my time.
  Ms. HOYLE of Oregon. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, H.R. 5063 would direct the Secretary of the Interior to 
address lifeguard staffing shortages at Federal beaches by entering 
into agreements with local governments to ensure continuity of staff 
and proper lifeguard coverage.
  This measure would apply to beaches managed by the National Park 
Service, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, the Bureau of Land 
Management, and the Bureau of Reclamation.
  Under these agreements, State and local governments are authorized to 
hire lifeguards to cover shifts at Federal beaches.
  Lifeguards perform vital public safety services. While this bill 
provides a mechanism to prevent empty chairs, relying on local 
municipalities should not become a permanent Band-Aid for understaffed 
Federal agencies. We can't lose sight of why, one of the key reasons 
why, we lack enough lifeguards in the first place: the DOGE effort 
dedicated to firing park workers, implementing hiring freezes, and 
gutting our Federal agencies.
  Last summer, fewer than half of the 7,700 seasonal National Park 
Service positions, including lifeguards, were filled. These cuts forced 
Federal managers to scramble, leaving communities to step in and fund 
these critical positions--also putting lives in danger.
  As a reminder, the National Park Service has lost nearly 25 percent 
of its permanent workforce since the beginning of 2025 due to this 
administration's firing of park staff and slashing agency budgets.
  While local agreements offer emergency relief--and I am very much in 
support of this bill--a real priority should be rebuilding a fully 
funded, fully staffed Federal workforce that is focused on efficiency 
and getting the job done while we are good stewards of taxpayer 
dollars.
  Mr. Speaker, I reserve the balance of my time.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield 2 minutes to the gentlewoman from 
Virginia (Mrs. Kiggans), the lead sponsor of this bill.
  Mrs. KIGGANS of Virginia. Mr. Speaker, I rise today in support of my 
bill, H.R. 5063, the Safe Beaches, Safe Swimmers Act.
  I have the privilege of representing a district that lives on the 
beach and the bay. For families across coastal Virginia and the 
millions of Americans who visit our Federal beaches every year, water 
safety is incredibly important.

  Last year, we saw firsthand what can happen when the Federal 
Government fails to adequately staff these Federal beaches.
  Following a Federal hiring freeze, Assateague Island National 
Seashore was unable to provide its normal lifeguard coverage at the 
Federal beach in Chincoteague, Virginia. Families continued visiting 
and swimming, but the lifeguards they had come to rely on were not 
there.
  Our local leaders stepped up. The town of Chincoteague and Accomack 
County took on unforeseen costs to make sure lifeguards returned to the 
beach and families could safely enjoy the water.
  Our local governments should never be left scrambling to solve a 
Federal staffing problem on their own. That is why I introduced the 
Safe Beaches, Safe Swimmers Act.
  My bill provides a commonsense solution. When the Department of the 
Interior determines that a Federal swimming area is facing a lifeguard 
staffing shortage, it can partner with local governments to provide the 
necessary coverage. Importantly, when a local government steps up to 
provide those lifeguards, the Federal Government will reimburse it for 
the reasonable costs it incurs.
  This applies to designated swimming areas managed by the National 
Park Service, Fish and Wildlife Service, Bureau of Land Management, and 
Bureau of Reclamation that are normally monitored by Federal 
lifeguards.
  Mr. Speaker, this bill is about public safety, accountability, and 
giving our communities a solution when Washington falls short.
  I am grateful to the local leaders on the Eastern Shore who worked 
with us after seeing this problem firsthand. I am proud that we have an 
opportunity to make sure communities across the country have another 
tool to keep their beaches safe.
  No family visiting a Federal beach should have to wonder whether a 
lifeguard will be there if and when an emergency happens.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support the Safe Beaches, Safe 
Swimmers Act.
```
