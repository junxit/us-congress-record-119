# TRANSFER OF ADMINISTRATIVE JURISDICTION OVER CERTAIN FEDERAL LAND IN THE STATE OF CALIFORNIA

**Speaking:** Westerman, Bruce (W000821) [R-AR], Hoyle, Val T. (H001094) [D-OR], McClintock, Tom (M001177) [R-CA]

**Measures:** H.R. 8454

```
 TRANSFER OF ADMINISTRATIVE JURISDICTION OVER CERTAIN FEDERAL LAND IN 
                        THE STATE OF CALIFORNIA

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and pass the 
bill (H.R. 8454) to provide for the transfer of administrative 
jurisdiction over certain Federal land in the State of California, and 
for other purposes, as amended.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 8454

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. TRANSFER OF ADMINISTRATIVE JURISDICTION OVER 
                   CERTAIN FEDERAL LAND IN THE STATE OF 
                   CALIFORNIA.

       (a) Transfer of Administrative Jurisdiction.--
       (1) National forest system land.--Administrative 
     jurisdiction over the approximately 160 acres of National 
     Forest System land more particularly described as T.1 S., 
     R.19 E., sec. 24, SE\1/4\NE\1/4\, NW\1/4\SE\1/4\, NE\1/
     4\SE\1/4\, and SE\1/4\SE\1/4\, Mount Diablo Meridian, 
     Tuolumne County, California, as generally depicted on the map 
     entitled ``Ackerson Meadow Land Interchange'' and dated 
     February 24, 2022, is transferred to the Secretary of the 
     Interior to be managed as part of Yosemite National Park, in 
     accordance with laws applicable to the National Park System.
       (2) National park system land.--Administrative jurisdiction 
     over the approximately 170 acres of National Park System land 
     more particularly described as the SE\1/4\ of sec. 23 and the 
     land to the north and west of Road 1S25 within the NW\1/
     4\SE\1/4\NW\1/4\ of sec. 24, T.1 S., R. 19 E., Mount Diablo 
     Meridian, Tuolumne County, California, as generally depicted 
     on the map entitled ``Ackerson Meadow Land Interchange'' and 
     dated February 24, 2022, is transferred to the Secretary of 
     Agriculture to be managed as part of Stanislaus National 
     Forest in accordance with laws applicable to the National 
     Forest System.
       (b) Corrections.--
       (1) Minor adjustments.--The Secretary of Agriculture and 
     the Secretary of the Interior may, by mutual agreement, make 
     minor corrections and adjustments to the Federal land 
     transferred under subsection (a) to facilitate land 
     management, including making a correction or adjustment to 
     any applicable survey.
       (2) Publications.--Any correction or adjustment made under 
     paragraph (1) shall be effective on the date of publication 
     of a notice of the correction or adjustment in the Federal 
     Register.
       (c) Effect on Existing Rights and Authorizations.--Nothing 
     in this section affects--
       (1) any valid existing rights; or
       (2) the validity or terms and conditions of any existing 
     withdrawal, right-of-way, easement, lease, license, or permit 
     on the land to which administrative jurisdiction is 
     transferred under this section, except that beginning on the 
     date of enactment of this Act, the head of the agency to 
     which administrative jurisdiction over the land is 
     transferred shall be responsible for administering the 
     interests or authorizations in accordance with applicable 
     law.

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I ask unanimous consent that all Members 
may have 5 legislative days to revise and extend their remarks and

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise in support of H.R. 8454, introduced by 
Representative McClintock, which executes a commonsense boundary 
adjustment between Yosemite National Park and the Stanislaus National 
Forest in California.
  Ackerson Meadow supports important wildlife habitat, outdoor 
recreation, and livestock grazing by the Erickson family, whose 
ranching history in the area dates to the late 1800s.
  A portion of Ackerson Meadow was officially donated and added to 
Yosemite National Park in 2016, but this inadvertently created a 
boundary that left parcels within the meadow under the administrative 
jurisdiction of two different Federal agencies.
  As a result, the National Park Service and Forest Service manage 
adjoining parcels within the same meadow, complicating restoration 
work, grazing operations, and recreational management.
  H.R. 8454 provides a straightforward solution. The bill transfers 
administrative jurisdiction over approximately 160 acres of land 
currently managed by the Forest Service to the National Park Service to 
be managed as part of Yosemite National Park.

                              {time}  1620

  In return, the Forest Service will assume jurisdiction over 170 acres 
of land currently managed by the National Park Service.
  This is not a Federal land acquisition, and it does not increase the 
size of the Federal estate. Instead, this bipartisan and bicameral 
legislation adjusts which Federal agency manages each parcel so map 
lines don't interfere with important restoration work on the ground.
  By placing these lands under the agency best suited to manage them, 
this legislation will improve coordination, support ongoing restoration 
work, and enhance the efficiency of grazing operations, while 
simplifying the management of recreation opportunities within Ackerson 
Meadow.
  Mr. Speaker, I thank Representative McClintock for his leadership on 
the legislation. I urge my colleagues to support H.R. 8454, and I 
reserve the balance of my time.
  Ms. HOYLE of Oregon. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, H.R. 8454 would authorize a land exchange between 
Yosemite National Park and Stanislaus National Forest in California.
  This is a straightforward exchange that will transfer 160 acres of 
National Forest System land to the National Park Service, and 
approximately 170 acres of National Park System land that will be 
transferred to the Forest Service.
  The purpose of this legislation is simply to make Federal land 
management more efficient. This is needed because the Service recently 
added a parcel known as the Ackerson Meadow to Yosemite National Park.
  Ackerson Meadow has significant scenic value and provides key 
habitats for endangered species, such as the great grey owl and the 
little willow flycatcher.

[[Page H5568]]

  Adding this land to Yosemite was a win-win. However, while much of 
the Ackerson Meadow meets the criteria for inclusion in the National 
Park, it is largely surrounded by the Stanislaus National Forest, and 
there is a conflict with existing uses. This includes one of the 
longest continuously running cattle drives in California, which is 
inconsistent with Park Service laws and regulations.
  H.R. 8454 is the result of negotiation and compromise, and I thank 
the bill's sponsor and Chair Westerman for that work. It will result in 
more efficient land management and noteworthy expansion of Yosemite 
National Park.
  This is exactly the kind of work that Congress is expected to do. It 
stands in clear contrast to the recent proposal to sell off a piece of 
Yosemite to a private developer. We should be working together to 
increase conservation and improve the management of our public lands, 
not trying to cut deals and exclusive access for millionaires.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support H.R. 8454, and I reserve 
the balance of my time.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield such time as he may consume to 
the gentleman from California (Mr. McClintock), the lead sponsor of the 
bill.
  Mr. McCLINTOCK. Mr. Speaker, I thank the gentleman from Arkansas and 
the gentlewoman from Oregon for their bipartisan support of this 
measure, and I want to assure the gentlewoman from Oregon that the land 
exchange that she had suggested in Yosemite will not happen.
  The Ackerson Meadow Land Interchange Act is a simple proposal to 
transfer administrative jurisdiction over approximately 160 acres of 
national forestland to Yosemite National Park, and then it would 
transfer approximately 170 acres of National Park System land to be a 
part of the Stanislaus National Forest.
  Senator Padilla is carrying identical legislation in the Senate. We 
don't agree on much, but we certainly agree on this.
  This legislation addresses a familiar problem in Federal land 
management in the Sierra Nevada, the checkerboard boundary lines that 
make land management disjointed and inefficient. This bill simply 
consolidates land jurisdictions between the National Park System and 
the National Forest System, without increasing the Federal footprint.
  At issue is Ackerson Meadow. This meadow supports livestock grazing 
operations that have been part of the history and economy of this 
region for generations. These operations are essential for brush 
control and fire suppression, maintaining meadow conditions, and 
sustaining the rural economy that depends on the productive use of 
these lands.
  Federal agencies, conservation organizations, and local ranchers have 
all worked together to improve the meadow while maintaining grazing 
opportunities.
  Restoration projects have improved wetland conditions, enhanced water 
storage, improved habitat, and supported continued public uses, 
including grazing.
  The proposed land exchange would make that cooperative approach 
easier by eliminating the confusion caused by split jurisdiction. 
Ranchers need to know where their permits apply, where infrastructure 
can be maintained, and who is responsible for decisions affecting their 
operations.
  The bill also protects existing rights and authorizations. It 
specifically provides that valid existing rights, including rights-of-
way, easements, leases, licenses, and permits remain protected after 
the transfer. This ensures that current users, including grazing permit 
holders, are not unfairly disrupted by a change in administrative 
jurisdiction.
  It also appropriately maintains Federal responsibilities for 
hazardous substance cleanup and other obligations.
  This bill reflects a principle that should guide Federal land policy. 
Management decisions should be based on the realities of the landscape 
and not simply of the boundaries drawn on a map decades ago.
  Our national forests and parks serve many purposes. They preserve 
wildlife habitat, protect watersheds, provide recreation, and support 
rural economies. A balanced approach recognizes all of these values.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge adoption of H.R. 8454.
  Ms. HOYLE of Oregon. Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support 
this good legislation, and I yield back the balance of my time.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I once again thank Representative 
McClintock for his leadership on this legislation. I urge my colleagues 
to vote ``yes'' on H.R. 8454, and I yield back the balance of my time.
```
