# RECOGNIZING TODD FAMILY FARM

**Speaking:** Young, Todd (Y000064) [R-IN]

```
                      RECOGNIZING TODD FAMILY FARM

  Mr. YOUNG. Mr. President, I rise today to recognize a living Hoosier 
landmark, Todd Family Farm.
  For 150 years, seven generations of a single family have stewarded 
the same soil in Carroll County, IN. The Todd family first came to 
Carroll County as Baptist ministers and, in 1876, established their 
farm on what grew to be 4,500 acres of land. Subsequent generations of 
the family founded the Todd Hybrid Seed Company. From their homestead 
in Indiana, the Todds developed and distributed seed corn across 
America, strengthening the Nation's food supply and playing a key role 
in Indiana's agricultural economy.
  It is very rare for family-run farms to endure for a century, let 
alone 150 years. This reflects the incredible and often difficult work 
of the Todd family across generations. Over that century and a half, 
the Todd family farm has survived transfers from one generation to the 
next, economic panics, a Great Depression, revolutions in agricultural 
technology, and, of course, fluctuations in commodity prices. Across 
those generations, members of the Todd family have also demonstrated a 
deep commitment to service, making sacrifices for their community, 
State, and country.
  This endurance reflects a spirit of hard work and resourcefulness, as 
well as a commitment to community. This tradition began when the Todds 
first reached Carroll County and continues to the present day. Their 
contributions to Indiana, our Nation, and future generations represent 
a legacy worthy of recognition. Congratulations to the Todd family for 
earning the Hoosier Homestead Program's Sesquicentennial Award.
```
