# HONORING MR. W. MILLER BARBOUR

**Speaking:** Perry, Scott (P000605) [R-PA]

```
                     HONORING MR. W. MILLER BARBOUR

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. SCOTT PERRY

                            of pennsylvania

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 14, 2026

  Mr. PERRY. Mr. Speaker, I rise to honor and recognize civil- and 
human-rights leader W. Miller Barbour, and commemorate his legacy and 
contributions to our Nation.
  While born in Philadelphia in 1908, Mr. Barbour was raised in 
Middletown, Pennsylvania. Very active in his local church, he witnessed 
barriers to opportunity in his community that shaped his commitment to 
equality and social work.
  Once graduating from Middletown High School, Mr. Barbour attended 
Elizabethtown College, where he became only the second African American 
graduate in 1932, and was the first known African American student-
athlete to play college football in Lancaster County.
  After college, Mr. Barbour pursued a career in social work, focusing 
on the effects of poverty and employment opportunities. He worked 
directly with young people at Philadelphia's Wharton Center, where he 
helped address juvenile delinquency and gang violence by promoting 
mentorship and education. His work was an expression of his belief 
that, in order to fix social issues, we must first address the poverty 
and neighborhood environments that lead to them.
  Mr. Barbour later earned a Masters degree in Social Work from the 
esteemed University of Pennsylvania. He joined the National Urban 
League in 1947, becoming Executive Secretary of its Denver affiliate 
branch, wherein he was a passionate advocate for fair employment and 
equal economic opportunity, and worked to break down barriers 
preventing African Americans from participating fully in American life. 
In 1952, he became Director of the National Urban League's Western 
Field Office in Los Angeles, where he continued his work fighting for 
meaningful civil rights reform. In this role, he commissioned a 1952 
study analyzing racial relationships and socioeconomic conditions in 
Richmond, California. He also co-authored The Prospect for Freedom: 
Human Rights, What Can be Done on the Local Level in 1951, which 
emphasized the responsibility of local communities to build equal 
institutions.
  Mr. Barbour sadly passed away unexpectedly in March 1957. His legacy 
has endured throughout our communities and Nation. Dauphin County 
recently proclaimed every July 16th as ``W. Miller Barbour Day,'' and 
his life also has been recognized by the Pennsylvania State 
Legislature.
  Mr. Speaker, I am humbled and honored to recognize Mr. W. Miller 
Barbour for his selfless and outstanding contributions to the Civil 
Rights movement, and for his enduring dedication to our local 
communities, Commonwealth, and our great Nation. His service, 
leadership, and tireless devotion set the standard by which all should 
be judged.
```
