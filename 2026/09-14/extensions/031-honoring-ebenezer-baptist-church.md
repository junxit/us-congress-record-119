# HONORING EBENEZER BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                    HONORING EBENEZER BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 14, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to acknowledge Ebenezer Baptist 
Church of South Baton Rouge, Louisiana, a congregation whose beginnings 
date back to November 12, 1926, when a group of devout Christians met 
at the home of Sister Louvenia Green to establish a place of worship. 
The late Reverend Johnny Banks, one of the organizers, was elected as 
the church's first pastor. Ebenezer Baptist Church was formally 
incorporated on July 30, 1931, and Reverend Banks served faithfully 
until his passing in 1960. Leadership continued under Reverend Johnny 
Cyrus, who served until his death in that same year, followed briefly 
by Reverend Joseph Martin, assistant pastor, until the election of 
Reverend John H. Thomas, II, who guided the church forward until July 
1971.
  In August 1971, Reverend Q.T. Ellis was elected pastor, with Reverend 
Lemmie Washington serving as assistant pastor. Following Reverend 
Washington's death, Reverend Alex Banks assumed the role of assistant 
pastor. Under Reverend Ellis's leadership, the church progressed 
spiritually and structurally. In 1985, a major addition was completed 
including a pastor's study, secretary's office, dressing rooms, 
restrooms, a dining and kitchen facility, and expanded parking. After 
Reverend Ellis's resignation in May 1988, Reverend Banks served briefly 
as interim pastor until Reverend Ellis was re-elected in March 1989. 
Weekly Bible study was instituted as a core part of Ebenezer's 
spiritual development. Reverend Banks later became pastor of Christ 
Land Baptist Church, and Reverend William Armstead was elected 
assistant pastor.
  On May 7, 2000, Reverend Q.T. Ellis became Pastor Emeritus, and 
Reverend Ronald A. Sutton was elected pastor. Under Reverend Sutton's 
leadership, the church developed its motto, mission, and vision 
statement, and the membership increased through Baptism and Christian 
Experience. In 2002, the church's name was changed to Ebenezer Baptist 
Church of South Baton Rouge. Reverend Sutton proposed remodeling the 
sanctuary, and construction began in January 2004, resulting in a new 
sanctuary, pews, organ, electronic piano, updated sound system, and 
additional parking. During construction, Greater Mt. Gideon Baptist 
Church and Greater Pleasant Green Baptist Church graciously opened 
their doors for worship and Bible study. By June 12, 2004, Ebenezer 
returned to its newly renovated home. In subsequent years, properties 
were purchased and improved along Carolina Street and Missouri Street 
as part of continued campus development.
  Mr. Speaker, Ebenezer Baptist Church of South Baton Rouge continues 
to stand as a pillar of service, faith, and community engagement. In 
January 2025, the church implemented its `Community Conversation' 
series addressing gun violence, education, domestic violence, voting, 
and economic development. In April and May 2025, Ebenezer hosted COPE 
training to strengthen community empathy and engagement. In January 
2026, the church hosted a three-month Community Trauma Awareness Summit 
addressing mental health, stress, anxiety, depression, and suicide. On 
November 15, 2026, Ebenezer Baptist Church will celebrate its 100th 
Centennial Anniversary--giving honor to God for the abundant spiritual 
and financial blessings bestowed upon the congregation. I ask my 
colleagues to join me in recognizing Ebenezer Baptist Church of South 
Baton Rouge for a century of faith, resilience, and devotion to 
carrying out the purpose of the Church as the light of the world and 
the salt of the earth.
```
