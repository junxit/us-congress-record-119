# HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF RAY GAESSER

**Speaking:** Nunn, Zachary (N000193) [R-IA]

```
              HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF RAY GAESSER

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. ZACHARY NUNN

                                of iowa

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 14, 2026

  Mr. NUNN of Iowa. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life and 
legacy of Ray Gaesser of Corning, Iowa, who passed away tragically in a 
farm accident on September 5, 2026, at the age of 73. His loss is felt 
by those who loved him, and Iowa mourns alongside the entire Gaesser 
family.
  Ray grew up on a small farm in southern Indiana, where he began 
farming at just 15 years old, taking over the family operation after 
the death of his father. In 1977, he and his wife, Elaine, began 
farming near Corning with a few hundred acres, and over the decades 
they built it into a family operation of more than 5,000 acres.
  Ray was a devoted family man, a trusted friend, and a leading voice 
for Iowa agriculture. He believed farming was a privilege, and he 
treated the land as something to be cared for, strengthened, and handed 
down to the next generation to carry on Iowa's oldest tradition.
  Ray's impact on Iowa agriculture reached far beyond his own fields. 
He was president of the Iowa Soybean Association in 2007 and, in

[[Page E905]]

2014, became president of the American Soybean Association, carrying 
the priorities of Iowa's farmers on trade missions around the world. He 
co-founded Solutions from the Land and led the Iowa Conservative Energy 
Forum, championing conservation and homegrown energy. His service 
extended beyond agriculture as well. He was appointed to the Iowa 
Department of Transportation Commission in 2022 and chaired it until 
this past July. Ray often said that ``when we work together, we all 
benefit.'' He lived by that motto, and Iowa agriculture is stronger 
because of it.
  I had the privilege of calling Ray a close friend, and time and again 
I saw that commitment up close. Whenever I had a question about 
agriculture, Ray was my first call. He had a lifetime of knowledge and 
never hesitated to share it. If I needed his counsel, Ray would drop 
what he was doing, drive to Des Moines, sit down with me, and talk 
through how we could deliver for Iowa's farmers. That is one of the 
things I will miss most about him.
  Ray and Elaine were in my corner from the very beginning, but more 
importantly, they were always in Iowa's corner. From his glass-topped 
silo looking over his slice of Heaven on the Gaesser farm, to leading 
delegations around the world, Ray brought an unmatched knowledge of 
agriculture and a genuine friendship wherever he went. Oftentimes, just 
his smile and a good farm story were enough to forge a new partnership, 
build trust in Iowa, and strengthen a legacy of service he spent a 
lifetime creating.
  Ray leaves behind his wife, Elaine; their children, Chris and 
Jennifer; and a legacy that will endure in the land he cared for and 
the farmers he spent his life fighting for.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring the life and 
legacy of Ray Gaesser: a devoted farmer, a national voice for American 
agriculture, and a faithful friend to all who worked the land.
```
