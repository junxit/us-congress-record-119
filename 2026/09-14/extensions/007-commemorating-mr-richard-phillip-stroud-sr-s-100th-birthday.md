# COMMEMORATING MR. RICHARD PHILLIP STROUD SR.'S 100TH BIRTHDAY

**Speaking:** Loudermilk, Barry (L000583) [R-GA]

```
     COMMEMORATING MR. RICHARD PHILLIP STROUD SR.'S 100TH BIRTHDAY

                                 ______
                                 

                         HON. BARRY LOUDERMILK

                               of georgia

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 14, 2026

  Mr. LOUDERMILK. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to commemorate and honor 
Mr. Richard Philip Stroud, Sr., of Georgia, as he calebrated his 100th 
birthday on September 9, 2026.
  It is without question that Mr. Stroud has led an extraordinary life, 
one that is a testament to service, hard work, profound Christian 
faith, and devotion to family. Over the course of a century, he has 
answered his Nation's call in the United States Navy, contributed to 
some of our Nation's most important aviation programs, raised a family, 
and built a multi-generational legacy.
  Richard was born on September 9, 1926, on a farm in Bonham, Texas, 
the eldest son of Coy and Lucille Stroud. His love and passion for the 
skies began when, as a boy, a Piper Cub airplane landed near his home. 
After some flight instruction, he made his first solo flight. That 
momentous occasion would set the course for the rest of his life.
  In 1944, with World War II raging across the globe, Richard joined 
the United States Navy, standing alongside thousands of other 
servicemembers prepared to defend our country and the freedoms we hold 
dear. At only 17 years old, Richard qualified for the Navy's flight 
training program. With the changing needs of the war effort, however, 
he was instead trained as a machine gunner for the SB2C Helldiver 
bomber. Sent to Memphis, Tennessee for training, Richard distinguished 
himself immediately, finishing first in his class and becoming the 
first sailor in his class to strike all 300 clay targets during his 
final shooting test.
  After the Allied victory, Richard continued his naval training, 
graduating from flight school and earning his wings as a naval aviator 
in Corpus Christi, Texas. In June of 1950, when war broke out in the 
Korean Peninsula, Richard once more answered his Nation's call, serving 
on an aircraft carrier, participating in patrol operations, and flying 
numerous missions for more than two years. He accumulated over 500 
landings, including one during a Category-4 hurricane, and piloted 
aircraft, such as the SNJ Texan, the UC-45 Expeditor, the R4D Skytrain, 
R6D Liftmaster, F6F Hellcat, F4U Corsair, and the TBM Avenger.

[[Page E901]]

  After seven years of active duty, Richard served in the United States 
Naval Reserve for another ten years. In 1952, he began what would be a 
62-year career as an aeronautical engineer with Lockheed Aircraft 
Company in Marietta, Georgia, serving first as a senior design engineer 
and later as supervisor of the check group, where he oversaw 296 
checkers and 40 secretaries, at the height of the C-5 program.
  Over the course of his career, Richard contributed to aircraft 
programs including the B-29 Superfortress, B-47 Stratojet, C-130 
Hercules, C-141 Starlifter, C-5, P-3, S-3, F-22, and F-35. His work 
helped sustain American military aviation across the world. Richard 
retired in 2015 at the age of 88, completing a career that stretched 
from the aircraft of the Second World War era to some of the most 
advanced military aircraft yet produced.
  Richard's legacy extends far beyond his military and professional 
accomplishments. In 1950, he married Dorothy Montgomery Larkins, with 
whom he raised four children, Phil, Ann, Mark, and Dana. After settling 
in Marietta, Georgia, the family became deeply involved at Roswell 
Street Baptist Church, where their Christian faith remained central to 
their family life.
  Following Dorothy's passing in 1971, Richard married Letha and 
welcomed her four children, Sharon, Andy, Myra, and Penny, into his 
family. In the decades since, his family has grown to include 
grandchildren, great-grandchildren, and great-great-grandchildren.
  Mr. Speaker, a 100 birthday is itself an extraordinary milestone, but 
in Richard Stroud's case, it serves as an example of a life lived in 
the service of others. From a young boy on a Texas farm fascinated by 
the sight of an airplane, to a sailor and naval aviator serving his 
country in times of war, to an aeronautical engineer whose life's work 
propelled innovation in American aviation for more than 60 years, 
Richard's life embodies the very best of what it is to be an American.
  It is therefore my great privilege to recognize Mr. Richard Philip 
Stroud, Sr. on the occasion of his 100th birthday. I join his family, 
his friends, and all those whose lives he has touched, in celebrating 
this remarkable milestone and I thank him for a lifetime of faithful 
service to his family, his community, and the United States of America.
  May God continue to bless Richard and his family, and may his example 
of faith, duty, and perseverance continue to inspire generations.
```
