# HONORING FAITH CHAPEL CHURCH OF GOD

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                  HONORING FAITH CHAPEL CHURCH OF GOD

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 14, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Faith Chapel Church of 
God, located at 995 Staring Lane in Baton Rouge, Louisiana, a steadfast 
congregation serving East Baton Rouge Parish since 1948. Faith Chapel's 
beginnings trace back to a letter sent by Rev. Rose Williams of Baton 
Rouge to Rev. George W. Burns, then pastor of Caffin Avenue Church of 
God in New Orleans. Rev. Williams' concern for a ``colored church'' 
affiliated with the National Church of God Fellowship (Anderson, 
Indiana) led Rev. Burns to establish prayer meetings at the home of 
Sis. Pearl Davis, near the current church site. These gatherings soon 
blossomed into Sunday School services and the formation of Faith Chapel 
Church of God.
  Faith Chapel has distinguished itself with a rich history of 
children's ministry, notably transporting children, including those 
from the Blundon Home orphanage, to Sunday School since its inception. 
The church's first pastor, Rev. Charles Wilson, III (1949 to 1952), 
played a vital role in recruiting and transporting these children, a 
tradition that continued through the mid-sixties. Over the years, the 
Lord has blessed Faith Chapel with godly leaders, including Rev. Rose 
B. Williams and the late Rev. Dr. J.D. Brown, who served the longest of 
all senior pastors. During Dr. Brown's 35-year pastorate, the church 
eliminated its mortgage, erected an educational facility, and saw five 
of its members ordained to Christian ministry, with a sixth, Rev. 
Chibuike Azuoru, ordained and supported in establishing Christ the King 
Christian Center in Baton Rouge.
  Dr. Brown also mentored numerous young preachers and led a prison 
ministry at Angola, leaving a lasting impact on prisoners and their 
families. Throughout his ministry, Reverend Rosella T. Williams 
faithfully served as Assistant Pastor, and though retired, she 
continues to support Faith Chapel through prayer ministry and mentoring 
young ministers. Today, Rev. Dr. Bartholomew M. Riggins, affectionately 
known as ``Pastor Bart,'' leads Faith Chapel, having been installed in 
2011. Under Pastor Bart's leadership, the church has flourished, 
expanding its ministries and daily operations, resulting in spiritual 
and numerical growth, and fostering partnerships with area churches, 
hospitals, and government organizations.
  Faith Chapel has been recognized as a leader in bringing healing and 
wholeness to the Gardere community, notably through its involvement in 
The Gardere Initiative. This partnership addresses substance abuse and 
other challenges impacting local families and neighborhoods. The 
church's ministries include neighborhood prayer walks, collegiate 
outreach, children's programs, marriage and singles ministries, men's 
and women's fellowship, tutoring, scholarship support for college 
students, bus ministry, breakfast ministry, and a growing electronic 
and social media presence.
  Currently, Faith Chapel is engaged in a building campaign to 
construct a much-needed multi-purpose facility to meet its expanding 
ministry needs and allow for continued growth. The first phase of this 
building project is nearing completion, marking another milestone in 
Faith Chapel's enduring legacy of faith, service, and leadership in 
Baton Rouge.
  Mr. Speaker, I encourage my colleagues to join me in celebrating 
Faith Chapel Church of God, Pastor Bart Riggins, and the devoted 
congregation for nearly eight decades of remarkable service that 
uplifts and inspires the Baton Rouge community.
```
