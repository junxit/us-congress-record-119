# HONORING MS. JOHNNIE BROOKS BOOKER ON THE OCCASION OF HER 85TH BIRTHDAY

**Speaking:** Bishop, Sanford D. (B000490) [D-GA]

```
HONORING MS. JOHNNIE BROOKS BOOKER ON THE OCCASION OF HER 85TH BIRTHDAY

                                 ______
                                 

                      HON. SANFORD D. BISHOP, JR.

                               of georgia

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 14, 2026

  Mr. BISHOP. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor a remarkable daughter 
of the State of Georgia, Ms. Johnnie Brooks Booker of Atlanta, on the 
occasion of her 85th birthday. A celebration of her birthday was held 
on Saturday, September 12, 2026, at the Whitley Hotel in Atlanta, 
Georgia.
  Mr. Speaker, Johnnie Brooks was born on July 31, 1941, in Forsyth, 
Georgia, to Willie Floyd Brooks and Lillian Goodwyne Brooks Graves. I 
would ask this House to sit with that date for a moment. Nineteen 
forty-one. In Georgia. A Black girl, born in a small middle Georgia 
town, into a state that had already written her ceiling before she drew 
her first breath--a state where she could not have been served at a 
lunch counter in her own hometown, could not have used the front door 
of most buildings on Main Street, and could not have expected the 
country of her birth to make room for her. Nobody asked that child what 
she intended to do about it. She told them anyway.
  She went to Hampton Institute in Virginia and earned her Bachelor of 
Science degree in social sciences and sociology. She went on to the 
Atlanta University School of Social Work--now Clark Atlanta 
University--and earned her Master of Social Work in community 
organization. And then, Mr. Speaker, she went to work, and she did not 
stop for sixty years.
  She began with the New York Department of Welfare. She taught in the 
Manpower Development and Training Program in Washington, D.C. and 
joined the D.C. Department of Rehabilitation in 1964. She served with 
the Neighborhood Youth Corps. She worked with the Los Angeles Community 
Redevelopment Agency, then the Western Regional Office of the National 
Urban League, and then the Urban League's Washington Bureau.
  She entered the federal service at the Federal Home Loan Bank Board 
as Director of Consumer Affairs and Civil Rights. In 1989 she was 
appointed Deputy Assistant Secretary at the United States Department of 
Housing and Urban Development. In 1991, she joined the Resolution Trust 
Corporation as Vice President of the Division of Minority and Women`s 
Programs. When that agency closed its doors, she moved to the Federal 
Deposit Insurance Corporation as Director of the Office of Equal 
Opportunity, where she served until her retirement in 1996.
  Mr. Speaker, I want my colleagues to notice something about that 
list. Consumer Affairs and Civil Rights. Minority and women's programs. 
Equal Opportunity. Community Organization. Look at every title she ever 
held. That woman spent her entire working life standing in doorways and 
holding them open for people who were never supposed to get through.
  Mr. Speaker, the late Shirley Chisholm sat in this very House, and 
she left behind a line that the women who came after her have carried 
like a lantern: ``if they don't give you a seat at the table, bring a 
folding chair.''
  Johnnie Brooks Booker heard that. But she was never once satisfied to 
bring a single chair for herself. She brought them by the truckload. 
She set them up around the table. She called in people whose names were 
nowhere on the invitation--and then she taught them how to sit there 
like they belonged, because they did. She was not finished.
  In 2001 she came home to Georgia and became the Global Director of 
Supplier Diversity at The Coca-Cola Company, where she served for a 
decade. In that role she built a mentoring program for minority- and 
women-owned business enterprises because she understood that a contract 
without capacity is a courtesy, and she was never interested in 
courtesies. She wanted those firms to be able to bid the next job, and 
the one after that, without her. Mr. Speaker, in 2007, came the moment 
I want this House to remember.
  When The World of Coca-Cola Museum was built in Atlanta--that great 
glass house where one of the most famous American companies on earth 
tells the story of itself--Johnnie Brooks Booker made certain that 
minority developers and minority contractors were in the room, on the 
plans, and on that job site.
  Think about what that means. A little girl born in Forsyth, Georgia, 
in 1941, who as a child that could not walk into a drugstore in her own 
town and sit down and order a CocaCola--that same woman, 66 years 
later, helped decide who would build the house that Coca-Cola tells its 
story in.
  Mr. Speaker, that is not irony. That is what a life of purpose looks 
like from the far end of it.
  She retired from Coca-Cola in 2012, at an age when most people are 
owed their rest, and promptly founded The Johnnie Booker Group, where 
she serves as President and Chief Executive Officer to this day.
  Her service has extended to the American Society for Public 
Administration, Delta Sigma Theta Sorority, Incorporated, The Links, 
Incorporated, the National Council of Negro Women, the NAACP, the 
National Urban League, the Hampton University Alumni Association, the 
Utopian Literary Club, and Circle-Let's of Atlanta. She has served on 
the boards of the National Minority Supplier Development Council, the 
Women's Business Enterprise National Council, Women in Housing and 
Finance, Women of Washington, the Delta Research and Educational 
Foundation--the philanthropic arm of her beloved sorority--and the Fort 
Valley State University Foundation--an institution in the district I am 
privileged to represent, and one more place where she invested herself 
in young people she would never meet.
  She has been recognized with two Congressional Resolutions of this 
House, named Hampton University Alumna of the Year in 2009, and honored 
with the Community Service Award of the Black Law Students Association 
at Quinnipiac University in 2022. In 2023,

[[Page E911]]

she sat for an oral history with The History Makers--so that her voice 
will still be telling this story long after all of us in this Chamber 
have finished ours.
  Mr. Speaker, she is the mother of S. Courtney Booker, III and the 
grandmother of Dalyn and Aiden. I would say this to those two 
grandchildren: the world will tell them a great many things about what 
they can and cannot become. Their grandmother has already proven every 
one of them wrong. They come from somebody.
  Mr. Speaker, on the 31st of July, Ms. Johnnie Brooks Booker turned 85 
years old. She has lived long enough to see a great deal of what she 
fought for, and she is still at her desk. Georgia gave her to the 
Nation, and she has spent 85 years giving back more than she ever 
received.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join my wife, Vivian, and me, 
along with the more than 765,000 people of Georgia's 2nd Congressional 
District in wishing a happy 85th birthday to Ms. Johnnie Brooks Booker 
of Atlanta, Georgia, in thanking her for a lifetime of extraordinary 
service to this country, and in wishing her continued health, joy, and 
many more years among the people who love her.
```
