# HONORING NAZARENE BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                    HONORING NAZARENE BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 14, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to acknowledge Nazarene Baptist 
Church of Baton Rouge, Louisiana, a congregation whose origins date 
back to 1888 when a small group of Christians organized what would 
become a pillar of faith within the community. For its first five 
years, the congregation met in a cottage on Stone Street and then in 
Pete's Alley before relocating to an area known as `The Lake,' where 
the first building was constructed out of crude materials. The church, 
then called `Need More,' elected Reverend Horace Williams as its first 
pastor. Reverend Williams served faithfully until his passing on 
February 14, 1913. He was succeeded by Reverend W.H. Hall, under whose 
leadership the church purchased land at North 17th Street and Boyd 
Avenue and erected a small red building. In November 1915, Nazarene 
filed its charter with an appointed Board of Trustees.
  In 1921, upon Reverend Hall's resignation, Nazarene elected its third 
pastor, Reverend C.J. Smith. During his pastorate, the church 
constructed a modern frame building and organized its first choir. 
After Reverend Smith's death in 1928, Reverend Sam Lundy ministered to 
the congregation until a new pastor was chosen. In 1930, Reverend F.K. 
Alpine became Nazarene's fifth pastor. Over four years of leadership, 
he organized the first Usher Board and led a spirited revival during 
which he baptized 135 believers in 1933. On November 28, 1934, Reverend 
T.R. Provost was elected as the sixth pastor. During his nineteen years 
of leadership, he oversaw construction of a modern edifice, organized a 
gospel chorus and youth choir, and purchased the church's parsonage. 
Reverend Provost resigned in 1953 to accept a pastorate in Detroit, and 
Reverend William Polk served as interim pastor until 1954.
  On September 9, 1954, Nazarene elected Reverend George R. Clark as 
its seventh pastor. Under his 51 years of leadership, the church 
thrived spiritually and numerically. Reverend Clark organized multiple 
choirs, formed new usher boards, combined the Senior Choir and Gospel 
Chorus into the now-iconic Mass Choir, and modernized church 
facilities. He also oversaw construction of a two-story annex and 
expansion of parking facilities. On September 7, 1991, a devastating 
fire destroyed the church building. Undeterred, the congregation 
rebuilt, and on January 23, 1994, a new sanctuary and educational 
complex were dedicated. A Cornerstone Ceremony followed on April 27, 
1997. After Reverend Clark's passing in May 1996, Nazarene elected Dr. 
Albert Green, Jr. as its eighth pastor in December 1997. Dr. Green 
strengthened the congregation through teaching, preaching, and 
administrative leadership, revived the tutorial program, reestablished 
new member education, formed a liturgical dance team and Women's 
Chorus, and expanded church outreach. Dr. Green served faithfully until 
his passing on June 30, 2014.
  Mr. Speaker, on December 17, 2015, Nazarene Baptist Church elected 
its ninth pastor, Reverend Kelvin C. Brooks, Sr. Reverend Brooks, 
baptized by the late Dr. R.B. House and raised within Nazarene, brought 
decades of service and dedication to his role. Under his leadership, 
the church the church continues to grow spiritually and numerically, 
building upon of 138 years of transition, perseverance, and faith. As 
an active member of the National Baptist Convention, USA, Inc., 
Louisiana Missionary State Convention, Fourth District Missionary 
Baptist Association, and East Baton Rouge Parish Ministers Conference, 
Nazarene remains a vital force in the Baton Rouge community and beyond. 
I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring Nazarene Baptist Church for 
its enduring legacy, its spiritual leadership, and its steadfast 
commitment to advancing the Kingdom of God.
```
