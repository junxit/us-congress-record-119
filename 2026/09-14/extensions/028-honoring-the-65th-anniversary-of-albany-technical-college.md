# HONORING THE 65TH ANNIVERSARY OF ALBANY TECHNICAL COLLEGE

**Speaking:** Bishop, Sanford D. (B000490) [D-GA]

**Measures:** H.R. 1187

```
       HONORING THE 65TH ANNIVERSARY OF ALBANY TECHNICAL COLLEGE

                                 ______
                                 

                      HON. SANFORD D. BISHOP, JR.

                               of georgia

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 14, 2026

  Mr. BISHOP. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Albany Technical 
College of Albany, Georgia, on the occasion of its 65th anniversary.
  Mr. Speaker, in 1961 this institution opened its doors as the Monroe 
Area Vocational Technical School with 175 students. One hundred 
seventy-five. In a segregated South Georgia, in a year when very few 
people in authority believed that the children of sharecroppers and 
mill hands and domestic workers needed anything more than a strong 
back, somebody in Albany, Georgia, decided otherwise.
  The Albany Area Vocational-Technical School followed shortly after, 
and in 1972 the two schools were joined together at the campus the 
college occupies today. In July of 1988, with the formation of what is 
now the Technical College System of Georgia, the school became Albany 
Technical Institute and was charged with serving a seven-county 
region--Baker, Calhoun, Clay, Dougherty, Lee, Randolph, and Terrell--
some of the most rural and most economically challenged counties in the 
United States of America. And on July 6, 2000, having met the standard 
set by the General Assembly in House Bill 1187 to award the associate 
degree, Albany Technical Institute became Albany Technical College.
  Mr. Speaker, 65 years is a long time to keep a promise. It has been 
kept by every person who ever led this institution by President Howard 
Water, by President Wayne Israel, by President Nathaniel Cross, by 
President Alvin Anderson, by President Dr. Anthony O. Parker, and today 
by Dr. Emmett L. Griswold--and by every instructor who ever stood in a 
shop with a clipboard and refused to let a student quit.
  Mr. Speaker, you cannot tell the story of this college without 
telling the story of two men.
  Dr. Anthony O. Parker became the fifth president of Albany Technical 
College in 1995, arriving in a community still digging itself out from 
the great flood of 1994, and he led the rebuilding. He stayed for 27 
years--the longest-serving president in the history of the college and 
the longest-serving president in the entire Technical College System of 
Georgia. He built the enrollment. He built the buildings. He built 
partnerships with the Marine Corps Logistics Base and with industry 
across this region. He was among the first technical college presidents 
in Georgia to raise a million dollars in private money for his campus. 
And he never stopped fighting for adult learners--for the grown man and 
the grown woman who walked in for a GED and walked out with a career.
  Mr. Speaker, in November of 2011, I stood on that campus as Albany 
Tech dedicated its new library and media center in Dr. Parker's honor. 
I read a tribute to him into this very Record that day, and I presented 
him a flag that had flown over this Capitol in his name. He told us 
then why that building meant what it meant to him: his mother had been 
a librarian, and as a boy he was made to stop at the county library and 
study on his way home before he was ever allowed to go outside and 
play. He said that at the time he did not appreciate it. He said that 
he had come to appreciate it very much.
  Mr. Speaker, on the morning of June 6, 2022, Dr. Anthony Parker 
passed away. He was 69 years old. He did not retire. He did not slow 
down. He had plans on the books for the next 5 years. He simply did not 
come to work one morning, and Southwest Georgia has not stopped missing 
him since.
  Here, Mr. Speaker, is the part of this story that I want the House to 
hear.
  In 2004, a man named Emmett Griswold walked through the front door of 
Albany Technical College to teach criminal justice. He had come out of 
law enforcement and corrections--a probation and parole officer for the 
Georgia Department of Juvenile Justice, a deputy warden for the Georgia 
Department of Corrections--a man whose working life had been spent 
among people the world had already given up on. He was a native of 
Milledgeville who had made Albany his home.

[[Page E906]]

He held his bachelor's and master's degrees from Albany State 
University, and he would go on to earn his doctorate from Valdosta 
State.
  Dr. Parker took him under his wing. And Dr. Griswold rose--department 
chairman, dean of academic affairs, vice president for academic 
affairs, executive vice president--every rung of it, at the same 
institution, for 18 years.
  When the college lost its president without warning, Albany Tech did 
not have to look outside for somebody to carry it. He was already 
there. On November 1, 2022, Dr. Emmett L. Griswold became the sixth 
president of Albany Technical College, standing on the foundation laid 
by his mentor and his friend.
  Mr. Speaker, that is what 65 years of an institution actually looks 
like. Not a building. Not a seal. A man who taught, mentored, and 
believed in another man--who then took up the same work and kept the 
doors open for the next one.
  Those doors, Mr. Speaker, have never asked a single student what 
their father did for a living. They have opened for the single mother 
working nights. For the man laid off at 45 who had to start over. For 
the young person nobody expected anything from, who is today a lineman, 
a nurse, a welder, a diesel technician, a logistics specialist--who 
owns a home, pays taxes, and raises children who will never know what 
it was to go without.
  That is the harvest of 65 years in Southwest Georgia.
  Therefore, Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in 
congratulating Albany Technical College on sixty-fiye years of service, 
in commending President Emmett L. Griswold, his faculty, his staff, and 
his Board of Directors, in honoring the memory of Dr. Anthony O. Parker 
and all who led before him, and in wishing this great institution many 
more years of transforming lives across Southwest Georgia.
```
