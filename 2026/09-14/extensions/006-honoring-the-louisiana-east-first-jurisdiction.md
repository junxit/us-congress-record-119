# HONORING THE LOUISIANA EAST FIRST JURISDICTION

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
             HONORING THE LOUISIANA EAST FIRST JURISDICTION

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 14, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the history of the 
Louisiana East First Jurisdiction in Louisiana, a legacy built on 
devotion, perseverance, and the unwavering faith of its founding 
fathers and mothers.
  Louisiana East First Jurisdiction history began in the early 
nineteenth century when Elders E.D. Smith and C. Coleman traveled to 
southeast Mississippi preaching the doctrine of holiness. These 
pioneers were not focused on establishing a church; instead, they 
spread the message of holiness and fostered what became the Holiness 
Movement. Among their converts were brothers Henry Feltus and James 
Feltus, Sr. of Centerville, Mississippi. Though initially opposed, the 
brothers were soon converted and became staunch supporters. The 
acceptance of holiness in Mississippi occurred in 1907.
  In 1910, Elder Henry Feltus began pastoring new converts of holiness 
at a gathering in Baton Rouge called the Railroad Avenue Church. Later 
that year, the founder and General Overseer of the Church of God in 
Christ, Bishop Charles Harrison Mason, journeyed to Baton Rouge and 
preached the doctrine of holiness. After hearing Bishop Mason's 
powerful and anointed teaching, Henry and James Feltus received the 
Baptism of the Holy Ghost.
  However, the Feltus brothers were still serving under Overseer Elder 
E.D. Smith, who taught an erroneous doctrine that saints would never 
die a natural death unless they had sinned in some way. The brothers 
rejected this teaching and invited Bishop Mason to enlighten Elder 
Smith regarding the so-called `never die doctrine.' When Bishop Mason 
came to New Orleans in 1913 to address the doctrine, Elder Smith did 
not receive the correction. Consequently, the Feltus brothers chose to 
follow Bishop Mason. This moment marked the beginning of the Church of 
God in Christ (COGIC) in Louisiana.
  From that point forward, the Church of God in Christ operated as one 
jurisdiction under Elder Henry Feltus. Bishop Mason appointed him State 
Overseer and appointed Mother Eliza Hollins as State Mother. Known for 
her strict discipline and distinctive black attire, Mother Hollins 
strongly influenced women's ministry throughout the jurisdiction.
  As membership grew, Overseer Feltus recognized the need for 
additional leadership. In 1914, he appointed Elders S. Lazard and C. 
Ealy as district superintendents. Overseer Feltus passed away in 1920, 
and Bishop Mason appointed his brother, Elder James Feltus, Sr., as the 
new State Overseer.
  Like his brother, Overseer James Feltus, Sr. founded and developed 
several churches across Louisiana. Under his leadership, many pastors 
were appointed to lead assemblies embracing the Holiness Movement. In 
1938, Overseer Feltus expanded the jurisdiction by creating additional 
districts and appointing Elders John W. White, C.B. Norris, and James 
Edwards as superintendents.
  As the Church grew, administrative challenges increased. Out of 
respect for Overseer Feltus' service, Bishop Mason kept Louisiana as 
one jurisdiction during his lifetime. After his passing in 1946, Bishop 
Mason divided the state into two jurisdictions. Elder Seaners Lazard 
was appointed to lead the churches east of the Mississippi River, while 
Elder John Walter White was appointed to lead the churches west of the 
river.
  During this period, Bishop Mason began conferring the title of 
``Bishop'' upon those leading jurisdictions. Bishop Lazard led the 
Eastern Louisiana Jurisdiction for 21 years until his passing in 1967. 
The brethren overwhelmingly united behind Superintendent Willie K. 
Gordon, Sr., who had tirelessly assisted Bishop Lazard during his 
tenure. Bishop Gordon was highly respected for his generosity and his 
mentorship of many preachers. Under his leadership, the jurisdiction 
grew significantly.
  In 1987, the Eastern Louisiana Jurisdiction was divided into three 
separate jurisdictions: Louisiana East First under Bishop James E. 
Gordon; Louisiana East Second under Bishop Joseph A. Thompson; and 
Louisiana East Third under Bishop Howard E. Quillen, Jr. Later, in June 
2001, a fourth jurisdiction--the Greater New Orleans Jurisdiction--was 
established under Bishop Lindell Benford Brown.
  Bishop James Earl Gordon served the Louisiana East First Jurisdiction 
with profound dignity. He was known for his generosity and loyal 
support of the national church. His capstone achievement was the 
purchase of a new, multi-million dollar jurisdictional headquarters--a 
30-plus acre campus owned by the saints of God. After 27 years of 
service, Bishop Gordon entered the Church Triumphant.
  In 2014, Bishop Alphonso Denson, Sr. was consecrated as Bishop of the 
Louisiana East First Jurisdiction by Presiding Bishop Charles E. Blake 
of the Church of God in Christ. Bishop Denson's leadership ushered in a 
new generation of vision for the 21st century church. With the help of 
the Lord, the jurisdiction continues to thrive, honoring its past while 
pressing boldly into its future.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in recognizing the 
Louisiana East First Jurisdiction for its historic legacy, its 
commitment to holiness, and for the visionary leadership that has 
guided its growth for more than a century.
```
