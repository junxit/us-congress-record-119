# HONORING MR. SCOTT K. BRADLEY

**Speaking:** Perry, Scott (P000605) [R-PA]

```
                     HONORING MR. SCOTT K. BRADLEY

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. SCOTT PERRY

                            of pennsylvania

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 14, 2026

  Mr. PERRY. Mr. Speaker, I rise to recognize and honor the service of 
Mr. Scott K. Bradley upon the auspicious occasion of his retirement 
after 41 years of cumulative service to our Nation in the U.S. Navy and 
federal government.
  As a child, Mr. Bradley had dreamed of serving on submarines ever 
since he witnessed two Canadian Oberon Class Diesel Submarines surface 
while on a family vacation. At 18, he joined the United States Navy. He 
thought himself to be a Northerner, so that February, he attended boot 
camp in NTC Great Lakes for 14 weeks, laughing at ``his cold self'' 
until he graduated.
  After graduating boot camp, the detailing team at Great Lakes tried 
mightily to get Bradley to accept a rate that called for a six-year 
enlistment, though his intent was to serve for four years and get a 
college degree with the GI Bill. The team offered him a choice between 
becoming a cook or a Torpedoman; he chose Torpedoman's Mate, marking 
the beginning of his decorated Naval career.
  After several school commands, TMSN Bradley was detailed to the USS 
George Washington Carver (SSBN-656) underway out of Holy Loch, 
Scotland. The only Sailor in a house full of Marines made for more than 
one wrestling match to get to the Thanksgiving table, but somehow, he 
managed to do that while earning his brothers' respect after he was 
promoted halfway through his third enlistment to Chief Petty Officer in 
just under nine years, and earned commission as an Underseas Ordnance 
Limited Duty Officer one year later, commissioned as an Underseas 
Ordnance Limited Duty Officer (LDO). Submarines were in his blood, and 
he crewed on several throughout his career. Later, as a Squadron Combat 
Systems Officer, he trained 18 weapons officers before ending his 24-
year Naval career as a Special Projects Officer at Electric Boat 
Shipyard with the Strategic Systems Programs.
  After culminating his Naval career, Mr. Bradley began his second 
career with the Defense Contract Management Agency (DCMA) in 2013, at 
which he's an essential part of the acquisition process from pre-award 
to sustainment. While postings at the Department of Energy and the 
Strategic Weapons Facility Pacific took him away at times, the 
importance of his work and the people with whom he served brought him 
back into the fold. Now, after 37 years of service to our Nation, Mr. 
Bradley is retiring to spend time with his wife and search for new 
challenges and adventures.
  Mr. Speaker, I am honored to recognize and congratulate Mr. Scott K. 
Bradley on his retirement after more than four decades of service to 
our Nation. His tireless service, outstanding performance, and 
dedication to the United States of America set the standard by which we 
all should be judged.
```
