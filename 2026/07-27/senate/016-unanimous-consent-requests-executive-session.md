# Unanimous Consent Requests (Executive Session)

**Speaking:** Husted, Jon (H001104) [R-OH], Moody, Ashley (M001244) [R-FL], Padilla, Alex (P000145) [D-CA], Moreno, Bernie (M001242) [R-OH]

**Measures:** S. 1498, H.R. 7008

```
                       Unanimous Consent Requests

  Mr. HUSTED. Mr. President, I rise today in support of H.R. 7008, a 
bill that contains two commonsense reforms. These are pretty easy--
banning members of Congress from purchasing stocks while in office and 
requiring a photo ID to vote. People have said that they want us to do 
this. I have heard it for years around here. It hasn't happened. Now it 
is time to happen. Tonight, we have an opportunity to do it.
  Both policies are overwhelmingly supported by Americans. If you want 
to listen to the American people, do these two things. Mr. President, 
86 percent of Americans support banning Members of Congress from 
purchasing stocks while in office, and 83 percent of Americans support 
showing a photo ID to vote.
  It has been 7 days since I last stood in this Chamber to speak about 
photo ID, and I am back to do it again because it is an important 
policy. I want to reiterate a point: Photo ID doesn't just protect the 
integrity of our elections; it makes our elections work better.
  If election integrity isn't reason enough to support photo ID, 
consider what it would mean to election administration. We have seen 
this in States like California, where it took them over a week to count 
votes in recent

[[Page S4279]]

elections. Elections officials shouldn't be spending time day after day 
after day going through ballots to see if this signature matches this 
signature. It takes too long. It is an ineffective way of determining 
the identification of an individual when many, many States--36 of 
them--have figured out a very efficient way to do this. It is called 
photo ID. It is something that everyone has. It is an efficient way to 
do it. It doesn't drag out the process. It doesn't force elections 
officials to become handwriting experts to figure it out.
  I mean, we used to do this in the 1800s before we had methods of 
determining someone's identity. You had to rely on a signature. But 
this is 2026, for crying out loud. Everyone has a photo ID. It is an 
easy way to do it, it is far simpler, and it will make our elections 
run more simply and effectively.
  Americans use a photo ID every single day. There is no reason they 
can't use it to vote. As a matter of fact, in Ohio, we did this. We 
have had photo ID for years. We had a mandate in the recent 
Presidential election to use a photo ID. It didn't suppress the vote. 
Actually, we had one of the highest voter turnouts in the history of 
our State.
  Don't give me the baloney that somehow this is going to discriminate 
against somebody and tell them it will make it harder for them to vote. 
We have evidence to the contrary. We know photo ID is common sense.
  Now let's move on to the other provision of this bill--legislation 
that includes stopping insider trading. It is called the Stop Insider 
Trading Act. It is another commonsense reform that is long overdue in 
Washington. People have been talking about it for years, did nothing 
about it. Tonight, we are trying to do something about it.
  The bill prohibits Members of Congress, their spouses, and their 
dependent children from purchasing new securities in publicly traded 
companies or similar financial instruments while they serve in 
Congress. It requires at least 7 days' public notice before anyone can 
sell any of these securities. It imposes significant legal and 
financial penalties for violators.
  These safeguards ensure lawmakers and their families cannot use 
nonpublic information to enrich themselves, as we know has gone on in 
this town for many years, at the expense of the American people. But 
nobody did anything about it. Tonight, we have a chance to do something 
about it.
  I want to commend one of our colleagues, Senator Pete Ricketts from 
Nebraska. Pete has led on the Senate side of this conversation on 
banning the insider trading. Senator Ricketts deserves credit for all 
of his work, and I am proud to be an original cosponsor of his Senate 
version of the legislation. I urge my colleagues to support its swift 
passage.
  I yield the floor.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER (Mr. Husted). The Senator from Florida.
  Mrs. MOODY. Mr. President, I appreciate the words of my colleague 
Senator Husted, who was sworn in on the same day as I was, and we both

  In a moment, I am going to ask unanimous consent for two very clear, 
very specific pieces of legislation that have already been voted on in 
the House in a bipartisan way: voter ID and keeping Members of Congress 
from purchasing stocks while in office. Very simple. It should pass 
here, and I dare someone to object because the people of this country 
are tired of us talking about it. It is time for us to do something. 
These are two issues that I care very deeply about. The voter ID 
portion of this, I was an original cosponsor of the SAVE America Act, 
and I have introduced similar legislation to ban Members from stock 
trading through my Restore Trust in Congress Act, which is bipartisan.

  These truly should be nondebatable, bipartisan issues that we can all

the very people--are the ones that support this.
  Mr. President, 76 percent of Black Americans, 82 percent of Hispanic 
Americans support voter ID, an overwhelming majority of Americans 
support voter ID--of all parties.
  Eighty-eight percent of Democrats, 81 percent of Independents support 
us banning Members of Congress from trading stocks.
  So I wanted to be very clear tonight, very obvious for anyone that is 
watching, not only here within the Senate but at home, which party is 
asking their colleagues to require voter ID and banning the purchasing 
of stocks while in office.
  I want to go back to voter ID for just a moment because I know, 
Senator Husted, this has been very important to you. Our great American 
experiment hinges on the sanctity and integrity of our elections, and I 
think we need to ask ourselves why is it that so many Democratic States 
refuse to comply with the law now and are objecting to us requiring 
voter ID. Why? What could be the reason?
  We have to fiercely safeguard our democracy, ensuring that only 
Americans are allowed to participate in American elections. The SAVE 
America Act upholds the rule of law, makes sure there are stronger 
protections in place across the country to ensure that only citizens 
can participate in one of the privileges of being a citizen, and that 
is voting in our election.
  Everyone should be outraged by what we just uncovered in New Jersey, 
where thousands of noncitizens were registered to vote through the 
State's motor vehicle system. So after telling us time and time and 
time and time again that that could never happen and does not happen--
oh my goodness, it is happening. Surprise, surprise.
  And they will still come here to the floor and object to voter ID 
after blaming thousands of individuals on the voter rolls as a software 
error.
  We also have to make sure that we are mindful of the fact that the 
Democratic Socialists of America have just said in their platform, they 
want noncitizens voting. They put it right out there in their platform.
  So people ask why now? Why do we need voter ID now? Maybe because we 
are uncovering thousands of people on voter rolls that shouldn't be on 
voter rolls, and maybe it is because you have an entire segment of the 
Democratic Party pushing for noncitizen voting. That is why now.
  Secure elections strengthen democracy. They increase participation. 
Ben Franklin famously replied to a bystander after the Constitutional 
Convention that we have a Republic if we can keep it. This is how we 
keep it.
  The House passed this in a bipartisan way. There is absolutely no 
reason that we should not pass this by unanimous consent in the Senate.
  I am going to keep fighting to bring in more accountability to our 
bosses, the people who are demanding this. And if somebody objects to 
this, I wonder whose interests they are fighting for. We cannot go back 
to our States without getting this done for them.
  We have to restore accountability in Washington. We have to show the 
people back home we are working in their interests.
  Mr. President, as if in legislative session and notwithstanding rule 
XXII, I ask unanimous consent that the Senate proceed to the immediate 
consideration of H.R. 7008, which was received from the House; further, 
that the bill be considered read a third time and passed; and that the 
motion to reconsider be considered made and laid upon the table.

  The SAVE Act would also devastate mail and absentee voting across the 
country by requiring photocopies of your ID to be included along with

constitution that requires an opportunity for a secret ballot, the same 
requirement for secret ballots that States across the country also

  This is the same element, by the way, that the Ohio Legislature

  But, of course, that is this bill's intent. For all the talk about

  It has come up for votes, procedural and otherwise. It keeps falling 
short. So why are we here?
  I believe if the proponents of the stock trading measure were sincere

Masto, Hawley, and Moreno.
  So why don't we try to do that?
  Mr. President, I ask that the Senator modify her request to instead
```
