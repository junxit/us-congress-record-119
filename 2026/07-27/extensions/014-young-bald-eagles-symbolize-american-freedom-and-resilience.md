# YOUNG BALD EAGLES SYMBOLIZE AMERICAN FREEDOM AND RESILIENCE

```
      YOUNG BALD EAGLES SYMBOLIZE AMERICAN FREEDOM AND RESILIENCE

                                 ______
                                 

                         HON. MICHAEL R. TURNER

                                of ohio

                    in the house of representatives

                         Monday, July 27, 2026

  Mr. TURNER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to draw attention to the birth 
of two bald eaglets at Carillon Historical Park in Dayton, Ohio, named 
``America'' and ``Independence'' in honor of our Nation's 250th 
birthday.
  Born two days apart on March 31, 2026, and April 2, 2026, America and 
Independence are now almost fully grown and recently embarked on their 
first flight. As we celebrate their births, let us not forget the 
special significance the Bald Eagle holds to our country and how this 
iconic bird's history is intertwined with our own,

[[Page E742]]

  Chosen as the Seal of our Nation by our Founding Fathers, the Bald 
Eagle is a strong and independent, majestic and bold, noble and 
steadfast creature that embodies the greatest qualities of our Nation. 
But, not unlike our own history, the Bald Eagle has faced perils of its 
own.
  In the late 1800s, there were 100,000 nesting bald eagles in the 
contiguous United States, but due to harmful pesticides, illegal 
hunting, and habitat loss, that number had dropped to only 400 breeding 
pairs by 1960. As recently as 1978, the Bald Eagle stood on the verge 
of extinction.
  However, due to the collective efforts of American scientists, the 
enactment of federal laws, including protection under the Endangered 
Species Act, and most importantly, public support from the American 
people for the bird's protection, the Bald Eagle has experienced a 
renaissance.
  By 1996, the Bald Eagle's classification was changed from endangered 
to threatened. The Bald Eagle was officially removed from the 
Endangered Species List in 2007. There are now 71,467 active nests in 
the United States, including 964 in Ohio across 87 of its 88 counties.
  In many ways, the story of the Bald Eagle mirrors the story of our 
Nation. From the trials of the Revolutionary War and Civil War to the 
sacrifices of two World Wars, the tensions of the Cold War, and the 
challenges of the War on Terror, our Nation has repeatedly faced grave 
dangers, persevered through adversity, and emerged stronger than 
before.
  So, as we celebrate the birth of the eaglets America and Independence 
and commemorate 250 years of our Republic, we are reminded that 
resilience, courage, and renewal remain among our greatest national 
virtues. And just as these two young eaglets have taken flight over 
Dayton, so too, does the United States stand ready to soar confidently 
into its next 250 years.
```
