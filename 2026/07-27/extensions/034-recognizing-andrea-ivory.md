# RECOGNIZING ANDREA IVORY

**Speaking:** Wilson, Frederica S. (W000808) [D-FL]

```
                        RECOGNIZING ANDREA IVORY

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. FREDERICA S. WILSON

                               of florida

                    in the house of representatives

                         Monday, July 27, 2026

  Ms. WILSON of Florida. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize and pay 
tribute to Andrea Ivory, a breast cancer survivor and the founder of 
The Women's Breast Health Initiative (WBHI) in Hialeah, Florida.
  Ms. Ivory was diagnosed with breast cancer in 2006. While she was 
fortunate that her breast cancer was detected early and that she had 
insurance and access to doctors, she knew that many other women did 
not. To better the odds for at-risk women with fewer resources, she 
founded the Women's Breast Health Initiative in 2008. WBHI is an 
outreach organization dedicated to educating women about the importance 
of breast health and providing them with the resources to beat the 
disease.
  Since it was founded, WBHI has delivered literature to 50,000 
households and by use of a mammography van, the organization has 
provided free breast cancer screening for thirteen hundred women. For 
her contributions to the fight against breast cancer, Ms. Ivory was 
named Cable News Network's (CNN) Hero, in 2009. CNN ``Heroes'' is a 
tribute to ordinary people changing the world.
  Experts estimate that about one out of every eight women born today 
will be diagnosed with breast cancer at some time during her

[[Page E747]]

life. The strongest risk factor for breast cancer is age. A woman's 
risk of developing this disease increases as she gets older. However, 
thanks the work Ms. Ivory is doing and improvements in cancer treatment 
and early detection, millions of women and thousands of men are 
surviving breast cancer today.
  Breast cancer is the most common cancer among women in the United 
States, other than skin cancer. It is the second leading cause of 
cancer death in women, after lung cancer. The disease occurs almost 
entirely in women, but men can get it, too. Today, there are more than 
2.9 million breast cancer survivors in the United States.
  I am honored to recognize Andrea Ivory, a breast cancer survivor and 
founder of The Women's Breast Health Initiative, for her dedication and 
commitment to our community.
```
