# Tribute to Alex Nguyen (Executive Session)

**Speaking:** Schumer, Charles E. (S000148) [D-NY]

```
                         Tribute to Alex Nguyen

  Madam President, finally, last but certainly not least is Alex--my 
dear Alex.
  In May of 2019, I hired a new national press secretary to join my 
leadership team. He had a strong resume, and he had glowing 
recommendations, but I will admit the fit took a little getting used 
to. I am from New York. He is from Los Angeles. I have been in the 
Senate a long time. He came straight from the House. He wears his 
glasses properly. I never have. And when things got stressful, he 
somehow got calmer, which I never quite understood. But over the next 7 
years, that press secretary from LA became one of my most trusted 
advisers and closest confidants.
  His name is Alex Nguyen. After 7 remarkable years--7 great years--
where he not only was a great press leader but just somebody who helped 
shape our office, shape our policy, guide us,

[[Page S1706]]

remind me all too often when I wanted to do something that I shouldn't 
do, in terms of policy or whatever the foibles were--usually successful 
in deterring me. When I made a mistake, it was my fault. It wasn't his, 
as I probably didn't listen to him. But after 7 years, he will be 
leaving my office for his next chapter.
  During his time here, he held a bunch of titles--national press 
secretary, communications director--but the titles that matter most 
didn't come from this building. He became a husband. This year, he 
became a father. And my hope is that the next chapter gives him more 
time with his wonderful wife Megan, who has had a very successful 
career herself, and his amazing son Ethan. Ethan is only 11 months old, 
but when you see Ethan, you know he has gotten the intelligence, the 
awareness, the alertness, and the perspicacity of his dad.
  Hi, Ethan.
  He stopped crying.
  Good boy.
  And, of course, the other person who is part of Alex's family--not 
quite a person--is, of course, George, the office dog, who somehow had 
better press access than most reporters.
  Now, look, I am really happy for Alex, but I won't pretend this is 
easy because his departure leaves a real hole in our team.
  Alex helped shape the message behind some of the most consequential 
accomplishments of our majority--the Inflation Reduction Act, the CHIPS 
and Science Act, infrastructure, gay marriage, gun control, and so much 
more. Let's just say we have a lot of wins under Nguyen. That is a lot 
of W-I-N-S under N-G-U-Y-E-N.
  In the mornings, he was often the first person I called--No. 72 on my 
speed dial. It got worn out. It is a ritual, I suspect, he is not going 
to miss very much.
  Everybody knows this place can be a tough place, and it is even 
tougher being the press guy for Chuck Schumer, but Alex never flinched. 
He was great in the good moments and even better in the tough moments.
  He was with me in China. He was with me in Israel in the days after 
October 7. He was here with me on January 6. And not a day went by over 
those 7 years when I didn't think: Thank God--thank God--Alex is here.
  On the most stressful days, he lowered the temperature, and when we 
needed urgency--real urgency--he raised the temperature. He did it all 
with sharp instincts, dry humor, and the courage to tell me when I was 
wrong or I could be better.
  So now I have a feeling I may still pick up the phone and dial 72--I 
am not taking you off my speed dial--every now and then to ask his 
opinion. Old habits die hard.
  The truth is what I will miss isn't just his advice. It is the 
judgment. It is the calm. It is the trust. Alex made everyone better 
around him, including me, and that is the highest compliment I can 
give.
  So, Alex, thank you for everything you gave this office. Thank you 
for the countless early mornings and late nights. Thank you for 
standing steady, no matter what the moment.
  You have made your mark here. We will miss you. We are proud of you, 
and we are better because of you.
  And, Alex, no matter where you go next, you will always, always have 
a home in the Schumer family.
  Are we allowed to hug on the floor, Madam President?
  Yes.
  I yield the floor.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from Texas.
```
