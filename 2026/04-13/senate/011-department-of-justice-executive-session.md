# Department of Justice (Executive Session)

**Speaking:** Schumer, Charles E. (S000148) [D-NY], Durbin, Richard J. (D000563) [D-IL]

```
                         Department of Justice

  Mr. President, earlier this month, President Trump announced that he 
was firing the Attorney General Pam Bondi. I would say it is no 
surprise. She has been politically controversial. She has done things 
in the little over a year that she served that are unprecedented. She 
transformed the Department from the Nation's preeminent law enforcement 
Agency into a political entity. Apparently, she didn't go far enough to 
please the President, and he fired her.
  At the core of this MAGA makeover at DOJ was a systematic purge of 
career professionals. I have met with them, men and women working for 
the Department of Justice with years of experience, prosecuting cases, 
going after criminals and terrorists and the like. Hundreds of these 
experienced prosecutors were forced out, stripping the Department of 
decades of institutional knowledge and experience.
  The acting Attorney General now, since Bondi was fired, is named Todd 
Blanche. He recently said that there remained no more employees in the 
Department of Justice and FBI that worked on the criminal 
investigations of President Trump. They have all been fired, forced 
out.
  The impact over the last year can't be overstated. The Department 
recently said its National Security Division is facing unprecedented 
personnel constraints at a time when our country is embroiled in a 
significant international conflict in Iran.
  At the same time, the Department under former Attorney General Bondi 
was quick to welcome individuals who were involved in violent efforts 
to overturn the 2020 election.
  I mentioned earlier that I have been in the Senate for 30 years. 
There are moments I will never forget, and one of those was January 6. 
That was a time when we were counting the ballots in

[[Page S1704]]

2021, and those ballots were being counted by the Vice President of the 
United States, Vice President Pence. He was sitting where the Presiding 
Officer is now. I was sitting at this desk.
  It was 2 in the afternoon. We were counting the electoral college 
ballots brought in from around the country. It was a rather routine 
process hardly ever controversial, but in this one something happened 
we will never forget.
  I watched as the Secret Service came up and literally dragged Vice 
President Pence out of that chair and through that door outside the 
Senate Chamber. The rest of the Senators were sitting here. We were 
looking at one another: What is going on?
  We knew there were demonstrators outside. We could hear them. We knew 
there were a lot of them. They had gone to a rally with the President a 
few blocks away from the Capitol, and now they had marched on the 
Capitol.
  So at that point a member of the Capitol Police, a wonderful group of 
law enforcement agents who protect all of the visitors, the Gallery, as 
well as the Senators and staff, one of the members of police in uniform 
stood before us and said: Don't worry. This is a safe place to be. Stay 
right where you are. Other people will come in and join you, and we 
will let you know what is going to happen next.
  We said OK. Ten minutes later the same individual came and stood 
before us and said: Forget what I told you earlier. In an orderly 
fashion, leave the Chamber as fast as possible. The demonstrators are 
right outside the door.
  So we all went through that door, ushered downstairs and outside 
along with Vice President Pence. It was a terrible scene. We had 150 
law enforcement agents with the Capitol Police who were protecting 
visitors to the Capitol as well as Senators, Congressmen, and their 
staffs; and many of those people were hurt, physically hurt.
  As a result of that, the Department of Justice under President Biden 
investigated and had over 1,500 of them who were arrested and 
prosecuted, and many of them were serving time in jail for what they 
did on that day.
  You have seen the videotapes. I am not going to describe them. Draw 
your own conclusions. But I can tell you inside, I have never quite 
seen the Senate in that situation as there was that day.
  What happened when President Trump was elected to a second term? The 
day he was sworn in, he signed a full and unconditional pardon for all 
of the 1,500 who had been arrested for descending on the Capitol on 
January 6.
  Regardless of whether or not they had been found guilty of a felony 
for hurting law enforcement agents, regardless of that, he gave full 
and unconditional pardons.
  The impact of that was significant. More than 1,600 people charged 
and convicted of offenses related to January 6 were subject to the 
President's blanket pardon. The Department of Justice hired a pardoned 
insurrectionist named Jared Weiss. This man who on January 6 was 
berating officers and calling them Nazis and Gestapo repeatedly urged 
the violent mob to ``kill them''--this is on videotape--as Capitol 
Police officers were being attacked. Thankfully, he left the Department 
the same day Attorney General Bondi was fired.
  An individual who descended on the Capitol, urged people to harm law 
enforcement agents trying to keep it peaceful, was then prosecuted, 
then subject to a pardon, then hired by the Department of Justice to 
serve as a senior adviser. He is gone, thank goodness.
  While the Department's leadership prioritized installing Trump 
cronies and forcing out experienced attorneys, they quietly closed more 
than 23,000 criminal investigations in the first 6 months of the Trump 
administration.
  To prioritize Trump's unpopular and cruel mass deportation campaign, 
the Department declined to pursue prosecutions in hundreds of 
terrorism, drug, and white-collar criminal investigations.
  The National Cryptocurrency Enforcement Team, which was dedicated to 
investigating crypto-related fraud and money laundering, was disbanded 
under the former Attorney General. All this took place while the 
President pardoned his wealthy allies who have defrauded Americans out 
of millions of dollars, founded notorious dark web drug markets, and 
supported President Trump's family businesses.
  This drastic shift away from the Department's core public safety 
mission highlights the indifference to the rights and safety of 
Americans.
  I tell you this story because we now have a vacancy in the Attorney 
General's position. Todd Blanche is there on a temporary basis. We have 
to decide whether to fill the ranks. I think Americans can agree on the 
basics.
  We want a Department of Justice which is not run for political 
purposes but to keep us safe in America, safe in our homes, safe in our 
neighborhoods, safe in our Nation. That is the bottom line.
  Getting even with people who are fair or unfair to the current 
President is not the mission of the Department of Justice. It is the 
administration of justice in a nonpolitical way.
  I certainly hope that in the weeks and months ahead, while we debate 
this issue, that we can go back to a Department of Justice that we can 
trust as an apolitical Agency doing its best to bring safety and peace 
to America.
  I yield the floor.
  Mr. President, I suggest the absence of a quorum.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The clerk will call the roll.
  The legislative clerk proceeded to call the roll.
  Mr. SCHUMER. Mr. President, I ask unanimous consent that the order 
for the quorum call be rescinded.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Without objection, it is so ordered.
```
