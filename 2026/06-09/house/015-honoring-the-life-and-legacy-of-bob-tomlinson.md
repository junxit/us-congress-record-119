# HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF BOB TOMLINSON

**Speaking:** Schmidt, Derek (S001228) [R-KS]

```
             HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF BOB TOMLINSON

  (Mr. Schmidt of Kansas was recognized to address the House for 5 
minutes.)
  Mr. SCHMIDT. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life and legacy 
of a devoted public servant, educator, and proud Kansan, my friend, Bob 
Tomlinson, who passed away in February 2025 at his home in Kansas.
  Bob Tomlinson lived a life defined by service to others. Whether in 
the classroom, the Kansas Statehouse, or around the family dinner 
table, Bob believed deeply in showing up for people, listening 
carefully, and leaving every place a little better than he found it.
  From an early age, Bob demonstrated a passion for public service and 
civic engagement. In fact, his family recalls that his first campaign 
began in junior high school when he ran for student council. That sense 
of responsibility to contribute, to lead, and to serve never left him.
  As a young man, Bob attended Boys State, worked with Republican U.S. 
Senator Bob Dole and Democratic State Senator Anthony Hensley, and 
built a bipartisan spirit that would become a hallmark of his public 
life. Bob understood that public service was never about headlines or 
partisanship. It was about people, especially those who felt unheard or 
overlooked.
  Bob earned his bachelor's degree from the University of Kansas and 
later a master's degree from Baker University. He went on to spend 
nearly two decades working in education, serving as a social studies 
teacher and counselor at the Shawnee Mission School District 
alternative education program at Horizons High School. Those who knew 
Bob understood that he had a special heart for students who needed 
extra care, extra patience, and someone who believed in them when much 
of the world often did not.
  Bob did not simply teach history. He taught young people that their 
lives mattered and that they could still shape their own futures.
  Bob's commitment to his community naturally led him into elected 
office. He served on the Roeland Park City Council before representing 
Kansas' 24th House District in the Kansas State House of 
Representatives from 1993 to 2003. During his decade in the 
legislature, Bob earned respect on both sides of the aisle for his 
thoughtful approach, his kindness, and his ability to build genuine 
friendships across political differences.
  He later served as assistant insurance commissioner for the State of 
Kansas from 2003 to 2012, continuing a

[[Page H4001]]

career centered on protecting and serving Kansans.
  If you knew Bob, you knew there was always a joke. There was always a 
story. In every situation, he could find a way to lighten the mood and 
make people feel at ease. He never met a stranger who would not later 
become a friend.
  Bob loved Kansas deeply. He traveled to every corner of the State 
multiple times during his years in public service. He cared about rural 
communities, small towns, working families, and the people whose voices 
too often went unheard. He believed government worked best when leaders 
listened first.
  However, of all the titles Bob carried, the title he treasured most 
was ``family man.'' Bob and his beloved wife, Carole, shared 38 years 
of marriage. Together, they raised two sons, Phillip and Christopher, 
and later welcomed a granddaughter, Audrey, who brought Bob enormous 
joy.
  Family traditions mattered to Bob. Every summer, the Tomlinsons would 
pack up and travel together to visit historic sites and State capitals 
across America. Thanks to Bob's love of history and their travel, his 
sons can say they visited 48 State capitals and our Nation's Capital 
here in Washington. Those trips reflected Bob's belief that learning 
never stops and that the best memories are made together.

  While Bob could discuss history, public policy, or baseball with 
anyone, his granddaughter, Audrey, may have known him best for yet 
another talent entirely: his unforgettable Donald Duck impression, 
which he happily performed for hours just to make her laugh.
  That was Bob Tomlinson. He was accomplished but humble, serious about 
service but never about himself, a man whose greatest joy came not from 
recognition but from bringing joy to others.
  On behalf of so many people in Kansas and of this U.S. House of 
Representatives, I extend my deepest condolences to Carole, Phillip, 
Chris, Megan, Audrey, and the entire Tomlinson family. May they find 
comfort in knowing that Bob's life made a lasting difference in the 
lives of so many others. May he rest in peace.
```
