# HONORING CHARLES "SMARTY PANTS" KING, JR.

**Speaking:** Thompson, Bennie G. (T000193) [D-MS]

```
              HONORING CHARLES ``SMARTY PANTS'' KING, JR.

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. BENNIE G. THOMPSON

                             of mississippi

                    in the house of representatives

                         Tuesday, July 14, 2026

  Mr. THOMPSON of Mississippi. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Mr. 
Charles ``Smarty Pants'' King, Jr., a gifted educator, artist, mentor, 
and servant leader whose life's work has touched the minds and hearts 
of children throughout Mississippi.
  A native of Memphis, Tennessee, Mr. King spent much of his childhood 
in Battle Creek, Michigan. As a young person, he was optimistic and had 
a powerful imagination, yet he often felt that traditional classrooms 
did not allow him to think freely. Rather than allowing that experience 
to defeat him, he turned it into purpose. He made it his mission to 
help children think for themselves, use their imaginations, and 
discover the greatness already inside them.
  In 1980, Mr. King founded Smarty Pants Educational Services, choosing 
the elephant as the face of the organization because, as he explained, 
it is the largest land mammal. When he moved to Mississippi in 1985 to 
care for his grandfather, he continued to grow his work and took a 
``catch them where they are'' approach to teaching. He understood that 
every child does not learn the same way, but every child deserves the 
opportunity to learn.
  Known affectionately as ``Smarty Pants,'' a name that became part of 
his legal identity, Mr. King used magic, brain teasers, artwork, 
riddles, and his beloved elephant character to make learning come 
alive. Those who encountered him quickly learned that his lessons often 
began with a question, a puzzle, or a moment of wonder. He loved the 
``whys'' in life, and that curiosity allowed him to connect with 
children in a language they could understand.
  Behind every smile and creative lesson was a deeper mission. He 
taught young people the importance of education, self-love, staying 
drug-free, making wise choices, and believing in the gifts God placed 
within them. His guiding belief says it best: ``If a child can't learn 
the way I teach, then I must learn to teach the way he can learn.'' 
That statement is more than a motto. It is a testimony of patience, 
compassion, and commitment to meeting children where they are.
  As an artist, Mr. King created thousands of pieces of artwork filled 
with symbols, puzzles, and hidden lessons. His art was not made simply 
to be seen. It was made to be studied, questioned, and understood. 
Through symbols and visual storytelling, he taught Black history, 
critical thinking, and life lessons in ways that reached young people 
beyond the walls of a traditional classroom.
  In 2024, the Mississippi House of Representatives recognized Mr. King 
for his many years of service to youth and congratulated him upon his 
retirement. Though retired, Mr. King's heart for students remains 
strong, and he still shares his gifts from time to time to encourage, 
teach, and inspire young learners.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring Mr. Charles 
``Smarty Pants'' King, Jr., a living example of imagination in service, 
education with heart, and the enduring truth that every child deserves 
to be seen, taught, and inspired.
```
