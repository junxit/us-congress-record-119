# CELEBRATING THE 61ST ANNIVERSARY OF MEDICARE AND MEDICAID

**Speaking:** Davis, Danny K. (D000096) [D-IL]

```
       CELEBRATING THE 61ST ANNIVERSARY OF MEDICARE AND MEDICAID

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. DANNY K. DAVIS

                              of illinois

                    in the house of representatives

                         Tuesday, July 14, 2026

  Mr. DAVIS of Illinois. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to celebrate the 
61st anniversary of Medicare and Medicaid. On July 30, 1965, President 
Lyndon B. Johnson signed these essential programs into law, 
guaranteeing that seniors, children, and families would no longer have 
to face a health crisis alone.
  Today, across the Nation, over 140 million Americans depend on 
Medicare and Medicaid for their fundamental health care needs. In our 
state, over 2.4 million Illinoisans--nearly 18 percent of our state--
rely on Medicare. Roughly 3 million--about one in four Illinoisans--
depend on Medicaid. Medicaid pays for approximately half of all births 
in Illinois and covers 68 percent of all nursing home

[[Page E684]]

days in our state. These numbers are not theoretical abstractions. 
These numbers are Illinoisans who rely on Medicare and Medicaid to 
thrive. These numbers are the doctor's visits, nursing home beds, and 
prescriptions that keep our neighbors alive.
  I am alarmed that, just 61 years later, the federal guarantee of 
health care for the elderly and needy is under greater threat than at 
any point since their establishment--especially for those who rely on 
Medicaid. Medicaid provides life-saving health insurance to low-income 
adults, children, pregnant women, elderly adults, and people with 
disabilities. Yet last year, President Trump and Congressional 
Republicans passed the Big Ugly Bill, ripping over $1 trillion out of 
Medicaid and the Affordable Care Act to bankroll tax breaks for 
billionaires and big corporations. Nationally, 8 million Americans 
already have lost their health coverage, and 15 million more are 
projected to lose coverage by 2034. More than 1,000 hospitals, clinics, 
and nursing homes across the country are closing, cutting services, or 
at risk of doing so.
  Illinoisans are suffering. An estimated 193,000 Illinoisans have 
already lost their coverage, including 176,000 whom the Administration 
kicked off Medicaid and CHIP outright. Up to 528,000 more are projected 
to lose their health care in the years ahead. Illinois faces a $57 
billion cut in federal Medicaid funding over the next decade, and our 
hospitals alone are projected to lose nearly $31.3 billion over that 
same period. Twenty-three rural Illinois hospitals lost money on 
patient services this past year. Sixteen are at risk of closing. Eight 
are at immediate risk.
  In my own Congressional District, 24 percent of families and 30 
percent of children under 18 live below the poverty line. A resident of 
the Loop can expect to live to 85; a resident of Garfield Park, just 
seven stops away on the CTA Blue Line, can expect to live only to age 
69. That 16-year gap is not an accident. It is what happens when 
disinvestment meets need, and it is why Medicaid and Medicare matter 
more in my district than in almost any other in this country. 
Forecasters project my own district will have 40,000 more uninsured 
residents by 2034 because of this Administration's cuts. I have more 
hospital beds in my district than any other Member of Congress, and I 
have spent my career working to make sure the people who fill those 
beds can afford and access quality health care.
  When my colleagues and I fought to enact the Affordable Care Act, we 
did so to ensure that no American would ever be denied the care needed 
to live out their rights to life, liberty, and the pursuit of 
happiness. My dedication to healthcare started long before I ever came 
to Washington, rooted deeply in my years working on the frontlines of 
Chicago's community health center movement. I saw firsthand that when 
you give a neighborhood a reliable clinic, you give them a fighting 
chance at a healthy life. Yet, instead of building on that foundation, 
this Administration's reckless cuts are pushing our local institutions 
directly to the brink.
  Health care is a right, not a privilege reserved for those who can 
afford it. Many philosophers and leaders have emphasized that we should 
judge a society by how it cares for its young, its elderly, and its 
vulnerable. I have spent my 30 years in Congress fighting to strengthen 
Medicare and Medicaid, and I will not stop now--not while my 
constituents are losing their coverage, not while our hospitals are 
closing their doors, and not while this Administration chooses 
billionaires over the people who built this country.
  As we mark 61 years of Medicare and Medicaid, I renew my commitment 
to defend these programs and to fight for the day when every American, 
regardless of zip code or income, can get the care they need.

                          ____________________
```
