# HONORING MATHEW H. "MATT" POLLACK WITH THE BENJAMIN O. DAVIS, JR. LEADERSHIP AWARD

**Speaking:** Brown, Shontel M. (B001313) [D-OH]

```
  HONORING MATHEW H. ``MATT'' POLLACK WITH THE BENJAMIN O. DAVIS, JR. 
                            LEADERSHIP AWARD

                                 ______
                                 

                         HON. SHONTEL M. BROWN

                                of ohio

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 22, 2026

  Ms. BROWN. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Mathew H. ``Matt'' 
Pollack as a recipient of the Benjamin O. Davis, Jr. Leadership Award 
and to honor his distinguished record of military service, public 
safety, and leadership in the Cleveland community.
  The Benjamin O. Davis, Jr. Leadership Award honors the legacy of 
General Benjamin O. Davis, Jr., a pioneering military leader and 
Tuskegee Airman. General Davis spent formative years in Cleveland, 
graduating from Central High School in 1929 and attending Western 
Reserve University. Later in life, he returned to Cleveland to serve as 
the city's Director of Public Safety. His legacy represents courage, 
integrity, perseverance, and a commitment to leading the way for those 
who follow.
  Mr. Pollack has demonstrated those same principles throughout a 
career dedicated to protecting and serving others.
  Mr. Pollack served four years in the United States Marine Corps, 
including service with a Fleet Anti-terrorism Security Team and as an 
Anti-Armor Assaultman. His military service included the period 
surrounding the September 11, 2001, terrorist attacks. The discipline, 
responsibility, and commitment to others developed during his time as a 
Marine would continue to guide his career long after his military 
service concluded.
  Following his service in the Marine Corps, Mr. Pollack continued his 
commitment to public service by joining the Cleveland Division of 
Police. Throughout his career, he has served the people of Cleveland as 
a patrol officer, a detective in the Vice Unit, and now as a sergeant.
  His leadership has extended beyond his individual responsibilities. 
While serving in Vice, Mr. Pollack helped mentor and guide newer 
officers as they developed their skills and experience. Today, as a 
sergeant, he continues to lead fellow officers while also contributing 
to the development of departmental policy, applying the knowledge 
gained throughout his career to help shape how the Cleveland Division 
of Police serves the community.
  Mr. Pollack has also demonstrated courage in moments of extraordinary 
danger. During one incident, he and his partner came under gunfire and 
his partner was wounded. Despite the danger, Mr. Pollack remained 
focused on protecting his fellow officer, responding to the threat, and 
helping bring the incident to a conclusion. His service and courage 
throughout his law enforcement career have been recognized with the 
Cleveland Division of Police Medal of Honor, Medal of Heroism, and 
Medal of Valor.
  The Benjamin O. Davis, Jr. Leadership Award recognizes veterans whose 
leadership is grounded in courage, integrity, and perseverance and who 
use their experiences to lead others with distinction. Mr. Pollack's 
career reflects those values. From serving our Nation as a United 
States Marine to protecting Cleveland residents, mentoring fellow 
officers, and helping shape the future of his department, he has 
consistently demonstrated leadership through service.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in congratulating Mathew 
H. Pollack on receiving the Benjamin O. Davis, Jr. Leadership Award and 
in thanking him for his service to our Nation, his leadership, and his 
continued dedication to the people of Cleveland and Northeast Ohio.
```
