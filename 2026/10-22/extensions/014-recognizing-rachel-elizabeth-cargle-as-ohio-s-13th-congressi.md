# RECOGNIZING RACHEL ELIZABETH CARGLE AS OHIO'S 13TH CONGRESSIONAL DISTRICT'S CHAMPION OF THE WEEK

**Speaking:** Sykes, Emilia Strong (S001223) [D-OH]

```
   RECOGNIZING RACHEL ELIZABETH CARGLE AS OHIO'S 13TH CONGRESSIONAL 
                    DISTRICT'S CHAMPION OF THE WEEK

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. EMILIA STRONG SYKES

                                of ohio

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 22, 2026

  Mrs. SYKES. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Rachel Elizabeth 
Cargle as Ohio's 13th Congressional District's Champion of the Week. 
This recognition honors her work as an author, entrepreneur, 
philanthropist, and public academic whose career sits at the 
intersection of truth-telling and care, and whose influence has 
reshaped conversations about race, feminism, and healing far beyond 
Akron's borders.
  She first built a national audience through her essays, pushing the 
feminist movement to confront the ways it has sidelined and ignored 
Black women. That writing caught the attention of major outlets, 
including The New York Times, The Washington Post, and Time Magazine, 
and it positioned her as one of the sharpest public voices of her 
generation on questions of race and equity. In the years since, she has 
expanded into lecturing and teaching, but the thread connecting all of 
it has stayed the same: telling the truth is itself an act of care.
  In 2019, she turned that principle into something concrete by 
launching The Loveland Foundation, which pays for therapy for Black 
women and girls. It started small, a birthday fundraiser powered by her 
own community, and has since become a genuine national resource: over 
210,000 hours of culturally competent therapy provided, and more than 
$10 million put toward the healing of a community that is too often 
expected to give everything and receive care from no one. Few 
grassroots mental-health efforts in the country have grown this far on 
the premise that a person's ability to pay should never gate their 
access to support.
  That same instinct showed up in her writing. Her debut book, A 
Renaissance of Our Own, is part memoir and part cultural critique, and 
it makes the case that people, Black women especially, deserve to build 
their lives by their own design rather than one inherited from someone 
else.
  Even with a national platform, Cargle has stayed rooted in Akron. She 
owns Elizabeth's Bookshop and Writing Center, a home for literature and 
storytelling in the city that raised her, and she runs Ninth House, a 
consultancy that helps authors and thought leaders build lasting 
careers in publishing. Both are, at their core, about building 
something that outlasts her--infrastructure for the writers who come 
next.
  I congratulate Rachel Elizabeth Cargle on her continued impact and 
thank her for the work she does in the spirit of Ohio's 13th 
Congressional District, proudly known as the ``Birthplace of 
Champions.''
```
