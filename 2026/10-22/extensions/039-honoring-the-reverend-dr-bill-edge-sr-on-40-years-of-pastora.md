# HONORING THE REVEREND DR. BILL EDGE, SR. ON 40 YEARS OF PASTORAL SERVICE TO EUREKA MISSIONARY BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Bishop, Sanford D. (B000490) [D-GA]

```
   HONORING THE REVEREND DR. BILL EDGE, SR. ON 40 YEARS OF PASTORAL 
              SERVICE TO EUREKA MISSIONARY BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                      HON. SANFORD D. BISHOP, JR.

                               of georgia

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 22, 2026

  Mr. BISHOP. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor a shepherd who has 
never left his flock: the Reverend Dr. Bill Edge, Sr., who on October 
19, 2026, marked 40 years as pastor of Eureka Missionary Baptist Church 
in Albany, Georgia. A dinner to celebrate this remarkable and singular 
accomplishment was held on Saturday, October 17, 2026, at Eureka 
Missionary Baptist Church.
  40 years, Mr. Speaker. That is not a tenure. That is a lifetime 
poured out, Sunday after Sunday, sickbed after sickbed, graveside after 
graveside, for one congregation on Lily Pond Road that the world may 
call small, but that heaven has always known to be great.
  On October 19, 1986, Bill Edge stepped into the pulpit at Eureka. He 
inherited a sanctuary built on faith and sacrifice. Under the Reverend 
Arthur L. Thompson, Sr., the members had purchased two acres on Lily 
Pond Road in 1975, paid for the land by 1977, and in September 1980 
traveled by motorcade from the old church at 307 South Jackson Street 
to dedicate their new house of worship. They had given what they had. 
Now they needed a shepherd who would give everything he had.
  And he did. Within four years, the congregation gathered to burn its 
mortgage. In 1991, a steeple rose over Lily Pond Road, pointing every 
passerby toward heaven. New doors, new heating and cooling, a paved 
parking lot: one by one, the prayers of the saints became bricks and 
mortar. But Mr. Speaker, a church is not measured in steeples. In 40 
years, a pastor holds infants at the altar and lives to see those same 
children stand there with infants of their own. He sits with families 
in hospital corridors at midnight. He stands at the graveside of the 
very deacons and mothers who first welcomed him, and then he climbs 
back into the pulpit the next Sunday and preaches hope to the ones they 
left behind. Dr. Edge has carried those joys and those sorrows for four 
decades, and he has never once set them down.
  Under his leadership, Eureka established its Annual Guest Day and 
Friends of Eureka Award, Annual Youth Week, Deacon and Deaconess 
Fellowship Day, Choir Day, Ushers Day, Christmas Candlelight Services, 
and Honors Day for Vacation Bible School. He organized the 
Inspirational Choir and the Nurses Unit, and he established the Bernice 
Mitchell Memorial Outstanding Youth of the Year Award so that the young 
people of Eureka would always know that their church sees them, 
believes in them, and expects great things from them. He taught the 
congregation to honor those who came before. Eureka named its 
fellowship hall the Mother Mattie P. Davis Fellowship Hall, in tribute 
to its oldest member, a living reminder that every generation stands on 
the shoulders of the one before it.
  Dr. Edge has never stopped learning, and he has never stopped 
teaching. He holds a Ph.D. and multiple doctorates in ministry and 
theology, earning summa cum laude. In 2011, he brought the Southwest 
Region of Bethany Divinity College and Seminary of Dothan, Alabama to 
Eureka's own campus, where men and women answering the call may pursue 
accredited study from the Bible diploma through the doctorate. Today he 
serves as Dean of Academics for Bethany and as its Albany Dean. 
Somewhere in Southwest Georgia this Sunday, a pastor he trained will 
rise to preach, and Dr. Edge's ministry will be multiplied once more.
  Eureka calls itself ``The Little Church with the Big Heart,'' and 
Albany knows it to be true. In 2021, Dr. Edge's congregation organized 
its Outreach Team to seek out the lost and the struggling, providing 
meals, clothing, and food boxes throughout the year and through every 
holiday season. In 2024, Eureka hosted its first community-wide 
giveaway of clothing, food, and household goods, serving more than 250 
neighbors alongside more than 20 partner organizations offering help 
with housing, health, and so much more. That same year, the church held 
its first Breast Cancer Awareness Month celebration, bringing voter 
registration, wellness screenings, and COVID and flu vaccines to the 
people of Albany.
  Mr. Speaker, that is what the Gospel looks like with its sleeves 
rolled up. Mr. Speaker, no pastor walks 40 years alone, and Dr. Edge 
would be the first to tell you so. At his side through every sermon, 
every struggle, and every season of this ministry has stood his beloved 
wife, Dr. Elois Edge.
  Dr. Elois Edge has answered a calling of her own. A midwife since 
1978, she has delivered more than 2,000 babies into the waiting arms of 
Southwest Georgia families. Think of it, Mr. Speaker: while her husband 
has been shepherding souls, she has been ushering new life into the 
world. Somewhere in Albany tonight, there are grown men and women, and 
their own children besides, whose very first moments on this earth were 
held in her hands.
  In 2023, Albany Area Primary Health Care honored her lifetime of 
service by opening the Elois Edge Health Center, so that her name will 
keep watch over the health of this community for generations to come. 
Together, Dr. Bill Edge and Dr. Elois Edge have lived out a ministry of 
both body and soul. He has stood at the altar; she has stood at the 
bedside. He has preached of new birth; she has delivered it. Eureka's 
pastor has never had to carry the weight of this calling by himself, 
because God gave him a partner equal to the task.
  Albany has recognized what Eureka has always known. Dr. Edge received 
the Key to the City of Albany in 1990, was named one of Albany's Most 
Influential Men, and was honored with the Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr. 
Dream Award, among many other distinctions. Yet those who know him best 
will tell you he needs no introduction and no flowery words. His life 
does the talking. For 40 years, he has lived by a motto drawn from the 
beloved hymn ``If I Can Help Somebody,'' and he has spent every one of 
those years doing exactly that: helping somebody and then helping 
somebody else.
  The great preacher Charles Haddon Spurgeon once counseled, ``A good 
character is the best tombstone. Those who loved you and were helped by 
you will remember you when forget-me-nots have withered. Carve your 
name on hearts, not on marble.'' Mr. Speaker, Bill Edge has never 
needed marble. For 40 years, he has been carving his name on the hearts 
of Albany, one baptism, one wedding, one home-going, one helping hand 
at a time. When Eureka looks back over the road it has traveled, it 
borrows the words of John Newton: ``Through many dangers, toils and 
snares, I have already come; 'tis grace hath brought me safe thus far, 
and grace will lead me home.'' For 40 of those years, Mr. Speaker, 
God's grace has walked that road wearing the face of Bill Edge.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join my wife, Vivian and me along 
with the more than 765,000 people of Georgia's 2nd Congressional 
District, the members of Eureka Missionary Baptist Church, Dr. Elois 
Edge the entire Edge family, the students and faculty of Bethany 
Divinity College and Seminary, and the entire Albany community in 
thanking Dr. Bill Edge, Sr., for 40 years of faithful shepherding. It 
is a profound privilege to recognize a man whose life bears witness to 
the promise of Scripture, that ``your labor is not in vain in the 
Lord.'' I thank Pastor Edge on a job well done. He is good and faithful 
servant.
```
