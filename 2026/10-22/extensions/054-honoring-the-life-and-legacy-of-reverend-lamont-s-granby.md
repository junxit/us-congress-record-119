# HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF REVEREND LAMONT S. GRANBY

**Speaking:** Torres, Ritchie (T000486) [D-NY]

```
       HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF REVEREND LAMONT S. GRANBY

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. RITCHIE TORRES

                              of new york

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 22, 2026

  Mr. TORRES of New York. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life 
and legacy of Reverend Lamont S. Granby, a faithful pastor, funeral 
director, community leader, mentor, husband, father, and grandfather.
  Reverend Lamont S. Granby was born on March 19, 1962, in New York 
City, the only son of Samuel Granby, Jr. and Ayris Granby. He was 
raised in the Bronx and New Rochelle, New York, where he was educated 
in the New Rochelle public school system. A gifted athlete, he swam on 
the New Rochelle High School swim team and played varsity basketball in 
his senior year.
  After graduating, he earned a Bachelor of Arts in Political Science 
from the University of Pittsburgh. There he joined the Epsilon Kappa 
Chapter of Phi Beta Sigma Fraternity, Inc., becoming a second-
generation Sigma man. Known for bringing people together through music, 
he became known for DJing college get-togethers as ``DJ ICE.'' It was 
during this time in college that he met Felita Yvette Ford, the love of 
his life, with whom he shared 38 beautiful years of marriage.
  In 1984, he answered the call to carry forward his family's legacy, 
attending the American Academy McAllister Institute of Funeral Service. 
Upon graduation, he joined the family business, Granby's Funeral 
Service, Inc. There he helped continue a tradition of compassionate 
care for families during some of life's most difficult moments. That 
same compassion, dignity, and concern for others would come to define 
his ministry.
  A son of the Bethesda Baptist Church of New Rochelle, Reverend Granby 
was licensed to preach on April 22, 1998. He earned his Master of 
Divinity from Drew University in 2001 and was ordained on February 16, 
2002, by the late Reverend Dr. Allen Paul Weaver, Jr. He served as 
Assistant to the Pastor under Dr. Weaver until he was called to pastor 
a church of his own.
  On March 28, 2010, Reverend Granby was installed as the 20th Senior 
Pastor of the historic First Baptist Church in Bronxville, New York. He 
was the first African American pastor in the church's more than 170-
year history. He took enormous pride in his congregation's diversity 
and the generations of families within it. To him, the church was more 
than a building; it was a family, a refuge, and a place where all were 
always welcome. He affectionately called it ``the little church with 
the big heart.''
  Reverend Granby's ministry reached far beyond the pulpit. He served 
as President of the 47th Precinct Clergy Coalition and as the lead NYPD 
Clergy Liaison for the 47th Precinct in the Bronx, working to 
strengthen relationships among clergy, law enforcement, and the 
community. A lifelong member of Phi Beta Sigma, he served as President 
and Chaplain of the Beta Psi Sigma Chapter, which serves Westchester 
County. He was Chaplain of the New York State Funeral Directors 
Association and a member of the Metropolitan Funeral Directors 
Association. He also served on many boards throughout Westchester 
County and the Bronx, including the Community Action Board of 
Montefiore Hospital and the College Careers Board in Westchester 
County. His service earned him countless awards, citations, and 
proclamations.
  The work closest to his heart was mentoring young men, both in the 
church and across the community. He gave them his time, his attention, 
and his example. He organized culturally enriching, fun-filled 
activities that encouraged fellowship, personal growth, and a sense of 
belonging, helping them recognize their potential and the value of 
character, responsibility, and service. Through Granby's Funeral 
Service, he also presented numerous scholarships to college-bound high 
school seniors throughout the New York metropolitan area. He was raised 
to believe in giving back in any way possible, and he lived that belief 
every day.
  More than any title or accomplishment, Reverend Granby cherished his 
family. He and Felita welcomed their first child, Martine Nicole, on 
July 31, 1983. They were married on September 10, 1988, at St. 
Catherine A.M.E. Zion Church in New Rochelle, and went on to welcome 
their son, Kyle Nicholas, on June 11, 1989, and their youngest 
daughter, Jordenn Nicollette, on May 19, 1993. Their three children 
were his greatest source of pride, and he loved watching each of them 
find their own path. That pride extended to the people his children 
loved, and he celebrated the ways each of them served their communities 
and pursued work that mattered.
  He cherished his three grandchildren, Lilie-Rae, Charlotte-Rose, and 
Chase. Watching them grow, celebrating every milestone, and savoring 
everyday moments.
  Reverend Granby believed that leadership is not about the positions 
one holds, but about

[[Page E1122]]

the lives one touches and the love one leaves behind. His legacy lives 
on in the congregation he shepherded, the young men and women he 
mentored, the students he helped send to college, the families he 
served, his fraternity brothers, and most of all, the family he loved 
so dearly.
  He leaves his wife, Felita Ford Granby; his children, Martine, Kyle, 
and Jordenn; his grandchildren, Lilie-Rae, Charlotte-Rose, and Chase; 
his parents, Samuel Granby, Jr. and Ayris Granby; his sister, Ayris; 
his aunt, Catherine Holley; his uncle, Howard Evans; and a host of 
cousins, extended family, dear friends, and fraternity brothers to 
cherish his memory. I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring the life 
and legacy of Reverend Lamont S. Granby. May he rest in peace, and may 
God comfort and strengthen his family.
```
