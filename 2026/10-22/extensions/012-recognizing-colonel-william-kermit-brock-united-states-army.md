# RECOGNIZING COLONEL WILLIAM KERMIT BROCK, UNITED STATES ARMY, RETIRED, WITH A CONGRESSIONAL VETERAN COMMENDATION

**Speaking:** Sessions, Pete (S000250) [R-TX]

```
RECOGNIZING COLONEL WILLIAM KERMIT BROCK, UNITED STATES ARMY, RETIRED, 
               WITH A CONGRESSIONAL VETERAN COMMENDATION

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. PETE SESSIONS

                                of texas

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 22, 2026

  Mr. SESSIONS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Colonel William 
Kermit Brock of Waco, Texas, with a 2026 Congressional Veteran 
Commendation. William's distinguished 33-year career as a chaplain in 
the United States Army and Army Reserve, years of service to fellow 
veterans and the Waco community, and lifelong commitment to the next 
generation exemplify the character, leadership, and dedication worthy 
of this recognition.
  Born in Darke County, Ohio, and raised in northern Indiana as the 
youngest of five brothers in a farm family, William graduated from a 
small high school with a class of only fourteen students. Two of 
William's older brothers were drafted into the Navy during World War 
II, so military service was part of his family long before it became 
his own. William attended Indiana Central College, now the University 
of Indianapolis, and then United Theological Seminary in Dayton, Ohio. 
When a fellow seminary student invited William to fill out the 
paperwork for the Army chaplaincy, his application was approved, and he 
was commissioned on September 4, 1958. After serving a church in 
northern Indiana for two years, William came on active duty in 1961 as 
the Berlin crisis led the Army to expand its chaplain program.
  William's service took him across the country and around the world. 
William began at Fort Riley, Kansas, with the 1st Infantry Division, 
and then served in Germany, with rotations to Wildflecken and Berlin in 
the years just after the Berlin Wall went up. In Berlin, William 
counseled soldiers, led Sunday school and youth programs, and visited 
the stockade each week to show films to the prisoners. William later 
served with an air defense missile battalion in Giessen, Germany, then 
at Fort Carson, Colorado, and in Korea as the support command chaplain. 
At Fort Myer, Virginia, William conducted funerals at Arlington 
National Cemetery every weekday, more than 580 in all, standing with 
families on some of the hardest days of their lives.
  In 1972 and 1973, William served in Vietnam as the senior chaplain 
for Military Region Three. As other chaplains left the country, William 
became the only chaplain covering roughly one-fourth of the land area 
of Vietnam, traveling by helicopter on weekends to reach small outposts 
where soldiers had no one else to turn to. William led the memorial 
service for a fellow service member, William Nolde, who is remembered 
in the history books as the last American casualty of the Vietnam War. 
When

[[Page E1110]]

the peace agreement was signed in January 1973, William was among the 
last Americans to leave, and he believes he was the last chaplain to 
depart the region.
  After Vietnam, William served as the only chaplain at Savanna Army 
Depot in Illinois and later ministered to soldiers at sixteen Nike 
Hercules missile sites and a Pershing missile site in Germany. 
Following active duty, William continued to serve in the Army Reserve, 
including as group chaplain for a Civil Affairs unit with subordinate 
units across Texas and Missouri. William retired in 1991 as a Colonel 
after 33 years of service.
  William's commitment to service did not end with the uniform. William 
went on to a career as a chaplain with the Department of Veterans 
Affairs, spending four years in Miami, Florida, before moving to Waco, 
Texas. In Waco, William worked alongside counselors in the early days 
of the post-traumatic stress program, helping veterans, many of them 
Vietnam veterans, work through guilt and grief at a time when the 
Nation was only beginning to understand what they carried home.
  William has also given generously to the community through talents 
learned during military service. William learned to perform magic 
tricks and to play the bagpipes, and he continued to share both long 
after retirement, performing at funerals, parades, and other occasions. 
William served as a Scoutmaster with Scouting America, and as a past 
commander of the Military Order of the World Wars, he has sponsored 
students in the organization's leadership training program, investing 
in the young leaders who will carry our country forward.
  William's family has been central to his life of service. William and 
his wife, Evelyn, raised two daughters, Karen and Cheryl, through many 
moves and assignments at home and abroad. Their family exemplifies the 
sacrifice and strength of the military families who stand behind every 
servicemember.
  William's life is ultimately defined not simply by the honors 
received, but by the way he has continued to serve, mentor, and comfort 
others. Asked what young people should take from his story, William 
spoke with humility of the camaraderie of fellow chaplains and 
encouraged young Americans not to fear military service. From the front 
lines of the Cold War and Vietnam to the graveside and the hospital 
ward, William has demonstrated an unwavering sense of duty and devotion 
to country.
  It is this extraordinary record of military service, ministry, 
mentorship, and volunteerism that I proudly commend Colonel William 
Kermit Brock of Texas to friends, family, community, and country. It is 
my honor to recognize William with a 2026 Congressional Veteran 
Commendation from the 17th Congressional District of Texas.
```
