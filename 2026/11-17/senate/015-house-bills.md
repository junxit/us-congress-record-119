# HOUSE BILLS

**Speaking:** Schmitt, Eric (S001227) [R-MO]

**Measures:** H.R. 5254, H.R. 8454

```
                              HOUSE BILLS

  Mr. SCHMITT. Mr. President, I rise today to speak about a monument 
that is near and dear to my heart in my hometown of St. Louis. The 
Gateway Arch stands as one of the most recognizable monuments in the 
United States, a soaring testament to the American spirit, a monument 
to the restless ambition of a people who refuse to be tied down, a 
people who looked into the void and saw more than danger. They saw 
destiny.
  In our country's then short history, Meriwether Lewis and William 
Clark were those men who stared into the unknown. From that fearless 
determination and restless spirit came the America we know today: the 
great rivers; the cities of the Midwest; the railroads, factories, 
farms, and settlements that would rise across an entire continent.
  The mighty Mississippi became America's first economic superhighway, 
carrying on her back the commerce of a growing republic.
  And at the center of this great national undertaking was my hometown 
of St. Louis, the ``Gateway to the West.''
  The Gateway Arch stands as a permanent monument to that uniquely 
American story. It was completed in 1965, built by another generation 
of Americans, who believed that the achievements of their ancestors 
were worthy of remembrance.
  It was 1965. Think about that. At a time when Americans were reaching 
for the Moon, they were also reaching into their past, erecting a 
monument to the pioneers who had carried the American flag across a 
continent.
  A truly confident nation honors its inheritance precisely because it 
intends to build something worthy of it. The arch represents not the 
dominion over an entire continent but the character of a people who 
have built a civilization upon it.
  In my hometown, the people of St. Louis continue to prove just how 
much the monument means to them through the CityArchRiver Project, one 
of the largest private investments in the national park to date.
  The Gateway Arch National Park is now, more than ever, a place to 
meet, gather, and to celebrate. That is why I introduced the Gateway 
Partnership Act.
  This bipartisan legislation, which I am proud to lead, is a 
commonsense measure that strengthens the partnership between the 
National Park Service and its official philanthropic partner, the 
Gateway Arch Park Foundation. The foundation has already demonstrated 
its commitment to preserving and improving this extraordinary national 
landmark. This modest piece of legislation would allow that partnership 
to continue in a new and meaningful way.
  If enacted, this would authorize the National Park Service's ability 
to enter into a defined agreement with the Gateway Arch Park Foundation 
to manage, market, and execute private events at Gateway Arch National 
Park. It would create opportunities for gatherings and events that 
bring people into closer contact with one of America's greatest 
monuments, while supporting the stewardship of the park itself. It 
would allow Park Service personnel to actually focus on the park and 
highlight a stellar public-private partnership.
  It does not surrender control of the park. It does not diminish its 
national significance. And it doesn't abandon the obligation to 
preserve this landmark for future generations.
  It simply recognizes the responsible private partnership that can 
help strengthen the institutions and places that belong to the American 
people.
  I ask my colleagues to support this bipartisan legislation and help 
preserve the ``Gateway to the West'' for generations to come.
  Notwithstanding rule XXII, I ask unanimous consent that the Senate 
proceed to the immediate consideration of the following bills en bloc: 
Calendar No. 617, H.R. 8454, Ackerson Meadow Land Exchange; and 
Calendar No. 662, H.R. 5254, Gateway Partnership Act.
  There being no objection, the Senate proceeded to consider the bills 
en bloc.
  Mr. SCHMITT. Mr. President, I further ask that the bills be 
considered read a third time and passed and that the motions to 
reconsider be considered made and laid upon the table with no 
intervening action or debate, all en bloc.
```
