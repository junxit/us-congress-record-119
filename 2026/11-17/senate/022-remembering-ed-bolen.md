# REMEMBERING ED BOLEN

**Speaking:** Marshall, Roger (M001198) [R-KS]

```
                          REMEMBERING ED BOLEN

  Mr. MARSHALL. Mr. President, I rise today to honor the life and 
legacy of Ed Bolen, a distinguished aviation leader and proud Kansan 
whose decades of service helped shape the future of our Nation's 
aviation industry.
  Born and raised in Salina, KS, Bolen developed a lifelong passion for 
aviation at an early age. His father, who served on the board of the 
Salina Regional Airport, introduced him to flying. Bolen earned a 
degree in economics from the University of Kansas, a law degree from 
Tulane University, and a master of laws degree from Georgetown 
University Law Center. Early in his career, he served as legislative 
director for U.S. Senator Nancy Kassebaum, playing an instrumental role 
in securing passage of the General Aviation Revitalization Act of 1994.
  For more than three decades, Bolen dedicated himself to advancing 
American aviation. He joined the General Aviation Manufacturers 
Association in 1995 and became its president and CEO in 1996. In 2004, 
he assumed the same role at the National Business Aviation Association, 
where he served for 22 years. Under his leadership, NBAA became a 
leading voice for business aviation, advocating for aviation safety, 
airport and airspace access, air traffic control modernization, and 
workforce development. He also championed sustainable aviation fuel and 
worked to protect the Nation's air traffic control system from 
privatization.
  Beyond his leadership in the aviation industry, Bolen served our 
Nation through several public appointments. He was nominated by 
President Clinton and confirmed by the Senate to serve on the Federal 
Aviation Administration's Management Advisory Council, which he chaired 
from 2000 to 2004. President George W. Bush later appointed him to the 
Commission on the Future of the U.S. Aerospace Industry.
  Yet Bolen's legacy extended far beyond his professional 
accomplishments. Known throughout the aviation community for his 
kindness and humility, he left a lasting impression on those he met. 
His dedication to aviation and to Kansas was recognized just one day 
before his passing, when he was inducted into the Kansas Aviation Hall 
of Fame.
  Though Ed Bolen will be greatly missed, his legacy will endure 
through the countless individuals he mentored and his many 
contributions to American aviation. He is survived by his wife Beth and 
their two sons Jack and Robert.
  I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring the life and legacy of Ed 
Bolen and in expressing our gratitude for his profound impact on Kansas 
and American aviation.
```
