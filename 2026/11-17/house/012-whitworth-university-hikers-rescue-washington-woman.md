# WHITWORTH UNIVERSITY HIKERS RESCUE WASHINGTON WOMAN

**Speaking:** Baumgartner, Michael (B001322) [R-WA]

```
                              {time}  1230
          WHITWORTH UNIVERSITY HIKERS RESCUE WASHINGTON WOMAN

  (Mr. Baumgartner of Washington was recognized to address the House 
for 5 minutes.)
  Mr. BAUMGARTNER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize three 
Whitworth University patriots and students, Nathan Elfers, Noah Mitton, 
and Solomon Ward, whose quick thinking and courage helped save a 
woman's life in the Okanogan-Wenatchee National Forest.
  This is an inspiring story. On October 18, while hiking the 
Enchantments in eastern Washington, these young men came across a woman 
stranded off the trail, weak, disoriented, and unable to stand after 
experiencing a sudden drop in blood sugar.
  Without hesitation, these patriotic young men stepped in to help. 
Working together, the group provided her with water and supplies, sent 
an emergency SOS, and built a makeshift stretcher using hiking poles, 
ropes, and other equipment to help carry her down the mountain to 
safety.
  Faced with difficult terrain and dropping temperatures, these 
students remained calm, relied on their training, and put someone 
else's well-being ahead of their own.
  Last week, my staff had the opportunity to present Nathan, Noah, and 
Solomon with certificates and letters of recognition on my behalf. I am 
proud to recognize their courage, compassion, and willingness to step 
up when it mattered most. I think the greatest compliment, I or any 
parent in America, could give to these three young men is to hope our 
own children would respond in such a brave, heroic and most worthy way 
if they were faced with the same situation.
  Mr. Speaker, I congratulate these young men for doing the right 
thing.


                        Great American Road Trip

  Mr. BAUMGARTNER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize two eastern 
Washington residents who took the opportunity to celebrate what makes 
America so special through an incredible cross-country journey.
  Greg and Daralyn Hollenbeck of Pend Oreille County participated in 
the Department of Transportation's Great American Road Trip initiative, 
which encouraged Americans to explore our Nation's highways, historic 
landmarks, and communities in recognition of the

[[Page H6234]]

250th celebration of the greatest country on God's green Earth.
  Over the course of 42 days, Greg and Daralyn traveled more than 9,100 
miles, visiting 23 States and documenting their journey through photos 
and a written journal.
  Having traveled around the world, they understand just how special 
this country is. Along the way, they met people from all walks of life, 
experienced different regional cultures, and were welcomed by 
communities eager to share what makes their part of America special.
  Their biggest takeaway was simple: Despite our differences in 
backgrounds, geography, and way of life, we are all united as 
Americans. Their story is a great reminder of the people, places, and 
shared values that make this country worth celebrating and is indeed a 
fitting tribute to the 250th year of our Republic.


             Recognizing Private First Class Joseph E. Mann

  Mr. BAUMGARTNER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today in great pride to honor 
Private First Class Joseph E. Mann, a Medal of Honor recipient who was 
born and raised in Reardan, Washington.
  Joe attended Reardan High School before enlisting in the Army in 
1942. He went on to serve with the 502nd Parachute Infantry Regiment, 
part of the legendary 101st Airborne Division.
  During Operation Market Garden in September 1944, Joe demonstrated 
extraordinary courage under fire, single-handedly destroying an enemy 
position while fighting in the Netherlands. Despite being wounded, he 
continued to serve. The following morning, during an enemy attack, he 
sacrificed his own life by throwing himself on a hand grenade to 
protect his fellow soldiers.
  I am going to say that again: He sacrificed his own life by throwing 
himself on a hand grenade to protect his fellow soldiers.
  For his extraordinary heroism, Private First Class Mann was 
posthumously awarded the Medal of Honor on August 30, 1945. His legacy 
also lives on in Spokane, where the Mann-Grandstaff VA Medical Center 
bears his name.
  Today, members of the Reardan community, including descendants of the 
Mann family, are working to establish the PFC Joe E. Mann and Area 
Veterans Memorial. Led by Gail Daniels and Wayne Terry, the project 
will include a veterans walkway, community amphitheater, eternal flame, 
and educational displays honoring Mann and so many other veterans from 
eastern Washington.
  Mr. Speaker, I commend Gail, Wayne, the Mann family, and all those in 
the Reardan community in eastern Washington. I commend them for their 
efforts to preserve this legacy. We have an obligation to remember the 
extraordinary Americans who gave everything in defense of our Nation, 
and I encourage every patriot across the country to support this 
important memorial. Indeed, all of us should know the story and the 
name of Private Mann.
```
