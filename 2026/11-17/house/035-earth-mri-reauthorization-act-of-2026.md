                               H.R. 9640

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Earth MRI Reauthorization 
     Act of 2026''.

     SEC. 2. EARTH MAPPING RESOURCES INITIATIVE REAUTHORIZATION.

       (a) Purpose.--Section 40201(c)(1) of the Infrastructure 
     Investment and Jobs Act (43 U.S.C. 31l(c)(1)) is amended by 
     inserting ``, which may include data derived from modern 
     sensing technologies, including advanced geophysical data 
     acquisition approaches such as airborne gravity gradiometry, 
     digital geochemistry and other advanced geochemical 
     characterization methods, real-time or near real-time data 
     acquisition systems, and data-driven analytical methods to 
     improve the characterization of critical mineral, geothermal, 
     geologic hydrogen, and other subsurface resource systems'' 
     after ``geophysical mapping''.
       (b) Integration of Data Sources.--Section 40201(g) of the 
     Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (43 U.S.C. 31l(g)) is 
     amended--
       (1) by redesignating paragraph (5) as paragraph (6);
       (2) in paragraph (4), by striking ``and'' at the end; and
       (3) by inserting after paragraph (4) the following:
       ``(5) the 3D Hydrography Program of the United States 
     Geological Survey; and''.
       (c) Reauthorization.--Section 40201(h) of the 
     Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (43 U.S.C. 31l(h)) is 
     amended by inserting ``and

[[Page H6245]]

     $84,000,000 for the period of fiscal years 2027 through 
     2031'' after ``2022 through 2026''.
       (d) Contracts.--Section 40201 of the Infrastructure 
     Investment and Jobs Act (43 U.S.C. 31l) is amended by adding 
     at the end the following:
       ``(i) Contracts.--
       ``(1) Preference given to certain entities.--In entering 
     into contracts pursuant to carrying out the Initiative, the 
     Director of the United States Geological Survey shall, to the 
     extent practicable, give preference to United States-owned 
     and United States-controlled entities while ensuring that the 
     selection process appropriately considers specialized 
     expertise essential to the objectives of the Initiative.
       ``(2) Disclosure of ownership.--To enter into a contract 
     with the Director of the United States Geological Survey 
     pursuant to the Initiative, an entity shall disclose the 
     ownership of the primary contractor and each subcontractor 
     expected to carry out any part of the contract.''.

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  I rise today in support of H.R. 9640, the Earth MRI Reauthorization 
Act of 2026.
  Since its conceptualization in 2019 under the Trump administration 
and Congress' authorization of the program in 2021, the Earth Mapping 
Resources Initiative has been a critical tool for the United States 
Geological Survey, industry, States, and Tribal governments.
  Congress created this program to address growing concerns over 
vulnerabilities and critical mineral supply chains. If the United 
States wants to secure its critical mineral supply chains, it must have 
robust data to understand the Nation's surface and subsurface 
resources.
  The Earth MRI program has successfully achieved the goals Congress 
outlined in its initial authorization. The USGS has quadrupled the 
Nation's geophysical survey data coverage since its inception. The USGS 
estimates that it is on track to collect high-resolution data for more 
than half of the areas of the U.S. with known potential for critical 
mineral deposits by the end of 2026 and to complete the initial 
comprehensive national surface and subsurface map by the 2031 deadline.
  The data collected through the Earth MRI program has many practical 
applications beyond mineral exploration including studying water 
resources, identifying sand and gravel deposits for infrastructure 
development, locating geothermal reservoirs, mapping natural hazards, 
characterizing mine wastes, and identifying orphan wells. Earth MRI and 
its partners are investing in State geologic surveys and rebuilding a 
skilled geoscience workforce.
  Industry, State and Tribal governments, and universities have widely 
applauded the program for filling a critical gap in national mapping 
efforts, forging invaluable partnerships, and making this data publicly 
accessible.
  H.R. 9640 is an important step toward advancing American energy 
security goals by providing the data needed to manage our surface, 
subsurface, and mineral estate. Reauthorizing the Earth MRI program 
will ensure America can build strong domestic critical mineral supply 
chains and make informed decisions about the responsible development of 
our vast natural resources.
  The bipartisan legislation reauthorizes the Earth MRI program through 
fiscal year 2031. It builds on House Republicans' commitment to 
strengthen domestic energy production and protect our national and 
economic security while ensuring American industrial competitiveness.
  I thank my colleague Mr. Wittman for his leadership on this important 
issue, and I urge all my colleagues to join me in support of H.R. 9640.

  I rise in support of the Earth MRI Reauthorization Act of 2026.
  The Earth Mapping Resources Initiative, or Earth MRI, was established 
in 2019 to modernize geologic, geophysical, and geochemical mapping 
across the United States through partnerships with States, Tribes, 
academia, and industry.
  The program provides the high-quality data we need to better 
understand the critical mineral resources beneath our feet. It is an 
essential program in helping to develop a secure domestic critical 
mineral supply chain and understand these resources.
  In 2021, Democrats secured additional funding for the program in the 
Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act, which allowed Earth MRI to 
scale up. It provided $320 million over 5 years and allowed the USGS 
and State geologic surveys to map new regions of the country and to 
collect modern data in regions where existing geologic maps are decades 
old.
  With the capacity built through this investment, Earth MRI data are 
now being used to understand critical mineral potential not only below 
the ground but above the ground in mine waste sites, contributing to 
the USGS Mine Waste Inventory published in January, as we seek to be 
good stewards of our lands and make full use of the infrastructure and 
resources that are already accessible to us.
  However, the benefits don't stop at critical minerals. These data 
inform decisions on infrastructure and land-use planning; hazard 
assessments for earthquakes, landslides, volcanoes, and floods; and 
water resources management.
  H.R. 9640 would not only reauthorize a portion of this investment but 
also build upon it by creating opportunities to further extend Earth 
MRI's value beyond mineral potential and provide foundational data 
supporting assessments of emerging energy resources like geothermal and 
geologic hydrogen.

why passing this legislation is so critical.
