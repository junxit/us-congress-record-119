# REMEMBERING REPRESENTATIVE JIM McDERMOTT

**Speaking:** Jayapal, Pramila (J000298) [D-WA]

```
                REMEMBERING REPRESENTATIVE JIM McDERMOTT

  (Ms. JAYAPAL asked and was given permission to address the House for 
1 minute.)
  Ms. JAYAPAL. Mr. Speaker, I rise to honor the life and legacy of 
former Congressman Jim McDermott, who represented Washington's Seventh 
Congressional District for 28 years. Congressman McDermott passed away 
last Thursday at the age of 89, but his legacy will never be forgotten.
  Congressman McDermott first served the country as a U.S. Navy 
psychiatrist, treating soldiers and sailors during the Vietnam War. 
Then, later, he served in our State legislature where he passed what is 
now called the Washington State Basic Health Plan, the first State 
program in the country to offer health insurance to the unemployed and 
working poor.
  In Congress, he was a proud member of the Congressional Progressive 
Caucus and continued his fierce advocacy for universal healthcare, 
including on the Ways and Means Committee. He also championed global 
health and development, leading the passage of the African Growth and 
Opportunity Act.
  Jim used every opportunity that he had to fight for working families 
and vulnerable people, standing up for collective bargaining rights, 
living wages, affordable housing, mental health services, and the 
dignity of working people.
  He was a vocal opponent against the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan, 
believing that diplomacy must come before war and presciently 
predicting that no weapons of mass destruction would be found.
  I got to know him in the immediate aftermath of September 11, when 
there

[[Page H6252]]

was a surge of hate crimes and civil liberties abuses against those in 
the Sikh, Muslim, and Arab communities.
  Jim did what he always did: immediately stood up for the rights and 
liberties of all people and helped organize an immediate press 
conference--that many of our Members here were at--to declare 
Washington a hate-free State.
  He was also a vocal champion for comprehensive immigration reform and 
for the Immigrant Workers Freedom Ride in 2003.
  Jim was an inspiration, a friend, and a mentor to so many of us in 
the Washington delegation and here in Congress.
  On behalf of our delegation and the many friends that he served with 
here in Congress, we extend our deepest condolences to his family, his 
loved ones, and all in the Seventh Congressional District who are 
mourning his loss and celebrating his legacy.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in a moment of silence to 
honor Congressman Jim McDermott's remarkable life and his enduring 
contributions to our community and to our country.
```
