# CALLING FOR FEDERAL EMPLOYEE PAY RAISE

**Speaking:** Subramanyam, Suhas (S001230) [D-VA]

```
                 CALLING FOR FEDERAL EMPLOYEE PAY RAISE

  (Mr. Subramanyam of Virginia was recognized to address the House for 
5 minutes.)
  Mr. SUBRAMANYAM. Mr. Speaker, the DOGE branding might be gone, but 
the Federal workers and contractors continue to be attacked 
relentlessly by this administration.
  For instance, a Federal employee at one office in my district said 
that they are being forced to work overtime and on holidays, like 
Veterans Day, to make up for shortages that DOGE created. Morale is 
cratering across agencies in our Federal Government.
  Now, the administration is denying our Federal civil servants their 
annual cost-of-living adjustments. This is at a time when the cost of 
living continues to skyrocket. This is absolutely unacceptable. It is 
bad not just for our Federal workers, but it is bad for the American 
people because we won't be able to hire and retain the most talented 
people to our Federal workforce.
  It will hurt service delivery, drive the best people out of our 
agencies, and make sure that we can't fulfill the promises we have made 
to the American taxpayers.
  Mr. Speaker, we must pay the Federal employees who work every single 
day to serve our country. Congress must pass a raise for Federal 
employees this year.


                 Honoring the Life of Asbury Lloyd, Jr.

  Mr. SUBRAMANYAM. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life of 
Asbury Lloyd, Jr., a devoted public servant, decorated Korean war 
veteran, and proud Virginian who passed away in October.
  Born in Philomont in 1928, Asbury dedicated his life to community and 
civic responsibility.
  In 1952, shortly after marrying his wife, Sarah, he was drafted into 
the United States Army. His bravery earned him the Bronze Star and 
later, in 2016, the South Korean Ambassador for Peace Medal.
  He was always a great athlete, taking the mound as the pitcher for 
the Middleburg Braves. He served his church as a chairman of the deacon 
board and served his community as a community organizer.
  Mr. Speaker, Asbury Lloyd, Jr.'s legacy of grace, resilience, and 
service lives on through the countless lives and communities that he 
touched. My deepest condolences go out to his family, and may he rest 
in peace.


                     Never Let a Crisis Go to Waste

  Mr. SUBRAMANYAM. Mr. Speaker, never let a crisis go to waste. That is 
the motto of this administration.
  We just had a major crisis, an airport disruption across the country, 
and over 9,000 flights were grounded on Friday alone. Rather than take 
responsibility, Secretary Duffy was quick to blame Congress, while also 
asking for an additional $50 billion. That is $50 billion.
  Remember that we didn't have $50 billion for food assistance or for 
healthcare, but the Secretary wants us to find that money to modernize 
the air traffic system. Let's remember that Congress just gave them $5 
billion for FAA telecom modernizations and system upgrades as part of a 
broader $12.5 billion FAA package.
  They have only spent $1 billion of those dollars. Nearly all of it 
has gone unspent, so now I am wondering: Why are they asking for this 
now when they have shown that they can't even spend the money that they 
have?
  What is more, the FAA problem that occurred on Friday was actually a 
configuration issue. Network line upgrades happen all the time, but it 
is clear that there wasn't due diligence here.
  I am calling for a moratorium on all nonvital network maintenance 
through 2026 to make sure that this does not happen again during this 
holiday season. I am calling on the FAA not to fire any more staff who 
keep our airspace safe and to make sure that we have the right 
expertise to implement these modernization efforts.
  No more surprises. No more sloppy tech updates. No more ducking the 
hard questions. I want a full investigation and action report about 
what happened. We need tighter communications between the FAA and their 
contractors, and we need a Secretary who has a clue.
  Mr. Speaker, let's come together, find real solutions to this 
problem, and not give the administration another blank check.
```
