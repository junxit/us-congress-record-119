# BARONA GROUP OF CAPITAN GRANDE BAND OF MISSION INDIANS LAND TRANSFER ACT OF 2026

**Speaking:** Westerman, Bruce (W000821) [R-AR], Stansbury, Melanie A. (S001218) [D-NM], Issa, Darrell (I000056) [R-CA]

**Measures:** H.R. 8483

```
 BARONA GROUP OF CAPITAN GRANDE BAND OF MISSION INDIANS LAND TRANSFER 
                              ACT OF 2026

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and pass the 
bill (H.R. 8483) to take certain land in the State of California into 
trust for the benefit of the Barona Group of Capitan Grande Band of 
Mission Indians of the Barona Reservation, California, and for other 
purposes, as amended.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 8483

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Barona Group of Capitan 
     Grande Band of Mission Indians Land Transfer Act of 2026''.

     SEC. 2. LAND TO BE TAKEN INTO TRUST FOR THE BARONA GROUP OF 
                   CAPITAN GRANDE BAND OF MISSION INDIANS.

       (a) In General.--Subject to valid existing rights and the 
     conditions described in subsections (c) and (d)--
       (1) administrative jurisdiction of Parcels 2 and 3 is 
     transferred to the Secretary; and
       (2) the covered land is taken into trust for the benefit of 
     the Tribe.
       (b) Administration.--
       (1) In general.--The land taken into trust under subsection 
     (a) shall be--
       (A) part of the reservation of the Tribe; and
       (B) administered in accordance with the laws and 
     regulations generally applicable to property held in trust by 
     the United States for the benefit of an Indian Tribe.
       (2) Forest service access.--The Forest Service shall retain 
     administrative vehicular access across the land transferred 
     under this Act.
       (3) Minor adjustments.--The Secretary and the Secretary of 
     Agriculture may, by mutual agreement, make minor corrections 
     and adjustments to the land transfer under this Act to 
     facilitate land management, including corrections and 
     adjustments to any applicable maps or surveys.
       (c) Maps and Boundaries.--
       (1) Official map.--Before the transfer of administrative 
     jurisdiction under subsection (a), the Secretary and the 
     Secretary of Agriculture shall jointly prepare, review, 
     approve, and maintain an official map depicting the covered 
     land taken into trust.
       (2) Map on file.--The official map prepared under paragraph 
     (1) shall be on file and available for public inspection in 
     the appropriate offices of the Department of the Interior and 
     Department of Agriculture.
       (d) Access.--The land taken into trust under subsection (a) 
     shall be subject to--
       (1) all valid liens, rights-of-way, reciprocal road rights-
     of-way agreements, licenses, leases, permits, special use 
     permits, and easements existing on the date of the enactment 
     of this Act;
       (2) the continuing right of the public to access the land 
     for recreational, scenic, scientific, and conservation uses, 
     to motorized access to roads, and to recreational access to 
     trails, including Bureau of Land Management and Forest 
     Service trails codesignated for public passage, existing on 
     the date of the enactment of this Act, subject to rules and 
     regulations in effect on the date of the enactment of this 
     Act;
       (3) such rights-of-way as the Secretary, in consultation 
     with the Secretary of Agriculture, considers necessary to 
     provide employees of the Bureau of Land Management and the 
     Forest Service access for operational and emergency purposes, 
     including--
       (A) search and rescue;
       (B) wildfire suppression;

[[Page H6241]]

       (C) health safety evacuation operations; and
       (D) necessary administration purposes such as resource 
     management, maintenance, fuels management, monitoring, and 
     law enforcement activities;
       (4) the authority of the Secretary and the Secretary of 
     Agriculture, in consultation and coordination with the Tribe, 
     to carry out fuels reduction or other landscape restoration 
     activities on the land, including the maintenance of any fuel 
     break existing on the date of the enactment of this Act; and
       (5) upon request of the Secretary of Agriculture, the grant 
     by the Tribe or the Bureau of Indian Affairs, as appropriate, 
     without consideration, of such easements and rights-of-way as 
     are necessary to provide the Forest Service continued access 
     to the land for the purposes described in paragraphs (3) and 
     (4).
       (e) Maintenance of Fuel Break.--The Tribe shall be 
     responsible for maintaining the fuel break within Parcels 2 
     and 3 in accordance with applicable Forest Service standards.
       (f) Gaming Prohibited.--The land taken into trust under 
     subsection (a) shall not be used for any class II gaming or 
     class III gaming under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act (as 
     those terms are defined in section 4 of that Act (25 U.S.C. 
     2703)).
       (g) Water Rights; Access.--Nothing in this Act shall be 
     construed to--
       (1) alter, or require the alteration of, any existing water 
     rights or service agreements; or
       (2) diminish existing Forest Service administrative access 
     across lands transferred under this Act.
       (h) Definitions.--In this Act:
       (1) Covered land.--The term ``covered land'' means all 
     right, title, and interest of the United States in and to 
     Parcel 1 and Parcels 2 and 3.
       (2) Parcel 1.--The term ``Parcel 1'' means the 
     approximately 164 acres of land generally depicted as 
     ``Bureau of Land Management Parcel 1 (164 Acres)'' on the 
     Transfer Map.
       (3) Parcels 2 and 3.--The term ``Parcels 2 and 3'' means 
     the approximately 683 acres of land generally depicted as 
     ``Forest Service Parcels 2 (168 acres) & 3 (515 acres)'' on 
     the Transfer Map, including the area depicted as ``Fuel 
     Break'' within the boundary of that area.
       (4) Secretary.--The term ``Secretary'' means the Secretary 
     of the Interior.
       (5) Transfer map.--The term ``Transfer Map'' means the map 
     titled ``Barona Land Transfer Proposal'' prepared by the 
     Forest Service.
       (6) Tribe.--The term ``Tribe'' means the Barona Group of 
     Capitan Grande Band of Mission Indians of the Barona 
     Reservation, California.

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise in support of H.R. 8483, the Barona Group of 
Capitan Grande Band of Mission Indians Land Transfer Act of 2026, 
introduced by the gentleman from California (Mr. Issa).
  The Barona Band of Mission Indians currently resides on the 7,500 
acre Barona Reservation in San Diego County, California. Originally, 
the Tribe's reservation spanned over 15,000 acres until the city of San 
Diego initiated a land sale in 1931 so it could build the El Capitan 
Reservoir. With the funds the Barona Band received as compensation for 
their former land, they purchased the Barona Reservation, where the 
Tribe resides today.
  However, the Barona Band continues to hold deep ancestral ties to a 
portion of their former reservation, where Barona Band members 
predominantly lived and buried their ancestors. The Tribe has long 
pursued reunification of their current reservation and their ancestral 
land.
  H.R. 8483 places approximately 847 acres of land into trust for the 
Barona Band, connecting the Tribe's current reservation to their 
ancestral lands. The Tribe has worked with the U.S. Forest Service and 
the Bureau of Land Management on the details of the land transfer. The 
land transfer will not impact or alter existing water rights and 
service agreements, and all valid, preexisting liens, rights-of-way, 
reciprocal road right-of-way agreements, licenses, leases, permits, and 
easements will remain unaltered.
  Mr. Speaker, I commend Mr. Issa for his work with the Barona Band, 
the U.S. Forest Service, and the Bureau of Land Management to develop 
this legislation, and I reserve the balance of my time.
  Ms. STANSBURY. Mr. Speaker, I rise in support of H.R. 8483, the 
Barona Group of Capitan Grande Band of Mission Indians Land Transfer 
Act, which would take approximately 847 acres of Federal land managed 
by the Bureau of Land Management and the United States Forest Service 
in southern California into trust for the benefit of the Barona Band of 
Mission Indians.

  Like many Tribal nations across the country, the Barona Band of 
Mission Indians experienced the loss and dispossession of their 
ancestral homelands. This history has had lasting impacts on the 
community.
  This bill would return ancestral lands with sacred sites, 
traditional-use areas, and important cultural resources to the Tribe so 
that they can steward the lands that have sustained their communities 
for generations.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support this legislation, and I 
reserve the balance of my time.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield 5 minutes to the gentleman from 
California (Mr. Issa), who is the lead sponsor of the bill.
  Mr. ISSA. Mr. Speaker, in the presentations of this bill, as often 
happens, the bill says that the Barona Group of Capitan Grande Band of 
Mission Indians would be the beneficiary, and that may be true. 
However, the real beneficiary is the Federal Government.
  Currently the Bureau of Land Management spends a not insignificant 
number of dollars every year and growing maintaining 847 acres of land 
that earns no money and whose value really belongs to the Native Tribes 
who have spent countless years not just living here but being buried 
here.
  This is a classic example of a win for the Federal Government and a 
return of land that once belonged to this band of Indians.
  Like many Tribes around the country, they have been displaced, but 
the Mission Indians were displaced completely. They were landless for a 
period of time. Only in the century before last did they begin getting 
land back, and, lo and behold, once they had their reservation, they 
were displaced again.
  For the Barona Band it really is a special opportunity to put 
together some semblance of what once had been their 15,000-plus acres.
  This is not the first time we have been before this committee, but it 
is similar in that we have worked with the Forest Service and the 
Bureau of Land Management to make sure that all fire easements and 
other activities that have taken place or could take place on this land 
will be retained, while at the same time, the Barona Tribe will take 
over the day-to-day management and maintenance of this facility.
  In our agreement, we went so far as to make sure that there is 
oversight by the Federal Government to ensure that that is taking place 
and a right of the Federal Government, if they are not satisfied, to 
re-intervene. Notwithstanding that, this land will remain in Federal 
hands in trust for the Barona Tribe in perpetuity to the benefit of 
both the American people and this band of the original American people.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask that the bill be considered.
  Ms. STANSBURY. Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support this 
legislation, and I yield back the balance of my time.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, H.R. 8483 resolves a decades-old 
separation of the Tribe from its former lands by transferring 
approximately 847 acres of Federal land into a trust for the Barona 
Group of Capitan Grande Band of Mission Indians.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support the bill. I thank my 
colleague Representative Issa for his hard work on it, and I yield back 
the balance of my time.
```
