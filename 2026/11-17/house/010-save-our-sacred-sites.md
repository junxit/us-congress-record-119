# SAVE OUR SACRED SITES

**Speaking:** Stansbury, Melanie A. (S001218) [D-NM]

```
                         SAVE OUR SACRED SITES

  (Ms. Stansbury of New Mexico was recognized to address the House for 
5 minutes.)
  Ms. STANSBURY. Mr. Speaker, there are places in this country that are 
more than land. They are sacred sites, hallowed spaces. They carry the 
stories and the footsteps of our ancestors, the histories of our 
communities, the waters that sustain our people, the landscapes that 
define who we are and that connect our past generations to the future 
generations yet to come.
  In New Mexico, these places have names: Mount Taylor, Chaco Canyon, 
the Pecos, the Chama; and these are the places that are currently under 
attack by an administration that sees these lands as nothing more than 
an opportunity to give away to multinational corporations for mining 
and oil and gas drilling.
  These are the places that are currently at stake due to a bill that 
is under consideration on the Senate floor as I stand here. That is why 
I am standing here today, to say ``no'' to the bipartisan 
infrastructure bill that is in front of the Senate, because these 
places are irreplaceable. Tribal sovereignty is nonnegotiable, and the 
future of our communities and their stories cannot be put on the line.
  These are our public lands. They are our watersheds. They are the 
ancestral homelands of the communities that have called them home since 
time immemorial, and these are the places that we could lose if this 
bipartisan bill passes on the Senate floor.
  Now, there is no question that the system is broken and that America 
must rebuild our infrastructure. That means building transmission. That 
means bringing clean energy online. That means modernizing our 
infrastructure and our grid, but that cannot be the building of 
infrastructure and transmission at any cost.
  We cannot forget what is at stake because behind every permit is a 
place, behind every permit is a community, and behind every decision 
that this body makes is a people who will face the consequences long 
after those of us who are standing here today are here.
  This conversation must ask what protections do we preserve, whose 
voices will we hear, and who will bear the consequences for the 
decisions that this body will make this week?
  This bill attacks the rights of our communities. Permitting reforms 
should not undermine the fundamental rights of our communities to have 
a say in the decisions that will affect their lives, their stories, 
their homes, and their sacred sites.
  That is why the NAACP, the ACLU, civil rights organizations, Tribal 
organizations, and attorneys general from almost a dozen States across 
the West have said ``no'' to this bill.
  This bill would undermine the Clean Water Act, the National 
Environmental Policy Act, and the Endangered Species Act, bedrock 
environmental

[[Page H6233]]

laws that have stood for decades and generations to be a backstop 
against the destruction of our planet.
  That is why The Wilderness Society, Sierra Club, Defenders of 
Wildlife, and our Tribal nations are saying ``no'' to this bill.
  This bill would undermine the fundamentals of Tribal sovereignty and 
the ability of our Tribal nations to protect cultural and sacred sites, 
and that is why hundreds of Tribal nations and organizations are saying 
``no'' to this bill.
  Why are our colleagues trying to push this bill in a lameduck 
session? Why are they trying to push it through at the moment that the 
President is trying to build an arch that would desecrate the hallowed 
ground of Arlington National Cemetery, and why are they so keen to do 
it before we are about to take a historic majority?
  We say ``no.'' We know this country must be continuing to move 
forward, but this is not the time.
  There is a difference between building infrastructure and progress, 
moving this country forward and doing it at any cost, doing it on the 
backs of our people, the backs of our heritage, the backs of our 
culture, the backs of our Tribal nations, and on our public lands that 
are here not just for us today, but for future generations.
  That is, Mr. Speaker, why I am here today, to say ``no'' to the 
permitting bill that is in the Senate, to say ``no'' to the Bipartisan 
American Affordability and Jobs Act, and to call on my colleagues to 
vote ``no.''

                          ____________________
```
