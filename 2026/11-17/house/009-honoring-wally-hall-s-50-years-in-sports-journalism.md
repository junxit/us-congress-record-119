          HONORING WALLY HALL'S 50 YEARS IN SPORTS JOURNALISM

  (Mr. Hill of Arkansas was recognized to address the House for 5 
minutes.)
  Mr. HILL of Arkansas. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize my 
friend Wally Hall and celebrate his remarkable 50-year career in sports 
journalism.
  In 1979, Wally became a sports columnist for the Arkansas Democrat, 
quickly establishing himself as one of Arkansas' most recognizable 
voices in sports journalism.
  For nearly five decades, Arkansans have turned to his ``Like It Is'' 
column for his candid opinions, sharp analysis, and unmistakable 
perspective on sports in Arkansas, particularly the Arkansas 
Razorbacks, and all the players, coaches, and personalities who have 
shaped Arkansas' athletics over the decades.
  His reporting has taken him to 6 Super Bowls, 3 Olympic Games, 2 
College World Series, 40 NCAA basketball tournaments, and 30 Final 
Fours. He has been named Arkansas sportswriter of the year 10 times, 
and earned a place in the Arkansas Sports Hall of Fame as well as the 
Arkansas Sportscasters and Sportswriters Hall of Fame.
  I congratulate Wally and thank him for giving Arkansans a reason to 
go first to that sports page for half a century.


              Recognizing America250 Essay Contest Winners

  Mr. HILL of Arkansas. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize the 
students across central Arkansas who participated in my America250 
Essay Contest.
  As our Nation celebrates its semiquincentennial, I invited students 
in grades 6-12 to reflect on what America means to them and the 
freedoms, values, and opportunities that make our country exceptional.
  I was encouraged by the thoughtful essays that I received and the 
patriotism and appreciation for our Nation's history expressed by these 
young students. Today, I want to recognize our three division winners.
  Our winner from the sixth and seventh grade division is Celine Boyd 
of Episcopal Collegiate School in Little Rock, who chose John Adams as 
her favorite Founding Father, writing: ``John Adams never gave up on 
what he believed. I admire him because he showed that serving America . 
. . means working hard, speaking honestly, and defending what is right 
even when it is difficult.''
  Our winner from the eighth and ninth grade division is Elena Porter 
of Mount St. Mary Academy in Little Rock, who reflected on what makes 
America exceptional, writing: ``Liberty provides Americans with the 
freedom to pursue their goals, the opportunity to succeed, and the 
protection for all of its citizens.''

strength has always been in its people and that the future of our great 
Nation rests with the next generation.
  I am proud of the students' winning essays, and I include them in the 
Congressional Record, ensuring that their words become a permanent part 
of our Nation's history.
  I congratulate our winners and thank every student who participated 
in this contest for helping us celebrate our semiquincentennial, 250 
years of American independence.

           John Adams--Not the First Name That Comes to Mind

       When learning about the Founding Fathers of our nation, 
     most of us remember George Washington for his leadership in 
     battle and as our first president, or Benjamin Franklin for 
     his inventions and for helping establish the U.S. postal 
     system.
       But fewer people speak of our second president, John Adams. 
     He is not featured today on any U.S. dollar bill, and, unlike 
     Washington and Jefferson, he is not carved into Mount 
     Rushmore. Yet John Adams caught my eye as a man of hard work 
     who fought for independence with his pen and wit in 
     Massachusetts, in the Continental Congress, across the sea in 
     Europe, and finally in the White House.
       Born on October 30, 1735, he grew up on the family farm as 
     the eldest son of John Adams Sr., a deacon, farmer, and 
     militia lieutenant, and Susanna Boylston. At only 16 years 
     old, he went to Harvard College, where he developed a passion 
     for law. In 1759, he was admitted to the bar and soon became 
     one of Boston's leading attorneys.
       Adams' opposition to the unfair Stamp Act, which taxed 
     Americans without representation, showed how he used his 
     knowledge of the law in the growing conflict with Britain. 
     For a long time, he had hoped the tensions between London and 
     the American colonies could be solved within the Empire. He 
     even defended the British soldiers who had opened fire in the 
     Boston Massacre. I admire this because it shows that Adams 
     cared about justice, even when it was unpopular.
       But Boston remained the center of unrest against British 
     attempts to control the colonies and tax their citizens 
     without proper representation, leading to the famous Boston 
     Tea Party. By that time, Adams had started a political career 
     that led to him representing Massachusetts at the Continental 
     Congress. He realized that Americans could secure their 
     rights only if they governed themselves.
       With his knowledge of the law and his powerful voice in 
     Congress, Adams became one of the leading supporters of 
     independence. He served on the committee that prepared the 
     Declaration of Independence and strongly defended it in 
     Congress. Without John Adams, the push for independence may 
     have been much weaker.
       His hard work for the young nation didn't stop there. 
     During the War of Independence, he served not on the front 
     line with musket in hand, but at a desk, with pen and paper, 
     helping organize and supply the Continental Army. He was then 
     sent to Europe as a diplomat to seek support and funds for 
     the United States. Upon his return, he was elected America's 
     first vice president for two terms, and then president for 
     one, before returning to his farm near Boston.
       John Adams never gave up on what he believed. I admire him 
     because he showed that serving America does not always mean 
     being the most famous person in the room. Sometimes it means 
     working hard, speaking honestly, and defending what is right, 
     even when it is difficult.

                          Keeping the Promise

       I believe that America is an exceptional nation because of 
     the set of values and ideals it was founded on rather than 
     being just a shared ethnicity. These ideals, such as liberty, 
     self-governance, and equality under God, serve as a promise 
     to serve and protect

[[Page H6232]]

     its citizens, and uphold its everlasting promise of rights. I 
     believe these ideals stand out because they are the very 
     foundation of this nation: they built this nation to be what 
     it is today. When you combine these principles with the 
     wealth of innovative ideas and vast diversity, it creates a 
     country that stands apart from other nations, a unique nation 
     with diverse cultures.
       The first and some may say the most important ideal is 
     liberty. This is the fundamental freedom that is promised to 
     all citizens since the very beginning of this nation. Liberty 
     provides Americans with the freedom to pursue their goals, 
     the opportunity to succeed, and protection for all of its 
     citizens. This freedom that is given to people lets them be 
     able to express their creativity and show their innovative 
     ideas. From liberty, we have inventions, independent 
     businesses, and many more advancements that move this country 
     forward to become an even better nation every day. Also, 
     liberty is everywhere, you may not know but it's what gives 
     us freedom of speech, expression, choice, and beliefs. It 
     creates a ``Nation of possibilities'', where America is free 
     to anyone regardless of background, and it gives them a 
     chance to succeed. It is a place to show everyone what they 
     can do, their talents, skills, and unique characteristics.
       The next ideal is self-governance, which is where the 
     people choose their own leaders and laws. Self-governance 
     entrusts the citizens with power and the freedom to choose 
     for themselves, ensuring that the power is in the hands of 
     the citizens rather than a ruler. It also provides people 
     with a voice in how the country is run, making the citizens 
     feel safe and heard. When people feel like they have a voice 
     and their opinions matter, they are more likely to want to 
     invest more of their time into protecting their own 
     communities and work with each other to solve problems more 
     efficiently rather than argue. This ideal is seen in everyday 
     life through the ballot box, local town meetings, and 
     volunteering in the community. This happens because the 
     government doesn't control us, it doesn't dictate every rule, 
     it lets its citizens take charge of their communities and 
     decide how they choose to interact with the government. 
     Having the government in the hands of the citizens ensures 
     that it exists mainly to serve and protect the citizens.
       The final ideal is equality under God, which is the belief 
     that everyone has equal value and inherited rights. This 
     ideal ensures that everyone has the same opportunities and 
     everyone is given a fair chance to succeed regardless of 
     their background. This ideal also implies that our rights are 
     unalienable, meaning that they are inherited into our 
     humanity and the government cannot take them away. While 
     America has faced many conflicts with this belief, this ideal 
     itself is what makes our nation great. It forces America to 
     go face-to-face with its flaws and to always continue to 
     expand the rights of its people; to ensure that every citizen 
     is protected and respected. Some examples are when people 
     have had to fight for rights and equality, and by having this 
     ideal America becomes a land where one's success comes from 
     talents and characteristics rather than their social class. 
     This equality is what makes the ``American Dream'' possible, 
     it's why people come here, to thrive with opportunities, and 
     for the freedom America offers to everyone.
       Ultimately, America's greatness comes from its people. We 
     aren't a nation that is defined by just a single origin, but 
     rather a nation with individuals from all over the world who 
     have come under a shared agreement. That agreement is found 
     in the Constitution, The Declaration of Independence, and the 
     Bill of Rights. It is a promise based on the belief that, 
     ``All people are created equal and have the right to life, 
     liberty, and the pursuit of happiness.'' While America isn't 
     perfect, and it may never be, we made a promise and from that 
     promise, America has the tools to fix its mistakes. When 
     people fight for justice and rights, they aren't trying to 
     change the rules, but rather trying to tell America to keep 
     its promise to its people. This ongoing commitment to our 
     ideals is what truly makes America exceptional among other 
     countries.
