# CROSSETT EXPERIMENTAL FOREST ACT OF 2026

**Speaking:** Westerman, Bruce (W000821) [R-AR], Stansbury, Melanie A. (S001218) [D-NM]

**Measures:** H.R. 10136

```
                CROSSETT EXPERIMENTAL FOREST ACT OF 2026

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and pass the 
bill (H.R. 10136) to require the Secretary of Agriculture to convey the 
Crossett Experimental Forest to the State of Arkansas, and for other 
purposes, as amended.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 10136

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Crossett Experimental Forest 
     Act of 2026''.

     SEC. 2. CONVEYANCE OF THE CROSSETT EXPERIMENTAL FOREST.

       (a) Definitions.--In this section:
       (1) Secretary.--The term ``Secretary'' means the Secretary 
     of Agriculture, acting through the Chief of the Forest 
     Service.
       (2) State.--The term ``State'' means the State of Arkansas.
       (3) Forest.--The term ``Forest'' means the Crossett 
     Experimental Forest, the R.R. Reynolds Research Natural Area, 
     and associated facilities, generally depicted as ``USDA 
     Forest Service'' on the map entitled ``Crossett Experimental 
     Forest'' and dated May 1, 2026.
       (b) Conveyance Required.--Subject to this section, if the 
     State submits to the Secretary a written request for 
     conveyance of the Forest not later than 180 days after the 
     date of the enactment of this Act, the Secretary shall convey 
     to the State all right, title, and interest of the United 
     States in and to the Forest.
       (c) Terms and Conditions.--The conveyance under subsection 
     (b) shall be--
       (1) subject to valid existing rights;
       (2) made without consideration;
       (3) made by quitclaim deed;
       (4) subject to the requirements under subsection (f); and
       (5) subject to such other terms and conditions as the 
     Secretary considers to be appropriate to protect the 
     interests of the United States.
       (d) Costs of Conveyance.--Any costs relating to the 
     conveyance under subsection (b), including costs for surveys 
     and other administrative costs, shall be paid by the State.
       (e) Map Corrections.--The Secretary and the State may, by 
     mutual agreement--
       (1) make minor boundary adjustments to the Forest to be 
     conveyed under subsection (b); and
       (2) correct any minor errors in the map, an acreage 
     estimate, or the description of the Forest.
       (f) Use of Conveyed Land.--
       (1) State forest designation.--As a condition of 
     conveyance, the State, upon conveyance, shall designate the 
     Forest as the ``Crossett Experimental State Forest'' and 
     manage and maintain the Forest under such designation and in 
     accordance with all laws and regulations applicable to 
     Arkansas State forests, to the extent that such laws and 
     regulations do not conflict with the requirements of this 
     section.
       (2) Use.--The State shall use the conveyed Forest--
       (A) for forest research, education, and demonstration 
     purposes in a manner consistent with the historic research 
     mission of the Forest; and
       (B) in coordination with the University of Arkansas at 
     Monticello and the University of Arkansas Division of 
     Agriculture, including by providing such entities with 
     reasonable access to the Forest for research and educational 
     purposes.
       (3) Reversionary interest.--If the Secretary determines 
     that the State has failed to manage and maintain the Forest 
     in accordance with paragraph (1) or use the Forest in 
     accordance with paragraph (2)--
       (A) the Secretary shall issue to the State written notice 
     of the failure; and
       (B) if the State does not correct the failure within 180 
     days after receiving such written notice, all right, title, 
     and interest in and to the Forest, or the portion of the 
     Forest affected by the failure, may, at the discretion of the 
     Secretary, revert to the United States.
       (g) Research Records and Data.--
       (1) Provision of records to state.--Not later than the date 
     on which the Forest is conveyed under subsection (b), the 
     Secretary shall provide to the State a complete copy, in the 
     form maintained by the Forest Service, of all study plans, 
     plot data, measurement histories, study archives, and 
     associated documentation that is related to research 
     conducted at the Forest and in the custody or control of the 
     Forest Service.
       (2) Rule of construction.--Nothing in this subsection shall 
     be construed to affect any requirement in chapter 21, 29, 31, 
     or 33 of title 44, United States Code, with respect to 
     Federal records.

                             General Leave

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I ask unanimous consent that all Members 
may have 5 legislative days to revise and extend their remarks and to 
include extraneous material on H.R. 10136, the bill now under 
consideration.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Is there objection to the request of the 
gentleman from Arkansas?
  There was no objection.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise in support of H.R. 10136, the Crossett 
Experimental Forest Act of 2026. This is legislation I introduced to 
restore a once-thriving hub of forestry research and demonstration in 
my district to its former glory after years of Federal Government 
neglect.
  Since its establishment in 1934, the Crossett Experimental Forest has 
played an important role in advancing forestry research, education, and 
demonstration to the betterment of Arkansas and the entire Nation.
  Although the forest encompasses just 1,685 acres in southern 
Arkansas, research conducted there has helped shape forest management 
practices across the country, particularly for southern pine forests.
  Much of Crossett's success stems from decades of collaboration among 
the Forest Service, the State of Arkansas, and the University of 
Arkansas at Monticello. These partnerships made the forest a valuable 
resource for researchers, foresters, students, private landowners, and 
the forest products industry.
  Because of declining Forest Service staffing and resources at 
Crossett dating back decades, it raised concerns about the future of 
its research mission. Recognizing these challenges, the State of 
Arkansas, the University of Arkansas at Monticello, local officials, 
and my office worked with local Forest Service officials to identify a 
path forward that would allow Crossett's historic mission to flourish 
again and nurture future generations of foresters.

[[Page H6244]]

  Through those discussions, a clear consensus formed: Transferring 
ownership and management of the forest to the State of Arkansas offered 
the best opportunity to continue its historic research and 
demonstration mission.
  The State is prepared to dedicate resources to Crossett and to work 
with the University of Arkansas at Monticello to support continued 
forestry and silviculture research.
  H.R. 10136 puts this locally developed solution into action by 
transferring the forest to the State of Arkansas to be managed as the 
Crossett Experimental State Forest. The legislation requires the State 
to continue managing the property for forestry research, education, and 
demonstration.
  The bill also includes a reversionary provision, allowing the 
property to return to Federal ownership if it is no longer dedicated to 
those purposes.
  Arkansas has a long history of responsible forest management. Our 
forest industry plays an important role in supporting healthy 
ecosystems, our State's economy, and local communities.
  By building on existing partnerships and placing Crossett under the 
management of the State of Arkansas, this legislation ensures Arkansas 
will continue to lead the Nation in research that supports the forest 
products industry of today and tomorrow.
  It also honors the decades of research done by many well-known 
researchers who have studied at the forest. It would be a shame to let 
all this research be packed away in the files somewhere because these 
forest research projects are ongoing and continuing. We have had 
decades of success there, and we can also have many years of future 
success by continuing this research.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support H.R. 10136, and I 
reserve the balance of my time.

  Ms. STANSBURY. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, this bill would authorize the U.S. Forest Service to 
convey the Crossett Experimental Forest in southwestern Arkansas to the 
State of Arkansas.
  Following the conveyance, Arkansas will be required to manage the 
land as the Crossett Experimental State Forest in accordance with all 
State laws applicable to the State forests.
  Additionally, the bill directs the State to use the land for forest 
research, education, and demonstration projects in a manner consistent 
with the historic research mission of the area and in conjunction with 
the University of Arkansas at Monticello and the University of Arkansas 
Division of Agriculture.
  If the State fails to use the land for these purposes, ownership of 
the land will revert back to the United States Forest Service.
  The Crossett Experimental Forest was established in 1933, and the 
forest is largely used to do forestry practices associated with pine 
species.
  The Forest Service has a longstanding partnership with the State of 
Arkansas and the university, and the conveyance authorized by this bill 
will improve management efficiency.
  I understand that moving this bill is a priority for the chairman, 
and I thank him and his staff for working with our team to ensure this 
bill could move on a bipartisan basis.
  That said, before wrapping up my remarks, I want to acknowledge that 
the research mission of the Forest Service is under attack by the 
administration. So, while I support targeted conveyance and 
partnership, I have to simultaneously stand up against efforts to 
decimate the Federal workforce and the agency's world-class research 
division and the proposed sell-off of public lands.
  America needs to lead on science, and Congress has a responsibility 
to ensure our agencies have the investment and resources they need to 
carry out their critical mission.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support this bill, and I yield 
back the balance of my time.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself the balance of my time.
  Mr. Speaker, it is not an exaggeration to say that our country's 
forest economy is in a dire crisis. It is also not an exaggeration to 
say that the best thing that we can do for the environment is to 
promote healthy forests.
  While sawmills and paper mills are closing all across the country, 
our Nation's forests are going up in flames. Meanwhile, the United 
States is the number one importer of wood and paper products. We are 
the largest importer of wood and paper products in the world, while we 
watch our mills close and our forests go up in flames.
  There is no silver bullet to address this problem, but research and 
demonstration that supports new markets for forest products is an 
important first step.
  Learning how to manage our forests in a way that keeps them healthy 
and thriving and able to meet the demands for new markets that are out 
there is why it is so critical that we keep investing in experimental 
forests, why we keep investing in research and development, and why 
this legislation is so vital.
  For nearly a century, the Crossett Experimental Forest has advanced 
forestry research that supported local jobs, our forest products 
industry, and rural economies.
  Here is a bit of history that goes along with that. The Yale School 
of Forestry is the oldest school of forestry in the United States. They 
started a partnership many decades ago to go down to the Crossett 
Experimental Forest, and they actually built a classroom down there so 
they could do work in this area.
  H.R. 10136 breathes new life into this historic mission by allowing 
the State of Arkansas to invest the dedicated resources and staffing 
necessary to make this forest thrive once again.
  The State has the expertise, partnerships, and resources to continue 
the important work.
  I urge my colleagues to support the legislation, which implements a 
locally driven, commonsense solution at a critical juncture in the 
future of our country's forest products industry.
  H.R. 10136 supports rural economies, empowers Arkansans, and will 
help educate the next generation of foresters and scientists.
  Mr. Speaker, I yield back the balance of my time.
```
