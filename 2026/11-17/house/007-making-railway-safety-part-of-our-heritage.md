# MAKING RAILWAY SAFETY PART OF OUR HERITAGE

**Speaking:** Joyce, John (J000302) [R-PA]

```
               MAKING RAILWAY SAFETY PART OF OUR HERITAGE

  (Mr. Joyce of Pennsylvania was recognized to address the House for 5 
minutes.)
  Mr. JOYCE of Pennsylvania. Mr. Speaker, it is with a commitment to 
the safety of my constituents and all Americans that I rise today to 
emphasize the importance of the Railway Safety Act.
  This legislation was introduced in response to the disastrous Norfolk 
Southern train derailment in East Palestine and would update safety 
regulations for freight cars, strengthen hazardous material oversight, 
and improve coordination for local first responders.
  Last month, Blair County experienced the fourth train derailment 
since February of this year, which included cars carrying hazardous 
materials. Blair County is the home of Altoona, Pennsylvania, which is 
known as Railroad City. Rail safety should be a part of our heritage.
  These glaring failures demand answers, which is why I sent letters to 
the CEO of Norfolk Southern and the Administrator of the Federal 
Railroad Administration specifically requesting explanations for the 
communities in which they operate.
  The terrain around Altoona, Pennsylvania, is historically difficult 
for trains to travel through, but this isn't a new phenomenon and 
cannot alone explain the increased derailments of four in 10 months.
  Mr. Speaker, Members of Congress have a responsibility to conduct 
oversight of actions that threaten the safety of their constituents, 
which is why I will continue to fight for accountability and take 
action to prevent further derailments in the communities where I serve.


           Congratulating Stultz Family on Agricultural Award

  Mr. JOYCE of Pennsylvania. Mr. Speaker, I rise to recognize the 
Stultz family of Williamsburg, Pennsylvania, for their selection as the 
2026 recipients of the Agricultural Community Excellence Award.
  Robert and Tammy Stultz have dedicated their lives to Blair County 
agriculture and to their community. The couple transitioned from dairy 
farming to operating a swine production facility while remaining active 
in local ag organizations.
  The family's involvement in agriculture also extends to Robert and 
Tammy's kids. Their son Hunter works with local dairy farms through his 
position with Select Sires. Their daughters, Kara and Rylee, have both 
served as Blair County Dairy Princesses.
  Mr. Speaker, join me in congratulating the Stultz family on their 
selection for this award and for their enduring commitment to Blair 
County and Pennsylvania agriculture.


              Recognizing Cambria County Transit Authority

  Mr. JOYCE of Pennsylvania. Mr. Speaker, I rise to recognize the 
Cambria County Transit Authority for 50 years of public transportation.
  Known as CamTran, the authority began operations in 1976. Buses have 
often been described as the backbone of regional transportation 
networks and as lifelines that connect people and places, families and 
jobs.
  In fiscal year 2023, I was proud to support a Community Project 
Funding grant for CamTran to replace critical buses. For five decades, 
CamTran has helped residents get to work, school, medical appointments, 
and other essential services.
  Mr. Speaker, I congratulate all former and current members of the 
board and their drivers, mechanics, professional staff, and employees 
for their continued commitment to CamTran.


                  Recognizing National Diabetes Month

  Mr. JOYCE of Pennsylvania. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize the 
more than 40 million Americans living with diabetes, one of the fastest 
growing health challenges of our time.
  November is National Diabetes Month, an opportunity to raise 
awareness about the importance of early detection, prevention, and 
access to care.
  In May, I joined my colleague Congresswoman Dr. Kim Schrier to 
reintroduce the Strengthening Collective Resources for Encouraging 
Education Needed for Type 1 Diabetes Act, or the SCREEN for Type 1 
Diabetes Act. This legislation aims to increase awareness of T1D 
screening.
  ESPN's Adam Schefter has been one of the leading voices on Capitol 
Hill advocating for greater education, more research, and improved 
treatment options for people living with T1D. His advocacy is personal, 
as his wife, Sharri, lives with the disease, but his efforts are 
supporting the countless families navigating this disease each and 
every day.
  Mr. Speaker, I will continue to support and advance policies that 
address this global health need, which concerns so many of my 
constituents and so many Americans.
```
