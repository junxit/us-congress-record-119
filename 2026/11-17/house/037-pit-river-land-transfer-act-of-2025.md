# PIT RIVER LAND TRANSFER ACT OF 2025

**Speaking:** Westerman, Bruce (W000821) [R-AR], Stansbury, Melanie A. (S001218) [D-NM]

**Measures:** H.R. 2400

```
                              {time}  1720
                  PIT RIVER LAND TRANSFER ACT OF 2025

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and concur in 
the Senate amendment to the bill (H.R. 2400) to take certain Federal 
land in the State of California into trust for the benefit of the Pit 
River Tribe.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the Senate amendment is as follows:
  Senate amendment:
Strike out all after the enacting clause and insert:

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Pit River Land Transfer Act 
     of 2026''.

     SEC. 2. LAND TO BE TAKEN INTO TRUST.

       (a) Administrative Transfer.--The Secretary of Agriculture 
     shall transfer administrative jurisdiction over the Four 
     Corners Federal land to the Secretary.
       (b) Land to Be Taken Into Trust.--Subject to valid existing 
     rights, the Four Corners Federal land shall be taken into 
     trust by the Secretary for the benefit of the Tribe.
       (c) Survey.--Not later than 180 days after the date of 
     enactment of this Act, the Secretary

[[Page H6248]]

     shall carry out a complete survey of the Four Corners Federal 
     land, including the areas described in subsection (g)(1)(B).
       (d) Administrative Provisions Corrections.--The Secretary, 
     the Secretary of Agriculture, and the Tribe may, by mutual 
     agreement, make minor corrections and adjustments to the land 
     taken into trust under this section to facilitate land 
     management, including corrections and adjustments to the 
     survey carried out pursuant to subsection (c).
       (e) Administration.--On being taken into trust under this 
     section, the Four Corners Federal land shall be--
       (1) considered part of the Reservation of the Tribe; and
       (2) administered by the Secretary in accordance with the 
     laws and regulations generally applicable to property held in 
     trust by the United States for an Indian Tribe.
       (f) Gaming Prohibited.--The land taken into trust under 
     this section shall not be used for any class II gaming or 
     class III gaming under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act (25 
     U.S.C. 2701 et seq.) (as those terms are defined in section 4 
     of that Act (25 U.S.C. 2703)).
       (g) Definitions.--In this section:
       (1) Four corners federal land.--
       (A) In general.--The term ``Four Corners Federal land'' 
     means the approximately 557 acres of land managed by the 
     Forest Service, as generally depicted within the ``Proposed 
     Land Conveyance'' boundary on the Map, including improvements 
     and appurtenances thereon.
       (B) Exclusion.--The term ``Four Corners Federal land'' does 
     not include the approximately 87 acres of roads, highways, 
     public rights-of-way, easements, and special use permit areas 
     subject to existing easements generally depicted within the 
     ``Proposed Land Conveyance'' boundary on the Map.
       (2) Map.--The term ``Map'' means the map prepared by the 
     Forest Service entitled ``Proposed Land Conveyance to the Pit 
     River Tribe'' and dated June 30, 2026.
       (3) Secretary.--The term ``Secretary'' means the Secretary 
     of the Interior.
       (4) Tribe.--The term ``Tribe'' means the Pit River Tribe, 
     California (includes XL Ranch, Big Bend, Likely, Lookout, 
     Montgomery Creek, and Roaring Creek Rancherias).

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I ask unanimous consent that all Members 
may have 5 legislative days in which to revise and extend their remarks

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, H.R. 2400, the Pit River Land Transfer Act of 2026, 
introduced by the late Congressman LaMalfa, would place approximately 
557 acres of U.S. Forest Service land, commonly known as the Four 
Corners, into trust for the Pit River Tribe.
  The Pit River Tribe, which was federally recognized in 1976, consists 
of 11 different California Tribal bands and resides on a 100-square-
mile reservation.
  In the late 1960s and early 1970s, the Pit River Tribe sought to 
regain control of the Four Corners property near Burney, California, 
citing its cultural significance and ancestral ties. Despite repeated 
efforts, the land remains under U.S. Forest Service ownership.
  This legislation would return the Four Corners property to the Tribe, 
which wishes to build an interpretive center there. Gaming on these 
lands, pursuant to the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act, would be 
prohibited.
  H.R. 2400 passed the House on suspension in December 2025. The Senate 
then made minor technical amendments and passed it by unanimous consent 
in September 2026.
  Our beloved former colleague, Mr. LaMalfa, worked tirelessly to get 
this bill done for the Pit River Tribe, and its passage by the House 
today would send the bill to the President's desk.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge support for this legislation, and I reserve the 
balance of my time.
  Ms. STANSBURY. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, H.R. 2400 would place lands currently managed by the 
U.S. Forest Service in northern California within Shasta County into 
trust for the benefit of the Pit River Tribe. These lands hold historic 
and cultural significance to the Tribe, which has worked tirelessly to 
reclaim these lands. This bill presents the opportunity for the Tribe 
to manage these lands for economic development, education, 
environmental stewardship, and cultural needs.
  Passing this bill would also be a meaningful tribute to the sponsor 
of the bill, our dear former colleague Doug LaMalfa, who we sadly lost 
earlier this year.
  The House passed a previous version of this legislation earlier this 
Congress. Since then, the Senate has made technical corrections, 
including updates to the acreage and associated map.
  Mr. Speaker, I support the amendments made by the Senate and urge my 
colleagues to once again support this legislation, and I reserve the 
balance of my time.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I have no further requests for time. I am 
prepared to close, and I reserve the balance of my time.
  Ms. STANSBURY. Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support the 
legislation, and I yield back the balance of my time.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself the balance of my time.
  Mr. Speaker, H.R. 2400 would place approximately 557 acres of U.S. 
Forest Service land, referred to as the Four Corners, into trust for 
the Pit River Tribe. The Tribe has pursued this goal through the 
administrative process for more than 50 years. It is time for Congress 
to act legislatively on the latter.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge passage of the bill, and I yield back the balance 
of my time.
```
