# RECOGNIZING PREMATURITY AWARENESS DAY

**Speaking:** McClellan, Jennifer L. (M001227) [D-VA]

**Measures:** H.Res. 1664

```
                 RECOGNIZING PREMATURITY AWARENESS DAY

  (Ms. McCLELLAN asked and was given permission to address the House 
for 1 minute and to revise and extend her remarks.)
  Ms. McCLELLAN. Mr. Speaker, when I was pregnant with my daughter, my 
placenta ruptured 9 weeks before my due date. We both nearly died. 
Fortunately, after an emergency c-section and 6 weeks in the NICU, I 
was able to bring her home. Today, she is a precocious sixth grader, 
almost as tall as I am.
  Each year on Prematurity Awareness Day, I am grateful that we came 
through that terrifying experience. Too many families in America are 
not so lucky.
  This morning, the March of Dimes released its 2026 Report Card on the 
state of maternal and infant health. For the fifth year in a row, the 
United States received a D-plus. For the fourth year in a row, 10.4 
percent of babies born in 2025 were pre-term. That is nearly 380,000 
babies born prematurely with the worst rates in the southern region of 
the United States. Babies born to mothers of color experienced 
significantly higher preterm rates.
  These statistics paint a dark picture. Pre-term births are 
preventable. The first step is raising awareness.
  Today, I introduced H. Res. 1664 with Representative Robin Kelly to 
designate November as Prematurity Awareness Month.
  Mr. Speaker, we cannot accept a D-plus. Every mother deserves access 
to quality maternal care. Every infant deserves the opportunity for a 
healthy start in life. This resolution is a critical first step 
Congress can take to make that happen. I urge my colleagues to support 
it.

                          ____________________
```
