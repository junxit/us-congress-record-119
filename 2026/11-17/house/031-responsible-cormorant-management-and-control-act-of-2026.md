# RESPONSIBLE CORMORANT MANAGEMENT AND CONTROL ACT OF 2026

**Speaking:** Westerman, Bruce (W000821) [R-AR], Stansbury, Melanie A. (S001218) [D-NM], Walberg, Tim (W000798) [R-MI]

```
        RESPONSIBLE CORMORANT MANAGEMENT AND CONTROL ACT OF 2026

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and pass the 
bill (H.R. 8195) to require the Secretary of the Interior to develop 
regional management frameworks for the take of double-crested 
cormorants, as amended.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 8195

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Responsible Cormorant 
     Management and Control Act of 2026''.

     SEC. 2. REGIONAL MANAGEMENT FRAMEWORKS FOR TAKE OF DOUBLE-
                   CRESTED CORMORANTS.

       (a) In General.--Not later than 1 year after the date of 
     the enactment of this section, the Secretary, in coordination 
     with the Regional Flyway Councils and in accordance with 
     section 3 of the Migratory Bird Treaty Act (16 U.S.C. 704), 
     shall issue regulations for the development of regional 
     management frameworks for the management of double-crested 
     cormorants based on the best available scientific 
     information.
       (b) Requirements.--Each regional management framework 
     developed pursuant to subsection (a) shall--
       (1) ensure that each breeding population of double-crested 
     cormorants is managed at a level that is sufficient to ensure 
     the conservation of double-crested cormorants, and that 
     cumulative authorized take of double-crested cormorants does 
     not exceed a biologically sustainable level using the best 
     available scientific information--
       (A) consistent with the Migratory Bird Treaty Act (16 
     U.S.C. 703 et seq.) and compatible with the applicable 
     conventions; and

[[Page H6239]]

       (B) as informed by the most recent population-level survey 
     of double-crested cormorants;
       (2) include, with respect to double-crested cormorants--
       (A) allowable methods of take;
       (B) allowed time periods of take;
       (C) required annual reporting of total take, including 
     target and non-target birds, which shall be made publicly 
     available;
       (D) measures to avoid the take of non-target birds and 
     other species;
       (E) documented conflicts at an identified location that 
     authorized take can feasibly resolve; and
       (F) non-lethal measures that shall be implemented;
       (3) identify entities that may conduct authorized take of 
     double-crested cormorants, which may include--
       (A) State and Tribal fish and wildlife agencies; and
       (B) entities permitted, trained, or otherwise authorized in 
     a manner the Secretary determines appropriate, which shall 
     include--
       (i) lake managers; and
       (ii) pond managers;
       (4) provide for--
       (A) States and Indian Tribes to take specific management 
     actions with respect to double-crested cormorants in 
     accordance with the regional management framework; and
       (B) the implementation of management actions identified 
     under subsection (c);
       (5) consider the documented effects of double-crested 
     cormorants within the applicable region on--
       (A) fisheries;
       (B) sensitive vegetation;
       (C) populations of other migratory birds;
       (D) human health and safety;
       (E) water quality; and
       (F) species listed as threatened species or endangered 
     species under section 4 of the Endangered Species Act of 1973 
     (16 U.S.C. 1533);
       (6) include a long-term plan for identifying and monitoring 
     the effects of the regional management framework on the 
     effects identified under paragraph (5);
       (7) consider the effects of take authorized under the 
     regional management framework on non-target birds, species 
     that are listed as birds of conservation concern pursuant to 
     the Fish and Wildlife Conservation Act of 1980 (16 U.S.C. 
     2901 et seq.), and species listed as threatened species or 
     endangered species under section 4 of the Endangered Species 
     Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 1533); and
       (8) develop best practices for additional measures, 
     including--
       (A) non-lethal deterrents;
       (B) exclusion and habitat modifications;
       (C) habitat restoration; and
       (D) other non-lethal methods to rebuild or stabilize the 
     populations of fauna and flora that have suffered detrimental 
     effects because of double-crested cormorants.
       (c) Incorporation of National Wildlife Refuge System.--In 
     developing each regional management framework under 
     subsection (a), the Secretary, in cooperation with the heads 
     of State and Tribal game and fish agencies and the applicable 
     Regional Flyway Council, shall make recommendations that can 
     be taken within units of the National Wildlife Refuge System 
     that are in compliance with the National Wildlife Refuge 
     System Administration Act of 1966 (16 U.S.C. 668dd et seq.) 
     and meet the management objectives of States, Indian Tribes, 
     the Regional Flyway Councils, and such units with respect to 
     double-crested cormorants while fulfilling the primary 
     purposes of such units.
       (d) Population Surveys.--Not later than 5 years after the 
     date of the enactment of this section and every 5 years 
     thereafter, the Secretary, in coordination with the Regional 
     Flyway Councils, shall carry out a survey of the population 
     of double-crested cormorants.
       (e) Updates.--
       (1) In general.--Not later than 5 years after the date of 
     the enactment of this section and every 5 years thereafter, 
     the Secretary, in coordination with the applicable Regional 
     Flyway Council, shall review and update each regional 
     management framework developed under subsection (a).
       (2) Incorporation of population surveys.--In reviewing and 
     updating a regional management framework under paragraph (1), 
     the Secretary shall incorporate the results of the most 
     recent survey carried out under subsection (d).
       (f) Report.--Not later than 90 days after the date of the 
     enactment of this section, the Secretary shall submit to the 
     Committee on Natural Resources of the House of 
     Representatives and the Committee on Environment and Public 
     Works of the Senate a report regarding the feasibility of 
     issuing licenses to hunt double-crested cormorants to meet 
     the requirements of the regional management frameworks 
     developed pursuant to subsection (a).
       (g) Sunset.--This Act shall have no force or effect 
     beginning on January 1, 2034.
       (h) Regulations.--The Secretary may issue such regulations 
     to carry out this section as the Secretary determines 
     necessary and appropriate.
       (i) Definitions.--In this section:
       (1) Double-crested cormorant.--The term ``double-crested 
     cormorant'' means the species Nannopterum auritum.
       (2) Regional flyway council.--The term ``Regional Flyway 
     Council'' means--
       (A) the Atlantic Flyway Council;
       (B) the Central Flyway Council;
       (C) the Mississippi Flyway Council; and
       (D) the Pacific Flyway Council.
       (3) Indian tribe.--The term ``Indian Tribe'' has the 
     meaning given the term in section 4 of the Indian Self-
     Determination and Education Assistance Act (25 U.S.C. 5304).
       (4) Lake manager.--The term ``lake manager'' means a person 
     that is licensed or otherwise authorized by a State 
     regulatory agency to manage a private lake.
       (5) Migratory bird.--The term ``migratory bird'' has the 
     meaning given the term ``migratory birds'' in section 11 of 
     the Migratory Bird Conservation Act (16 U.S.C. 715j).
       (6) Pond manager.--The term ``pond manager'' means a person 
     that is licensed or otherwise authorized by a State 
     regulatory agency to manage a private pond.
       (7) Secretary.--The term ``Secretary'' means the Secretary 
     of the Interior, acting through the Director of the United 
     States Fish and Wildlife Service.
       (8) State.--The term ``State'' means--
       (A) each of the several States;
       (B) the District of Columbia;
       (C) the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico;
       (D) Guam;
       (E) American Samoa;
       (F) the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands; and
       (G) the United States Virgin Islands.
       (9) Take.--The term ``take'' has the meaning given the term 
     in section 10.12 of title 50, Code of Federal Regulations (as 
     in effect on the date of the enactment of this section).

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise in support of H.R. 8195, the Responsible 
Cormorant Management and Control Act of 2026, introduced by Chairman 
Walberg of Michigan.
  Double-crested cormorants have recovered significantly since their 
listing under the Migratory Bird Treaty Act in 1972. However, growing 
populations in certain areas have created serious challenges for 
commercial aquaculture, wild fisheries, and other wildlife.
  The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service currently manages cormorant 
populations through a population take limit model. As the Committee on 
Natural Resources held hearings on cormorant management, we heard time 
and again that this model requires the Service to guess where the main 
problems will be each year. This approach has failed to manage 
conflicts with double-crested cormorants adequately.
  To address this, H.R. 8195 directs the Service to develop regional 
management frameworks in consultation with the four regional flyway 
councils. These frameworks must manage double-crested cormorants in a 
biologically sustainable manner, while ensuring their management and 
allowable take consider the impacts that cormorants have on fisheries, 
sensitive vegetation, other migratory birds, and federally listed 
species.
  This represents the collaborative, science-based decisionmaking that 
should guide the management of federally protected species.
  Mr. Speaker, I commend Chairman Walberg for his work on this 
legislation. I urge my colleagues to support H.R. 8195, and I reserve 
the balance of my time.

  Ms. STANSBURY. Mr. Speaker, H.R. 8195, the Responsible Cormorant 
Management and Control Act of 2026 would create regional management 
frameworks and authorize the management of double-crested cormorant 
populations, a species that is protected under the Migratory Bird 
Treaty Act.
  Cormorants have been protected under the Migratory Bird Treaty Act 
for over 100 years. The species declined sharply in the mid-20th 
century but has been on a successful path to recovery ever since. 
Today, the species is classified as ``least concern.''
  Unfortunately, with its recovery, the double-crested cormorant has 
become a nuisance species in some areas of the

[[Page H6240]]

country, especially for fisheries and aquaculture operations.
  The Migratory Bird Treaty Act contains a pathway for sensible 
permitted management of a species that may pose a threat to other 
management objectives. This bill uses the existing framework to 
creatively address cormorant issues at a regional level. The committee 
has supported this bill moving forward and worked across the aisle to 
improve the language.
  Double-crested cormorants still play an important role in aquatic and 
coastal ecosystems. The Federal agencies, States, and stakeholders 
developing these regional management plans must continue to balance the 
needs of the ecosystem and the status of the birds under bird treaties.
  The committee strongly urges the use of strategies that are 
nonlethal, such as fish habitat restoration, and nonlethal methods to 
provide lasting benefits for fish populations and habitat restoration. 
The committee appreciates bipartisan efforts to improve the bill and 
supports it moving forward.

  Mr. WALBERG. Mr. Speaker, I thank Chairman Westerman for yielding me 
the time and for his leadership in bringing my legislation to the 
floor.
  The Great Lakes are one of our Nation's most cherished natural 
resources. They hold over 20 percent of the world's freshwater, and the 
basin is home to millions of Americans, many of whom enjoy the 
recreational opportunities the lakes provide. Fishing in the Great 
Lakes alone contributes more than $5 billion to the regional economy 
and supports over 75,000 jobs.
  However, the populations of double-crested cormorants have posed a 
significant challenge to this cherished resource. Each double-crested 
cormorant eats up to 1\1/2\ pounds of fish per day. With an estimated 
population total between 871,000 and over 1 million cormorants, these 
birds can wreak havoc on an ecosystem, consuming millions of pounds of 
fish per year.
  The Great Lakes are home to the largest breeding population of 
cormorants in the United States, with over 70 percent of the population 
nesting in the Great Lakes alone. Large nesting populations also damage 
trees and surrounding vegetation, degrading habitat used by other 
wildlife. This results in a significant imbalance in the ecosystem, and 
the current management framework is simply not working.
  However, this isn't just a problem in the Great Lakes. Populations of 
double-crested cormorants are exploding in regions across the country, 
and in some cases doubling every 4 years. This poses a significant 
threat to the fisheries, environments, and ecosystems many of us 
cherish.
  As an avid fisherman and a representative of many hardworking men and 
women who make their living on the water, I am proud to lead this 
commonsense legislation that provides greater tools to States, Tribes, 
and sportsmen to manage populations of double-crested cormorants.
  The Responsible Cormorant Management and Control Act requires a 
science-based management framework with regional partnerships in 
consultation with the Regional Flyway Councils. It would also require 
periodic population surveys to ensure a sustainable breeding population 
of double-crested cormorants.
  Mr. Speaker, our fisheries are essential to our Nation's economy and 
our way of life. Overabundant cormorant populations are damaging our 
fisheries, water quality, and ecosystems across the country. This 
legislation supports environmental stewardship and conservation, and I 
urge my colleagues to support the bill so that future generations of 
Americans can enjoy the same world-class fisheries as we do today.
```
