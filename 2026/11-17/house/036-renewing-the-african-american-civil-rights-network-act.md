# RENEWING THE AFRICAN AMERICAN CIVIL RIGHTS NETWORK ACT

**Speaking:** Westerman, Bruce (W000821) [R-AR], Stansbury, Melanie A. (S001218) [D-NM], Clyburn, James E. (C000537) [D-SC]

**Measures:** H.R. 9269

```
                              {time}  1710
         RENEWING THE AFRICAN AMERICAN CIVIL RIGHTS NETWORK ACT

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and pass the 
bill (H.R. 9269) to extend the authorization of the African American 
Civil Rights Network, as amended.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 9269

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Renewing the African 
     American Civil Rights Network Act''.

     SEC. 2. EXTENSION OF AUTHORIZATION.

       Section 308404 of title 54, United States Code, is amended 
     by striking ``7 years after the date of enactment of this 
     chapter'' and inserting ``7 years after the date of the 
     enactment of the Renewing the African American Civil Rights 
     Network Act''.

  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Pursuant to the rule, the gentleman from 
Arkansas (Mr. Westerman) and the gentlewoman from New Mexico (Ms. 
Stansbury) each will control 20 minutes.

                             General Leave

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I ask unanimous consent that all Members 
may have 5 legislative days to revise and extend their remarks and 
include extraneous material on H.R. 9269, the bill now under 
consideration.

[[Page H6247]]

  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Is there objection to the request of the 
gentleman from Arkansas?
  There was no objection.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise in support of H.R. 9269, introduced by 
Representative Clyburn, which extends the African American Civil Rights 
Network authorization for an additional 7 years.
  Established in 2018 during President Trump's first administration, 
the African American Civil Rights Network recognizes significant 
people, places, and events associated with the civil rights movement. 
Administered by the National Park Service, the network connects 
historic sites and educational programs across the country to tell the 
story of Americans who fought to secure equal rights under the law.
  Today, the network consists of over 200 sites, including Little Rock 
Central High School National Historic Site in my home State of 
Arkansas, the Martin Luther King, Jr. Memorial in Washington, D.C., and 
the Brown v. Board of Education National Historical Park in Kansas, 
just to name a few.
  These sites offer Americans opportunities to learn about a defining 
chapter in our Nation's history. By extending the African American 
Civil Rights Network authorization for an additional 7 years, this 
important bipartisan bill allows the National Park Service to continue 
supporting educational and interpretive efforts that will help ensure 
future generations understand the full, complex history of civil rights 
in this Nation.
  Mr. Speaker, I support H.R. 9269, and I reserve the balance of my 
time.
  Ms. STANSBURY. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, H.R. 9269 would reauthorize the African American Civil 
Rights Network, a program administered by the National Park Service, 
for an additional 7 years.
  Among its many important functions, the African American Civil Rights 
Network shares the stories of the people, places, and events associated 
with the civil rights movement. It ensures that the important history 
of the movement is honored and accessible to all Americans.
  The network includes 29 States with designated sites open to 
visitors. These sites are hubs for heritage-based tourism and provide 
education on the civil rights movement.
  At the Natural Resources Committee hearing on this bill, we heard 
powerful and important testimony about how the program authorized by 
this bill supports research, public education, heritage tourism, and, 
perhaps most importantly, connecting all Americans to the powerful 
story of the United States and our civil rights movement.
  At a time when we have seen attempts to try to rewrite history, 
particularly in our national parks, it is more critical than ever to 
support the preservation of Black and African-American history.
  Mr. Speaker, I thank Representative Clyburn for his leadership and 
his relentless work in support of this bill and the bipartisan support 
it has received, including 10 bipartisan cosponsors. It signals a 
broad agreement that we must continue to learn from our past and 
continue to support our communities moving forward.

  Mr. Speaker, I yield such time as he may consume to the gentleman 
from South Carolina (Mr. Clyburn), the sponsor of this bill.
  Mr. CLYBURN. Mr. Speaker, I thank the gentlewoman for yielding the 
time.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise today in support of H.R. 9269, Renewing the 
African American Civil Rights Network Act, as amended.
  I introduced this bipartisan legislation, along with Representative 
Mike Carey of Ohio, to seek reauthorization of the National Park 
Service's African American Civil Rights Network.
  ``Network'' is the operative word here. The importance of this 
legislation, which sunset in January 2025, is embodied in that one 
word. If I were to mention the name of Rosa Parks here today, most 
people would know who that was, but if I were to mention the name of 
Sarah Mae Flemming, I doubt that more than two or three people would 
recognize that name.
  However, a year and a half before Rosa Parks sat down on that bus in 
Montgomery, Alabama, Sarah Mae Flemming, 6 days before her 21st 
birthday, sat down on a bus in Columbia, South Carolina, and was 
physically removed from the bus. She filed a lawsuit, and the United 
States Supreme Court decided that she was entitled to ride in any 
vacant seat on that bus.
  If you were to look at the Rosa Parks case, which was Browder v. 
Gayle, you would find a footnote explaining that the Court had already 
decided on the public transit issue in Sarah Mae Flemming v. South 
Carolina Electric and Gas. Most people are not aware of that history, 
and that is the importance of this legislation.
  When I came here in 1993, South Carolina's primary industries were 
textiles and tobacco. I need not tell you what has happened to both of 
those industries. Today, our primary industries are transportation and 
tourism, and the fastest growing segment of tourism is heritage 
tourism.
  This legislation is important to South Carolina and the network of 29 
States in designating and developing cultural and historic sites for 
visitation that will help grow local economies, especially in rural 
communities.
  Mr. Speaker, I thank Representative Mike Carey and the seven other 
bipartisan original cosponsors of this legislation, and I urge my 
colleagues to support it. I also thank the gentlewoman again for the 
time.
  Ms. STANSBURY. Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support this 
legislation, and I yield back the balance of my time.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself the balance of my time.
  Mr. Speaker, the African American Civil Rights Network connects 
Americans with the people and places that shaped the civil rights 
movement. From my home State of Arkansas to right here in our Nation's 
Capital, the network brings the vast story of the civil rights movement 
together so our Nation can honor and remember that part of our history.
  Furthermore, as Representative Clyburn was speaking, it reminded me 
of the story of Daisy Gatson Bates from Arkansas. The State of Arkansas 
chose to tell her story and put her statue out here in Statuary Hall to 
remind people of the great work that she did with the Little Rock Nine 
and the integration of Little Rock Central High School.
  It is an amazing story. She was born in my district. Her mother was 
raped and murdered when Daisy was very young. Daisy took that 
heartbreak, that anger that she had, and she did something good with 
her life. That is to be commemorated, but there are a lot of stories 
that aren't told. This network allows those stories to happen.
  Mr. Speaker, I support this legislation, and I yield back the balance 
of my time.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore. The question is on the motion offered by the

  A motion to reconsider was laid on the table.
```
