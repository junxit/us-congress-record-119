# COMMON SENSE 250 ACT OF 2026

**Speaking:** Westerman, Bruce (W000821) [R-AR], Stansbury, Melanie A. (S001218) [D-NM], Raskin, Jamie (R000606) [D-MD]

**Measures:** H.R. 9600

```
                      COMMON SENSE 250 ACT OF 2026

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and pass the 
bill (H.R. 9600) to approve the location of a commemorative work for 
Thomas Paine, America's indispensable and unsung Founding Father.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 9600

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Common Sense 250 Act of 
     2026''.

     SEC. 2. APPROVAL OF LOCATION OF COMMEMORATIVE WORK FOR THOMAS 
                   PAINE.

       Notwithstanding the requirements described in section 
     8908(b)(1) of title 40, United States Code (commonly known as 
     the ``Commemorative Works Act'')--
       (1) the location of a commemorative work authorized by 
     section 709 of division DD of the Consolidated Appropriations 
     Act, 2023 (40 U.S.C. 8903 note) within Area I, as depicted on 
     the map entitled ``Commemorative Areas Washington, DC and 
     Environs'', numbered 869/86501 B, and dated June 24, 2003, is 
     approved; and
       (2) such commemorative work shall be located within such 
     Area I.

  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Pursuant to the rule, the gentleman from 
Arkansas (Mr. Westerman) and the gentlewoman from New Mexico (Ms. 
Stansbury) each will control 20 minutes.
  The Chair recognizes the gentleman from Arkansas.


                             General Leave

  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I ask unanimous consent that all Members 
have 5 legislative days to revise and extend their remarks and to 
include extraneous material on H.R. 9600, the bill now under 
consideration.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Is there objection to the request of the 
gentleman from Arkansas?
  There was no objection.
  Mr. WESTERMAN. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise in strong support of H.R. 9600, introduced by 
Representative Raskin, which approves the location of a commemorative 
work honoring Thomas Paine, one of America's most influential Founding 
Fathers, in a prominent location within our Nation's Capital.
  Thomas Paine is best known for his pamphlet ``Common Sense,'' which 
made a powerful case for breaking from British rule. Published in 
January 1776, his pamphlet lit the fire that sparked the American 
Revolution and the fight for our country's independence. As his words 
spread across the Colonies, momentum grew to adopt the Declaration of 
Independence, which was signed just 6 months after the publication of 
``Common Sense.''
  In 2022, Congress authorized the Thomas Paine Memorial Association to 
establish a privately funded commemorative work honoring Thomas Paine 
for his contribution to our Nation's founding. H.R. 9600 takes the next 
step by authorizing the memorial's placement within Area I, a prominent 
area in our Nation's Capital, located near The National Mall, reserved 
for monuments and memorials of preeminent historical and lasting 
significance to the United States.
  As we celebrate the 250th anniversary of American independence, it is 
fitting that we recognize the individuals whose ideas helped shape our 
Nation. Paine's writings on representative government, individual 
rights, and political liberty inspired generations of Americans and 
helped lay the intellectual foundation for our government.
  Mr. Speaker, I support H.R. 9600, and I reserve the balance of my 
time.
  Ms. STANSBURY. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, H.R. 9600, introduced by my colleague from Maryland (Mr. 
Raskin), would authorize the location for the Thomas Paine Memorial, 
securing it at a prominent position near The National Mall.
  Thomas Paine was a patriot of the American Revolution who paved the 
way for America's independence. He was a visionary for democratic 
freedom. He opposed tyranny and promoted freedom from oppression. He 
supported religious liberty and the separation of church and State at 
great political and personal cost.
  In 2022, the bill to create the Thomas Paine Memorial was signed into 
law, and in 2024, the National Capital Memorial Advisory Commission 
recommended the memorial's placement in what is known as Area I, which 
is reserved for memorials with preeminent historical and lasting value 
to the Nation. Thomas Paine certainly meets that mark, and I am glad 
that my colleagues across the aisle have joined us to recognize his 
significant legacy to our Nation and to our democracy.
  The passage of this bill will serve as the final legislative step to 
ensure the memorial's construction.
  In contrast to how the current administration is attempting to 
plaster Washington, D.C., with vanity projects requested by the 
President in violation of current law, Representative Raskin's bill 
demonstrates that a national memorial can and must be created lawfully 
with integrity using a transparent process through an act of Congress 
that Thomas Paine himself would have approved of.
  I congratulate Representative Raskin for his years of work and 
dedication to securing this memorial and its place of national 
distinction. I urge my colleagues to support this bill, and I reserve 
the balance of my time.

  Ms. STANSBURY. Mr. Speaker, I yield such time as he may consume to

  Mr. RASKIN. Mr. Speaker, I thank Ms. Stansbury for yielding time and

  Mr. Speaker, I rise in support of H.R. 9600, the Common Sense 250 
Act, a bill which after two-and-a-half centuries would finally 
establish a memorial on The National Mall dedicated to the great Thomas 
Paine.
  Tom Paine arrived in America in 1774, 2 years before the American 
Revolution. He fell in love with the promise of our land, its values, 
and its ideals.
  He said that if we live up to our aspirations and our beliefs, then 
we will become an asylum to humanity, and the cause of America will 
become the cause of all mankind.
  ``The time hath found us,'' he wrote in ``Common Sense.''

  The pamphlet, as the floor leader commented, ignited the American 
Revolution. It spread across the Colonies and became the best-selling 
book of all time, other than the Bible, in the Colonies.
  George Washington had the troops at Valley Forge read the words of 
``Common Sense'' to each other by campfire light, through the 
Revolutionary War. Paine wrote it in January 1776.
  In December 1776, he wrote another pamphlet to get people through a 
very difficult time. I will update the language a little bit so as not 
to offend modern sensibilities. He said:

       These are the times that try men's and women's souls. The 
     summer soldier and the sunshine patriot will shrink at this 
     moment from the service of their cause and their country, but 
     everyone who stands with us now will win the love, favor, and 
     affection of every man and woman for all time. Tyranny, like 
     hell, is not easily conquered; yet we have this saving 
     consolation: the more difficult the struggle, the more 
     glorious in the end will be our victory.

  We have lived in the subsequent glory of Tom Paine. He has 
undoubtedly garnered the love and affection of every man and woman for 
a long time. Yet, we have never recognized it through the creation of a 
memorial.
  In 2022, we passed bipartisan congressional legislation to authorize 
the establishment of a memorial dedicated to Tom Paine in a place of 
prominence in our Capital.
  In 2024, the National Capital Memorial Advisory Commission, in 
recognition of Thomas Paine's enduring significant contributions to our 
country, recommended that the memorial be placed in Area I of The Mall. 
That recommendation has yet to be transmitted to Congress, which has 
slowed down our work.

[[Page H6243]]

  Despite concurrence between Congress and the commission on the 
extraordinary historical significance of Paine, this delay has slowed 
down the memorial's completion, which is why I worked with our 
distinguished colleague from Indiana, Victoria Spartz, to introduce 
this legislation. The Common Sense 250 Act would codify the 
commission's recommendation and direct the Department of the Interior 
to move forward with an Area I site selection.
  Mr. Speaker, I thank Representative Spartz for her strong partnership 
in this bipartisan work, as well as the dedicated advocates from the 
Thomas Paine Historical Association all over America, and everyone else 
who has fought hard to secure the legacy of this indispensable, but 
often overlooked, Founding Father.
  Mr. Speaker, I hope every Member will proudly support this effort.

  He also, I believe, made a milestone on the timeline of history and 
the expansion of Western civilization and democracy with the 
publication of ``Common Sense.''
  H.R. 9600 provides a fitting opportunity to recognize his 
contributions to our Nation's Capital as we mark 250 years of American 
independence.
  Mr. Speaker, I support this legislation, and I yield back the balance
```
