# HONORING GALILEE BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                    HONORING GALILEE BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 17, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Galilee Baptist Church 
of Shreveport, Louisiana, one of the city's most historic and enduring 
institutions of faith, community service, and African American 
leadership. Established in 1877 by formerly enslaved African Americans, 
only a little more than a decade after the end of the Civil War, 
Galilee has been a place where families could worship freely, educate 
and uplift future generations, and work together toward a stronger 
community. For nearly 150 years, the congregation has remained an 
important part of the religious, social, and cultural life of 
Shreveport.
  For Galilee's founders and the generations that followed, the church 
was far more than a house of worship. At a time when African Americans 
faced significant social, political, and economic barriers, churches 
like Galilee became essential institutions, providing a place to 
organize, develop leaders, support families, and pursue greater 
equality and opportunity. Generations of pastors, ministers, deacons, 
ministry leaders, volunteers, and members have built a congregation 
known for its faith, perseverance, and service.
  Galilee also holds a significant place in the history of the Civil 
Rights Movement in Shreveport and Northwest Louisiana. As African 
Americans across the nation organized for voting rights, equal 
treatment, educational opportunity, and an end to segregation, Galilee 
served as an important center of faith and civic engagement. Among the 
most significant moments in the church's history were visits by 
Reverend Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr. in 1958 and again in 1962, which 
reflect Galilee's standing within the movement and its commitment to 
faith-based leadership, equality, and positive social change.
  A defining chapter in the church's history began in 1959 with the 
pastoral leadership of Reverend Dr. E. Edward Jones, Sr. For more than 
five decades, Reverend Jones provided extraordinary spiritual, civic, 
and community leadership that shaped both Galilee and the broader 
Shreveport community. A respected advocate for civil rights, education, 
economic opportunity, housing, and community development, he carried 
forward the tradition of the African American church as both a house of 
worship and an institution dedicated to social progress.
  One lasting example of that vision is the establishment and 
development of what became known as ``Galilee City.'' Through efforts 
connected to housing, economic development, education, and community 
empowerment, Galilee demonstrated that its mission includes both 
spiritual transformation and tangible improvements in the lives of the 
people and neighborhoods it serves.
  Over the decades, Galilee has remained a spiritual home for 
generations of families. Children have been introduced to the Christian 
faith, young people have developed leadership skills, families have 
celebrated life's most meaningful occasions, and community members have 
found strength in times of hardship. Through worship, Christian 
education, outreach, fellowship, and youth engagement, the church 
continues to strengthen individuals and families and prepare future 
generations for leadership.
  In 2022, Reverend Craig B. Collier, Sr. was called to serve as 
Galilee's 12th pastor. Under his leadership, the congregation continues 
to honor the foundation laid by those who came before while advancing 
the church's ministry for a new generation, with a continued commitment 
to meaningful worship, Christian discipleship, community engagement, 
and service to the people of Shreveport.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in recognizing Galilee 
Baptist Church for nearly 150 years of faith, courage, leadership, and 
service, and in commending Reverend Collier and the members of Galilee 
for their continued dedication to the Shreveport community.
```
