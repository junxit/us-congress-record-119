# HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF MATILDA MADDOX

**Speaking:** Costa, Jim (C001059) [D-CA]

```
             HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF MATILDA MADDOX

                                 ______
                                 

                             HON. JIM COSTA

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 17, 2026

  Mr. COSTA. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life and legacy of 
Matilda Maddox; the matriarch of her family, a dairyman's wife, mother, 
grandmother, and great-grandmother, who passed away on September 19, 
2026, at the age of 91.
  Born on September 10, 1935, in Downey, California, Matilda was the 
oldest of ten children born to Dutch Immigrant parents: John and 
Hubertha Vink. Eventually the family moved to Hardwick, California. 
Matilda began her education at Hardwick Elementary School, before 
continuing to Laton High School where she met her future husband, 
Douglas Maddox.
  She continued her education at College of Sequoias in Visalia and 
worked while Douglas attended Cal Poly, San Luis Obispo. The two 
married in 1955 at the end of Douglas's sophomore year. To be closer 
together, Matilda moved to San Luis Obispo and worked in the admissions 
office at Cal Poly. This move coincided with the birth of their first 
child in 1956, Stephen Maddox.
  In 1957, Doug graduated with a degree in Dairy Science from Cal Poly. 
Doug and Matilda then went into partnership with Doug's parents, 
purchasing a 500-acre farm and dairy. RuAnn Dairy began with a humble 
herd of 14 cows and 14 heifers that Doug had raised while at Cal Poly. 
During this time more children were welcomed: Patrick, Julia, and 
Elizabeth.
  Matilda was a diligent dairywoman, she supported her husband, handled 
financial books, payroll, and paid the bills. In addition, she picked 
up parts, drove the tractor, and boasted that she painted all the new 
metal pipe fences on the dairy. Her hardworking spirit permitted her to 
be the RuAnn payroll clerk for another 68 years.
  Always determined to help young people set goals and achieve them, 
she felt it was her duty to teach kids the value of hard work and 
offered jobs to many. Matilda involved herself in her own children's 
activities such as PTA, 4-H, Catechism, school trips, sporting events 
and cattle shows. As a community leader, she oversaw dairy industry 
activities such as the local Dairy Princess contest, served on the 
National Holstein Women's Scholarship committee, and attended numerous 
state and national Holstein conventions where she was always involved 
in the Ladies' program,
  Through their business, Matilda and Doug had the opportunity to 
travel the world. They hosted over 100 trainees from many different 
states and countries, all of whom came to

[[Page E1217]]

learn about working on a dairy. Often, these people did not speak any 
English, yet Matilda always made the effort to share her culture and 
traditions and take the time to learn about theirs.
  An adventurous spirit, Matilda was always ready for the experience. 
She loved to travel and continued to visit Holland after her husband 
passed. And, into her early 80's, she rafted in the Grand Canyon and 
embarked on an African Safari.
  Always taking the time to encourage the best in people, the legacy 
Matilda Maddox leaves behind is that of a hardworking matriarch, who 
served her family and community.
```
