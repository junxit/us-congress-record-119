# HONORING THE LIFE OF LOUISIANA LEGEND MRS. CATHERINE BRIDGEMAN FLENTROY

**Speaking:** Carter, Troy A. (C001125) [D-LA]

```
HONORING THE LIFE OF LOUISIANA LEGEND MRS. CATHERINE BRIDGEMAN FLENTROY

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. TROY A. CARTER

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 17, 2026

  Mr. CARTER of Louisiana. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life 
of a phenomenal native Louisianian and Grambling Alum, who was a 
fixture in educating young Louisianians for decades. Born on December 
17, 1928, in Haynesville, Louisiana, Mrs. Catherine Bridgeman 
Flentroy's journey was one of passion, dedication, and an unwavering 
commitment to the betterment of society. She passed away on November 8, 
2025, in her own home, leaving behind a legacy of love, service, and 
devotion. Her tireless efforts and compassionate spirit touched 
countless lives, and her memory will continue to inspire future 
generations.
  In 1947, Mrs. Flentroy graduated as Salutatorian of her Haynesville 
High School class. In 1948, she enrolled at Grambling College, where 
she worked as a student assistant in the college library and dining 
hall. While at Grambling, she excelled academically and was actively 
involved in campus life. She was an honor student, cheerleader, 
president of the YWCA, Miss Junior, and Miss Senior. On November 13, 
1950, Mrs. Catherine Bridgeman Flentroy became a charter member of 
Delta Iota Chapter of Delta Sigma Theta Sorority, Incorporated.
  In 1951, Catherine married the love of her life, Lee Arthur Flentroy. 
To this union, two daughters were born: Daphne Colette and Vonda Kay. 
Mrs. Flentroy started her professional career as an elementary school 
teacher at Webster High School. After four years in the classroom, she 
transitioned to become the school's elementary librarian and later 
assumed the same role at J.L. Jones Elementary School. She went on to 
earn 22 units in Library Science from four different universities. 
While living in Minden, Mrs. Flentroy was also a charter member of the 
Minden Alumnae Chapter of Delta Sigma Theta Sorority, Incorporated.
  In 1968, Mr. Flentroy joined the football coaching staff at Southern 
University, and in 1970, Mrs. Flentroy and their daughters joined him 
in Baton Rouge, Louisiana. She became the elementary librarian at 
Dufrocq Elementary School, where she served with dedication. During the 
summers of 1970 to 1973, Mrs. Flennoy coordinated enrichment 
programming for the National Youth Sports Program (NYSP) at Southern 
University. While balancing her professional and family 
responsibilities, she earned a master's degree in Elementary Education 
from Southern University. In 1986, she retired and joyfully embraced 
her favorite role--being a full-time grandmother to her first 
grandchild, Britt Ashley.
  The family worshipped at Immaculate Conception Catholic Church, where 
Mrs. Flentroy served faithfully as chair of the Nursing Home Committee. 
She was a member of the Knights of Peter Claver Ladies Auxiliary 
Wilfred B. Ricard Court No. 116, Fourth Degree Ladies of Grace 
Constance Butler Chapter 9. She deeply believed in the mission of St. 
Vincent de Paul and was passionate about volunteering her time and 
energy to serve those in need through its outreach programs.
  She was a Golden Life Member of the Baton Rouge Sigma Chapter of 
Delta Sigma Theta Sorority, Incorporated, and an active member of the 
Black Teachers Association, Grambling State University Alumni 
Association, Silhouettes Club, Cardettes, and PALS. She was beyond 
honored to have her two daughters, Daphne Flentroy and Vonda Flentroy 
Rice, as well as her granddaughter, Britt Ashley Dawson to follow in 
her footsteps as Legacy Members of Delta Sigma Theta Sorority, 
Incorporated. Mrs. Flentroy enjoyed attending balls, parties, and 
athletic events--reflecting her love for people and social gatherings. 
She truly embraced life to the fullest, and her warm smile and 
welcoming spirit touched everyone she met.
  For over 30 years, she served in the classroom and as librarian at 
Webster High School, J.L. Jones Elementary School, and later Dufrocq 
Elementary School. Mrs. Flentroy was the epitome of a devoted wife, 
mother, and grandmother, always putting the needs of her family first. 
Her home was a place of warmth, love, and unwavering support. She 
approached everything with grace, integrity, and determination to make 
a positive impact. Beyond her roles as an educator and homemaker, Mrs. 
Flentroy's dedication to her community was truly exemplary, and her 
efforts made a tangible difference in the lives of so many.
  She was a source of strength and support to those closest to her, 
always offering a listening ear and a word of encouragement. Her warmth 
and generosity created a welcoming space for all who knew her, and her 
home was a place of laughter, love, and genuine connection. As we 
reflect on Mrs. Flentroy's life, let us remember her as a role model 
for us all. Her dedication to education, her commitment to service, and 
her unwavering compassion serve as an inspiration to strive for a 
better world. Her legacy will live on through the countless lives she 
touched, her devoted daughters, Daphne Flentroy and Vonda Rice 
(Charles); beloved grandchildren, Britt Dawson (Yancey), Ryan Rice 
(Seane), Jordan Rice (Ashlei), and Charles Rice, III; and great-
grandchildren, Dyllan Robertson, Jaxsen Dawson, Ryan, Jr., Brayden, 
Reagen, and Bailey Rice; and the positive impact she had on our 
community.
  May her memory be a blessing to us all.
```
