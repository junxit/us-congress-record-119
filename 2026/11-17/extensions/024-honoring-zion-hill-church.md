# HONORING ZION HILL CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                       HONORING ZION HILL CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 17, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Zion Hill Church of 
Pineville, Louisiana, a congregation whose faith has anchored Central 
Louisiana for a century. From its first church building to a ministry 
that now reaches national and international audiences, Zion Hill has 
remained committed to worship, service, and the Word of God, and it has 
long been a pillar of the Pineville faith community.
  In 1925, the first Zion Hill church was built on a lot donated by 
Reverend Hall and his wife. It was built on a solid foundation of 
Christian believers who withstood the trials

[[Page E1222]]

and tribulations of their time. From 1920 to 1960, the church was led 
by a succession of men of God: Reverend O. Simon, Reverend S.S. 
Simmons, Reverend Don Smith, Reverend J. Todd, Reverend Elvin Wells, 
and Reverend Samuel T. Johnson, who rebuilt the church while serving 
without a salary. Reverend George Smith then faithfully served as 
pastor from 1960 to 1962. The sacrifice and perseverance of these early 
leaders gave the congregation a lasting place in the life of Pineville 
and ensured that generations of believers would have a church home.
  In 1963, Reverend Dr. Richard Bennett became pastor. Under his 
leadership, members became tithers, the Baptist Street location was 
constructed, revivals were held, membership increased, and the Matrons' 
Ministry was formed. Reverend Bennett served until his death in 1980. 
During these years, Zion Hill became a church used for God's glory, 
drawing families together in worship and fellowship and strengthening 
the spiritual life of the community around it.
  Reverend Edmond D. Pointer became pastor in 1980 and was Zion Hill's 
first full-time pastor. Under his leadership, the church began 
community outreach ministries, including a partnership with the 
Shepherd's Center, a summer tutoring program, and a feeding program for 
the needy. These efforts extended the church's ministry beyond its 
walls and reflected a conviction that faith is lived out through 
service to neighbors. Serving alongside her husband, Mrs. Pointer 
started the church's first newsletter, the ZIONITE, and served as its 
editor, giving the congregation a new way to share its news and its 
message.
  In 1996, Dr. Joshua Joy Dara, Sr. moved to Central Louisiana and 
became Zion Hill's current pastor. He has now served longer than any 
pastor in the church's history. Through his charismatic and unique 
style of preaching and teaching the Word of God, membership has grown 
from 75 to more than 3,500, making Zion Hill one of the largest 
congregations in the Pineville faith community. Today, the church has 
over 50 ministries that serve every aspect of life, from children and 
families to those in need, and its services are broadcast weekly on 
multiple television stations across Louisiana and on social media 
platforms that reach national and international audiences.
  Throughout its history, Zion Hill has demonstrated what a church can 
mean to the community it serves. It has been a place of worship, a 
source of encouragement in difficult times, a partner in meeting 
practical needs, and a training ground for generations of leaders in 
the faith. The dedication of its pastors, ministers, and members, from 
the early leaders who served without pay to the congregation of 
thousands today, has helped strengthen Pineville and the surrounding 
region.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring Zion Hill 
Church, Dr. Dara, and the faithful members of this congregation for a 
century of faith and service to Pineville and Central Louisiana.
```
