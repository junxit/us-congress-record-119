# HONORING AND COMMEMORATING THE 115TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE ST. MARY ARMENIAN APOSTOLIC CHURCH

**Speaking:** Costa, Jim (C001059) [D-CA]

```
   HONORING AND COMMEMORATING THE 115TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE ST. MARY 
                       ARMENIAN APOSTOLIC CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                             HON. JIM COSTA

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 17, 2026

  Mr. COSTA. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor and commemorate the 
115th Anniversary of the St. Mary Armenian Apostolic Church in Yettem, 
California.
  Fleeing persecution from the Ottoman Empire, a group of Armenian 
refugees came to the San Joaquin Valley and decided to create their own 
settlement in 1901. Drawn to its unique location due to its rich soils, 
and view of the Sierra Nevada, it reminded them of their home in 
Armenia. After 11 years of searching for a proper settlement, Yettem, 
California was established.
  Yettem, which translates to Eden in English; was given this name 
because to Armenian refugees, this town was the only place where they 
could escape persecution from the Ottoman Empire. The land also 
provided the necessary soil and climate for a vibrant agricultural 
community.
  In 1903, under a grove of trees, the first religious services were 
held in Yettem on Pentecost Sunday. That same year, ``Yettem'' was 
officially adopted as the town name.
  St. Mary Apostolic Church was consecrated on July 3, 1911, by Bishop 
Moushegh Sarajian. Immediately, the church became the beating heart of 
the community and the first Armenian Apostolic Church in the Western 
United States. In addition to being a place of worship, the church 
became a beacon for more Armenian families to find refuge in Yettem.
  Yettem suffered through the Great Depression, then on June 12, 1945, 
a fire took out the wooden church. When the Supreme Patriarch-
Catholicos of All Armenians, Kevrok VI was elected, he had received 
news of the burning church and simply replied ``Don't worry about the 
church having been burned. We will yet build a better one.''
  And so it happened, under the leadership of Garabed Simonian and with 
the active participation of the members of the parish, the new Church 
was consecrated on November 16, 1947, by Bishop Vartan Kasparian and 
Archbishop Mampre Calfayan. The new Church was even visited by the then 
Catholicos, His Holiness Vazken I.
  However, tragedy struck again when the church hall caught fire in 
1967. The hall was rebuilt in 1968, and with donations from the 
community, rebuilt even bigger than before.
  This tradition of giving back and engaging parishioners has continued 
over for many years. Since 1991, programs and fundraisers such as the 
Yettem Open Golf Tournament, vacation bible school, student 
scholarships, and foreign missionary trips, have benefitted the church 
community.
  Since its inception, the church remains at the heart of Yettem. For 
more than a century, St. Mary's has stood as not only a beautiful house 
of worship, but as a symbol of resiliency for the proud Armenian 
community of California's San Joaquin Valley.
```
