# RECOGNIZING RHONDA SALISBURY

**Speaking:** McClintock, Tom (M001177) [R-CA]

```
                      RECOGNIZING RHONDA SALISBURY

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. TOM McCLINTOCK

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 17, 2026

  Mr. McCLINTOCK. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Rhonda 
Salisbury for more than four decades of dedicated service to the 
Yosemite region, the people of Madera County, and the millions of 
visitors who come each year to experience one of America's most 
treasured national parks. Beginning her career in tourism in 1985 and 
later serving as Chief Executive Officer of Visit Yosemite-Madera 
County, she became one of the most respected and effective advocates 
for the region and its communities.
  Throughout her career, Rhonda championed the principle that our 
national parks should remain accessible to the American people while 
being responsibly managed and protected for future generations. She 
consistently advocated for practical solutions that improved the 
visitor experience, encouraged exploration beyond Yosemite's most 
congested areas, and supported the long-term health of both the park 
and the communities that surround it.
  Just as importantly, Rhonda was a steadfast defender of the interests 
of Yosemite's gateway communities. During wildfires, government 
shutdowns, and periods of significant policy change, she provided 
trusted leadership and ensured that local residents, workers, and 
businesses had a strong voice in discussions affecting their future.
  Rhonda was also instrumental in the success of Secretarial Order 
3434, Strengthening Coordination with Gateway Communities. She was a 
leading advocate for the idea that those who live and work alongside 
our national parks should have a meaningful seat at the table when 
decisions that impact access, tourism, and local economies are made. 
Her support for greater collaboration helped transform that principle 
into reality.
  Her leadership earned statewide recognition through her service with 
the California Travel Association and her selection as the recipient of 
the 2026 Brad Burlingame Award, a fitting tribute to a career defined 
by service, generosity, and results.
  Above all, Rhonda will be remembered by the countless visitors who 
made their way to the Yosemite region from around the world. For many, 
a trip to Yosemite National Park was a once-in-a-lifetime experience at 
one of America's greatest natural treasures. Whether they knew her by 
name or not, they benefited from her tireless efforts to ensure their 
visit was welcoming, enjoyable, and memorable. In that way, her 
influence extends far beyond the communities she served and into the 
lasting memories of generations of travelers.
  Mr. Speaker, as Rhonda Salisbury concludes her distinguished career, 
she leaves behind an enduring legacy of service. Thanks in no small 
part to her efforts, Yosemite National Park enjoys a closer and more 
productive relationship with its gateway communities than ever before. 
Local leaders and residents now have a stronger voice in decisions that 
directly affect their communities, their livelihoods, and the visitor 
experience. For her tireless advocacy, her unwavering commitment to 
public access, and her devotion to the people of the Yosemite region, 
she has earned our gratitude and admiration. On behalf of the United 
States Congress, I wish her the very best in her well-deserved 
retirement.
```
