# HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF MRS. JULIA "JUDY" WATKINS BOWLES

**Speaking:** Bishop, Sanford D. (B000490) [D-GA]

```
   HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF MRS. JULIA ``JUDY'' WATKINS BOWLES

                                 ______
                                 

                      HON. SANFORD D. BISHOP, JR.

                               of georgia

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 17, 2026

  Mr. BISHOP. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life and legacy of 
a woman who

[[Page E1227]]

spent her life making her hometown more beautiful, in every sense of 
that word, Mrs. Julia Watkins Bowles, known and loved by all as 
``Judy.'' Judy went home to be with the Lord on November 12, 2026, at 
her home, surrounded by the family she adored. On November 14, 2026, 
family and friends gathered at Crown Hill Cemetery in Albany to 
celebrate a life that was itself a gift to the people of Middle and 
Southwest Georgia.
  Judy was a daughter of Albany from her very first breath. She was 
born at Phoebe Putney Memorial Hospital to Harold E. Watkins and 
Frances Cornell Watkins, grew up in the city she would spend a lifetime 
serving, graduated from Albany High School in 1959, and attended 
Wesleyan College in Macon. Her studies carried her up the road, but her 
heart never left home.
  Her faith was the root from which everything else grew. Judy was a 
lifetime member of First United Methodist Church of Albany, where she 
gave of herself in nearly every way a person can. She served as 
Chairman of the Council of Ministries, Vice Chair of the Administrative 
Board, and Assistant Financial Secretary. She taught Sunday school, 
sang in the choir, chaired a church walk-a-thon, and served on the 
church staff under Dr. Bill Hinson and John Whorton. Her church was not 
simply a place she attended; it was a family she helped to hold 
together.
  For 33 years, Judy served as Director of Keep Albany Dougherty 
Beautiful, and Albany will bear her fingerprints for generations. She 
planted more than 660 trees for the Trees of Courage along Westover 
Boulevard. She helped secure the funding for the ``Welcome to Albany'' 
signs that greet every traveler who enters our cjty. And when the 
waters of the Flint River rose in the Great Flood of 1994 and so much 
of our community was underwater, Judy helped organize some 33,000 
volunteers to lift Albany back up. In the darkest days our city has 
known, she was a light.
  Her leadership reached far beyond Dougherty County. Judy was selected 
to serve on the National Board of Directors of Girls Inc., the Board of 
Directors of Keep America Beautiful and its National Directors, the 
State Keep Georgia Beautiful Foundation, and the Board of Directors of 
the Georgia Recycling Coalition. She served as President of the Keep 
Georgia Beautiful Directors Association. She was appointed by the 
Lieutenant Governor of Georgia to the Board of Directors of the Flint 
River Regional Water Planning and Policy Center, was a member of the 
2002 Inaugural Class of the Institute for Georgia Environmental 
Leadership and completed the Executive Directors course at the 
University of Georgia.
  At home, there was hardly a table of service at which Judy did not 
sit. She was a member of the Leadership Albany Class of 1988, Women in 
Network, the Albany Area Chamber of Commerce, and the Rotary Club. She 
served as President of Girls Inc. from 1988 to 1989, President of the 
Junior League, and a member of the Ballet Theatre South Board of 
Directors from 2007 to 2010. She served on the Technical Review 
Committee of Marine Corps Logistics Base Albany, the Albany Gang Task 
Force, the Hospice Resource Advisory Committee, and the Merck Chemicals 
Community Advisory Panel, and she chaired the Mayor's Called to Service 
initiative.
  Her work did not go unnoticed. Judy received the Keep America 
Beautiful Leadership Award, the Georgia Department of Community Affairs 
Commissioner's Leadership Award, and the Albany Police Department 
Superior Service Award. Yet those who knew her best will tell you she 
never sought the spotlight. She simply saw what needed doing, rolled up 
her sleeves, and did it.
  Mr. Speaker, as much as Judy gave to Albany, her greatest treasure 
was her family. She was preceded in death by her beloved husband of 66 
years, Jim L. Bowles, and by her parents. Today, the love that Judy and 
Jim built lives on in their sons, Bill E. Bowles and his wife, Tonya, 
and Chip Bowles and his wife, Joy, of Albany; their grandchildren, 
Brandon Bowles, Sydney Bowles and her husband, Ethan, of Leesburg, Bill 
Bowles, II of Sasser, and Lauren Tabb and her husband, Cody, of 
Colquitt; their greatgrandchildren, Olivia Hughes, Savannah Rusk, Avery 
Tabb, and Bristol Tabb; her brother, Joe C. Watkins and his wife, 
Ellen, of Albany; and her sister, Catherine W. Ward of Moultrie.
  There is an old saying that a society grows great when people plant 
trees whose shade they will never sit in. Judy Bowles planted more than 
660 of them. In the springs and summers to come, children who never had 
the privilege of knowing her will walk beneath those branches along 
Westover Boulevard and find relief from the Georgia heat. Travelers 
will pass a sign that says ``Welcome to Albany'' and feel at home. 
Every bloom, every clean street, every neighbor who came together after 
the flood is a living testament to a woman who loved this place with 
her whole heart. Albany is more beautiful because Judy Bowles lived, 
and it is a little less bright now that she is gone.
  Mr. Speaker, my wife, Vivian, and me, along with the more than 
765,000 people of Georgia's 2nd Congressional District, extend our 
deepest condolences to the Bowles and Watkins families and to all who 
loved Judy. We pray that they will be consoled and comforted by an 
abundance of friends and cherished memories in the days, weeks, and 
months ahead, and that they will find peace in the assurance of Matthew 
25:23: ``Well done, thou good and faithful servant: thou hast been 
faithful over a few things, I will make thee ruler over many things: 
enter thou into the joy of thy lord.''
  I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring the life, legacy, and 
enduring service of Mrs. Julia ``Judy'' Watkins Bowles.
```
