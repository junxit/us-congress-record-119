# REMEMBERING DR. WALTER BOYNTON

**Speaking:** Hoyer, Steny H. (H000874) [D-MD]

```
                     REMEMBERING DR. WALTER BOYNTON

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. STENY H. HOYER

                              of maryland

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 17, 2026

  Mr. HOYER. Mr. Speaker, I rise to pay tribute to a great advocate for 
the Chesapeake Bay, Dr. Walter Boynton, who passed away on September 
14, 2026.
  Walter first came to work at the Chesapeake Biological Institute in 
1975, after completing his doctorate in environmental engineering from 
the University of Florida and obtaining a master's in marine sciences 
from the University of North Carolina, Chapel Hill. When he arrived, he 
found an estuary facing ecological collapse. Underwater grass beds were 
dying, destroying critical habitats for crabs and fish as well as 
making low-oxygen conditions in the Bay even worse. State and federal 
agencies were only just starting to work together to understand what 
was going wrong and find solutions, and Walter answered the call to 
help
  In a five-decade career that spanned science research and advocacy, 
Walter pursued a relentless mission to restore the Bay's essential 
habitats and restore its health. By reducing nutrient and sediment 
runoff from across the Chesapeake Bay watershed, he was confident that 
ecologists could make the Bay more hospitable for the growth of 
underwater grasses. That same strategy, he believed, would also limit 
the occurrence of low oxygen ``dead zones'' in the Bay. His research 
helped state and federal leaders develop a restoration strategy that is 
still in place today and that helps guide federal and state investment.
  Without Walter's innovative thinking and leadership, it is difficult 
to imagine the Bay in the cleaner, healthier, and more widely enjoyed 
condition it is today. Moreover, without his tireless advocacy, several 
major investments in the Bay's future might not have come to fruition. 
These include a nearly $80 million authorization for federal partners 
to help engage in Chesapeake Bay conservation efforts in 2008 and 
further investment in research vessels in 2021, on both of which I was 
proud to partner with Walter.
  Walter was more than a scientist and advocate. He was a mentor to 
many, and he was a friend to many more--myself included. I hope the 
House will join me in offering condolences to his beloved wife of 53 
years, Mary Ellen, and to his daughters, Jessica and Sarah, and their 
families--including Walter's cherished granddaughter Eliza. May his 
memory continue to inspire all of us who love the Chesapeake Bay and 
its watershed to do all we can to conserve it for future generations to 
enjoy.
```
