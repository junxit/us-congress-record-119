# CELEBRATING THE 50TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE LEGISLATIVE COORDINATING COMMISSION'S OFFICE ON THE ECONOMIC STATUS OF WOMEN

```
   CELEBRATING THE 50TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE LEGISLATIVE COORDINATING 
          COMMISSION'S OFFICE ON THE ECONOMIC STATUS OF WOMEN

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. BETTY McCOLLUM

                              of minnesota

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 17, 2026

  Ms. McCollum. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to celebrate the 50th 
anniversary of the Legislative Coordinating Commission's Office on the 
Economic Status of Women for shining a light on women in Minnesota and 
the economic issues that they face.
  Established by the Minnesota State Legislature in 1976 as the Council 
for the Economic Status of Women, this nonpartisan office dedicated to 
study and report to the legislature all matters related to the economic 
status of women in Minnesota, has survived different configurations and 
names over the years as it has battled to continue to exist. As the 
original Director, Nina Rothchild led the Council on the Economic 
Status of Women from 1976 to 1983. In 1983, the council became the 
Legislative Commission on the Economic Status of Women. In 2005, it was 
aligned under the Legislative Coordinating Commission and renamed the 
Office on the Economic Status of Women.
  The Office on the Economic Status of Women has always operated with a 
very small staff and included some state legislators in its membership. 
During much of my time as a state legislator, I was honored to serve in 
leadership at the Legislative Commission on the Economic Status of 
Women--from 1993 to 1995 as Vice-Chair and as Chair from 1995 to 1997. 
I had the pleasure of serving under the direction of Aviva Breen, who 
led the Legislative Commission on the Economic Status of Women as its 
Director from 1983 to 2001. Serving on the commission gave me the 
opportunity to see firsthand how impactful their work is. The Office on 
the Economic Status of Women has published newsletters, annual 
legislative summaries, fact sheets, and reports on a wide range of 
important topics that affect women's lives. In 2026, publications have 
included fact sheets on Minnesota's Women-Owned Businesses, Women's 
Labor Force Participation, Women's Educational Attainment, Vital 
Statistics, Women in the Minnesota Legislature, Marriage & Divorce, and 
Sexual Violence in Minnesota.
  Through its steadfast work informing and advising the state 
legislature on advancing women's economic issues, The Office on the 
Economic Status of Women has assisted in moving Minnesota policymaking 
forward over these past five decades. As a result of the task force 
efforts by the Council on the Economic Status of Women, in 1982, the 
Minnesota legislature passed groundbreaking legislation--the State 
Government Pay Equity Act. This also led to the Local Government Pay 
Equity Act becoming law in 1984. The work of the Office on the Economic 
Status of Women also helped lead to the passage of the Women's Economic 
Security Act (WESA)--landmark legislation which was signed into law on 
Mother's Day 2014 and has been strengthened several times since then.
  Mr. Speaker, please join me in celebrating the 50th anniversary of 
the Office on the Economic Status of Women and commending them for 
providing valuable information and recommendations to the Minnesota 
state legislature, which address women's economic issues.
```
