# PRIORITIZING BASICS IN PUERTO RICO

**Speaking:** Hernandez, Pablo Jose (H001103) [D-PR]

```
                   PRIORITIZING BASICS IN PUERTO RICO

  (Mr. Hernandez of Puerto Rico was recognized to address the House for 
5 minutes.)
  Mr. HERNANDEZ. Mr. Speaker, the people of Puerto Rico want us to 
prioritize the basics of water and energy, and to fight corruption.
  Last year in this Congress, I denounced alleged irregularities in the 
procurement process for an emergency energy generation contract.

                              {time}  1100

  That concern was bipartisan. Former Congressman Mark Green and I 
cosigned a letter asking questions that weren't answered.
  It turns out those concerns were warranted.
  Let's take a cold look at the facts. On August 28, 2025, last year, 
Power Expectations and Reyes Contractor won a $5.9 billion contract for 
temporary energy generation in Puerto Rico. But Power Expectations and 
Reyes Contractor didn't have the required expertise or financial 
capacity to perform the contract. The government agency in charge of 
procurement essentially told them: find a partner who does, create a 
consortium, and then you have a deal.
  Eventually Power Expectations and Reyes Contractor found Enchanted 
Rock. A person called Jhoby Weaks, COO of Caribbean Operations, signed 
the contract on Enchanted Rock's behalf.
  The oversight board, which must authorize contracts over $10 million, 
approved the contract. Everyone was happy. Puerto Rico would have 
emergency energy generation in time for the summer peak.
  The happiness didn't last. It turns out, Enchanted Rock had warned 
the government that there was a mistake: that they didn't sign the 
contract, that Jhoby Weaks was not an Enchanted Rock employee, and that 
the position of COO for Caribbean Operations did not even exist within 
their company.
  Apparently, someone forged the firm's signature.
  But it gets worse. Before the alleged forgery, Enchanted Rock got a 
letter from a law firm. They claim the letter attempted to blackmail 
them, and when they replied, the law firm denied having sent that 
letter. The firm denied having ever represented Power Expectations or 
Reyes Contractor. Apparently, someone forged the law firm's letter.
  Enchanted Rock informed the Government of Puerto Rico even before the 
contract was executed. You would expect that after all these 
irregularities, the relevant government agencies would have responded 
decisively, that they would have rescinded the contract, that they 
would have reported the fraud. But they didn't. Instead, they went and 
substituted Enchanted Rock with another company through an assumption 
and assignment agreement.
  Nearly 2 months later, someone blew the whistle. An investigative 
journalist broke the news. Someone forged a signature in a $5.9 billion 
energy contract.
  The government knew, and the government hid it from the public, hid 
it from the oversight board, and hid it from the power authority.
  When the oversight board found out, they canceled the contract. Under 
public pressure, the government referred the matter to law 
enforcement--again, 2 months late.
  The people of Puerto Rico deserve answers and accountability.
  Congress, which has assigned $17 billion for Puerto Rico's energy 
grid, deserves answers and accountability. What did the Government of 
Puerto Rico know? When did the government know it? Why did the 
government hide it? Was it corruption, incompetence, or both?
  This scandal has direct consequences over the people. There are power 
outages because we don't have emergency energy generation.
  As the people of Puerto Rico's Representative in Congress, I want to 
emphasize the following: The vast majority of Puerto Ricans are neither 
corrupt nor incompetent. Like the vast majority of American citizens 
and Members of Congress, we detest corruption, and we stand up to it.
  I urge my colleagues to join me in this crusade of good government. 
Let's ask the questions and demand the answers. Let's work together to 
hold the crooks accountable, to fix the basics, and turn the lights 
back on in Puerto Rico.

                          ____________________
```
