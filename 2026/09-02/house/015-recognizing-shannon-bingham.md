# RECOGNIZING SHANNON BINGHAM

**Speaking:** Bacon, Don (B001298) [R-NE]

```
                      RECOGNIZING SHANNON BINGHAM

  (Mr. Bacon of Nebraska was recognized to address the House for 5 
minutes.)
  Mr. BACON. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Shannon Bingham of 
Omaha, Nebraska.
  Shannon is an entrepreneur, keynote speaker, author, and advocate 
whose life and service have been profoundly shaped by the power of 
adoption. Recently named a two-time Nebraska Merit Mother of the Year, 
Shannon has dedicated her life to strengthening families, supporting 
children, and raising awareness about adoption.
  Her advocacy began in her own home when Shannon adopted her son Seth. 
She quickly realized that adoption is not simply about welcoming a 
child into a family. It is a lifelong commitment to unconditional love, 
understanding, and growth. Like many adoptive families, their story has 
been filled with both joy and significant challenges. Through it all, 
Shannon developed an understanding of the unique needs of adoptees and 
the importance of ensuring families have the resources they need to 
thrive.
  What began as a personal family journey has grown into a powerful 
mission to help others. In 2023, Shannon, Seth, and their family stood 
alongside Nebraska Governor Jim Pillen as he proclaimed National 
Adoption Awareness Month in the State of Nebraska. Since then, this 
inspiring mother-and-son duo have continued to use their voices to 
support families navigating their own adoption paths.
  Today, Shannon continues as an adoptive family mentor with the 
Nebraska Children's Home Society. In

[[Page H5432]]

this role, she provides support and encouragement to prospective and 
adoptive families. She has also shared her family's story across 
numerous television media and speaking platforms, using every 
opportunity to educate, advocate, and inspire.
  Through her platform, Adoption is an Option, Shannon works to 
increase awareness, encourage families to consider adoption, and 
advocate for children awaiting permanent loving homes. She believes 
that every child deserves a true sense of connection, belonging, and 
the knowledge that they are deeply loved.
  By sharing both the beautiful moments and the real challenges of 
adoption, Shannon has become a champion for children and families 
across Nebraska and beyond. Her story is a testament to this belief 
that while adoption begins with a single act of love, its impact echoes 
for generations.
  Shannon and her husband, Kurt, have three children, Seth, Ian, and 
Nora.


                         Recognizing Sue Morris

  Mr. BACON. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Sue Morris, a 
leader and a true champion of community transformation in Nebraska.
  For 25 years as president of Heritage Omaha, she shaped the culture 
and civic landscape of our region. Under her leadership, the 
organization realized over $1 billion of community projects and raised 
more than $750 million in private philanthropic funds. From the Holland 
Performing Art Center and the Joslyn Art Museum to the University of 
Nebraska Omaha's Baxter Arena and Charles Schwab Field, her work has 
transformed our community.
  Sue's approach to philanthropy is to always focus on stewardship, 
ethics, and deep community impact. She mastered the art of aligning a 
donor's unique passion with a meaningful public need, viewing herself 
not just as a fundraiser but as a facilitator for the common good.
  Her recent work represents what she calls the crown jewel of her 
professional life. As president of the Veterans Trust, Sue spearheaded 
the development of the new Ambulatory Care Center at the Omaha VA 
Medical Center, which serves thousands of veterans across Nebraska and 
western Iowa. The Ambulatory Care Center was the first-of-its-kind 
public-private partnership under the landmark legislation known as the 
CHIP-In for Veterans Act, which Sue helped shepherd through Congress. 
Her efforts on behalf of veterans continue to see the CHIP-In for 
Veterans Act become the permanent pathway for construction of veterans' 
healthcare facilities through public-private partnerships.
  After leaving Heritage Omaha in 2020, Sue served as an adviser at 
Nebraska Philanthropic Trust until retiring in July 2026. Today, Sue 
remains a vital force in our community, driven by a simple and enduring 
desire to make a positive impact.
```
