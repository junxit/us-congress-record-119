# RECOGNIZING CAROLYN BROWN

**Speaking:** Wied, Tony (W000829) [R-WI]

```
                       RECOGNIZING CAROLYN BROWN

  (Mr. Wied of Wisconsin was recognized to address the House for 5 
minutes.)
  Mr. WIED. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize an exceptional 
teacher in northeastern Wisconsin who retired this summer after decades 
of dedicated service in education.
  Mrs. Carolyn Brown began teaching at my alma mater, Notre Dame 
Academy, in Green Bay, in 1996, following nearly three decades in 
education. She taught English and journalism and was notably Notre Dame 
Academy's first International Baccalaureate-trained teacher.
  Beyond the classroom, Mrs. Brown was known as the school's biggest 
cheerleader. She could be found at games, plays, musical performances, 
and just about every other school event, always showing up to support 
her students.
  She also started the school's annual Fall Fest Week and the Academy 
Awards talent show, giving students a chance to share their school 
spirit and talents while, of course, being there to cheer them along 
the way.
  Mr. Speaker, we are so blessed to have educators such as Mrs. Carolyn 
Brown in northeast Wisconsin. I thank her for her many years of service 
to the youth of Green Bay, and I wish her the very best as she begins 
her much-deserved retirement.


    Recognizing Brown County Deputy District Attorney Wendy Lemkuil

  Mr. WIED. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Brown County Deputy 
District Attorney Wendy Lemkuil as she enters retirement after years of 
dedicated public service.
  For three decades, Deputy District Attorney Lemkuil has served the 
people of Brown County in the courtroom, doing work that is often 
difficult, demanding, and unseen by the public.
  Throughout her career as a prosecutor, Deputy District Attorney 
Lemkuil has primarily handled drug- and homicide-related cases, 
successfully prosecuting hundreds of cases in her commitment to 
protecting our communities and keeping Brown County safe.
  Through her service to northeastern Wisconsin, she has made a lasting 
impact not only on our communities but also on the people with whom she 
has worked. Those who know her and have learned from her have said that 
it will be nearly impossible to fill her shoes.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in congratulating Deputy 
District Attorney Wendy Lemkuil on her retirement and on thanking her 
for her years of honorable service to Brown County and northeastern 
Wisconsin.

             Recognizing Winnebago County Sheriff John Matz

  Mr. WIED. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Winnebago County 
Sheriff John Matz as he enters retirement after serving 16 years as 
sheriff and a total of 36 years with the Winnebago County Sheriff's 
Department.
  Sheriff Matz has lived a life defined by service, leadership, and a 
commitment to helping others. He served in the Wisconsin National Guard 
for 30 years and worked as a deputy throughout that time. During his 
service, he deployed to Iraq in support of Operation Iraqi Freedom and 
retired as the command sergeant major of Wisconsin's 120th Field 
Artillery Battalion.
  Few people have devoted as much of their lives to their nation and 
community as Sheriff John Matz.
  Through decades in the military and law enforcement, he has shown his 
dedication and commitment to serving the people of Winnebago County and 
has made a lasting impact on countless lives across northeastern 
Wisconsin.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in congratulating Sheriff 
John Matz on his retirement and thanking him for his decades of 
honorable service to Winnebago County, northeast Wisconsin, and our 
Nation.


             Recognizing Kewaunee County Sheriff Matt Joski

  Mr. WIED. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Kewaunee County 
Sheriff Matt Joski as he enters retirement after more than 33 years of 
dedicated public service.
  Sheriff Joski's career reflects a deep commitment to serving his 
community, his State, and his country. Before his law enforcement 
career, Sheriff Joski served for 11 years in the United States Marine 
Corps. He later continued that life of service as Kewaunee County 
sheriff and through his dedicated work with the Wisconsin National 
Guard.
  Throughout his career, Sheriff Joski has been known not only for his 
dedication to public safety, but also for his writing on mental health, 
physical safety, and community issues, and for his mentorship with 
first responders and military personnel. The trust he built between law 
enforcement and the communities across Kewaunee County has made a 
lasting impact on northeastern Wisconsin.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in congratulating Sheriff 
Matt Joski on his retirement and in thanking him for his decades of 
honorable service to Kewaunee County, northeastern Wisconsin, and the 
United States of America.
```
