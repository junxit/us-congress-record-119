# RECOGNIZING LITTLE LEAGUE WORLD SERIES

**Speaking:** Thompson, Glenn (T000467) [R-PA]

```
                 RECOGNIZING LITTLE LEAGUE WORLD SERIES

  (Mr. Thompson of Pennsylvania was recognized to address the House for 
5 minutes.)
  Mr. THOMPSON of Pennsylvania. Mr. Speaker, for more than 75 years, 
Williamsport, Pennsylvania, has welcomed hundreds of baseball players 
and spectators across the world to participate in the Little League 
World Series.
  Founded by Carl Stotz, this event has long been a direct reflection 
of the American spirit and love for the game.
  During the inaugural game in 1947, only teams from Pennsylvania and 
New Jersey competed, and a local team from Williamsport won.
  Today, Little League participation has expanded both in the United 
States and across the globe. Approximately 2 million boys and girls 
play Little League worldwide, representing more than 80 countries and 
all 50 States.
  While the game has not changed, participation has. Since 1947, more 
than 20 girls have played in the series.
  Mr. Speaker, children who participate in Little League learn about 
sportsmanship, responsibility, hard work, and, of course, build 
friendships that last a lifetime.
  Former players have gone on to play baseball and softball in college, 
been drafted into professional teams, become coaches, and often serve 
as leaders in their communities.
  Every year, the Little League World Series welcomes more than 300,000 
fans. This event brings communities together and would not be 
successful without the hard work of volunteers who help support the 
teams, the fans, and the coaches.
  This annual event is a great way to continue celebrating our Nation's 
founding and reveling in our shared love for baseball.
  For many years, I have attended the games and loved seeing the teams, 
fans, and volunteers supporting one another.
  Mr. Speaker, I congratulate all the teams that competed and thank all 
the volunteers, umpires, and officials who make this annual event 
possible.
```
