# EXTEND TEMPORARY PROTECTED STATUS FOR SALVADORANS

**Speaking:** Raskin, Jamie (R000606) [D-MD]

```
           EXTEND TEMPORARY PROTECTED STATUS FOR SALVADORANS

  (Mr. Raskin of Maryland was recognized to address the House for 5 
minutes.)
  Mr. RASKIN. Mr. Speaker, in Maryland's beautiful Eighth Congressional 
District, Montgomery County, Maryland, something shocking is about to 
happen.
  In 1 week, tens of thousands of people in our community will lose 
their Salvadoran temporary protected status. They will lose their work 
authorizations, and many will lose the careers that they have worked 
hard to build here, packing up whatever belongings they can fit into a 
couple of suitcases. They may be separated from the rest of their 
families and forced to return to a country where they will face 
violence and human rights violations. Their departure will create great 
dislocation and heartbreak in many neighborhoods.
  For the past 25 years, Salvadoran TPS holders have raised their 
families here and sent their kids to our schools, started small 
businesses, and added to the vibrant culture of Maryland's Eighth 
District.
  They have believed in America, and they form an essential part now of 
our communities. The Free State is the third-largest population of 
Salvadoran TPS holders in America. Every year, the 23,000 of them who 
call our State home contribute an estimated $732 million to our 
community. They have been a powerful driver of our prosperity.
  One of my Salvadoran constituents who obtained TPS and arrived in 
Maryland when she was just a baby has built an outstanding career here 
as an educator at an elementary school where she herself went to school 
in the only State and in the only country she has ever known.
  As a product of Montgomery County Public Schools, she is proud to 
give back to the community that helped her family establish a life in 
the United States. She hopes to keep educating the next generation of 
leaders. Yet the looming end of TPS next week threatens to strip her 
away from her students. The abrupt revocation of her status also 
threatens to separate her from her U.S. citizen spouse.
  Another constituent also arrived in Maryland as a child. After 
escaping brutal poverty in El Salvador, she went on to achieve success 
by providing professional cleaning services to households throughout 
our community. She has been an active member of her local church and 
congregation. With her TPS designation now in question, she worries 
about having to abandon her livelihood, her family, and the community 
she loves. She does not know what the future holds.

  Other hardworking Salvadorans have played an important role in the 
growth of one of Montgomery County's finest paint manufacturing 
companies. In their many years working for the company, these 
constituents have made impressive use of their work authorizations.
  They manage business affairs for the company, test its new paint 
formulas, and efficiently facilitate the delivery of products to 
doorsteps across our community.
  These neighbors have enriched the social, economic, and cultural life 
of our State. Their loss will be felt in classrooms left without 
teachers, unbuilt houses, renovations unfinished, landscaping projects 
abandoned, friendships severed, and families deprived of breadwinners 
and loved ones.
  The Trump administration, if you are listening, please extend this 
vital program. Do not turn a blind eye to what is taking place. America 
has always served as an asylum for humanity. As Tom Paine said, it is a 
place of refuge for hardworking people fleeing economic, intellectual, 
political, religious repression and poverty from all over the world.
  The administration should extend TPS and assure Salvadorans of their 
place in our community.

                          ____________________
```
