# OLOWALU FIRE STATION

**Speaking:** Tokuda, Jill N. (T000487) [D-HI]

```
                          OLOWALU FIRE STATION

  (Ms. Tokuda of Hawaii was recognized to address the House for 5 
minutes.)
  Ms. TOKUDA. Mr. Speaker, after the Lahaina wildfire, West Maui knew 
we could not afford to wait to strengthen fire protection and emergency 
response, so the community got to work.
  They donated land and raised $3 million to build a new fire station 
in Olowalu. This station will cut emergency response times by up to 15 
minutes. That can mean the difference between life and death, between a 
home burning and a home being saved. It will also cut fire insurance 
costs by nearly half for homeowners, saving families thousands of 
dollars annually.
  The land was donated. The money was raised. The project was fully 
funded. Then President Trump's steel tariff slapped over $300,000 to 
the cost, not because the project changed, not because it went over 
budget, but because the President changed our trade policy. This is 
what reckless tariffs look like in real life.
  Today, it is a fire station on Maui, but across our country, 
families, businesses, and communities are paying more because of the 
President's tariffs, including for the things that they need to keep 
themselves safe.
  A fire station is not a luxury, not in West Maui, not after Lahaina. 
I am fighting to get relief for Olowalu, but we cannot fight these 
tariffs one fire station, one small business, or one community at a 
time. Congress has the constitutional authority over tariffs and trade, 
and it is time we take that responsibility back.
  No President should be able to unilaterally impose tariffs that make 
it harder and more expensive for Americans to protect their homes, 
their communities, and their lives.
  West Maui has already paid too high a price. We should be making 
communities safer, not making safety more expensive.


                             Hurricane Lala

  Ms. TOKUDA. Mr. Speaker, Hurricane Lala never technically made 
landfall in Hawaii, but disasters aren't measured by whether the eye of 
a storm crosses a shoreline. They are measured by what they leave 
behind, and Lala left devastation.
  Over 3 days, an estimated 1.3 trillion gallons of rain fell across 
our State. Hawaii Island took the brunt of it, with some communities 
seeing 20 to 40 inches. I saw the damages myself.
  At Na'alehu Elementary, buildings were pushed off their foundations 
by the force of water and debris. Parts of Highway 11 were washed away. 
At Na'alehu Cemetery, floodwaters tragically disturbed the resting 
place of generations of loved ones.
  Hundreds of homes across Hawaii were damaged or destroyed. At the 
height of the storm, over 200,000 people were left without power.
  However, I also saw Hawaii at its best: neighbors checking on 
neighbors, community hubs feeding families, supplies flown in by 
helicopter and carried on horseback to communities cut off by damaged 
roads, school workers preparing food and hygiene supplies for our 
``children,'' ``keiki,'' even while their campuses were closed.
  When disaster strikes, Hawaii shows up. Now Washington needs to show 
up for Hawaii. I am grateful the President approved an emergency 
declaration, bringing critical FEMA resources to Hawaii, Maui, and 
Honolulu counties, but we need a major disaster declaration and 
individual assistance to help our families, farmers, and small 
businesses recover.
  The storm may be gone, but the emergency is not. Families are still 
without power. People still need food, medicine, clothing, and basic 
necessities. Roads, homes, schools, farms, and businesses need to be 
repaired and rebuilt. Even as we recover, we have to prepare, because 
the Pacific is still churning. More storms are coming, and they will 
not wait for Hawaii to recover.
  We need to replenish supplies, strengthen critical infrastructure, 
restore essential services, and make sure our most isolated communities 
are prepared to withstand whatever comes next.
  Help our people recover, prepare for what is coming next, and do it 
now. That is what Hawaii needs from Washington.

                          ____________________
```
