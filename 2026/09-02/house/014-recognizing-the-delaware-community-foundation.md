# RECOGNIZING THE DELAWARE COMMUNITY FOUNDATION

**Speaking:** McBride, Sarah (M001238) [D-DE]

```
             RECOGNIZING THE DELAWARE COMMUNITY FOUNDATION

  The SPEAKER pro tempore (Mr. McDowell). The Chair recognizes the 
Member from Delaware, Representative McBride, for 5 minutes.
  Ms. McBRIDE. Mr. Speaker, last month, the Delaware Community 
Foundation celebrated 40 years of contributing to the social and 
financial health of the First State.
  Initially pioneered by my predecessor in this Chamber and our former 
Governor, Pete DuPont, the Delaware Community Foundation is one of the 
top 100 community foundations in the country and has spent the last 
four decades infusing financial support into critical initiatives, 
community partners, nonprofits, and education for Delawareans across 
our State.
  Today, DCF is led by my friend and neighbor, Stuart Comstock-Gay, a 
community leader who is dedicated to improving the quality of life for 
all Delawareans.
  Over the last 40 years, DCF has engaged in philanthropy, community 
collaboration, and thoughtful investments in medical research, 
scholarships for students, nonprofit support, and improving health 
equity.
  DCF, like community foundations across the country is a critical 
driver of investment that can and have made a difference in the lives 
of every Delawarean.

  Mr. Speaker, I congratulate DCF on this tremendous milestone, and I 
thank them for their decades of enduring support and community impact 
that have shaped the lives of countless Delawareans.


                             Remember 9/11

  Ms. McBRIDE. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to remember the lives lost 25 
years ago on September 11, 2001. What started as a beautiful Tuesday in 
September turned into a day of horror, as nearly 3,000 lives were lost 
in the deadliest terrorist attack against our Nation in history. Two 
and a half decades later, our hearts go out to all those for whom that 
day brought a fateful call, for some from a loved one in a plane or 
trapped in a burning building and for others, in the hours or days 
after, to learn that their missing parent, child, or sibling had 
perished.
  Across our country, Americans will gather for this somber 
commemoration to pay tribute to the lives lost in Lower Manhattan, 
Northern Virginia, and western Pennsylvania, and to recommit to the 
charge echoed so often in the aftermath of the attacks to never forget.
  Let us never forget the tragedy and loss of that day nor the heroism 
that illuminated a nation in one of its darkest hours. As planes were 
hijacked, fires raged, and fear struck in the hearts of Americans 
everywhere, heroes literally ran into the fire and charged a cabin, 
sacrificing themselves to save others; first responders flocked to the 
Twin Towers and into the Pentagon to save lives; and firefighters, 
police officers, medical providers, and ordinary citizens confronted 
death and destruction to save as many lives as they could.
  As the long effort to find those missing began after the towers fell, 
many left their own families to focus full time on digging through 
smoldering rubble to find as many survivors as possible. Many who 
dedicated months of their lives to that rescue mission saw their own 
lives cut tragically short from the toxins they inhaled at Ground Zero.
  The sacrifice of those on the ground was matched by those in the air 
above Pennsylvania that morning, as the bravery of passengers on Flight 
93 saved countless lives in our Nation's Capital. Once they realized 
the fate before them--targeting either the White House or the very 
building we stand in today--they decided to act. These passengers, who 
were strangers just 2 hours before, joined together to intervene in a 
cruel plot to attack the very symbols of freedom and democracy.
  That was the power of the heroism on and after September 11. There 
were no strangers to those who risked everything to save anyone.
  As I stand in this Chamber 25 years later, I remain as inspired by 
their service and sacrifice as I was as an 11-year-old watching on TV.
  When I think about how scared all of us were on that day, I am 
reminded of one of my favorite quotes from Mr. Rogers, who shared that 
when he was young and used to see something scary on the news, his 
mother would tell him to look for the helpers.
  On that day and in the days and weeks that followed, helpers were 
everywhere. Heroes were everywhere. Mr. Speaker, 25 years later, let us 
not only remember, but let us also recommit ourselves to summoning just 
a fraction of the courage of the helpers in the wake of that tragedy.
  May God bless the memories of those lost, and may we remember the 
enduring legacy of courage that saved lives two and a half decades ago.
```
