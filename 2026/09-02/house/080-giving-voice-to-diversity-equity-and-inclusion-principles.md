  First, diversity. Diversity simply means the quality or condition of 
having or being composed of different elements, such as forms, types, 
and ideas. There was a time that our military wasn't diverse, and then 
the American people said there is nothing wrong with African Americans 
serving in the military. There is really nothing wrong with women 
serving in the military. There were some folk all across the country 
who said, oh, you are going to weaken the military. Did it make the 
military weaker? No, you bet it didn't. It made the military stronger. 
When we became diverse, it became the most fierce fighting machine in 
the world.
  We need to talk about what is good about diversity, equity, and 
inclusion and not talk about all of these scare tactics that we have 
heard from people all across the country.
  Second is equity. What is equity? The quality of being fair, just, 
especially in a way that takes account and seeks to address existing 
inequities.
  Let's be honest about it. There are people in this country who have 
been facing all types of inequities from the day they were born.
  We used to debate affirmative action, for example. Folk used to say 
affirmative action is two parallel lines where you take somebody who is 
unqualified and elevate them to the level of somebody who is qualified. 
That wasn't affirmative action. Affirmative action should be viewed as 
a circle instead of two parallel lines. When you take a certain person 
out of the circle, they are all equally qualified. We have been 
fighting those battles all along.
  There has been some talk that there is no need for equity. Businesses 
across America have decided to end their diversity, equity, and 
inclusion programs as a result of the Executive Order No. 14151 signed 
by the President on January 20, 2025.
  Finally, Mr. Speaker, inclusion. Who can be against inclusion? 
Inclusion means inclusion. People of all walks of life. There was a 
time in this country that Blacks weren't able to even register to vote. 
This Nation said that was wrong, and they changed it and passed the 
15th Amendment.

  Our STEM education workforce has suffered because of sweeping cuts to 
DEI initiatives. Since January 2025, the Trump administration has made 
dramatic cuts to NSF programs, many aimed at broadening opportunity for 
underrepresented students in STEM education.
  Of the 1,500 grants that NSF has canceled, nearly 90 percent had 
references to DEI.
  One of those terminated grants was an NSF award at the University at 
Albany, which I represent in the 20th Congressional District of New 
York. Called Striving to Achieve Gender Equity in STEM, this program is 
essential. It aims to increase the number of women in STEM fields.
  Just last month, the Department of Justice declared three Federal 
educational programs aimed at diversifying STEM as unconstitutional.
  University at Albany has done great work with funding from the Louis 
Stokes Alliance for Minority Participation programming to develop 
inclusive teaching practices and increase participation by 
underrepresented students in STEM.

  Terminating programs like this sends a clear signal by this 
administration of who is allowed to participate in science.
  Upholding scientific integrity means protecting the independence of 
science and the civil rights of our scientific workforce.
  Beyond attacks on our scientific education system, we have seen 
attacks on our historical institutions and museums in the name of anti-
wokeness: from policing content at national parks and monuments, to 
removing artwork and displays referencing slavery, to attacking the 
independence of the Smithsonian and how stories are told.
  This manipulation of history serves to consolidate political power by 
erasing marginalized voices and experiences and limiting whose humanity 
is considered valid.
  DEI is not radical, wasteful, and certainly is not just an acronym or 
a policy. It means our histories, our stories, no matter how 
uncomfortable and challenging, continue to be shared. It means first-
generation college students can join programs that connect them to 
research and open doors to new career paths.
  Mr. Speaker, it is a commitment to removing barriers and expanding 
opportunity so that every person can succeed. It is a commitment we 
must continue to fight for, making certain that there is equity, 
inclusion, and diversity that brings the great diversity of this Nation 
into a celebratory moment, one that speaks to fairness and justice, to 
opportunity, to make certain that all people, no matter what their 
background, have a chance to succeed and share their talent with the 
American experience.
