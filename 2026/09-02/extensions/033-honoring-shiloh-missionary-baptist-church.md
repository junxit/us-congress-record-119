# HONORING SHILOH MISSIONARY BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
               HONORING SHILOH MISSIONARY BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                      Wednesday, September 2, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor a distinguished church 
in my District, Shiloh Missionary Baptist Church in Baton Rouge, 
Louisiana, which has served this community for more than 150 years, 
with a history rooted in faith, service, education, and community 
advancement.
  The church was organized in 1872 by Reverend George Byrd, with its 
first house of worship located on Boyd Avenue in the Spanish Town area 
of Baton Rouge. Reverend Byrd faithfully served as Shiloh's first 
pastor for thirty-four years.
  Following Reverend Byrd's retirement in 1906, Reverend Jack Mitchell 
became Shiloh's second pastor. During his pastorate, the church moved 
from its original Boyd Avenue location to 14th Street. As the 
congregation continued to establish itself in the community, the 
existing church structure was demolished in 1932 and replaced with a 
new building on South 14th Street.
  As Reverend Mitchell advanced in age, Dudley T. Smith was ordained to 
the Gospel ministry at his request and served as his assistant. After 
Reverend Mitchell's retirement, Reverend Dudley T. Smith succeeded him 
as pastor in 1944, continuing Shiloh's legacy of spiritual leadership 
and service.
  Following the death of Reverend Dudley T. Smith in February 1962, the 
congregation elected his youngest son, Reverend Charles T. Smith, as 
pastor in April of that year. His fifty-year pastorate would become a 
significant period of growth and development in Shiloh's history.
  Beginning in the 1970s, Shiloh undertook a major expansion of its 
physical and educational presence. Between 1972 and 1980, the church 
constructed its present sanctuary and first educational building on 
South 13th Street at a total cost of approximately seven hundred 
thousand dollars, including the land. Demonstrating the congregation's 
commitment and stewardship, the debt was retired in 1988, seven years 
ahead of schedule.
  Shiloh's commitment to education took another important step forward 
in 1975 with the establishment of a scholarship program for church 
members under the leadership of Reverend Charles T. Smith. What began 
as an investment in the educational aspirations of Shiloh members grew 
into a program that, according to the church's history, awards more 
than sixty-five thousand dollars annually.
  The church campus continued to grow in 1997 with the completion of a 
new social, educational, fellowship, and office complex. The one-and-a-
half-million-dollar facility included eighteen classrooms, five 
offices, a library, and a greatly enlarged gymnasium, further expanding 
Shiloh's capacity for Christian education, fellowship, administration, 
and community ministry. The debt incurred for the project was retired 
in 2007.
  That same year, Shiloh expanded its charitable reach through the 
creation of Shiloh's Charitable Foundation, Incorporated. Established 
in 2007, the Foundation provided another avenue through which the 
church could address community needs, with areas of focus that include 
education, economic development, and social services.
  After fifty years of pastoral leadership, Reverend Charles T. Smith 
retired in 2012 and passed away in September of that year. In February 
2013, the congregation elected Reverend Fred Jeff Smith as Shiloh 's 
fifth pastor, continuing a pastoral legacy that had extended through 
his father and grandfather.
  Today, Shiloh has grown from the congregation established on Boyd 
Avenue in 1872 into a church with more than three thousand members and 
over fifty employees. Throughout generations of pastoral leadership, 
physical expansion, educational investment, and community outreach, 
Shiloh has remained committed to its mission as a holistic ministry, to 
grow, teach, and help others come to know the Shalom, or peace, of God.
  From its beginnings in Spanish Town to its present ministry in Baton 
Rouge, the history of Shiloh Missionary Baptist Church reflects more 
than the growth of a congregation. It is a story of faith passed from 
generation to generation, investment in education and community, 
responsible stewardship, and an enduring commitment to serving the 
whole person and the whole community.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in recognizing Shiloh 
Missionary Baptist Church for more than 150 years of faithful service, 
and in wishing its pastor and congregation many more years of blessings 
and growth.
```
