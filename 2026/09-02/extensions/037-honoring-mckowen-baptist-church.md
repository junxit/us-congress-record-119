# HONORING McKOWEN BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                    HONORING McKOWEN BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                      Wednesday, September 2, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise to honor McKowen Baptist Church, a 
cornerstone of Baton Rouge, Louisiana, since its founding in 1910. For 
over a century, McKowen Baptist has exemplified unwavering faith, 
service, and leadership throughout the community. Guided by Reverend 
Gerard Robinson's steadfast hand, the church has flourished, nurturing 
its congregation and expanding its outreach with compassion and 
purpose.
  In 1910, a devoted group of Christians, members of McKowen Baptist 
Church Number 1 in West Feliciana Parish, found themselves in Baton 
Rouge without a church home. Led by Rev. John Davis, they gathered at 
Brother Israel Davis's home to lay the foundation for McKowen Baptist 
Church Number 2. As their numbers grew, they moved into their first 
wooden church building, organized a deacon and steward board, and 
appointed Bro. Z. R. Hardy as church secretary.
  Despite limited financial resources, these pioneers were rich in 
faith, hope, and determination. Their first revival in 1911 saw two 
conversions, and under Rev. Davis's two-year leadership, eight new 
members joined. In 1911, Rev. Hardy was ordained as pastor and began 
his remarkable thirty-year tenure, during which he baptized 
approximately 600 individuals and welcomed 400 by Christian experience. 
In March 1921 alone, fifty-two candidates were added to the church. 
Under Rev. Hardy, the church was repaired and expanded three times, 
eventually relocating to McCalop and Swart Streets.
  When Rev. Hardy fell ill in 1941, Rev. Oscar Livous, Sr., became 
assistant pastor and was later elected pastor in 1943, Rev. Livous's 
leadership saw hundreds christened, baptized, and married, as well as 
the licensing of ministers. In 1956, McKowen was rebuilt as a beautiful 
brick structure, a source of great pride for the congregation. Civic 
expansion in 1961 prompted a search for a new church site, with Sis. 
Jessie Spand instrumental in securing the property. Several members 
placed their homes as security for the purchase, demonstrating 
remarkable commitment. In 1962, after months of dedicated effort, the 
congregation triumphantly moved into the nearby church. Following Rev. 
Livous's passing in 1965, Rev. Alvin McCrory was elected pastor.
  Under his guidance, the church flourished spiritually and 
financially, cancelled the mortgage, and expanded its programs to 
engage all age groups. New ministries and groups, including the General 
Missionary Society, Concert Choir, Junior Ushers, Drama Club, and 
Futurettes, were established. Rev. McCrory also launched the 
Brotherhood and the Annual Easter Breakfast, and initiated youth 
services. McKowen Baptist Church, under Pastor McCrory's leadership, 
opened its doors to the Head Start early childhood program, serving 
families for three years. He implemented public ordination of deacons 
and oversaw the completion of a multipurpose addition to the church.
  After Rev. McCrory's death in 1979, local ministers and the East 
Baton Rouge Ministers' Fellowship ensured the continuation of worship 
services and ceremonies. Dr. S.H. Freeman was elected pastor in 1980, 
bringing dynamic leadership and fresh ideas. McKowen expanded its 
ministries, including a Day Care Learning Center, adult and youth Bible 
classes, and reorganized Christian education. The church welcomed 61 
new members in 1981 and continued to thrive. In 1990, Reverend Dr. 
Gerard A. Robinson, Sr. became the sixth pastor, guiding McKowen 
Missionary Baptist Church to new heights. Pastor Robinson revitalized 
ministries such as Music, Christian Education, and Pastor's Care, and 
introduced numerous new programs including Boyz-n-the Hood, Females of 
Faith, Drama, GLAM, Mission, Greeters, Brothers & Sisters of Faith, 
Dance, Nursing, Senior Adult, Youth Mission, Children's Church, Male 
Chorus, Inspirational Choir, Junior Deacons and Deaconess, Van, 
Website/Media, Weekly National Intercessory Prayer Call, and a 
televised worship service.
  In 2005, Pastor Robinson established Freedom Tax Services, empowering 
members to prepare taxes affordably and gain valuable job training. 
Facility improvements included sanctuary remodeling, business office 
expansion, a new pastor's study, and a conference room. Pastor Robinson 
also enhanced Wednesday Bible Study and initiated a youth Bible class 
for ages 2-11. His leadership and talents have earned him numerous 
honors, including the Good Samaritan Award, and recognition for his 
musical and oratory gifts. He has served in many key roles across 
ministerial alliances, community organizations, and advisory boards, 
and contributed significantly to public service and community 
engagement. Under Pastor Robinson's stewardship, 1,189 individuals have 
been called and baptized, 741 by Christian experience and 448 by 
baptism.
  Mr. Speaker, I invite my colleagues and the community to join me in 
celebrating McKowen Baptist Church, Reverend Robinson, and the faithful 
congregation for their extraordinary legacy and ongoing impact. Their 
commitment to faith, service, and leadership continues to inspire and 
uplift families across Baton Rouge, leaving a legacy for generations to 
come.
```
