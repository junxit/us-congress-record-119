# HONORING THE LIFE OF JOE "JOEY" COLUMBUS SANDERS, JR.

**Speaking:** Aderholt, Robert B. (A000055) [R-AL]

```
        HONORING THE LIFE OF JOE ``JOEY'' COLUMBUS SANDERS, JR.

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. ROBERT B. ADERHOLT

                               of alabama

                    in the house of representatives

                      Wednesday, September 2, 2026

  Mr. ADERHOLT. Mr. Speaker, I would like to honor the life and memory 
of my longtime friend, Joe ``Joey'' Columbus Sanders, Jr., of 
Birmingham, Alabama, who passed away on August 30, 2026, at the age of 
65.
  I had the privilege of knowing Joey from the very beginning of my 
political career, and throughout the years, I was proud to consider him 
a friend. Anyone who spent time in Alabama political circles over the 
past several decades knew Joey, or at least knew of his work. He had a 
remarkable understanding of people and politics, a natural instinct for 
campaigns, and, most importantly, a genuine love for Alabama and its 
people.
  Joey was raised in Greenville, Alabama, and graduated from Fort Dale 
Academy in 1979. He went on to earn a degree in Broadcast Journalism 
from The University of Alabama in 1983. While at Alabama, he served as 
president of the College Republicans, beginning what would become a 
lifetime of involvement in Republican politics and the conservative 
movement.
  His service eventually took him to the national stage as president of 
the National Young Republicans and as a member of the site selection 
committee for the 1988 Republican National Convention. He also served 
on the staff of Senator Jeremiah Denton, both in Washington and as his 
State Field Director.
  Joey possessed a true gift for political campaigns. Over the years, 
countless candidates and public officials sought his counsel, 
creativity, and expertise. He eventually founded Master Image, Inc., 
where he put those talents

[[Page E845]]

to work, helping candidates at the national, state, and local levels. 
His influence on Alabama politics extended far beyond any single 
election or campaign, and many who have served our state benefited from 
his friendship, advice, and experience.
  But politics was only one part of Joey's life. He was deeply involved 
in his community, serving organizations including the Homewood Library 
Foundation, City Stages, the Birmingham Irish Cultural Society, the 
Birmingham Jefferson Convention Complex Authority, and the Vestavia 
Hills Civitan Club. During his service on the BJCC board, he was 
involved in a transformative period for the complex that included its 
renovation and the development of Protective Stadium and the Coca-Cola 
Amphitheater.
  Those who knew Joey personally will remember much more than his 
professional accomplishments. He was a storyteller, a historian, a 
cook, an outdoorsman, and someone who simply enjoyed bringing people 
together. Some of his happiest times were spent at Sanders Farm in 
Greenville, surrounded by family and friends, often gathered around a 
fire with a pot of his homemade camp stew. He was generous with his 
time, his friendship, and, as many people across Alabama can attest, 
his homemade pepper sauce and salsa.
  Joey never forgot where he came from, and he remained a strong 
advocate for the outdoors, wildlife conservation, hunters' rights, the 
Second Amendment, and the conservative principles that guided him 
throughout his life.
  Above all, Joey loved his family. He leaves behind his wife of 30 
years, Luanne; his children, Holton and Anne Heaton; his 
granddaughters, Poppy and Honey; his sister, Linda; and a large 
extended family and countless friends who will miss him greatly.
  For me personally, it is difficult to say goodbye to someone who has 
been a friend for virtually my entire political career. Joey was part 
of Alabama politics for decades, but his legacy cannot be measured 
simply by campaigns won, candidates elected, or projects completed. It 
is found in the friendships he built, the people he helped, the stories 
he told, and the memories he leaves with all of us who were fortunate 
enough to know him.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring the life of 
Joe ``Joey'' Columbus Sanders, Jr., and in extending our deepest 
condolences to Luanne, his children and grandchildren, and the entire 
Sanders family. I am grateful to have called Joey my friend, and I know 
his presence will be deeply missed across Alabama.
```
