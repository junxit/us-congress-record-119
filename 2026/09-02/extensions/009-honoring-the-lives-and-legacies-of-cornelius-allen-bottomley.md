# HONORING THE LIVES AND LEGACIES OF CORNELIUS ALLEN BOTTOMLEY, JR. AND JOHN WILLIAM HUGHAN

**Speaking:** Gottheimer, Josh (G000583) [D-NJ]

```
 HONORING THE LIVES AND LEGACIES OF CORNELIUS ALLEN BOTTOMLEY, JR. AND 
                          JOHN WILLIAM HUGHAN

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. JOSH GOTTHEIMER

                             of new jersey

                    in the house of representatives

                      Wednesday, September 2, 2026

  Mr. GOTTHEIMER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the lives of 
Cornelius Allen Bottomley, Jr., known to everyone who loved him as 
Allen, and his nephew, John William Hughan. It is fitting that we 
remember them here together, because that is how their family holds 
them in their hearts.
  Allen Bottomley, of Hanover, Massachusetts, passed away on September 
13, 2024, at the age of 54, surrounded by his loving family. He was 
born on December 18, 1969, in Boston to the late Dorothy Warnock 
Bottomley and Cornelius Allen Bottomley, Sr. He graduated from 
Plymouth-Carver High School in 1988, earned his bachelor's degree in 
computer science from Bucknell University in 1993, and completed his 
Master of Business Administration at the F.W. Olin School of Business 
at Babson College in 2000.
  Allen's strong work ethic showed up in everything he did, at home and 
on the job. After Bucknell, he spent six years working alongside his 
father, Neil, as Director of Operations for their company, Evaluation 
Associates, in Plymouth, Massachusetts. After earning his MBA, Allen 
served for a short time as a financial analyst at Send.com in Waltham 
before beginning a twelve-year run at Fidelity Investments' Boston 
Coach, where he held many positions and rose to Senior Vice President 
of Strategic Operations Analysis. Beginning in 2015, he brought that 
same steadiness to Granite Telecommunications in Quincy, where he 
served as Vice President of Treasury and Financial Operations.
  Mr. Speaker, the resume tells you what Allen did. It does not tell 
you who he was. Allen was known for his quick wit, his boisterous 
laugh, his kind heart, his brilliant mind, and a fierce loyalty to 
family and friends. He chose to surround himself with genuine, 
trustworthy, and intelligent people. He loved boating on Cape Cod, 
skiing in Vermont, and sitting contentedly at home with his family in 
front of a beautiful fire.
  Allen cooked and carved the turkey on Thanksgiving. He chose and cut 
down the family Christmas tree each December. He offered sage advice to 
family and friends, and he managed to fix anything and everything that 
broke around the house. Those are small things, and they are the things 
families hold onto.
  Allen is survived by Jane (Connolly) Bottomley, his wife of 26 years, 
and their two children, Cornelius Haraden Bottomley, III, known as 
Haraden, and McKenzie Jane Bottomley. He was immensely proud of all 
three, and he loved watching his son and daughter grow into the amazing 
young adults they are today. Jane is quick to admit that the first 
thing that drew the two of them to one another was their ability to 
make each other laugh, and that laughter grew into a deep love, a deep 
respect, and an uncomplicated happiness rooted in simply being 
together. His family has said that Allen's ultimate goal was their 
happiness, and that they intend to keep trying to succeed and make him 
proud. Mr. Speaker, I cannot think of a better tribute to a husband and 
a father than that.
  Allen also leaves behind his sister, Allison Bottomley Hughan, his 
brother-in-law, Peter, and his niece, Emmeline, of Norwell, 
Massachusetts. He was predeceased by his nephew, John.
  John William Hughan was taken from his family far too soon. He was 
born in Boston and lived in South Boston until he was five years old. 
He moved to Norwell, where he attended the William Gould Vinal 
Elementary School and took up new sports, the trumpet, and Cub Scout 
Pack 66. He went on to Norwell Middle School, where he worked hard to 
excel academically.
  John had a natural talent for music. He worked at the trumpet for 
years, and he was selected for the Massachusetts Music Education 
Association Southeastern District Honor Band and the Southeastern 
Massachusetts School Bandmasters Association Honor Band in both seventh 
and eighth grade, along with the Bridgewater State Middle School Honor 
Band in those same two years. He was a member of the United Church of 
Christ Norwell, where he had taken part in the music program since he 
was five years old, starting in Cherub Chimes, moving on to the bells 
in Rockin' Ringers, and eventually performing trumpet solos. He had 
just completed his confirmation, and over the years he took part in 
numerous service events. John loved every genre of music, and his 
family often caught him singing or humming along to a song.
  John was an athlete, too. He played club lacrosse with the 
Laxachusetts program beginning in third grade and was looking forward 
to joining his Laxachusetts 2028 team and the Norwell High School team. 
His love of soccer grew in seventh grade, and he had just started 
tryouts at Norwell High School, excited to play alongside his 
classmates. He was a member of Boy Scout Troop 38 in Hanover, 
Massachusetts, where he had recently achieved his Life Rank and was 
working toward becoming an Eagle Scout. For his Eagle project, he 
wanted to build an outdoor classroom at Norwell Middle School.
  John spent his summers on Cape Cod, and he spent most of those days 
on the water. He raced Optis for many summers and made friends at yacht 
clubs across the Cape. He had started sailing 420s and was looking 
forward to racing more the next summer with his friends at Chatham 
Yacht Club. He spent many hours on the golf course, and his game 
improved to the point that his goal was to beat his dad. Weekends were 
often family days on the powerboat, heading over to Hyannis, to 
Nantucket, or just putting around Bass River. He almost always had a 
smile.
  Mr. Speaker, John was a conversationalist. He could talk with anyone 
and would talk with anyone, and from an early age he believed everyone 
wanted to talk with him. He was curious and full of questions. He was 
the kind of friend who would try to make you laugh, make you feel 
heard, and make you feel important. His friends and classmates describe 
him as kind, always with a smile.
  John was a wonderful big brother. He was proud of his sister 
Emmeline's accomplishments, watched her sporting events, cheered her on 
from the sailing racecourse, and beamed when she succeeded. He was 
forever trying to improve her lacrosse game, talk through sailing 
strategy, and battle it out with her in the front yard playing soccer. 
They had a special bond and a real reliance on each other. He was a 
remarkable son, too. His mother hugged him every day, sometimes having 
to chase him out the door to be sure she got her hug.
  Mr. Speaker, Allen Bottomley's greatest accomplishments were being a 
father, a husband, a son, a brother, an uncle, a brother-in-law, and a 
friend, and his happiest moments were the ones he spent with his 
family. John Hughan will be remembered for how hard he worked at 
everything he tried, for the kindness that came through in everything 
he did, and for his compassion toward his friends and family. Both of 
them will be missed beyond words.
  On the two-year anniversary of their passing, I ask my colleagues to 
join me in honoring the lives and legacies of Cornelius Allen 
Bottomley, Jr., and John William Hughan, and in keeping the Bottomley 
and Hughan families in our thoughts. May God continue to bless and 
watch over their souls.
```
