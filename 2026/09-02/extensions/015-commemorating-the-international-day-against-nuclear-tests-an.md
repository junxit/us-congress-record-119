# COMMEMORATING THE INTERNATIONAL DAY AGAINST NUCLEAR TESTS AND KAZAKHSTAN'S ENDURING CONTRIBUTION TO GLOBAL SECURITY

**Speaking:** Titus, Dina (T000468) [D-NV]

```
     COMMEMORATING THE INTERNATIONAL DAY AGAINST NUCLEAR TESTS AND 
         KAZAKHSTAN'S ENDURING CONTRIBUTION TO GLOBAL SECURITY

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. DINA TITUS

                               of nevada

                    in the house of representatives

                      Wednesday, September 2, 2026

  Ms. TITUS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to mark the International Day 
Against Nuclear Tests and recognize Kazakhstan's enduring contribution 
to world peace and security.
  August 29, 2026, marked 35 years since Kazakhstan's historic decision 
to close the Semipalatinsk nuclear test site. In tribute to that 
decision, the United Nations General Assembly unanimously designated 
August 29 as the International Day Against Nuclear Tests in 2009, 
drawing global attention to the devastating human and environmental 
consequences of nuclear testing. Similarly, on September 8, Kazakhstan 
and its Central Asian neighbors will mark the 20th anniversary of the 
signing of the Treaty on a Nuclear-Weapon-Free Zone in Central Asia.
  The consequences of nuclear testing are deeply understood in both our 
countries. More than one thousand U.S. nuclear tests exposed 
servicemembers, workers, and downwind communities in Nevada and 
throughout the West to radioactive fallout. At Semipalatinsk, 456 
Soviet nuclear tests between 1949 and 1989 exposed approximately 1.5 
million people and left a lasting human and environmental toll. The 
Nevada-Semipalatinsk Movement, which united citizens affected by 
nuclear testing in Kazakhstan and the United States, helped generate 
the public momentum necessary to end testing at the site.
  After inheriting the world's fourth-largest nuclear arsenal following 
the dissolution of the Soviet Union, Kazakhstan voluntarily 
relinquished those weapons and chose security through diplomacy, 
international cooperation, and nonproliferation policy. The United 
States was a close partner in this historic transformation. Through the 
Nunn-Lugar Cooperative Threat Reduction Program, our countries 
eliminated weapons and delivery systems, dismantled nuclear 
infrastructure, sealed test tunnels, and secured dangerous materials in 
the former Soviet Union. Project Sapphire alone removed nearly 600 
kilograms of highly enriched uranium from Kazakhstan.
  Kazakhstan quickly translated its national commitment into a regional 
security framework. In 2006, the five Central Asian states signed the 
Treaty on a Nuclear-Weapons-Free Zone in Central Asia at Semipalatinsk. 
The Treaty remains an important pillar of regional stability and the 
global nonproliferation regime, transforming a former center of nuclear 
testing into a symbol of arms control cooperation. Kazakhstan has 
continued to put these principles into practice. It hosted two rounds 
of P5+1 negotiations with Iran in 2013 which led to the Joint 
Comprehensive Plan of Action, supported implementation of the 2015 
nuclear agreement, and now hosts the IAEA Low Enriched Uranium Bank at 
the Ulba Metallurgical Plant. Kazakhstan's commitment to 
denuclearization and stability demonstrates how the credibility earned 
through disarmament and reinforced by decades of cooperation with the 
United States can help address one of today's most difficult nuclear 
challenges.
  Mr. Speaker, we should recognize Kazakhstan not only for its historic 
decisions, but also for the responsibilities it continues to assume. 
Its example proves that nonproliferation is not an abstract aspiration, 
but a practical instrument of national security and diplomatic problem-
solving.
  I urge the Senate to ratify the Protocol to the Central Asian 
Nuclear-Weapon-Free Zone Treaty, which the United States signed in 
2014. This step would reinforce American nonproliferation objectives 
and recognize the obligations Kazakhstan and its neighbors have upheld 
for two decades. I also urge Congress to repeal the Jackson-Vanik 
amendment and establish permanent normal trade relations with 
Kazakhstan. This outdated Cold War-era restriction bears no reasonable 
relationship to modern trade relations with Kazakhstan, its partnership 
with the United States, or its extraordinary contribution to global 
nuclear security.
  Kazakhstan has demonstrated that disarmament can strengthen 
sovereignty and national security. The United States should recognize 
this unprecedented record not only with words, but through concrete 
action.
```
