# HONORING NEW HOPE BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                    HONORING NEW HOPE BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                      Wednesday, September 2, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor a distinguished church 
in my District, New Hope Baptist Church in Baton Rouge, Louisiana, as 
it approaches eight decades of worship, discipleship, and service to 
the Eden Park community and the greater Baton Rouge area.
  The New Hope Baptist Church, with its seating capacity of two 
hundred, was organized on North 46th Street on Sunday, September 28, 
1947, under the leadership of the late Reverend J.S. Wicks, with 
approximately fifty members who ventured in faith to make an impact on 
the community and together secured governmental services and 
educational resources beneficial to Eden Park. New Hope grew 
numerically and spiritually with Church Sunday School, Vacation Bible 
School, evangelistic efforts, and the formation of the Adult Music 
Ministry, while musicals, culinary endeavors, and trips to beaches 
garnered funds to meet needs.
  Pastor Wicks served as the under-shepherd for twenty-nine years, and 
then, in the spirit of love, Dr. Leo Daniel Cyrus, Sr., was installed 
as the church's second pastor on the fourth Sunday, August 22, 1976. 
The first Children's Choir was formed, Bible study began, the usher 
ministries increased, and the deaconess ministry was organized.

[[Page E843]]

  Under his leadership, New Hope experienced significant spiritual and 
numerical growth, and as a consequence of spirit-led sermons and 
worship experiences the North 46th Street sanctuary could no longer 
accommodate the increasing numbers. Pastor Cyrus and a Relocation 
Committee explored several sites before the majority selected a site on 
Greenwell Springs Road, where construction began in 1985 with 
relocation and dedication services held in 1988. The church later 
expanded its facilities with the Hope Center and continued its 
involvement in education, youth development, and community service.
  In 2025, Reverend Samuel Calvin Lofton, Jr., was elected as the third 
pastor of New Hope Baptist Church, succeeding Dr. Cyrus, who became 
Pastor Emeritus after nearly forty-nine years of service, and today the 
membership continues to strive to keep the vision of the church and its 
longstanding mission of worship, fellowship, ministry, evangelism, 
discipleship, and service to the Baton Rouge community.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in commending New Hope 
Baptist Church for nearly eighty years of faithful service, and in 
wishing its pastor and congregation continued blessings in the years to 
come.
```
