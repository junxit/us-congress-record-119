# HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF MR. BRUCE JOHN

**Speaking:** Courtney, Joe (C001069) [D-CT]

```
             HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF MR. BRUCE JOHN

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. JOE COURTNEY

                             of connecticut

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, September 24, 2026

  Mr. COURTNEY. Mr. Speaker, I rise to honor the life of a beloved 
local musician from Connecticut's 2nd District, Mr. Bruce John who 
sadly passed away on June 29, 2026. Mr. John lived in downtown Storrs, 
Connecticut, for over 70 years and, in that time, he became a highly 
sought after, revered community musician. Since the 1960s, Mr. John has 
perfomned in coffee houses, outdoor festivals, schools, senior centers, 
and at countless folk events with both his guitar and sweet voice. As 
the last troubadour of Willimantic, as he was known, he has provided 
Eastern Connecticut with more than the gift of fine music, but also a 
life dedicated to service to others and selfless philanthropy.
  Mr. John grew up in Mansfield, Connecticut, graduated from E.O. Smith 
High School in 1969, and attended Eastern Connecticut State University. 
He started his musical career in elementary school with a memorable, 
rousing performance of ``Blue Suede Shoes'' at the school's talent 
show. From that point on, he was hooked on performing for audiences big 
and small, who never stopped applauding his charisma and joy on stage. 
Despite that heady success, he stayed down to earth. He remained 
closely connected with his family, friends and neighbors in Eastern 
Connecticut despite rubbing elbows with world renowned musical stars.
  Mr. John's authentic brush with stardom originated when he cofounded 
the hallowed music venue, The Shaboo Inn, which hosted local artists 
and world-renowned giants like Aerosmith, the Police, Blondie, and 
Miles Davis until its closing in 1982. He also performed as the 
``Singasaurus'' in The Kidsville Kuckoo Revue, providing family-
friendly entertainment around Tolland County since he founded it in 
1989. He was a part of other bands as well including The Gas Company, 
Tonight Only, Dinosaurs After Dark, and The Bruce John Genre Jam. Mr. 
John was not only part of the cultural life of his community as a 
talented musician, but he prepared the stage for others to enrich the 
lives of his neighbors both young and old.
  Mr. John was able to convert his musical and entrepreneurial talents 
to benefit multiple local charities. Mr. John founded the Bread Box 
Folk Music Theater in Willimantic with his wife, Therese, to raise 
funds for the Covenant Soup Kitchen. So far, the theater has raised 
over $275,000 since its creation in 2009 to benefit the soup kitchen. 
He also ran benefit dances for 20 years with his band, the ``Dinosaurs 
After Dark'', to pay the rent of the Project Hope Homeless Shelter in 
Willimantic. He has raised over $175,000 for Project Hope, while 
personally giving to many other local Eastern Connecticut charities. 
These a just a small sample of the substantial help he donated to his 
neighbors in need and in crisis, that made such a positive difference 
in their lives. On September 27, 2026, there will be a memorial concert 
in his beloved town of Willimantic, with some of his musical friends 
and colleagues in front of hundreds and hundreds of fans to pay tribute 
to his extraordinary, inspiring life.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me and the people of 
Willimantic to express our deep admiration for Mr. Bruce John's 
tremendous life of service. May he serve as an inspiration to us all on 
how we might use our special gifts to serve one another. Join me also 
in expressing our deepest condolences to his wife, Therese John; his 
siblings, Kim and Gary; his daughters, Emma and Mia; his sons-in-law, 
Sean and Joshua; and his grandchildren.
```
