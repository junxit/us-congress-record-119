  I keep this goal front and center as a member of the Senate 
Agriculture, Nutrition, and Forestry Committee. And I am proud to say

certainty that they need.

relief without subsidies, by strengthening domestic demand for corn and 
ethanol.

E15 at the gas pump and to save some money.

                        Artificial Intelligence

  Mr. WARNER. Madam President, I think we would all probably agree that 
we are living through in realtime the biggest technological 
transformation in generations.
  I was lucky enough to be involved in the beginnings of the wireless 
industry. As an investor, I invested in companies in the internet. As 
significant as both of those are, they pale in comparison to what we 
are facing now with this transformation, and there is a lot of good 
that will come out of artificial intelligence. We all know that AI is 
already changing the way Americans work, the way businesses compete, 
the way scientists conduct our research, and, frankly, as to what our 
government should do to protect our country. Let me be clear at the 
outset, America has got to continue to lead this transformation.
  I am down, today, with two of my colleagues--the Senator from Hawaii 
and the Senator from New Jersey--to say that we can't dawdle at this 
moment. Each of us has got a whole series of AI legislative items. I 
have got ideas about data centers, about kids' safety, about liability 
for AI agents, national security. Senator Rounds and I, for example, 
today, talked about a workforce transformation.
  But we have been at a moment in time, over the last few weeks, where 
the AI companies themselves have acknowledged that they are creating 
tools and entities that they may not even be able to control. We have 
seen one company, OpenAI, launch a series of 3,000 AI agents that 
jumped over the security barriers, attacked a different company, 
Hugging Face, came back and tried to attack their own company--these AI 
agents together. The

[[Page S4972]]

remarkable thing is, these 3,000 agents talked to each other, 
communicated, tried to hide from humans, and literally left little bits 
of code on their paths so that subsequent groups of AI agents might 
come and discover those.
  I have met, over the last couple of weeks, with business leaders from 
finance and from across corporate America who all say: What are you 
guys doing?
  This is the issue of our time. Yes, I have got frustrations on data 
centers, but this is the issue of our time. This is going to be the 
biggest transformation of our lifetimes, and we don't have even de 
minimis safety protocols. We are completely relying on the good will of 
these large AI frontier tech companies to kind of do the right thing. 
That is irresponsible. You know, I am proud of the role I play in the 
national security domain. I have been chair or vice chair of the Intel 
Committee for the last decade.
  Another company--not OpenAI. That was the Hugging Face incident. 
Anthropic created a new model called Mythos, and our head of cyber 
security--CYBERCOM--and the National Security Agency, General Rudd, 
said, basically, that Mythos is going to change warfare almost as much 
as drones. Luckily, we got a first look at that but only because the 
one company was willing to give that forward.
  So we ought to have a time to have a fulsome debate about data 
centers, kids' safety, liability, and other issues, but right now--and 
we were going to do this today, but we are going to come back on Monday 
because the Senate has left. We are, kind of, the three of us--and we 
are talking to other colleagues on both sides of the aisle--saying: 
Let's strip back all of our personal ideas, and let's just put a marker 
down that potentially we could act on because we hear from some of the 
technology leaders that we might lose, you know, the ability to control 
some of these tools not in years or months, but some folks say--and I 
am not fully believing them--in weeks. I don't believe--I am not a 
``doomer'' on AI--I don't think the robots are going to take over the 
world--but I do think, if we had an event where an AI tool went out and 
took over a hospital or took over a water system or shut down a power 
system--and Americans are already concerned about the rate of this 
technology change--they could shut down this innovation, and that would 
be the worst for our country. We need to lead this in terms of some of 
our competitors like China.
  So what we are going to be proposing is, I think, as simple as 
possible. Before any AI model coming out of these frontier labs is 
released to the public, it ought to be tested to make sure it is safe. 
A lot of these big companies also do some self-testing, but they do it 
in what is called a sandbox, where they are supposed to protect it. 
Well, we have seen that some of these sandboxes are pretty sloppy. We 
ought to create basic cyber security standards for that sandbox. When 
you start at the very beginning of how an AI model is developed, we 
ought to make sure that company has some basic safety protocol in 
place. Then, God forbid, if there is an incident, there ought to be an 
incident reporting so we know what is happening because the difference 
is, in past innovation, you could have a mistake, but it might--the 
amount of damage that could happen would be fairly limited. 
Potentially, this could be so much greater, and we literally are having 
the companies themselves say: Help us.
  Now, Lord knows we have got to make sure this is not an effort to 
simply ensconce the four or five biggest companies. We have got to make 
sure smaller tech--VC--has a place on this safety panel, but we need 
national security.
  We need our top scientists. We propose NIST. We are open on how to do 
it better, but it needs to be a public-private relationship that has a 
safety board. We wouldn't climb on an airplane or you wouldn't get in a 
car or you wouldn't get a new financial product without some regulatory 
oversight. With something that is as big as this, we should not miss 
the moment. God forbid, you know, we go away for our campaign season, 
and in the interim, something happens, and we have no safety in place. 
I spent a career in technology before I got involved in politics, but I 
never felt a moment like this. So I hope that we will act.

Hawaii.
