                            Healthcare Fraud

  Mr. GRASSLEY. Happy news in the fight against fraud: This week, the 
White House Task Force to Eliminate Fraud took steps to stop fraud and 
to prevent bad actors from gaming ObamaCare.
  The administration is working to stop fraudulent and improper 
ObamaCare enrollment. Now, just think, over 600,000 consumers attested 
to unauthorized enrollment or plan switching.
  This task force has the potential to save billions in taxpayer 
dollars. This action will ensure resources are only going to those who 
are eligible and have signed up or authorized their enrollment.
  Too often, taxpayer money is lost to fraud, waste, and abuse. This 
not only wastes taxpayer dollars, but it also limits how effectively 
those dollars could be used to help the most vulnerable: those people 
that Medicaid and ObamaCare were intended to serve.
  The administration's action complements my work in the Senate and the 
work of Iowa's Representatives Hinson, Miller-Meeks, Nunn, and Feenstra 
in the House, accomplished when we passed the 2025 Working Families Tax 
Cut law.
  The tax cuts law not only prevented the largest tax increase in 
American history, but it also established program integrity reforms to 
stop fraud, waste, and abuse as well as preserve programs like Medicaid 
and ObamaCare.
  In the past year, I have come to this floor highlighting how my own 
oversight along with inspector general audits and Government 
Accountability Office reports have found taxpayer dollars being wasted 
on deceased enrollment and providers, enrollees participating in two 
different State Medicaid Programs and who are dually enrolled in 
Medicaid and ObamaCare, excess ObamaCare subsidies not being paid back, 
and consumers being fraudulently enrolled in ObamaCare.
  The Working Families Tax Cut Act put an end to much of this fraud, 
waste, and abuse in the Medicaid and Obama programs.
  To bolster these efforts, last month I introduced a bill with the 
title ``Healthcare Fraud Prevention and Improvement Act.'' This bill 
will improve and will invest in the Healthcare Fraud and Abuse Control 
Program. That program was created to combat fraud across Federal health 
programs. But that program needs improvement.
  Federal healthcare spending is expected to exceed $24 trillion over 
the next decade. Through the Working Families Tax Cut Act and by 
improving the Healthcare Fraud and Abuse Control Program, we can crack 
down on bad actors stealing the taxpayer's money and really protect 
resources for the most vulnerable Americans.

                             Democrat Party

  Mr. THUNE. Mr. President, if there is one thing that quickly became 
clear at the start of the 119th Congress, it was that anything we were 
going to accomplish for hard-working Americans we were going to have to 
accomplish alone. There is always some partisanship in Congress--and 
healthy disagreement--but it became clear very quickly that Democrats 
were going to be taking partisanship to a new and rarefied level in the 
119th Congress. That started with Presidential nominations.
  It has long been standard practice in the Senate to expedite 
uncontroversial nominations to allow a President to get his team in 
place. But from the very beginning of the 119th Congress, Democrats 
made clear that the history of bipartisan cooperation was over. 
Democrats chose to slow down every single nomination, no matter how 
uncontroversial, for the majority of the 119th Congress. But slow-
walking nominations was just the beginning.
  At the top of our priority list for this Congress was preventing the 
massive tax hike that was set to hit hard-working Americans. And for a 
party that claims to be the party of working men and women, you would 
think the Democrats would have been eager to work with us. They 
weren't. Not only were they not interested in working with us, but 
every single Democrat in both Houses of Congress voted against tax 
relief for the American people. They voted against preventing a massive 
tax hike, and they voted against all the new tax relief Republicans 
implemented--from no tax on overtime to a new bonus deduction for 
seniors.
  And just in case you thought Democrat opposition was a matter of 
high-minded principle, since Republicans passed the Working Families 
Tax Cuts, a number of Democrats have tried to associate themselves with 
some of the tax relief in the bill despite universally opposing the 
legislation in Congress.
  And the partisanship continues. Last week, the Senate attempted to 
take up legislation on an issue of great importance to working 
families; namely, energy prices. The legislation we tried to take up, 
the Ratepayer Protection Act, would have protected hard-working 
Americans from the increased energy costs associated with data centers. 
The bill was so obviously commonsense that it passed the House 
overwhelmingly, with almost every Democrat as well as Republican 
supporting it. But in the Senate, Senate Democrats objected to passing 
the bill, and so it failed. Presumably, Democrats wanted to deny 
Republicans the legislative success in the lead-up to the elections, 
even though hard-working Americans are the ones that are going to pay 
the price.
  The same thing happened yesterday. Republicans and the White House 
have been negotiating for the better part of a year with Democrats over 
permitting reform legislation--a long-overdue priority that would help 
lower energy bills for American families by streamlining Federal 
regulations for energy projects. And we closed a deal with them that we 
could bring to the floor that incorporated essentially all of the 
Democrats' demands. But when it began to look like we could consider 
the bill this work period, Democrats changed their tune. Because while 
they may acknowledge the need for permitting reform, what they really 
care about is denying Republicans another legislative win. This is an 
example of, again, just blatant partisanship.
  Republicans and Democrats have been working for months on a 
bipartisan basis with two committees of jurisdiction: the Environment 
and Public Works Committee and the Energy and Natural Resources 
Committee, the two committees that have jurisdiction over all of the 
elements related to permitting reform. And I have had Democrats come up 
to me on the floor countless times and say: We really want to get this 
done. We are working together. The White House will work with us on 
this or that.
  So we communicated with the White House. The White House has been 
engaged in these conversations and discussions for the past several 
weeks and essentially gave the Democrats the things they were asking 
for. Democrats can't take yes for an answer. It is like they caught the 
car and they don't know what to do. They said ``we want to work on 
this'' because permitting reform affects not just energy projects but 
projects in every sector of the economy. Energy projects--of course, 
obviously, both conventional energy and renewable energy projects are 
being affected by the delays and the bureaucracy and the redtape and 
the excessive litigation that comes with trying to do anything in this 
country of major consequence.
  So both sides had agreed this was a worthwhile objective: Let's work 
together. Let's see if we can sit down and hammer out a compromise that 
incorporates the concerns, the issues, that both sides bring to the 
table. And that has been going on for months. I have been watching it, 
getting regular readouts, talking to Members on both sides--Democrats 
and Republicans--on both committees of jurisdiction and others who are 
interested in this issue. And we get to a point where Democrats flat 
can't take yes for an answer.
  So why is that? Well, if you agree on the substance, if you come to 
an agreement on the policy, which they had--I mean, everybody in these 
negotiations, in the discussions, have basically said: OK. This is the 
deal. Here is the counteroffer; counteroffer back; counteroffer back. 
The last counteroffer from the Democrats was agreed to by the White 
House, by our Senate Republicans. They couldn't take yes for an answer.
  So the only conclusion one can draw is that the Democrats have 
concluded that it is good politics for them not to allow 
the Republicans to ``get a victory'' or the President to ``get a 
victory'' in the middle of a political season. That is really the only 
conclusion you can draw, after literally months and months and months 
of bipartisan negotiations in which the very last offer put on the 
table was one made by Democrats and accepted by the White House and 
Senate Republicans.

  That is where we are. That is what it is. Let's call it what it is. 
It is blatant politics.
  So my Democrat colleagues may talk a good game about working 
families, but the truth is that hard-working Americans take a distant 
back seat to Democrats' political priorities.
  Perhaps the most spectacular example of the party of ``no'' came last 
fall when Democrats shut down the government for 43 days--the longest 
full government shutdown in history--over a clean, temporary, and 
nonpartisan spending bill. That is right. It was clear that Congress 
would need more time to get the fiscal year 2026 appropriations bills 
finished, so Republicans proposed a clean, short-term funding measure 
free from poison pills or partisan policies. Let's just fund the 
government so we can continue the conversation about how we are going 
to fund it for the full year.
  Democrats, well, they just said: No, not going to do it. It didn't 
matter to them that the bill was nonpartisan. It didn't matter to them 
that it would only be in effect, actually, for a few weeks. And it 
certainly didn't matter to them that they were putting literally 
thousands of Americans out of work. They blocked it. A clean, 
nonpartisan spending bill, and they shut down the government for 6 
weeks.
  Of course, the fall 2025 government shutdown wasn't the only time 
this Congress that Democrats just said no to government funding. In the 
spring, Senate Democrats initiated the longest partial government 
shutdown in history due to their unwillingness to fund immigration law 
enforcement or border security. That is right. The Democrats kept the 
Department of Homeland Security--the Department of Homeland Security--
shut down for an unprecedented 76 days. And while they eventually 
agreed to reopen most of the Department, they still refused to fund 
immigration enforcement or Border Patrol.
  Clearly, democratic socialism and its philosophy of zero immigration 
enforcement and open borders has gained a lot more than a foothold in 
the Democrat Party.

[[Page S4961]]

  Whether the subject is protecting opportunities for women and girls 
in sports or tax relief for hard-working Americans, Democrats have 
spent the past 21 months throwing up every roadblock that they can. And 
I am proud that, despite the incredible amount of Democrat opposition, 
we still managed to accomplish a lot for the American people--from 
regulatory relief and border security to the Working Families Tax Cuts.
  There is more work to be done, and I hope that we will get the chance 
to continue our work not only in this Congress but also in the next. 
Given the Democrats' obsession with opposing President Trump and their 
demonstrated opposition to bipartisan cooperation, I think it is safe 
to say that hard-working Americans would undoubtedly take a back seat 
in a Democrat-led Congress. And the American people, they deserve 
better than the party of ``no.''
  I yield the floor.
  I suggest the absence of a quorum.

                          Freedom of the Press

  Mr. SCHUMER. Mr. President, on the Trump media ban, you can tell 
Trump's desperate, repressive media ban is flagrantly unconstitutional 
when it gets blocked by a judge he appointed.
  Trump can attack the First Amendment all he wants, but the roots of 
our democracy run deeper than he could ever fathom. No President should 
be able to use censorship to escape accountability, to deprive 
Americans of the truth, and to take our country down the path to 
authoritarianism.
  Is Trump so fragile, so thin-skinned, so desperate that he can't 
handle reporting that reflects the truth that he doesn't want to hear? 
If Trump's Presidency and economy are going as well as he claims they 
are, then he should have nothing to fear from the cameras.
  We all know what this media ban was about. Trump himself told the 
real truth when he said he was trying to protect his reputation with 
this ban. To protect his reputation--can you believe it? The chutzpah 
of a President to say, ``I am going to shut down the media because I 
don't like what they are saying,'' when he knows that 250 grand years 
of freedom of speech has been the hallmark of this country.
  Of course, his statements were so out of line--and even his 
supporters criticized it--that Trump had to backtrack by trying to 
label these news outlets a so-called ``national security risk.''
  No one bought that excuse for a second. The only thing Trump was 
trying to defend was himself.
  Trump knows he is in rough shape. He knows the American people are 
furious with him and his party. He knows just how much of a disaster 
his record is. And since Trump can't clean up his mess, he tries to 
sweep it under the rug, and he failed miserably.
  Trump can't outrun the press, and he can't outrun his record.
  You hear that, Donald? You can't outrun the press, and you can't 
outrun your miserable record.


                                 China

  Mr. President, on the Xi Jinping visit, Trump's soft-handed treatment 
of the Chinese Communist Party is on full display this week during Xi 
Jinping's visit. No sooner had Xi landed yesterday, than the Trump 
administration announced it is extending its trade relief to China for 
another 2 months.
  Really? Trump ought to take that trade war truce up north to Canada, 
instead of giving it to China and Xi and his authoritarian regime.
  As usual, Trump acts based solely on who flatters him at the moment, 
where the nearest photo-op is, and what headline he covers.
  If you are foreign or authoritarian, Trump just won't invite you to 
America; he will greet you planeside. That is what Trump did, sort of 
as a supplicant for Xi yesterday. That is what Trump did for Putin in 
Alaska last year.
  This man has an atavistic attraction to dictators, to bad people. 
Something is so wrong up there with him.
  And now Trump and Rubio want to roll out the red carpet for Putin 
again at the G20 in Florida this year.
  Trump needs to finally show some backbone. America can't afford for 
Trump to roll over on blocking China's access to our advanced AI tech, 
stopping the flow of fentanyl, protecting oil workers, strengthening 
American innovation, and holding the CCP accountable for aiding our 
adversaries or any of the other pressing issues we face with the PRC.
  Meeting an authoritarian at the bottom of the stairs is easy. 
Standing up to them at the negotiating table is the hard part. So far, 
Trump hasn't managed it.


                                  Iran

  Mr. President, on the Iran war, as Trump tries to tread water as his 
disastrous war with Iran gets worse by the day, the American people are 
drowning in costs. Gas is nearly $4.50 a gallon, and diesel is still 
breaking highs at $6.51 a gallon because of Trump's war.
  It is madness. Americans are getting crushed at the gas pumps. 
Working families are getting cleaned out at the grocery store counters. 
And family farms are going under because Trump took this country to war 
without authorization, without a plan, without a second thought to the 
consequences.
  We have lost at least 19 brave servicemembers. Hundreds are wounded 
or maimed, and tens of thousands remain in harm's way. That is just 
what we know, since this administration keeps withholding information 
from Congress and the American people about the true number of 
casualties in this war.
  All the while, Iran's grip on the Strait of Hormuz and other key 
chokepoints has grown tighter, and the entire situation in the Middle 
East has gotten worse. Trump's war has been one of the biggest foreign 
policy catastrophes in the history of this country. And, of course, as 
I said, the price of gasoline and the price of diesel keep going up.
  From the start, I have said this war is bigger than politics, and 
Senate Republicans--if they knew what was good for them--would have 
joined us in pushing for an end to Trump's war 7 months ago.
  Well, Senate Democrats will force a vote on another War Powers 
Resolution to end Trump's disastrous war and bring our troops home, at 
last, very soon. Americans will remember who votes to end Trump's war 
today just as they will remember who voted for the war to continue, 
time and time and time again, for 7 long months.


                                Big Oil

  Mr. President, and on the Trump Big Oil corruption report, Donald 
Trump's flagrant corruption costs the American people dearly every 
single day. We all know that.
  Just this morning, I joined Senator Whitehouse to unveil our Anti-
corruption Working Group's staggering new report on how Trump's corrupt 
dealings with Big Oil billionaires are costing the American people a 
fortune. It is a tale as old as time in Trump's America.
  In 2024, this then was brazen. I knew we were headed for trouble when 
I saw it back then in the papers, because in 2024 Trump invited Harold 
Hamm and all those Big Oil guys down to Mar-a-Lago and asked them for a 
$1 billion--$1 billion--for his campaign in exchange for political 
favors to boost their bottom line.
  It was appalling then--pay-to-play--and openly done, bragging boldly. 
I mean, that shows these people have no shame. They have no worries 
about the strictures of democracy, of morality, of what America has 
always been all about. No, they brag about it: We will give you $1 
billion. Help our oil companies, ho ho ho.
  But the American people paid the price. Big Oil paid up and, boy, did 
Donald Trump deliver.
  Trump blocked clean energy projects that would have brought down 
families' energy costs and powered millions of homes. The 
administration shut down the cheapest and fastest source of energy to 
build and get--the cheapest source of energy, the fastest source; it is 
the one that goes quickest on the grid--just so billionaires, Big Oil 
billionaires, could get even richer.
  Many of the same people making these policies are profiting off them. 
Trump appointed numerous fossil fuel industry leaders to his 
administration.
  Trump's corruption is not a victimless crime; it never is. Every
