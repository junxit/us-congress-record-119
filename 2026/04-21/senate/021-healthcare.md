# HEALTHCARE

```
                               HEALTHCARE

  MR. CASSIDY. Madam President, wherever I go in Louisiana, people are 
asking about the affordability of groceries, gasoline, and healthcare.
  So most families in my State don't have a thousand dollars in their 
bank account. And yet they have insurance plans with a multithousand-
dollar deductible. So when a patient says: I am on this infusion for my 
arthritis and my deductible is $6,000 and I have got to pay that much 
almost for my first dose, it resonates. And it resonates because we 
know, in many cases, there are middlemen--insurance companies--
increasing the price of that drug so that families with 1,000 bucks and 
a big deductible cannot afford that medication.
  Now, maybe it is more personal to me. I practiced medicine in a 
hospital for the uninsured and poorly insured for two decades. I saw 
middle-income families who couldn't afford insurance and came to the 
hospital for the uninsured.
  Now, at the root of this problem is a healthcare system that treats 
the patient as an excuse for the bill and not as a reason for the care. 
And the status quo sends billions of taxpayer dollars to insurance 
companies, and those companies take 20 percent off for profit and 
overhead.
  So has healthcare become more affordable for someone living in Acadia 
Parish, as an example? Has the quality of care improved in Natchitoches 
Parish? Well, Natchitoches Parish has pretty good quality of care at 
their local hospital, but for the family--if they can't afford it--it 
is as if that care is not there at all.
  We need a system which supports the family directly, not channeling 
the dollars through the insurance company. And I said that because 
those insurance companies--they serve a purpose. They are not evil, but 
they are there to make a profit, and they are increasing their margins 
so they have a better return to their shareholder. Our shareholder is 
the American people. We should think of a plan that speaks to them.

  Now, part of the problem is that there is no price transparency. 
Families can't say: The prescription is cheaper here or more expensive 
there. They don't have those tools yet. So they end up getting stuck 
with the more expensive option oftentimes with extra costs hidden in 
the pricetag.
  Now, I will go back to when I practiced medicine. If the patient has 
the power--if the patient is the reason for the care and not an excuse 
for the bill, prices tend to come down. So we need a different 
approach: a healthcare system giving power to the patient--not to the 
shareholder, not to the CEO, not to the bureaucrat but to the patient.
  Last week, I unveiled the Money and Value for Patients--or the MVP 
agenda--to put money directly into an account for the American family, 
directly into their pocket to pay for their out-of-pocket.
  This is advance money in a health savings account so a family in 
Bossier Parish with a $6,000 deductible is not forced to choose between 
going to the doctor or pumping gas in their truck or putting food on 
the table. Under this system, a family of four may get $2,000--$2,000--
to pay for those upfront costs.
  Now, we also strengthen price transparency. So the mom whose child 
has an earache could pull out her phone--take their phone like I am--
say: Urgent care centers near me and what is their pricing quality for 
a routine earache? And it would pop up on the map: Five choices: $100 
here. $500 there. She, with the money going straight into her account, 
could then go and have her daughter seen and have that visit paid for 
by the money in the account.
  The doctor writes a prescription: Pharmacies near me and what is the 
price for this particular drug? It pops up: Five pharmacies. This is 
the best price. She can go there and with that money in her account--
the money in her pocket for her out-of-pocket--get the best value--the 
money and the value--for the patient, her daughter. The MVP agenda.
  Finally, we need to empower patients to stay healthy. That begins 
with knowing what is in the food we eat. For example, a mother in 
Terrebonne Parish shouldn't be wondering whether her shrimp is from the 
gulf or whether it is radioactive, pumped with chemicals, imported from 
an East Asian seafood farm. I am not making that up. Some of the shrimp 
from elsewhere is radioactive.
  Mothers in Livingston Parish shouldn't be wondering if there is added 
ingredients to the food in the grocery store which are unsafe for their 
children.
  So let's summarize. People in Louisiana are concerned about 
affordability. The cost of living--the cost of healthcare as part of 
that--is too high. They are looking for folks here in Washington to do 
our part to better enable them to do their part.
  We have a responsibility to deliver and make life better for our 
constituents. The Money and Value for Patients--or the MVP agenda--is a 
way to do that. Let's give power to the patient. Let's make healthcare 
affordable again.
  I yield the floor.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER (Mr. Banks). The Democratic leader.
```
