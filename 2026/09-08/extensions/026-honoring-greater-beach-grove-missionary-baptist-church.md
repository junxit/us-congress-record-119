# HONORING GREATER BEACH GROVE MISSIONARY BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
         HONORING GREATER BEACH GROVE MISSIONARY BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, September 8, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor a historic 
congregation in my district, Greater Beach Grove Missionary Baptist 
Church in Baton Rouge, Louisiana, as it marks more than a century of 
faith, family leadership, and service to our community.
  The congregation was organized on August 18, 1920, in the home of its 
founder and first pastor, the late Reverend Noah Charles Chinn. A small 
group of Christian men and women met for prayer, and from this assembly 
Little Beach Grove Baptist Church was formed. Its founding members were 
Brothers Willie Chinn, Jeff Chinn, Sam Chinn, Shedrick Chinn, and 
Norris Smith, and Sisters Delilah Chinn, Sallie Chinn, Minnie Chinn, 
Emma Lemons, Lula Hives, and Emmie Benton. Sister Sallie Chinn, mother 
of Reverend Chinn, served as the church's first Mother, succeeded by 
Sister Dollie Butler, then Sister Julia Butler until her passing in 
1992, and then Sister Emma Starks, after whose passing in 1993 the 
position was retired. Brother Shedrick Chinn served as the first head 
deacon, succeeded by Brother Allen Butler, Jr., and Sister Emma Lemons 
served as the first head deaconess, succeeded by Sister Mary Julia 
Quiett, a position likewise retired upon Sister Quiett's passing.
  As membership increased, the small congregation outgrew Reverend 
Chinn's home, and through the guidance of the Holy Spirit the members 
rented a small house across the road to accommodate more worshippers. 
Following the church's first revival, membership continued to grow, and 
in 1927 the young church began to spread its wings. During this period 
Reverend Chinn organized the Usher Board in 1926 and a Choir in 1932. 
The late Brother Bernard Freeman served as the first Usher Board 
president, succeeded by Brother Thomas Hudson, Jr., who served until 
being called to serve his country; Brother James Wilson succeeded him 
and served twenty-six years, followed by Brothers Thomas Guerin, Jr., 
and Joe Thomas, and Sisters Elouise Thomas, Joyce Taylor, Beverly 
Carter, and presently Sister Debra Tatman. Brother C.C. Stewart served 
as the first musician and choir director, succeeded over the years by 
Sisters Dorothy Jackson, the late Juanita Turner, Joyce Howard, and 
Linda Spurlock, the late Brother Lafayette Harrison, Jr., who served 
twenty-six years until 1996, Brother Alfred Williams, Brother Horace 
Polk, the late Brother Craig McKinley until 2015, and Minister Chris 
Plant, who served briefly. It was reported that people walked from 
miles around to hear Reverend Chinn preach, and he baptized many souls 
from surrounding communities including Scotlandville, Dixie, Zion City, 
Brookstown, Birdstation, and the Comite area.
  Through divine providence, the growing congregation purchased a plot 
of land on Hooper Road, where the church was built. In 1930, an 
addition extended the pulpit and increased the size of the church. 
Brother Thomas Hudson, Sr., organized and served as the first Sunday 
School superintendent, succeeded by Brothers Johnny D. Brown, Sr., 
Allen Butler, Jr., Jesse R. Taylor, Thomas Guerin, Jr., and Thomas 
Hudson, Jr. In 1943, the original building was demolished and a larger 
structure built on the same site under the supervision of Mr. Foot 
Chinn, who was unable to complete the project; the work was finished by 
Brother Allen Butler, Jr., and Brother Mack Hudson. Sisters Hester 
Taylor and Virginia ``Ginny'' Hills served as the first sponsors of the 
young people's choir, reportedly walking young members through the 
fields from Zion City to the Hooper Road location. Sister Yvonne Benton 
Bradford, Brother Henry Scott, Jr., and Brother Johnny D. Brown, Sr., 
reorganized the youth choir in 1947 under Pastor Chinn's leadership, 
and were succeeded over time by the late Sisters Mary Stone, Viola 
McGee, Johnny M. Holiday, Lois Guerin, Elouise Thomas, and Judy Scott, 
then Sister Beverly Carter, and presently Sisters Joyce Taylor and 
Dontina Taylor.
  On September 17, 1952, the church was incorporated and recorded in 
the East Baton Rouge Parish Circuit Court, with articles established to 
remain in place for ninety-nine years, and it was at this time the 
congregation adopted the name Greater Beach Grove Missionary Baptist 
Church. In 1953, the church was rebuilt in the Zion City community on 
Ford Street; Mr. Zeke Brown drew the plans under the direction of 
Brother Allen Butler, Brother Robert Stone laid the brick pillars for 
the foundation, and Brother Butler completed construction. Around 1957, 
the frame building was remodeled and enclosed in brick, and the first 
cornerstone was laid by the St. John Grand Lodge. The deacons serving 
at that time were Chairman Allen Butler, Jr., and Brothers Melbert 
``Preacher'' Black, Johnny D. Brown, Sr., Severe Sims, Thomas Hudson, 
Sr., Elvin Hudson, Nathaniel Scott, Isaiah Norwood, Thomas Guerin, Sr., 
Thomas Guerin, Jr., Gabe Foster, Jr., Hosea Curry, Sr., and Hosea 
Curry, Jr. Trustees included President Eddie Williams and Brothers C.B. 
Robinson, Willie Green, Charlie Wright, and Monroe McGee, with several 
members serving in dual capacities. Sister Edna Perry served as church 
secretary, and Hosea Curry, Jr., as church treasurer, succeeded by 
Brother Gabe Foster, Jr., and then Brother Thomas Guerin, Jr. Sister 
Mary Brown preceded Sister Perry as secretary, and Brother Jesse R. 
Taylor and Sister Bernice Freeman succeeded her, followed by Sister 
Gussie R. Tansey; Sisters Freeman and Tansey served more than twenty 
years until 1977, later assisted by Sister Linda Jones Montague as 
church clerk.
  Reverend Chinn gave his life to the church, praying and ministering 
there for more than forty years until his health failed. He remained 
the congregation's leader even during his illness, kept informed by his 
head deacon, and was remembered as a beloved community pastor. Reverend 
E. Lloyd Johnson shepherded the flock during Reverend Chinn's illness. 
On November 26, 1965, Reverend Chinn was called from labor to reward, a 
day of great sorrow for the congregation. In April 1966, Brothers Demas 
Martin, Jr., James Foster, and Thomas Guerin, Jr., and Sisters Juanita 
Turner, Viola McGee, Pearl Henry, and Elouise L. Thomas were elected to 
the Trustee Board, and the process of electing a new shepherd was 
undertaken under the leadership of Trustee Board President Eddie 
Williams and Deacon Allen Butler, Jr. In June 1966, with the assistance 
of the Fourth District Baptist Association, led by the late Reverend 
Dr. E.D. Billoups, Reverend Emmitt Spurlock, Sr., was elected the 
church's second pastor.
  The late Reverend Emmitt Spurlock served as pastor for thirty-three 
years, guided by his motto: ``We are saved to serve and not to sit.'' 
He initiated many new programs and oversaw the remodeling and 
improvement of the church's physical structure. In 1968, additional 
lots were purchased, and in 1978 plans for a new sanctuary and 
education wing were drawn and submitted to Chairman Butler and the 
Deacon Board; the Trustee Board was reorganized, and Reverend Spurlock 
appealed to the membership for five-hundred-dollar donations, with 
Allen and Julia Butler, Emel and Julia Quiett, Henry and Lillian Scott, 
Bernice J. Freeman, and Beverly J. Carter and Linda J. Montague among 
the first to commit. In March 1979, the new building and parking lot 
were completed and dedicated, and the second cornerstone was laid by 
the Grand Lodge. Officers at that time included Chairman of the Board 
Allen Butler, Jr., and Brothers Thomas Guerin, Jr., John H. Brown, Jr., 
Johnny D. Brown, Sr., Hosea Curry, Jr., Willie Paul, Emel Quiett, Sr., 
Wash Robinson, Larry Spurlock, and John Pope. Trustee President Jesse 
R. Taylor succeeded Brother Williams, joined by Brothers Earnest 
Johnson and Albert Daigre and Sisters Beverly J. Carter, Linda J. 
Montague, Doretha Johnson, and Elouise M. Thomas. Sister Doretha 
Johnson served as church secretary and Brother Albert Daigre as 
treasurer, succeeding Brother Guerin, while Sister Linda J. Montague 
was officially named the church's clerk and announcer. Brothers Willie 
Patterson and Matthew King were among the congregation's most 
supportive laymen during construction. The building's mortgage was 
burned on November 5, 1989, during the church's sixty-ninth anniversary 
celebration.
  Believing the whole church membership should work together as 
Christians united in love, Reverend Spurlock implemented the Junior and 
Young Adult Usher Boards, Junior Deacon and Deaconess ministries, 
Sunday School Teacher's Study Hour, Baptist Training

[[Page E882]]

Union, Bible Class, Laymen's Auxiliary, Vacation Bible School, After 
School Study and Tutoring programs, Boy Scouts, Girl Scouts, Brownie 
Troops, Hospitality, First-Aid and New Members Training Committees, 
Puppet Ministry, Mission and Elderly Care Ministries, Kitchen Ministry, 
and the Pastor's Aide Committee, while increasing the congregation's 
participation in the National Congress of Christian Education, the 
National Baptist Convention, and the Fourth District Baptist 
Association. Under his leadership the church's mortgage was retired in 
1985 after only seven years, and the membership grew abundantly and 
steadily. Brother John H. Brown, Jr., was appointed Chairman of the 
Board following Deacon Butler's passing, followed briefly by Reverend 
Donald Hunter and Brother Wash Robinson, who also served briefly under 
the church's present pastor. Reverend Emmitt Spurlock, Sr., was called 
home on January 13, 1998.
  After fervent prayer, the congregation named the Reverend Keno M.L. 
Spurlock, Sr., its third pastor in 1998. Under his guidance and care, 
the congregation has rededicated itself to Christ and to the service of 
others, striving toward its goal of ``the mark of a higher calling'' 
and trusting in Philippians 4.13. His assistants include Reverends 
Eddie Bradford, James Banks, and Darrell Riley, and Ministers Mark 
Edwards, Robby Mitchell, and Barry Follins, and under his leadership 
the physical structure has been remodeled and brought into the age of 
technology. The late Brother Felton Brown served as Chairman of the 
Deacon Ministry until his health failed and was named Chairman 
Emeritus; Brother Charlie Jarrell, Jr., is the present chairman, joined 
by Brothers Aaron Butler, Larry Bowman, Jesse Taylor, John Robinson, 
Donald Taylor, and Kevan Hills. Brother Jesse R. Taylor chairs the 
Trustee Ministry, with present members Brothers Carl Pearson, Aaron 
Butler, Larry Bowman, Charlie Jarrell, Jr., Donald Taylor, and John 
Alex, and Sisters Beverly Carter, Gwendolyn Scott, Rosalind Scott, and 
Juanita Horton. Brother Taylor also chairs the Finance Ministry, with 
Sisters Carol Joseph and Linda J. Montague serving as secretaries. The 
late Brother Albert Daigre, Sr., served as church treasurer for many 
years until 2014, when Brother John E. Robinson, III, was elected 
treasurer in July 2016. Sister Rosalind Scott serves as the present 
Sunday School Superintendent, assisted by Brother Larry Bowman, and 
Brother Charlie Fisher, Jr., serves as musician and music director, 
with Brother Zedrick Comena on drums, assisted by Brother Couren Bowman 
and Minister Barry Follins.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring Greater Beach 
Grove Missionary Baptist Church for more than a century of faithful 
service, and in wishing its pastor and congregation continued blessings 
in the years to come.
```
