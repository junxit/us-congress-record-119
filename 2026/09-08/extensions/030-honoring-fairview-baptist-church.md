# HONORING FAIRVIEW BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                    HONORING FAIRVIEW BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, September 8, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor a resilient 
congregation in my district, Fairview Baptist Church in Baton Rouge, 
Louisiana, as it marks more than ninety years of faith, perseverance, 
and rebuilding in the face of adversity.
  Fairview Baptist Church was organized in 1934 under the leadership of 
the late Reverend R.H. Hall. The first meeting was held at the home of 
Sister Ida Edwards, and after organizing, church services were held at 
New Bethel Baptist Church on Matilda Street until the congregation 
could locate a building of its own. That same year, the congregation 
purchased property on the corner of South 13th and Louise Streets, 
where its forty-four members, a group of hardworking, dedicated, and 
determined people, built a wood-frame building. The church began to 
increase its membership and moved steadily forward.
  On December 18, 1947, the Lord called Reverend R.H. Hall to his 
eternal rest, and Reverend M.J. Hubbard was elected as the new pastor 
on February 20, 1948. Under Reverend Hubbard's leadership, membership 
continued to grow, and the congregation worked to raise money for a 
better building. In 1951,

[[Page E883]]

the East Baton Rouge Parish School Board asked to purchase the church's 
property for the extension of McKinley Senior High School. The 
congregation agreed to the sale and located new property on Braddock 
Street, where, with dedicated and determined people and their pastor, 
they erected a new building and moved from ``Fairview on the Hill'' to 
1636 Braddock Street. The new edifice was dedicated April 17 through 
20, 1952.
  Once again God's angel of death paid a visit to Fairview, and 
Reverend M.J. Hubbard was called to his eternal rest on April 12, 1953. 
The late Reverend Q.T. Ellis, who had been serving the congregation at 
Reverend Hubbard's request during his illness, was elected pastor on 
June 11, 1953. Under Reverend Ellis's guidance the church grew 
immensely, and the congregation purchased the lot adjacent to the 
building.
  After many years under Reverend Ellis's leadership, the church 
undertook a major renovation. The first phase consisted of painting and 
replacing the roof, the second phase was a complete upgrade of the 
interior, with new pews, windows, pulpit furniture, and carpet giving 
the church a new look. On Sunday, October 27, 1985, the congregation 
rededicated Fairview to God and its founders, and members continued to 
work and contribute financially to the project's success. The mortgage 
financing the renovation was paid off in seven years, and with much 
determination and sacrifice, God blessed Reverend Ellis and his 
congregation to burn the mortgage on October 11, 1987.
  On February 7, 1988, the congregation suffered the loss of its church 
building to a five-alarm fire that totally destroyed the structure, as 
many members stood and watched on that cold Sunday afternoon. Reverend 
Ellis advised the members to assemble the following Sunday at New Ark 
Baptist Church on Terrace Street, only a few blocks away, and the 
members of New Ark opened their doors and their hearts to give the 
congregation a place to worship. In May 1988, the church broke ground 
to begin construction of its new edifice, and many members purchased 
symbolic shovels of dirt as the new foundation was prepared. On Sunday, 
January 29, 1989, the congregation, led by the late Reverend Q.T. 
Ellis, marched from New Ark Baptist Church to its new home, a glorious 
day attended by members of the East Baton Rouge Ministers Conference 
along with family and friends who gathered to dedicate the new church 
to God.
  In 1995, additional property adjacent to the church was purchased, 
and it was paid off by 1996, another example of the Lord's blessing 
upon Fairview in a short period of time. In the history of the church, 
Fairview had at that point had the service of only four pastors. As 
Reverend Ellis advanced in age, the deacons of the church sought the 
services of Reverend Bennie Veal, Jr., as assistant pastor, and God 
also blessed Fairview to receive the calling of Minister Johnnie R. 
Griffin, who served as a pulpit associate.
  As Reverend Ellis's health began to fail, Fairview moved to elevate 
him to Pastor Emeritus, and a celebration in his honor was held on 
Sunday, July 5, 2003, when he retired after fifty years of loyal and 
dedicated service. The church then searched for a new full-time pastor, 
and on December 14, 2003, Reverend Keith B. Coward was installed as the 
new pastor of Fairview Missionary Baptist Church. Under Pastor Coward, 
the church continued to grow, with many new members among its young 
people, and he brought a new leadership style through religious 
institutes and revivals.
  Fairview Baptist Church has always stood on a solid foundation as a 
spirit-filled congregation, able through the years to bounce back from 
any adversity that has come its way. Looking back to the founders who 
made the ultimate sacrifice to build this church, the congregation 
reviews its goals and achievements, its battles fought and won, with 
thankful hearts for all of God's blessings. The gospel has been 
proclaimed within these hallowed walls, and many souls have come to 
Christ through them, many members have passed on to their reward, their 
earthly hardships forever gone, and the congregation remains mindful of 
the saints of years past as it looks toward its ultimate destination in 
Heaven.
  Reverend Keith B. Coward resigned as pastor on January 18, 2009, 
following Holy Communion, and Minister Johnnie R. Griffin was elected 
Interim Pastor, giving loyal service to the church until a search 
committee could be formed. After the search committee screened a number 
of applicants, Reverend Gus Charles Spurlock, Sr., was elected the new 
pastor of Fairview Missionary Baptist Church during a called business 
meeting on Tuesday, November 24, 2009, standing on the promises of God 
on a solid and firm foundation. Pastor Spurlock was installed during a 
ceremony led by the Fourth District Missionary Baptist Association on 
Sunday, January 24, 2010, becoming the sixth pastor of Fairview 
Missionary Baptist Church.
  Pastor Spurlock came to the congregation with great vision and proved 
to be a teaching leader, holding various training workshops, each 
backed by Scripture, for church leaders and members interested in 
developing their respective ministries. Pastor Spurlock has also proven 
himself a mentor to young people, who have embraced him as both pastor 
and mentor as they pursue their development as Christians preparing for 
future generations.
  Under Pastor Spurlock's leadership, ``The View'' continues to work 
toward the mark of a higher calling in Christ Jesus, with a vision that 
includes building a Multipurpose Q.T. Ellis Christian Education Center 
to provide senior citizen enhancement programs, tutoring for children, 
and developmental programs for the disadvantaged, among other services. 
Fairview Baptist Church was founded on a firm foundation by Christian 
men and women who were determined as they struggled through hard times 
to establish the solid legacy and wonderful memories that endure today.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring Fairview 
Baptist Church for more than ninety years of faithful service, and in 
wishing its pastor and congregation continued blessings in the years to 
come.
```
