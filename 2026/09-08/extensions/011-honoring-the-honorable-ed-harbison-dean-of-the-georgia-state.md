# HONORING THE HONORABLE ED HARBISON, DEAN OF THE GEORGIA STATE SENATE, FOR THIRTY-FOUR YEARS OF FAITHFUL SERVICE

**Speaking:** Bishop, Sanford D. (B000490) [D-GA]

```
 HONORING THE HONORABLE ED HARBISON, DEAN OF THE GEORGIA STATE SENATE, 
               FOR THIRTY-FOUR YEARS OF FAITHFUL SERVICE

                                 ______
                                 

                      HON. SANFORD D. BISHOP, JR.

                               of georgia

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, September 8, 2026

  Mr. BISHOP. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor a man I have known, 
admired, and leaned upon for the better part of my life in public 
service--the Honorable Ed Harbison, Dean of the Georgia State Senate, 
who gave thirty-four years to the people of the Fifteenth Senate 
District. The community he served for a lifetime gathers once more to 
say thank you Mr. Speaker, some men are handed a title. A rare few are 
handed a trust. Ed Harbison was handed a trust, and he never once set 
it down.
  Long before he ever rose to speak beneath the Gold Dome, Ed Harbison 
wore the uniform of the United States Marine Corps. He went to Vietnam. 
He came home with a Purple Heart and with wounds that no ceremony can 
undo. I begin there, Mr. Speaker, because everything that followed 
makes sense only in that light. A young man who was willing to lay down 
his life for strangers came home and spent the lest of his days giving 
his hours, his patience, and his heart to his neighbors. For Ed 
Harbison, service was never a campaign word It was a scar he carried 
and a promise he kept.
  Mr. Speaker, I know the seat Ed Harbison held, because I sat in it 
before him. When the people of Georgia sent me to this House in 1993, I 
left that Fifteenth District seat behind--and I did not leave it to 
chance. I left it to Ed. For thirty-four years, West Central Georgia 
has been better for that decision than for almost any I ever made. He 
became the longest-serving member of that chamber and its Dean, the 
senator other senators went to when a room needed wisdom, steadiness, 
and a conscience.
  And what he built will outlive all of us. It was Ed Harbison who 
carried the legislation making Georgia a Purple Heart State, so that no 
veteran's sacrifice would pass unmarked. It was Ed Harbison who 
established a separate Veterans Court, so that men and women who had 
served would be met with understanding rather than indifference. It was 
Ed Harbison who founded the Georgia Military Veterans Hall of Fame, of 
which he is himself a proud member. He won a contracting preference for 
businesses owned by disabled veterans. He fought to see that veterans 
received fair academic credit for the training and sacrifice of their 
service. He chaired the Senate Committee on State Institutions and 
Property, served as Vice-Chairman of the Veterans, Military, and 
Homeland Security Committee, and led the Georgia Legislative Black 
Caucus. And before any of it, he served his community as a broadcast 
Journalist, as second vice-president of the Muscogee County School 
Board, and on the Columbus Charter Review Commission.
  Mr. Speaker, if you want the true measure of Ed Harbison, do not look 
at the headlines. Look at the quiet bills--the ones that never made the 
evening news. In 2008 he sponsored a law to cut through the red tape 
that was punishing the children of military families, boys and girls 
uprooted from state to state through no fault of their own and made to 
start over again and again. Nobody was marching for those children. Ed 
Harbison simply saw them, and he wrote a law so that the moving would 
not cost them their education. He authored the legislation 
consolidating Chattahoochee County and Cusseta, strengthening the very 
ground his people stand on. And he fought for the emergency call boxes 
along Interstate 185 from LaGrange to Columbus--a small thing, Mr. 
Speaker, until it is your mother stranded on the shoulder of that 
highway after dark, and then it is the whole world.
  That was always his instinct: the traveler in the dark, the soldier 
far from home, the child who needed one grown person in his corner. The 
people of Chattahoochee, Macon, Marion, Schley, Talbot, Taylor, and 
Muscogee Counties did not merely have a senator who represented them. 
They had a senator who belonged to them. Ed has said the rule he lived 
by came from his mother, who taught him to do unto others as he would 
have them do unto him. For thirty-four years he did exactly that--
quietly, faithfully, and without ever needing the credit.
  Mr. Speaker, no honest account of this good man is complete without 
the love that carried him. Ed shared his life and his calling with his 
beloved late wife, Cecilia, who sustained him through every long 
session and every hard campaign, and who paid the private price that 
public service always exacts at home. Though she is no longer with us, 
I have no doubt she is present in every room where his name is called, 
and that she looks on him with pride. And anyone who knows Ed Harbison 
knows what his son, Edward, means to him. Above every title he has 
earned, every gavel he has held, and every honor he has received, Ed 
Harbison is a devoted father. The same tenderness he wrote into the law 
for a stranger's child, he has lived out every single day for his own. 
A man may give thirty-four years to his state, but the truest 
accounting of him is the love he gives his family--and by that 
reckoning, Ed Harbison is a wealthy man.
  Mr. Speaker, there comes a moment in every life of service when a man 
must lay the work down and trust others to carry it forward. Ed 
Harbison has come to that moment, and he comes to it with his head high 
and his hands clean. He has called his years in the Senate the greatest 
honor of his life. Mr. Speaker, the honor was ours. It belonged to 
every family made stronger, every veteran finally seen, every neighbor 
who knew that somewhere under the Gold Dome there was a man who would 
do right by them when nobody was watching.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues in the United States House of 
Representatives to join my wife, Vivian, and me, along with the more 
than 765,000 people of Georgia's Second Congressional District, in 
honoring the extraordinary life and service of State Senator Ed 
Harbison. We thank Ed, for the wounds he carries and the country he 
served. We thank him for the seat he tended and the trust he kept. We 
thank him for thirty-four years of doing unto others. May God bless 
him, may God bless the State of Georgia, and may He grant his every 
good and peaceful day he has so richly earned. Well done, my friend. 
Well done.
```
