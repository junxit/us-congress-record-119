# HONORING CAPITAL MISSIONARY BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
               HONORING CAPITAL MISSIONARY BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, September 8, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor a steadfast 
congregation in my district, Capital Missionary Baptist Church in Baton 
Rouge, Louisiana, as it marks more than six decades of preaching, 
discipleship, and service to our community.
  The history of Capital Missionary Baptist Church begins in 1964, when 
the late Reverend E.B. Robertson answered God's call to establish a 
house of worship in the city of Baton Rouge. The young congregation 
gathered on a street then known as Capital Avenue, the thoroughfare 
that gave the church the name it still carries today. That street is 
now known as Gus Young Avenue, but the name Capital has remained fixed 
to the church as a permanent reminder of where God first planted this 
people.
  What began as a small body of believers was built on the plain 
preaching of the Word, fervent prayer, and the sacrificial giving of 
members who had far more faith than they had resources. Reverend 
Robertson led with a pastor's heart and a builder's vision, and under 
his ministry the church grew in number, strength, and reputation 
throughout the surrounding community.
  In the early 1970s, the congregation's growth and the changing 
landscape of the city made a move necessary. The church relocated to 
Avenue D in the Scotlandville community of North Baton Rouge, and there 
Capital Missionary Baptist Church put down deep roots. For roughly 
three decades, the Avenue D location served as the congregation's 
spiritual home. It was there that generations were baptized, married, 
and buried; there that children were raised in the nurture and 
admonition of the Lord; and there that the church established itself as 
a steady and trusted presence in North Baton Rouge. The Avenue D years 
were years of formation, the seasons in which the character, worship, 
and mission of the congregation were shaped.
  In 1993, the mantle of pastoral leadership passed to the founder's 
son, Dr. Ronald L. Robertson, Sr. What his father had begun in faith, 
his son was called to continue, an uncommon and God-ordained succession 
that joined the founding vision of one generation to the leadership of 
the next without breaking stride. Under Dr. Robertson's pastorate, 
Capital Missionary Baptist Church has expanded its ministry, deepened 
its teaching, and broadened its reach into the community, remaining 
anchored to the same convictions that marked its founding: sound of 
doctrine, faithful preaching, and genuine care for people, even as it 
presses forward into new seasons of service.
  In 2002, the vision carried and prayed over for years became visible 
in brick and mortar. The present sanctuary was constructed on thirteen 
acres of land, a facility built not merely to seat a congregation, but 
to serve a community and accommodate the growth of ministries yet to 
come. That campus stands today as a monument to what God can do through 
a people who will not quit. From a small gathering on Capital Avenue, 
to a faithful congregation on Avenue D, to a thirteen-acre campus 
today, the hand of the Lord has been evident at every step.
  More than six decades after its founding, Capital Missionary Baptist 
Church remains committed to the mission entrusted to it: to preach the 
Gospel of Jesus Christ, teach the whole counsel of God, make disciples, 
and serve the city of Baton Rouge. The buildings have changed and the 
streets have been renamed, but the calling has not moved. The 
congregation honors the memory of its founder, the late Reverend E.B. 
Robertson, and gives thanks for every member, living and departed, 
whose labor, offering, and prayer built what stands today.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to Join me in honoring Capital 
Missionary Baptist Church for more than six decades of faithful 
service, and in wishing its pastor and congregation continued blessings 
in the years to come.
```
