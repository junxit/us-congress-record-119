# LUNAR LANDING DAY ACT

**Speaking:** Armstrong, Alan (A000383) [R-OK]

**Measures:** S. 5123

```
                         LUNAR LANDING DAY ACT

  Mr. ARMSTRONG. Mr. President, I ask unanimous consent that the Senate 
proceed to the immediate consideration of S. 5123, which is at the 
desk.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The clerk will report the bill by title.
  The legislative clerk read as follows:

       A bill (S. 5123) to amend title 36, United States Code, to 
     designate July 20 as Lunar Landing Day.

  There being no objection, the Senate proceeded to consider the bill.
  Mr. ARMSTRONG. I ask unanimous consent that the bill be considered 
read a third time and passed and that the motion to reconsider be 
considered made and laid upon the table.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Without objection, it is so ordered.
  The bill (S. 5123) was ordered to be engrossed for a third reading, 
was read the third time, and passed as follows:

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Lunar Landing Day Act''.

     SEC. 2. DESIGNATION.

       (a) In General.--Chapter 1 of title 36, United States Code,

     146 as section 120;
       (6) by redesignating the second section designated as 
     section 118, the second section designated as section 119, 
     and sections 120 through 135 as sections 121 through 138, 
     respectively;
       (7) by moving section 144 so as to appear after section 
     138, as so redesignated, and by redesignating such section

     ``Sec. 117. Lunar Landing Day

       ``(a) Designation.--July 20 is Lunar Landing Day.
       ``(b) Recognition.--All private citizens, organizations, 
     and Federal, State, and local governmental and legislative 
     entities are encouraged to recognize Lunar Landing Day 
     through proclamations, activities, and educational efforts--
       ``(1) commemorating the first successful landing of humans 
     on the Moon on July 20, 1969; and
       ``(2) inspiring the continued leadership of the United 
     States in space exploration and science.''.
```
