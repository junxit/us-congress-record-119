# Working Families Tax Cut Act (Executive Session)

**Speaking:** Thune, John (T000250) [R-SD], Schumer, Charles E. (S000148) [D-NY]

```
                      Working Families Tax Cut Act

  Mr. THUNE. Mr. President, it has been almost a year since Republicans 
passed the Working Families Tax Cut, 1 year since Republicans delivered 
permanent tax relief to the American people and put more money in their 
pockets.
  I am proud to say that it is working. At the start of this year, 
hard-working Americans saw their take-home pay go up as the new tax law 
took effect. They saw its impact again in April when they filed their 
taxes. Many Americans saw a significant boost in their tax refunds.
  The New York Times interviewed three dozen Americans, who reported 
that ``many of the taxpayers said they had received their largest 
refund in years,'' allowing them to pay down debt, save more, or cover 
the cost of a vacation or a new bed.
  Last week, we got yet more news that shows that the Working Families 
Tax Cut is working. The Treasury Department released data showing that 
97 percent of Americans who filed taxes this last year actually got a 
tax cut. Take a look at that. That is a pretty stunning number, if you 
think about it--97 percent. Thanks to the Working Families Tax Cut, 97 
percent of taxpayers got a tax cut this year, collectively saving about 
$82 billion on their tax bills.
  And who was it--who was it--that benefited from the Working Families 
Tax Cut? Well, I will give you a hint. It is in the name: working 
families. Almost every one of the 97 percent of American taxpayers who 
saw a tax cut this year earned less than $200,000. Nearly 70 percent 
earned less than $100,000.
  So if you take a look at this chart, you will be able to see just 
exactly what I am talking about. If you look at the dark-blue line, 
those are the number of people who are making less than $100,000 a year 
who benefited from the new tax deductions in this bill. And if you look 
at the light one, that is the number of people under $200,000 who got a 
benefit. I will get into each of those specifics here in just a minute,

[[Page S2708]]

but I think it is important to point out that the beneficiaries of the 
Working Families Tax Cut were working families. In other words, the 
Americans benefiting from this bill were people who got up every day to 
go to work to provide for their families. They are not rich. They are 
people who spend nights at the kitchen table putting together their 
budgets, trying to make ends meet and save a little bit for the future. 
And that is who Republicans were thinking about when we wrote the 
Working Families Tax Cut, and as the numbers show, that is who is 
benefiting.
  I have said that the Working Families Tax Cut is about putting more 
money in hard-working Americans' pockets, and that is exactly what this 
bill did. It let working Americans keep more of their hard-earned 
money--money that they would have had to send to the IRS if not for the 
Working Families Tax Cut and that they can now spend, invest, and save 
as they see fit.
  The Working Families Tax Cut delivered real taxing savings to the 
American people through a number of tax relief policies. Permanent 
lower tax rates reduced Americans' tax burdens across the board, and 
the now permanently doubled standard deduction helped 127 million 
Americans reduce and simplify their taxes this year.
  Nearly 40 million families claimed the newly enhanced $2,200 child 
tax credit in 2025. And as you can see from the chart, almost all of 
those families had incomes below $200,000. And I said before, two-
thirds earned less than $100,000. And again, that is from the enhanced 
child tax credit, which is the bottom number down there.
  A New York Times story in April on this year's larger tax refunds 
highlighted Nicole Mendoza, a 68-year-old Georgian who ``works a couple 
of shifts a week at a Cracker Barrel in Canton, Ga., to supplement her 
Social Security income and savings. All told, Ms. Mendoza said, she 
earned about $38,000 last year and paid $551 in federal income taxes.''
  And Ms. Mendoza told the Times:

       I got back every penny.

  This year, millions of hard-working Americans like Ms. Mendoza were 
eligible for additional tax relief thanks to the Working Families Tax 
Cut. Americans earning between $100,000 and $200,000 last year who took 
advantage of new tax relief in this bill saw an average tax cut of 
$1,250. Older Americans were able to take advantage of the enhanced 
senior deduction, which helped 35 million seniors this past year.
  And 7.5 million tipped workers were able to take advantage of no tax 
on tips. Fully 90 percent of Americans who took advantage of no tax on 
tips earned less than $100,000; 99 percent--99 percent--earned less 
than $200,000.
  So if you look at the tipped wages, the number there on top, the 
dark-blue bar represents the number of people who were making less than 
$100,000 who benefited from no tax on tips. The lighter-blue bar 
represents the number of people making less than $200,000 in their 
income this year who benefited from no tax on tips.
  So look at that. I mean, 90 percent of people--90 percent of people--
who get tipped wages and get the benefit of no tax on tips made less 
than $100,000 a year, and 99 percent, less than $200,000 a year. That 
is pretty stunning, if you think about it, if you talk about actually 
working families out there, people who are trying to make ends meet. 
And those numbers, I think, represent just how important that 
particular provision was in the bill, and the savings they are seeing 
is significant.
  A bartender in Beloit, WI, said that he more than doubled his Federal 
tax refund. Katie, who is a DoorDash driver in Oklahoma, said:

       It increased my refund from $300 to $2,000.

  Another DoorDash driver in Oregon said:

       [No Tax on Tips] has changed life. I worry less about being 
     able to cover all my expenses and I am able to work less 
     which is better for my health and family.

  That is the Working Families Tax Cut in action. And I haven't even 
talked about no tax on overtime, which, like everything else I have 
talked about this morning, is overwhelmingly benefiting middle- and 
lower-income Americans.
  Last year, Republicans promised to deliver lower tax bills for hard-
working Americans, and the data released last week proves that is 
exactly--exactly--what this bill does. The Working Families Tax Cut is 
delivering for working Americans.
  As they have every time that Republicans have lowered taxes for 
working people, Democrats have resurrected their dusty, old talking 
points about tax cuts for the rich. I can't count the number of times 
that I have heard a Democrat Senator say this bill was just about tax 
cuts for billionaires.
  Well, look at the data. Does this look like a billionaire tax cut to 
you? Seventy-five percent of Americans taking the overtime deduction 
made less than $100,000 a year; 90 percent of those taking no tax on 
tips made less than $100,000 a year; and 97 percent of Americans who 
filed their taxes this year got a tax cut. This is real tax relief for 
hard-working Americans, and the American people are seeing it with 
their own eyes.
  I yield the floor.
  I suggest the absence of a quorum.
  The ACTING PRESIDENT pro tempore. The clerk will call the roll.
  The senior assistant legislative clerk proceeded to call the roll.
  Mr. SCHUMER. Mr. President, I ask unanimous consent that the order 
for the quorum call be rescinded.
  The ACTING PRESIDENT pro tempore. Without objection, it is so 
ordered.
```
