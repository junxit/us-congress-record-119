# HONORING THE 2026 INDUCTEES TO THE FLORIDA INVENTORS HALL OF FAME

**Speaking:** Castor, Kathy (C001066) [D-FL]

**Measures:** S.J.Res. 1756

```
   HONORING THE 2026 INDUCTEES TO THE FLORIDA INVENTORS HALL OF FAME

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. KATHY CASTOR

                               of florida

                    in the house of representatives

                     Wednesday, September 16, 2026

  Ms. CASTOR of Florida. Mr. Speaker, as our Nation commemorates the 
250th anniversary of its founding, I rise today to honor the ten 
inventors selected as the 2026 Inductees to the Florida Inventors Hall 
of Fame. The 2026 Inductees are inventors who were nominated by their 
peers nationwide and evaluated through the rigorous review of the 
Florida Inventors Hall of Fame Selection Committee. They are being 
recognized for innovations that significantly impact the quality of 
life, economic development, and welfare of their communities, the 
residents of Florida and the United States.
  The Florida Inventors Hall of Fame was founded in 2013 at the 
University of South Florida and formally recognized by the Florida 
Senate through Senate Joint Resolution 1756, adopted on April 30, 2014. 
As our Nation marks 250 years of independence, the Florida Inventors 
Hall of Fame carries that spirit of ingenuity forward by catalyzing the 
cycle of discovery and innovation across the state of Florida by 
recognizing pioneering inventors, empowering future problem-solvers and 
changemakers, and propelling the American legacy of innovation through 
transformative experiences and meaningful storytelling.
  Nomination to the Florida Inventors Hall of Fame is open to all 
Florida inventors, living or deceased, who are or have been residents 
of Florida. Nominees must be named inventors on a patent issued by the 
United States Patent and Trademark Office, and their inventions must 
have made a significant impact on society through commercialization, 
utilization, or the innovations they inspired.
  The Florida Inventors Hall of Fame proudly celebrates the 2026 class 
of Inductees whose inventions have helped shape the American story. 
Their achievements underscore the critical role that innovation has 
played in driving progress for two and a half centuries, strengthening 
our economy and enhancing our Nation's global competitiveness. The 2026 
Inductees collectively hold more than 300 United States patents, 
representing industry, academia, technology and medical/health 
institutions. Since its founding, the Florida Inventors Hall of Fame 
has inducted 96 inventors, who collectively hold over 6,000 United 
States patents.
  Mr. Speaker, on behalf of my neighbors in Tampa Bay and the residents 
of Florida, I am proud to honor the 2026 Inductees to the Florida 
Inventors Hall of Fame for this outstanding achievement. As the United 
States marks 250 years since its founding, innovation remains at the 
heart of the American story, and Florida continues to help write its 
next chapter. I applaud these accomplished inventors, and the 
institutions and communities that support them, for their tireless 
pursuit of discoveries that improve lives and expand what is possible. 
It is because of the vision and perseverance of inventors like these 
that future generations will be encouraged to reach beyond their limits 
and extend the boundaries of innovation.
  I include in the Record the following list of 2026 Inductees:

       Reza Abdolvand: Professor and Chair of the Department of 
     Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of 
     Central Florida, who is recognized for significant 
     contributions to MEMS-based timing and sensing technologies 
     that power high-speed communications, 5G networks, and 
     connected devices, enabling the infrastructure of the modern 
     digital world.
       Clara Rivero Baleine: Lockheed Martin Fellow at Lockheed 
     Martin Missiles and Fire Control, who is recognized for 
     breakthrough developments to gradient refractive index

     optical materials and metamaterial coatings that transformed 
     infrared sensing systems, enabling compact, high-performance 
     technologies across defense and advanced photonics 
     applications.
       Ni-Bin Chang: Professor of Environmental Systems 
     Engineering, and Director of the Stormwater Management 
     Academy at the University of Central Florida, who is 
     recognized for the groundbreaking development of green 
     sorption media, and advancing sustainable water treatment 
     technologies that remove contaminants, improve water quality, 
     and enable scalable environmental solutions across 
     infrastructure systems.
       Mark A. Frankie: Chief of Shoulder and Elbow Surgery at 
     Florida Orthopaedic Institute, and Clinical Professor at the 
     University of South Florida's Morsani College of Medicine, 
     who is recognized for revolutionizing shoulder joint 
     arthroplasty through reverse prosthesis design, restoring 
     function for hundreds of thousands of patients and 
     transforming orthopedic surgery worldwide.
       Patrick Hwu: President and Chief Executive Officer of 
     Moffitt Cancer Center, and Professor of Oncologic Sciences at 
     the University of South Florida's Morsani College of 
     Medicine, who is recognized for pioneering chimeric receptor 
     gene technologies that enabled CAR T-cell therapies, 
     transforming cancer treatment and advancing targeted 
     immunotherapy for patients worldwide.
       S.S. Iyengar: Distinguished University Professor, Ryder 
     Professor, and Director of the United States Army-funded 
     Center of Excellence in Digital Forensics at Florida 
     International University, who is recognized for foundational 
     contributions to sensor fusion and intelligent systems that 
     power modern computing, advancing critical applications in 
     national defense, disaster modeling, and healthcare 
     technologies.
       Donald K. Jones: Chief Executive Officer of Empirilon 
     Technology, LLC, who is recognized for pioneering innovations 
     in neurovascular and endoscopic medical devices, enabling 
     minimally invasive procedures that improved patient outcomes 
     and transformed treatment of stroke and gastrointestinal 
     disorders worldwide.
       Donald B. Keck: Professor at the University of South 
     Florida's Institute for Advanced Discovery & Innovation, and 
     former Vice President and Research Director at Corning 
     Incorporated, who is recognized for the landmark invention of 
     low-loss optical fiber technologies that revolutionized 
     global communications, replaced copper transmission systems, 
     and enabled the high-speed data networks that power today's 
     interconnected digital world.
       Maynard ``Mike'' Ramsey, III: Chief Executive Officer and 
     Chief Technology Officer of Cardio Command, Inc., and Founder 
     of Ramsey Medical, Inc., who is recognized for foundational 
     advances in patient monitoring technologies that transformed 
     modern medicine, enabling continuous automatic measurement of 
     noninvasive blood pressure, improving outcomes in hospitals, 
     clinics, and home care.
       Subrata Roy: Professor of Aerospace Engineering at the 
     University of Florida, and Founding Director of the Applied 
     Physics Research Group, and Founder and President of 
     SurfPlasma, Inc., who is recognized for pioneering 
     innovations in plasma-based technologies enabling 
     breakthroughs in aerospace efficiency, sterilization, and 
     environmental systems) transforming applications in 
     healthcare, food safety, water treatment, and space 
     exploration.
```
