       Mandate Effects:
       Contains intergovernmental mandate? No.
       Contains private-sector mandate? No.
       H.R. 1320 would prohibit considering whether a person 
     receives portable benefits to determine if they are an 
     employee for the purposes of any federal law. Portable 
     benefits include those commonly provided to full-time 
     employees, such as workers' compensation, professional 
     development, paid leave, health insurance coverage, and 
     retirement savings. Some of those benefits receive 
     preferential tax treatment. Whether a worker is classified as 
     an employee depends on many other factors, and the provision 
     of tax-preferred benefits, such as health insurance, is 
     subject to other laws and rules not modified by H.R. 1320.
       CBO expects that, if the bill were enacted, some employers 
     would offer nonemployees the option of participating in group 
     health benefits. As a result, some nonemployees would 
     participate in employer-subsidized group coverage rather than 
     purchasing individual coverage. CBO and the staff of the 
     Joint Committee on Taxation estimate that such a shift in 
     coverage would reduce federal tax revenues by less than 
     $500,000 over the 2026-2036 period.
       Enacting H.R. 1320 also would decrease collections of civil 
     monetary penalties that the Department of Labor (DOL) is 
     authorized to impose on employers that violate the Fair Labor 
     Standards Act (FLSA). Those penalties are recorded in the 
     federal budget as revenues, and a portion may be spent 
     without further appropriation. Under current law, DOL 
     typically collects less than $20 million each year in such 
     penalties for FLSA violations, according to agency 
     enforcement statistics.
       Because worker classification depends on several factors, 
     and H.R. 1320 would only restrict one of those factors, CBO 
     estimates that enacting the bill would change the 
     determination of employee status for a small number of cases 
     and reduce collections of civil monetary penalties by less 
     than $500,000. That reduction would decrease revenues and 
     direct spending and result in a net increase in the deficit 
     of less than $500,000 over the 2026-2036 period.
       CBO estimates that implementing H.R. 1320 would not 
     significantly affect the operating costs of DOL or the 
     National Labor Relations Board over the 2026-2031 period; any 
     change in related spending would be subject to the 
     availability of appropriated funds.

     Rufe. The estimate was reviewed by Christina Hawley Anthony, 
     Deputy Director of Budget Analysis.
