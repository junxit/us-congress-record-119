# HONORING ST. MARY BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                    HONORING ST. MARY BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                     Wednesday, September 16, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor St. Mary Baptist 
Church, a fixture in Baton Rouge since 1929 and a congregation whose 
nearly century-long history has been defined not simply by growth, but 
by a steadfast commitment to showing up for Baton Rouge's families and 
neighborhoods whenever practical help was needed.
  St. Mary Baptist Church began humbly, with a small group of believers 
worshiping together in a wood-framed cottage, and built its first 
sanctuary the very next year. From that modest beginning, the 
congregation grew into a major neighborhood institution, expanding its 
campus over the decades to meet the needs of an ever-growing 
congregation and community. Yet what has always distinguished St. Mary 
Baptist Church is not merely its physical growth, but the depth of its 
commitment to living out its faith through service. Today, that legacy 
continues under the leadership of its current pastor, Reverend Conway 
Knighton, who carries forward the congregation's long tradition of 
faith and service to the Baton Rouge community.
  For nearly 100 years, St. Mary Baptist Church has served as far more 
than a place of Sunday worship. It has been, and continues to be, an 
anchor of the Baton Rouge faith community, a place where generations of 
families have come to deepen their walk with God, to find comfort in 
times of hardship, and to be reminded that faith is best expressed 
through action. Among the church's most lasting contributions are its 
prison ministry, which carries the message of hope and redemption to 
those who are incarcerated, and its nursing-home outreach, which 
ensures that Baton Rouge's elderly residents are not forgotten and 
continue to feel the love and presence of their faith community even 
when they can no longer attend service themselves. In 2004, the church 
established its free Clothes Closet, providing clothing and shoes to 
local residents in need, a ministry that reflects St. Mary's 
understanding that caring for the body is inseparable from caring for 
the soul.
  St. Mary Baptist Church has also taken an active role in addressing 
the broader challenges facing Baton Rouge, including through its 
partnership with Together Baton Rouge, standing alongside other 
congregations and community organizations to advocate for the wellbeing 
of the city's families and neighborhoods. In doing so, St. Mary has 
exemplified the vital role that houses of worship play within the 
broader faith community: not only nurturing the spiritual lives of 
their own members, but extending that faith outward, becoming trusted 
partners in the work of building a stronger, more compassionate 
community for all of Baton Rouge.
  Through prison ministry, nursing-home outreach, the Clothes Closet, 
and its continued partnership in community advocacy, St. Mary Baptist 
Church has shown, generation after generation, that faith and service 
are inseparable. It stands today as a testament to what a congregation 
rooted in scripture and driven by compassion can mean to a city, and it 
continues to be a beacon of hope, comfort, and practical support for 
all who call Baton Rouge home.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring St. Mary 
Baptist Church, Reverend Conway Knighton, and the congregation for 
nearly a century of unwavering faith and devoted service to the Baton 
Rouge community. May St. Mary Baptist Church continue to be a light of 
hope and a hand of help for many generations to come.
```
