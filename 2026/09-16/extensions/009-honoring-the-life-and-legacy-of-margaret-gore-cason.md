# HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF MARGARET GORE CASON

**Speaking:** Elfreth, Sarah (E000301) [D-MD]

```
          HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF MARGARET GORE CASON

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. SARAH ELFRETH

                              of maryland

                    in the house of representatives

                     Wednesday, September 16, 2026

  Ms. ELFRETH. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life and legacy 
of Margaret Gore Cason, who passed away peacefully at the age of 95. 
Margaret Cason was a pioneering mathematician, public servant, mentor, 
and beloved mother, aunt, and grandmother.
  Born on November 10, 1930, in Pax, West Virginia, Margaret was raised 
in a close-knit community. She attended segregated local schools in the 
Pax and Beckley area and distinguished herself academically from an 
early age. Margaret went on to attend West Virginia State College (now 
West Virginia State University), graduating in 1951 with a Bachelor of 
Science degree. During her college years, she became a proud member of 
the Nu Chapter of Alpha Kappa Alpha Sorority, Inc. as one of a 
remarkable group of young women. Several of her mathematician Sorors 
from that time period would later gain national recognition in the 
story depicted in the film Hidden Figures.
  Following graduation, Margaret married Everett W. Crawford and moved 
to Havre de Grace, Maryland to begin her professional career working 
for the United States Army at Aberdeen Proving Ground. Holding the job 
title of ``Computer'' before the advent of modern computing technology, 
she performed advanced mathematical and military ballistic research 
calculations at APG's Ballistics Research Laboratory (BRL) in support 
of the Nations' defense efforts. Her work reflected both her 
exceptional analytical abilities and her commitment to public service.
  Margaret remarried and relocated to Birmingham, Alabama with Dr. 
William P. Cason, where she dedicated herself to helping others. In 
1987, she became the State Director of Women in Community Service 
(WICS) for Alabama. In that role, she devoted her life to assisting 
young women in overcoming adversity, finding new opportunities, and 
building brighter futures.
  In her final years, Margaret made her home in Ellicott City, 
Maryland, where she was a cherished member of the community and 
matriarch to her extended family.
  Margaret will be remembered by all who knew her for her intelligence, 
grace, strength of character, and genuine concern for others. 
Margaret's life spanned nearly a century of extraordinary change, and 
throughout it she remained a mentor, and a source of inspiration. From 
the coalfields of West Virginia to the laboratories of Aberdeen Proving 
Ground and the communities she served in Alabama, she left an enduring 
legacy of achievement, service, and love.
  On behalf of the United States Congress, I extend my deepest 
condolences to the Casons and her entire extended family and join them 
in celebrating the life of an extraordinary woman.
```
