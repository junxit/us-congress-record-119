# CELEBRATING THE NEW CLARENCE C. SMALLS CITY HALL IN GEORGETOWN, SOUTH CAROLINA

**Speaking:** Bynum, Janelle S. (B001326) [D-OR]

```
 CELEBRATING THE NEW CLARENCE C. SMALLS CITY HALL IN GEORGETOWN, SOUTH 
                                CAROLINA

                                 ______
                                 

                         HON. JANELLE S. BYNUM

                               of oregon

                    in the house of representatives

                     Wednesday, September 16, 2026

  Ms. BYNUM. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to celebrate the dedication of 
the new Clarence C. Smalls City Hall in Georgetown, South Carolina, and 
to honor the extraordinary life of public service that inspired its 
name.
  For decades, Clarence C. Smalls--affectionately known throughout 
Georgetown as ``Syrup''--has served his country, his city, his church, 
and his neighbors with humility, integrity, and an unwavering 
commitment to others. Now, at 96 years old, his name will permanently 
grace the home of Georgetown's city government, a fitting tribute to a 
man whose life has been so deeply intertwined with the community he 
loves. Georgetown City Council voted to name the new facility in his 
honor in recognition of his decades of service, including nearly three 
decades on the City Council and 27 years with the Georgetown Police 
Department.
  I include in the Record the following account of Mr. Smalls's 
remarkable life and legacy:

                    A Life of Service to Georgetown

       Clarence C. Smalls is the oldest son of Marion Smalls and 
     Aleathia F. Smalls. From an early age, he learned the values 
     of faith, hard work, responsibility, and respect--principles 
     that would guide him throughout a lifetime devoted to his 
     family, his community, and his country.
       As a young man, Clarence answered the call to serve his 
     nation as a member of the United States Army during the 
     Korean War. When he returned home, his commitment to service 
     continued. He joined law enforcement and spent more than 28 
     years serving communities in Georgetown and Florence, 
     including 27 years with the Georgetown Police Department. His 
     contributions to public safety extended beyond his years 
     wearing the uniform. As a member of the Georgetown City 
     Council, he helped secure funding for a new police department 
     facility, and his service was later recognized through his 
     induction into the Georgetown Police Department Hall of Fame.
       Clarence would go on to serve the people of Georgetown for 
     more than 27 years on the City Council, including service as 
     mayor pro

[[Page E943]]

     tempore. Through changing administrations and generations of 
     residents, he remained a steady presence in local government. 
     His leadership was grounded not in recognition or title, but 
     in a practical concern for the people he represented and a 
     belief that public service meant being available, listening, 
     and doing the work necessary to improve his community.
       His contributions extended far beyond City Hall. Clarence 
     spent 32 years as a member of the Longshoremen's Union, 
     serving as a local president and later as a trustee. He spent 
     four decades providing transportation services to his 
     community, operating a taxi business that did far more than 
     take people from one place to another. For many residents, 
     Clarence was a trusted person they could count on to help 
     them get to work, appointments, errands, and wherever else 
     life required them to go.
       Faith has been another constant throughout Clarence's life. 
     A member of Bethel AME Church for more than 90 years, he has 
     served the congregation as a trustee and has helped 
     strengthen its connection to the wider Georgetown community. 
     His faith is reflected in the manner in which he has 
     approached every responsibility--with compassion, humility, 
     generosity, and a willingness to help.
       Clarence's greatest legacy, however, can be found in his 
     family. Together with his wife, Mary G. Smalls, he built a 
     family that today includes five children, ten grandchildren, 
     eight great-grandchildren, and nine great-great-
     grandchildren, in addition to his siblings, nieces, nephews, 
     extended family, and countless friends. Through his example, 
     he has passed on the same values of faith, perseverance, 
     generosity, responsibility, and respect that his own parents 
     instilled in him.
       Over the course of his life, Clarence has earned numerous 
     honors, including induction into the Georgetown Police 
     Department Hall of Fame, the Georgetown Arborist Citizen 
     Award, and the Bethel AME Church Lay Organization Outstanding 
     Service Award. Yet perhaps the most enduring recognition is 
     the one now attached to the institution in which he spent so 
     many years serving his neighbors.
       After nearly a decade without a permanent City Hall, 
     Georgetown opened its new municipal home at 420 Hazard Street 
     in the West End in August 2026. The facility brings city 
     offices, public works, utility services, and the City Council 
     chambers together under one roof and is intended to make city 
     services more accessible to Georgetown residents. Above all, 
     it now bears the name Clarence C. Smalls City Hall.
       That name represents more than one man's time in elected 
     office. It represents a veteran who served his country, a 
     police officer who protected his neighbors, a councilman who 
     helped guide his city, a labor leader, businessman, church 
     trustee, husband, father, grandfather, mentor, and friend.
       Clarence C. Smalls has spent a lifetime showing what it 
     means to serve a community faithfully and without seeking 
     recognition for himself. Generations of Georgetown residents 
     have benefited from his steady presence, his wisdom, and his 
     willingness to help. It is fitting that generations to come 
     will walk into a building bearing his name and be reminded 
     that the strength of a community rests on people who dedicate 
     themselves to making it better.

  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in congratulating 
Clarence C. Smalls on this tremendous honor and in recognizing his 
extraordinary lifetime of service to Georgetown, South Carolina. May 
the Clarence C. Smalls City Hall stand as an enduring tribute to his 
service and an example for those who follow in his footsteps.
```
