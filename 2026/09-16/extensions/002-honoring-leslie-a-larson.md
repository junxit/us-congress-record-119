# HONORING LESLIE A. LARSON

**Speaking:** Larson, John B. (L000557) [D-CT]

```
                       HONORING LESLIE A. LARSON

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. JOHN B. LARSON

                             of connecticut

                    in the house of representatives

                     Wednesday, September 16, 2026

  Mr. LARSON of Connecticut. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Leslie 
A. Larson. I want to inform my fellow citizens of East Hartford, 
family, friends, and professional acquaintances of my enduring 
gratitude for their kind words, their letters, text and social media 
messages they have sent me since the August 11, 2026 Primary for which 
I gratefully thank them.
  On the evening of August 11, 2026, I did not get to thank all the 
people who have been so helpful to me over my many years of public 
service. There is especially one person who rarely receives thanks or 
appreciation for her help, and has sacrificed more than any individual 
or organization, and that is my wife Leslie. Frankly, she never seeks 
the spotlight and prefers the shadows, yet no one comes close to what 
she has persevered through over my lifelong career in public service. 
Incredibly, she has not only stood by me over the years, but also has 
raised and nurtured our three children, often times alone. The children 
have never known their father when he was not in public office and have 
had to endure not just an absentee father, but the unfair expectations 
placed on them and the falsehood that they got treated special, not 
because of who they were individually, or what they had accomplished on 
their own, but who their father was. A totally unfair attribute that 
only a caring mother with patience and perseverance was able to explain 
to get them through the rough spots.
  Both Leslie and I have always been grateful for a large and devoted 
family, including her father Norm and my mother, who along with our 
extended family have always been both willing and helpful. The reality 
of public life for Leslie is most often unseen. At the end of the day 
when the children are in bed, there is no one there to talk to or 
confide in about the daily occurrences with the family or simply 
empathize with about the day she and the kids have had. She deals with 
the daily set of circumstances that only a select few can. The 
realization that, when I get home, we will have the opportunity to sit 
down and have the time to talk it all through, never seems to happen. 
No matter how well intentioned, when I finally do get home, the 
unresolved issues remain because yet another crisis in the 
Congressional District arises and puts her everyday concerns along with 
nurturing and caring for a family on hold again. The crisis of the 
moment always seems to come up and needs to be dealt with, while the 
concerns of the family end up taking a back seat to the issue of the 
day and the urgency to be addressed. There is no denying who bears the 
burden of so many unresolved issues that were put on hold. This takes 
someone of enormous strength and character with the will to persist and 
persevere through the neglect that is simply the reality of public life 
and the life of a caring spouse.
  As incredible, Leslie puts on the best public face and is the one who 
uplifts and explains to the children when, unintentional or not, her 
needs and those of her children are seemingly subjugated to the needs 
of the public. This of course was never part of any marriage contract 
or pall of any proposal, or what either party envisioned when they were 
married, it is, however, the reality of public service and public life.
  Leslie and other spouses and families are always on display whether 
they want to be or not. Leslie has never sought the spotlight or 
frankly the glitter of public life. While there may be some advantages, 
and some opportunities, they pale to the realities of being alone with 
all the responsibility, with the lack of privacy and the security she 
and her children receive because of who she is married to.
  It is remarkable how she and spouses of other public officeholders do 
it. The best insight I can provide is that my wife is a master 
gardener. She is amazing in the way she is able to grow, nurture, and 
watch her flowers blossom and witness the plants that she has cared for 
from seedling to flowering come to fruition. She has held our family 
and our children together with the same tender care. She pays 
exceptional attention to detail, analogous to her emphasis on good 
soil, fertilizing, and the amount of sunlight and rain critical to 
growth. She applies those same gardening skills to holding our family 
together and watching it grow.
  There is no applause, no thank you from the garden or from the 
plants, no thank you from the flowers, only observing and knowing that 
helping them flourish and blossom happens because of her efforts. For 
Carolyn, Laura, and Ray, and certainly me, we benefitted from that same 
loving care. Leslie has never sought acknowledgment; like so many 
spouses of elected officials, she carries out her responsibilities out 
of a sense of duty and love. Without her, without the unselfish manner 
that she protects her children and her husband with, my life in public 
service would not have been possible. She is as remarkable as she is 
rare, and clearly my children and I have benefited from her efforts, 
and so have the 1st Congressional District and my hometown of East 
Hartford have as well.
  While I deeply appreciate all the constituent calls, notes, text 
messages, and acknowledgments, they were only made possible because of 
the sacrifice of Leslie A. Larson.
```
