# HONORING THE RETIREMENT OF ASSISTANT CHIEF PUBLIC DEFENDER PATRICIA "PATTI" LEE

**Speaking:** Simon, Lateefah (S001231) [D-CA]

```
  HONORING THE RETIREMENT OF ASSISTANT CHIEF PUBLIC DEFENDER PATRICIA 
                             ``PATTI'' LEE

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. LATEEFAH SIMON

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                     Wednesday, September 16, 2026

  Ms. SIMON. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor a beacon of justice and 
reform in the Bay Area, Ms. Patricia ``Patti'' Lee, the Assistant Chief 
Public Defender of the San Francisco Public Defender's Office.
  Ms. Lee was born in San Francisco's Chinatown and raised in the 
Richmond District. A lifelong San Franciscan, Ms. Lee attended 
Lafayette Elementary School, Presidio Middle School, and Washington 
High School. Ms. Lee went on to receive her B.A. at the University of 
California, Berkeley and earned her J.D. at Lincoln Law School.
  In 1977, Ms. Lee began her career with the San Francisco Public 
Defender's Office, joining what was then known as the Juvenile Unit. 
Ms. Lee would go on to become the Managing Attorney of the Youth 
Defender Unit and serve in that role for more than three decades. As 
Managing Attorney, Ms. Lee spearheaded a revolution in the office's 
approach to juvenile defense, expanding access to social workers and 
defense-based disposition reports, reducing state institutional 
commitments for San Francisco youth, and championing alternatives to 
incarceration. Under Ms. Lee's leadership, juvenile commitments to 
state institutions were nearly eliminated, creating new hope and 
opportunities for hundreds of justice-involved youths. Due to Ms. Lee's 
indefatigable advocacy, the San Francisco Board of Supervisors voted in 
2019 to permanently close the city's Juvenile Hall, becoming the first 
major city in the Nation to vote to end the practice of jailing 
children.
  In addition to her work with the Youth Defender Unit, Ms. Lee has 
also overseen the Public Defender's Office's Defend Division, providing 
sound guidance to dozens of public defenders, attorneys, and client 
advocates working to represent San Franciscans facing juvenile, 
immigration, post-conviction, and mental health challenges.
  Ms. Lee has been recognized at the local, state, and national level 
for her fearless advocacy and service. In 2017, Ms. Lee was appointed 
to then-President Barack Obama's Science Advisory Board on Juvenile and 
Criminal Justice. Ms. Lee has also served as Co-Director of the Pacific 
Juvenile Defender Center and served on the John D. and Catherine T. 
MacArthur Research Network on Adolescent Development and Juvenile 
Justice, the Family and Law Advisory Committee of the Judicial Council, 
the Center for Families, Children and the Courts, and has participated 
in the California Courts Family and Juvenile Law Advisory Committee. 
Additionally, Ms. Lee has served on the boards of the Center on 
Juvenile and Criminal Justice and East Bay Children's Law Offices. For 
her ceaseless efforts to advocate for juvenile justice reform, Ms. Lee 
has received the San Francisco Collaborative Against Human 
Trafficking's Modern Day Abolitionist Award for Outstanding Policy and 
Legal Achievements, the Pacific Asian American Women Bay Area 
Coalition's Woman Warrior Award for Outstanding Achievement in Law, the 
National Juvenile Defender Center's Leadership Award for Excellence in 
Juvenile Defense, the Pacific Juvenile Defender of the Year Award, and 
the California Wellness Foundation's Peace Prize Award for her 
commitment to promoting peace in local communities.
  Throughout her storied career, Ms. Lee has doggedly pursued the fight 
for justice, dignity, and reform, placing the rights and futures of 
young people at the center of her work. Ms. Lee has been an exemplary 
public servant and an inspiration for generations of public defenders, 
advocates, and reformers. Her impact will be felt by Californians for 
decades to come. As she enters her retirement, I thank Ms. Lee for her 
decades of service and wish her health, hope, and happiness in the 
years ahead.
```
