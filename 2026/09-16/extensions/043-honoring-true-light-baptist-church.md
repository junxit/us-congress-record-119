# HONORING TRUE LIGHT BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                   HONORING TRUE LIGHT BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                     Wednesday, September 16, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor True Light Baptist 
Church, a congregation whose faith and perseverance have carried it 
through 85 years of ministry. As Matthew 16:18 declares, ``And I say 
also unto thee, that thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my 
church; and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it.'' This 
scripture has proven true in the story of True Light Baptist Church, 
which began with a handful of believers and grew, by faith, into a 
lasting pillar of its community.
  In 1941, a few Christian believers, led by the Spirit of God, came 
together to organize a church in the name of Jesus Christ, and the name 
True Light Baptist Church was decided upon. A young and dynamic 
minister, Minister Chester Arthur Forrest, was asked to lead the 
congregation and assist in its organization. In January of 1941, the 
church held prayer meetings and nightly services in what had once been 
an old macaroni factory on North 40th Street, with a total membership 
of just seven.
  On April 14, 1941, the first communion was served by Rev. J. E. Lee, 
as Minister Forrest had not yet been ordained; the first offering and 
dues collected that day totaled $3.35, of which $1.50 was given to Rev. 
Lee for his services. On May 7, 1941, Minister Forrest was ordained, 
and on May 11, 1941, Rev. C. A. Forrest served his first communion, 
with offerings and dues netting $18.75. A revival service on May 12, 
1941, brought six souls to salvation, and on the second Sunday in June 
1941, the congregation held its first baptismal service at an outdoor 
pond known as Little Misery. Three more souls were saved in July 1941, 
and by May 1942, the congregation had grown to 27 members.
  Between 1941 and 1943, True Light Baptist Church relocated from the 
old macaroni factory to North 39th and Zion Street, continuing to grow 
in membership and in faith. In April 1955, the congregation constructed 
a new church building to accommodate its growth. As the church 
continued to flourish, that building, too, became too small for its 
congregation. In 1975, property on North Street was purchased, a 
groundbreaking ceremony followed

[[Page E946]]

in 1976, and on May 22, 1977, the church's present building was 
dedicated.
  Following the death of Rev. Forrest, Rev. John Earthly served the 
congregation untiringly as pastor for almost two years. On December 5, 
1989, Rev. Dennis R. Hebert was elected as the church's new pastor, a 
position in which he continues to serve faithfully and diligently, 
honoring the legacy left by the church's founders. Under Pastor 
Hebert's leadership, several ministries have been combined to 
strengthen their work and improve their service to both the 
congregation and the surrounding community. To this day, under his 
guidance, True Light Baptist Church continues pointing men, women, 
boys, and girls to Christ, which remains its members' only hope for 
salvation.
  True Light Baptist Church is, and has always been, a church where 
Jesus Christ is Lord, led by ministers with a vision of hope, and 
sustained by a people filled with the Spirit of Christ. In honoring 
this congregation, we also acknowledge the trailblazers who, like 
Nehemiah upon the wall, had no desire to come down until their work was 
done: Brother Charlie Dawson, Sr., Brother Kemp Thomas, Sr., Sister 
Luvenia Thomas, Brother Morgan Christentary, Brother Andrew Williams, 
Sister Rachel Hawkins, Sister Selena Williams, Sister Elsie Williams, 
and Sister Geneva Williams.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring True Light 
Baptist Church, Pastor Dennis R. Herbert, and the congregation's 
faithful trailblazers for 85 years of unwavering faith and devoted 
service. May True Light Baptist Church continue to shine as a beacon of 
hope for generations yet to come.
```
