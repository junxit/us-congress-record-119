# HONORING MR. MAJOR JEWEL McDONALD

**Speaking:** Thompson, Bennie G. (T000193) [D-MS]

```
                   HONORING MR. MAJOR JEWEL McDONALD

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. BENNIE G. THOMPSON

                             of mississippi

                    in the house of representatives

                     Wednesday, September 16, 2026

  Mr. THOMPSON of Mississippi. Mr. Speaker I rise today to honor Major 
Jewel McDonald of Louisville, Mississippi, a man whose life stands as a 
living testimony to faith, family, perseverence, and the enduring 
strength of the human spirit.
  Mr. McDonald was born on November 1, 1915, in Winston County, 
Mississippi, to Henry and Georgia McDonald. He came into this world 
during a time very different from the one we know today. He has lived 
through Prohibition, the Great Depression, World War II, the Jim Crow 
era, the civil rights movement, the 1918 Influenza pandemic, COVID-19, 
and more than a century of change in our Nation.
  One of thirteen children raised by a single mother, Mr. McDonald 
learned early that life required hard work, patience, and faith. He 
remembers a childhood in which the family did not have much and 
transportation meant little more than a mule and a wagon. Yet from 
those humble beginnings came a man whose life would stretch across 
generations and remind us that greatness is often born in places the 
world overlooks.
  As a young man, Mr. McDonald left Mississippi for Ohio during the 
years when many Black families journeyed north in search of work and 
opportunity. He worked for the Kroger Company for more than three 
decades before retiring. But Mississippi was always home. After 
retirement, he returned to Louisville, where his roots, his family, and 
his memories remained.
  Mr. McDonald's life has never been measured only by years, but by the 
people he has loved and the community he has touched. He is a husband, 
father, grandfather, neighbor, fisherman, and friend. He is known in 
his community for his kindness, his gentle spirit, and for the fish he 
caught and shared, including a catch that helped inspire what became an 
annual church fish fry tradition. His life teaches us that service does 
not always require a title. Sometimes it looks like feeding people, 
loving family, and showing up for one's community.
  In 2025, Mr. McDonald celebrated his 110th birthday with family and 
friends, becoming a supercentenarian. His age has been validated by 
longevity researchers, and he is now recognized as the oldest validated 
living man in the United States. Even now, he remains a symbol of 
strength and grace, still living in the community that helped shape 
him.
  Mr. McDonald has encouraged young people to slow down, reminding them 
of the Scripture that those who wait upon the Lord shall renew their 
strength. That wisdom, spoken by a man who has seen more than most of 
us can imagine, carries the weight of history and the warmth of lived 
experience.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring Major Jewel 
McDonald, a living bridge between Mississippi's past and future and a 
reminder that faith, family, work, humility, and patience remain 
pillars of a meaningful life.
```
