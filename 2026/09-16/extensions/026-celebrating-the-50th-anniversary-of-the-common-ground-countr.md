# CELEBRATING THE 50TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE COMMON GROUND COUNTRY FAIR

**Speaking:** Pingree, Chellie (P000597) [D-ME]

```
   CELEBRATING THE 50TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE COMMON GROUND COUNTRY FAIR

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. CHELLIE PINGREE

                                of maine

                    in the house of representatives

                     Wednesday, September 16, 2026

  Ms. PINGREE. Mr. Speaker, I rise to celebrate the 50th Anniversary of 
the Common Ground Country Fair and the organization that makes it 
possible, The Maine Organic Farmers and Gardeners Association (MOFGA).
  If you are from Maine, you have most likely attended the Common 
Ground Country Fair. This celebration of food, friends, and organic 
farming takes place every September, and for over 50 years has brought 
together hundreds of thousands of Mainers of all ages and from all 
walks of life. The first fair, conceived as a harvest celebration, drew 
over 10,000 people to Litchfield--now nearly 70,000 people visit this 
wonderful gathering--from Maine and many distant locations.
  While its location has changed and size has grown, the character has 
stayed the same, and that is the fair's beauty. When most Americans 
think of fairs, they envision Ferris wheels, a brightly lighted midway, 
and cotton candy.
  None of that can be found at the Common Ground Fair--but it is a very 
unique and fun gathering and people wait all year for the fair weekend 
to come around again. At the fairgrounds in Unity, there are animal 
barns, crop plots, demonstrations of everything from canning tomatoes 
to making compost to sheering a sheep. Perhaps most valued are the rows 
and rows of food booths--ice cream to fresh cider, baked beans to lamb 
kebobs--virtually all Maine grown and organic--and absolutely 
delicious.
  The fair features 1,000 plus exhibits and speakers that capture the 
importance of rural and sustainable living in our state. It celebrates 
farmers, artisans, crafters, and musicians--all the incredible 
individuals who make our food system possible and our communities so 
vibrant.
  This anniversary is near and dear to my heart. I have been associated 
with MOFGA nearly from its beginning--as an employee, a board member 
and a beneficiary of its programs. In 1974, I had the pleasure of 
serving as MOFGA's first apprentice working for Tony and Mary Bok on 
their Hillside Farm in Camden. I planted and weeded vegetables, packed 
loose hay into the loft of their historic barn, and learned to milk 
their Guernsey cows by hand. After that experience, I helped to 
formalize MOFGA's apprentice program, which has given hundreds of young 
people a start in farming, learning the best way possible with their 
own hands from the people who work our farms every day.
  This program fostered my love of organic, sustainable, and locally 
focused agriculture and food system reform. In my D.C. and District 
offices, my walls are covered in Common Ground Country Fair posters 
designed each year by Maine artists. There is no better reminder of the 
spirit of our state's history and culture and what it's all about--
coming together to celebrate and carry forward responsible agricultural 
and rural living practices.
  I congratulate MOFGA and all the people who, for the past 50 years, 
have made this amazing gathering happen. I hope to see everyone in 
Unity soon.
```
