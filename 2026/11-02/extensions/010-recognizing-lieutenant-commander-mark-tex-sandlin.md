# RECOGNIZING LIEUTENANT COMMANDER MARK "TEX" SANDLIN

**Speaking:** Van Duyne, Beth (V000134) [R-TX]

```
         RECOGNIZING LIEUTENANT COMMANDER MARK ``TEX'' SANDLIN

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. BETH VAN DUYNE

                                of texas

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, November 2, 2026

  Ms. VAN DUYNE. Mr. Speaker, I rise today on behalf of a grateful 
Nation and the citizens of the 24th District of Texas, the 2026 
Congressional Veteran Commendation is hereby presented to Lieutenant 
Commander Mark Sandlin, United States Navy, of Bedford, Texas, for his 
honorable service in the Armed Forces of the United States of America 
and for his continued service to his community.
  Lieutenant Commander Sandlin served with distinction in the United 
States Navy from 1982 to 1998 as a Naval Flight Officer and F-14 Tomcat 
Radar Intercept Officer. He accumulated more than 1,600 flight hours, 
including 110 in combat, and completed 330 carrier landings, including 
136 at night. During combat operations in the Arabian Gulf, he led the 
planning, training, and execution of tactical strikes involving twenty 
aircraft. His leadership in twenty-eight combat missions earned him the 
Air Medal. His decorations also include the Defense Meritorious Service 
Medal, three Navy Commendation Medals, and the Navy Achievement Medal.
  As Director of Combat Search and Rescue Training, Lieutenant 
Commander Sandlin authored the Navy's first updated tactical doctrine 
in fifteen years and personally trained more than 3,000 Naval Aviators 
and SEALs. Aboard the USS Abraham Lincoln, he oversaw training for 
approximately 5,700 personnel and helped coordinate the evacuation of 
approximately 5,000 civilians following the 1991 Mount Pinatubo 
eruption in the Philippines. He later served as Deputy Planning Chief 
at U.S. Strategic Command, contributing to national defense planning.
  Following retirement, Lieutenant Commander Sandlin continued his 
dedication to education and mentorship, having taught more than 20,000 
students across his military and civilian careers. From 2013 to 2017, 
he volunteered more than 1,700 hours at the George W. Bush Presidential 
Library and Museum, developing educational programs, training 
volunteers, and co-chairing its Education Committee. His contributions 
earned the Library's Distinguished Service Award, its highest honor. 
After earning a STEM Master of Business Administration from Texas Tech 
University in 2017 as the top graduate in his cohort, he continued 
mentoring undergraduate and graduate students at Texas Tech and Rice 
University.
  Lieutenant Commander Sandlin's distinguished military career, 
courageous combat service, and enduring commitment to education and 
mentorship reflect the highest traditions of the United States Navy and 
bring great credit upon himself, the State of Texas, and the United 
States of America.
```
