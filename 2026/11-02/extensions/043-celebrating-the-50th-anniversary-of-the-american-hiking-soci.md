# CELEBRATING THE 50TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE AMERICAN HIKING SOCIETY

**Speaking:** Schneider, Bradley Scott (S001190) [D-IL]

```
    CELEBRATING THE 50TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE AMERICAN HIKING SOCIETY

                                 ______
                                 

                      HON. BRADLEY SCOTT SCHNEIDER

                              of illinois

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, November 2, 2026

  Mr. SCHNEIDER. Mr. Speaker, I rise to recognize and honor American 
Hiking Society (AHS) as it celebrates its 50th Anniversary.
  Founded in 1976 by Jim Kern, William ``Backpacker Bill'' Kemsley, and 
Paul Pritchard, American Hiking Society has been leading the 63 million 
strong hiking community as the national voice of hikers.
  For half a century, American Hiking Society has brought together tens 
of thousands of volunteers to build and maintain trails across our 
Nation. The Society's history of stewardship traces back to the 
Volunteer Conservation Corps, founded by conservation champion Bill 
Ruskin, who built a national trail stewardship movement beginning with 
the first project at Pike National Forest in 1974. This would become 
American Hiking Society's foundational Volunteer Vacations program.
  The scope of stewardship is truly remarkable. As they celebrate their 
50th Anniversary, AHS volunteers have improved more than 49,000 miles 
of trails across 15,159 projects on public lands in all 50 states. 
These dedicated individuals have contributed 5.2 million volunteer 
hours, providing labor valued at $101.125 million in donated time and 
effort. This volunteer-driven stewardship keeps our hiking trails safe, 
intact, and accessible, while instilling a conservation ethic in 
communities nationwide. Over the last 50 years, 673,540 people have 
joined trail service projects through impactful programs like Volunteer 
Vacations, Alternative Breaks, and National Trails Day. National Trails 
Day alone stands as the single biggest trail event and day of service, 
occurring each year on the first Saturday in June.
  Beyond their hands-on stewardship, American Hiking Society has been a 
powerful voice for hikers and public lands. They led the historic 
coast-to-coast ``HikaNation'' walk in 1980 and served as a catalyst for 
founding the American Discovery Trail in 1989. The organization also 
launched the annual Hike the Hill event that brings hikers from across 
the country to Washington D.C., which will celebrate its 30th 
Anniversary in 2027. Their efforts on Capitol Hill fostered the 
creation of the bipartisan House Trails Caucus. The Society's tireless 
advocacy played a key role in the enactment of landmark laws and 
policies including amendments to the National Trails System Act in the 
1980s, the creation of the Recreational Trails Program in 1991, the 
Forest Service Roadless Rule in 2001 and the Travel Management Rule in 
2005, the National Forest System Trail Stewardship Act in 2016, the 
John D. Dingell, Jr. Conservation, Management, and Recreation Act in 
2019 to permanently authorize the Land and Water Conservation Fund, the 
Great American Outdoors Act in 2020, the Expanding Public Lands Outdoor 
Recreation Experiences (EXPLORE) Act.
  In its first 50 years, American Hiking Society has blazed a trail of 
leadership, community partnership, and deep devotion to our Nation's 
public lands and trails. As they embark on their next 50 years, I 
extend my great appreciation to their service and commitment to the 
outdoors. I have no doubt their impact will continue to shape our 
trails and natural spaces for years to come.
```
