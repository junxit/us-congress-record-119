# RECOGNIZING THE SERVICE AND LEGACY OF CORPORAL BYRON FRANKLIN BEARD, JR.

**Speaking:** Griffith, H. Morgan (G000568) [R-VA]

```
 RECOGNIZING THE SERVICE AND LEGACY OF CORPORAL BYRON FRANKLIN BEARD, 
                                  JR.

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. H. MORGAN GRIFFITH

                              of virginia

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, November 2, 2026

  Mr. GRIFFITH. Mr. Speaker, I rise in recognition of Corporal Byron 
Franklin Beard, Jr. to honor his courageous service to the U.S. Army 
Air Force, in which he lost his life after valiantly fighting Japanese 
forces during World War II.
  Corporal Beard, a native of Scott County and graduate of Dungannon 
High School, felt a calling to serve his country and protect the values 
and freedoms that Americans hold dear. He joined the U.S. Army in 1940 
and served with the 409th Signal Company, Aviation, U.S. Army Air 
Force.
  Following the Japanese bombing of Pearl Harbor on December 7, 1941, 
which killed more than 2,400 Americans, the Japanese invaded the 
Philippines on December 8, 1941. The United States would shortly 
thereafter formally declare war on Japan and engage Japanese forces in 
the Pacific Theater and the Philippines. As part of U.S. engagement, 
Corporal Beard fought in the Philippines.
  The early battles in the Pacific dealt great losses to U.S. forces. 
In the Philippines, Japanese invading forces delivered severe blows 
that led to the surrender of American forces. The enemy captured 
Corporal Beard and placed him with an estimated 75,000 Filipino and 
American prisoners of war. These prisoners underwent a torturous 65-
mile march towards a Japanese prison camp on what is known as the 
Bataan Death March.
  The thousands of prisoners like Corporal Beard faced inhumane 
conditions and barbaric treatment from their Japanese captors. Intense 
tropical heat, thirst, starvation and disease contributed to immense 
suffering. These brutal circumstances resulted in the deaths of many 
prisoners, including Corporal Beard, who died at the prisoner of war 
camp on July 22, 1942, at the age of 23.
  Because of this, for nearly a century, Corporal Beard's remains were 
never identified. His family back in Southwest Virginia had to endure 
the heartbreak of losing their beloved family member and never seeing 
him again for the rest of their lives.
  This January, DNA testing helped identify Corporal Beard's missing 
remains. Accordingly, this Veterans Day, Corporal Beard will finally be 
laid to rest back home in Scott County with full military honors. I 
join the people of Scott County, Southwest Virginia and all veterans to 
honor our veterans and recognize the inspiring service of Corporal 
Beard. Thanks to his bravery and actions, like those of the many other 
American heroes who surrendered their lives during World War II, 
America stands today as the world's greatest, most free and prosperous 
superpower.
```
