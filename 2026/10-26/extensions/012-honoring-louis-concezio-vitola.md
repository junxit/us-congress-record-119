# HONORING LOUIS CONCEZIO VITOLA

**Speaking:** Fitzpatrick, Brian K. (F000466) [R-PA]

```
                     HONORING LOUIS CONCEZIO VITOLA

                                  _____
                                 

                       HON. BRIAN K. FITZPATRICK

                            of pennsylvania

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 26, 2026

  Mr. FITZPATRICK. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Louis Concezio 
Vitola, a World War II veteran whose life reflects the values of 
patriotism, faith, hard work, and devotion to family.
  Born on October 23, 1926, in South Philadelphia, Louis was the second 
of four children born to Italian immigrants. After leaving high school 
to help support his family when his father became ill with cancer, 
Louis worked at Heppenstall Steel Mill and Baldwin Locomotive, 
contributing to the production of equipment essential to our Nation's 
wartime efforts. While working, he continued his education through 
evening classes and earned his high school diploma.
  At just 18 years old, Louis was drafted into the United States Army 
on February 7, 1945. He served during World War II and the postwar 
occupation of the Philippines and Japan, witnessing the historic events 
surrounding the end of the war. Assigned to the 77th Infantry Division 
and later to a medical detachment, he served as a dental hygienist, 
helping care for his fellow service members. He earned the Expert 
Infantry and M1 Rifle badges, the Good Conduct Medal, the World War II 
Victory Medal, the Asiatic-Pacific Campaign Medal, and the Philippine 
Liberation Ribbon. He attained the rank of Technical Sergeant and was 
honorably discharged on November 27, 1946.
  Following his military service, Louis built a successful career in 
the men's clothing industry. He and his beloved wife, Mary, shared 72 
years of marriage and raised six children, whose families grew to 
include 15 grandchildren and 17 great-grandchildren. After living in 
Pennsylvania for much of their lives, they moved to Florida before 
returning to Pennsylvania in 2003 to be closer to their extended 
family. Louis also remained active in his community through his 
involvement with the Veterans of Foreign Wars and the American Legion.
  Throughout his life, Louis demonstrated remarkable resilience, 
maintaining an active lifestyle well into his later years and 
cherishing his faith, friendships, and family. His enduring love for 
his country and his commitment to those around him stand as a testament 
to the character of the Greatest Generation. Although Mary passed away 
in 2025, the legacy they built together lives on through their 
children, grandchildren, and great-grandchildren.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring Louis 
Concezio Vitola for his honorable service to our Nation during World 
War II and for a lifetime defined by perseverance, patriotism, faith, 
and devotion to family. May we express our deepest gratitude for his 
service and celebrate the extraordinary legacy he continues to leave 
for generations to come.
```
