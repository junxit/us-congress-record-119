# HONORING THE NOMINATION OF THE UNITED STATES AGENCY FOR INTERNATIONAL DEVELOPMENT FOR THE NOBEL PEACE PRIZE

```
 HONORING THE NOMINATION OF THE UNITED STATES AGENCY FOR INTERNATIONAL 
                 DEVELOPMENT FOR THE NOBEL PEACE PRIZE

                                  _____
                                 

                        HON. JAMES R. WALKINSHAW

                              of virginia

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 26, 2026

  Mr. WALKINSHAW. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize the nomination 
of the United States Agency for International Development (USAID) for 
the Nobel Peace Prize by former Liberian President and Nobel Peace 
Prize laureate Ellen Johnson Sirleaf.
  For more than six decades, USAID represented the best of American 
leadership abroad. Established by President John F. Kennedy in 1961, 
the agency helped prevent famine, combat infectious diseases, 
strengthen democratic institutions, and rebuild communities devastated 
by war and natural disasters.

[[Page E1127]]

Its work saved millions of lives and advanced the cause of peace around 
the world.
  President Sirleaf knows the importance of that work firsthand. As 
Liberia emerged from a devastating civil war, USAID helped restore 
essential services, rebuild democratic institutions, and support the 
country's recovery. During the Ebola epidemic, USAID helped Liberia 
confront another national emergency by coordinating response and relief 
efforts. In her nomination, President Sirleaf describes the agency's 
contributions to her country's recovery and to international 
development over more than six decades.
  USAID's accomplishments extend far beyond Liberia. The agency played 
an essential role in the global campaign to eradicate smallpox, the 
Green Revolution that dramatically expanded agricultural production, 
and the President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief, which has saved 
millions of lives. Through Food for Peace and the Famine Early Warning 
Systems Network, USAID helped prevent humanitarian catastrophes before 
they occurred. Its investments in education, women's economic 
empowerment, and democratic governance strengthened societies and 
addressed the root causes of conditions that can contribute to 
conflict.
  These achievements were made possible by generations of dedicated 
public servants, including thousands of Americans from Northern 
Virginia who devoted their careers to advancing our country's 
humanitarian and national security interests.
  This nomination comes at a consequential moment. The Trump 
administration's dismantling of USAID has disrupted lifesaving 
programs, undermined decades of American investments, and weakened our 
nation's ability to respond to humanitarian crises. The consequences 
will be felt by vulnerable communities around the world and by the 
United States, which has long understood that preventing instability 
abroad is essential to our security at home.
  The Nobel Peace Prize recognizes those who advance peace among 
nations. USAID's history demonstrates that peace is not achieved 
through diplomacy alone. It is also advanced when children survive 
preventable diseases, families have enough to eat, democratic 
institutions take root, and communities recover from conflict.
  Mr. Speaker, I commend President Ellen Johnson Sirleaf for nominating 
USAID for the Nobel Peace Prize. I also recognize the generations of 
USAID employees and humanitarian partners whose service made this 
nomination possible. Their work represents an extraordinary American 
contribution to the cause of peace, and their legacy deserves to be 
preserved and honored.
```
