# HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF ROBERT W. MOLT, SR.

```
          HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF ROBERT W. MOLT, SR.

                                  _____
                                 

                         HON. JAMES P. McGOVERN

                            of massachusetts

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 26, 2026

  Mr. McGOVERN. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize the life and 
legacy of Robert W. ``Bob'' Molt, Sr., a beloved member of the 
Worcester community, longtime golf coach at the College of the Holy 
Cross, and dedicated mentor to generations of student-athletes, on the 
occasion of his posthumous induction into the Holy Cross Varsity Club 
Athletic Hall of Fame.
  For more than four decades, Bob Molt was synonymous with golf at Holy 
Cross. From 1976 until his retirement in 2017, Bob served as head coach 
of the men's golf program, and from 2000 to 2013, he also led the 
women's team. Over those 41 years, Bob built successful teams, but more 
importantly, he built relationships that lasted long after his players 
left Mount St. James.
  Under Bob's leadership, the Holy Cross men's golf team made 
consecutive NCAA Tournament appearances in 1982 and 1983 and won 
numerous Worcester City Championships. He coached 13 All-Patriot League 
selections, two New England individual champions, a Patriot League 
champion, and a Metro Atlantic Athletic Conference champion. In 2006, 
his peers honored him with the Gordon McCullough Coaching Award for his 
outstanding dedication to college golf and his contributions to 
players, fellow coaches, and the broader golf community.
  Bob knew the game from every angle. A Worcester native, he attended 
the University of Arizona on a golf scholarship, captained the golf 
team, and graduated in 1972. He went on to become a PGA Professional, 
earning his PGA Tour card in 1982 and competing in tournaments in the 
United States and overseas. For 22 years, he served as Director of Golf 
at Pleasant Valley Country Club in Sutton, and he later owned and 
operated the Solomon Pond Golf Center in Berlin, MA.
  But statistics and championships tell only part of Bob's story.
  To Bob, golf was fundamentally a game of integrity. It demanded 
patience, discipline, perseverance, and doing the right thing even when 
no one was watching. Those were the lessons he sought to pass along to 
every young person he coached.
  His record off the course may therefore be his most impressive: in 41 
years at Holy Cross, Bob proudly noted that he never had a student-
athlete fail to graduate.
  When Bob prepared to retire in 2017, he was asked what he would miss 
most after four decades of coaching. His answer was simple: the 
student-athletes.
  That response captures Bob well. His career was never just about 
golf. It was about people.
  Former players continued to visit him at the Solomon Pond Golf Center 
years after they had graduated. They sought his advice, shared stories, 
and maintained friendships that began when Bob was their coach. For 
countless golfers throughout Worcester and New England, he was not 
simply a coach or golf professional, but a teacher, mentor, and friend.
  Bob was also deeply devoted to his family. Of all the tournaments, 
championships, and honors he experienced during his remarkable career, 
Bob once identified two moments as particularly special. The first was 
receiving the Gordon McCullough Award in 2006. The second was coaching 
his daughter, Alicia, at the Big South Championship in 2009.
  Bob passed away on February 26, 2020, at the age of 73. He left 
behind his wife, Krystina; his son, Dr. Robert W. Mob, Jr.; his 
daughter, Alicia Molt West; his brother, Karl; his grandchildren, 
Charlotte, Grace, and Julian; and an extraordinary community of former

[[Page E1130]]

players, colleagues, students, and friends whose lives were made better 
by knowing him.
  This year, the College of the Holy Cross will formally recognize what 
generations of his players have long understood by inducting Robert W. 
Molt, Sr. into its Athletic Hall of Fame.
  It is a fitting honor for a man who devoted 41 years to the College 
and to generations of its student-athletes. Bob's legacy can be found 
not simply in tournament results or record books, but in the people he 
taught, the relationships he built, and the values he passed down.
  Mr. Speaker, on behalf of the United States Congress and the people 
of the Second District of Massachusetts, I ask my colleagues to join me 
in honoring Robert W. Molt, Sr. on his induction into the Holy Cross 
Varsity Club Athletic Hall of Fame and in recognizing a life defined by 
family, friendship, integrity, and an enduring love for the game of 
golf.
```
