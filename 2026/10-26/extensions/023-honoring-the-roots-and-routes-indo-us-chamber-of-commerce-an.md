# HONORING THE ROOTS AND ROUTES INDO--US CHAMBER OF COMMERCE AND ITS 2026 30 UNDER 30 HONOREES

```
HONORING THE ROOTS AND ROUTES INDO--US CHAMBER OF COMMERCE AND ITS 2026 
                          30 UNDER 30 HONOREES

                                  _____
                                 

                      HON. SANFORD D. BISHOP, JR.

                               of georgia

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, October 26, 2026

  Mr. BISHOP. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the Roots & Routes 
Indo--US Chamber of Commerce, its Chairman, Dr. Piyush B. Patel, its 
President, Dr. Pravinchandra Patel, its Founder Director, Mr. 
Nandkishor Shah, its officers, directors, advisors, and members, and 
the thirty-four extraordinary young leaders it celebrated at its 30 
Under 30 Awards on Sunday, October 25, 2026, at the Columbus Civic 
Center in Columbus, Georgia. I was honored to join them for that 
memorable afternoon.
  Mr. Speaker, the name of this organization tells a story. Roots speak 
of where we come from: a heritage, a homeland, a family, a faith. 
Routes speak of where we are going: the roads we travel, the bridges we 
build, the futures we dare to imagine. Roots & Routes was founded to 
honor both, and to connect Indian and American entrepreneurs, 
executives, community leaders, and public officials across Georgia in a 
shared work of business growth and community advancement.
  Mr. Speaker, every great institution begins with a vision held by one 
person who refuses to let it go. Mr. Nandkishor Shah saw a community 
rich in talent, enterprise, and generosity, and he saw that too many of 
its small-business owners were building alone, without the 
relationships, visibility, and guidance that success so often requires. 
He set out to change that, believing that when people, ideas, and 
opportunities come together communities grow stronger. Together with 
his colleagues, he has built what the Chamber proudly calls a community 
of doers, mentors, and bridge-builders across Georgia and beyond.
  Mr. Speaker, that vision is carried forward by a remarkable board of 
directors sitting state officials, founding entrepreneurs, and trade 
advisers, all doing the work of connecting Georgia to India. I commend 
the Chamber's officers: Chairman Dr. Piyush B. Patel; President

[[Page E1132]]

Dr. Pravinchandra Patel: Vice President Mr. Ashok Naik; Secretary Mr. 
Viraj Shah; and Treasurer Ms. Shikara Reddy. I commend its directors: 
Founder Director Mr. Nandkishor Shah, Mr. Saurin Patel, Mr. Ankit 
Patel, Mr. Alkesh Patel, Mr. Suresh Kumar, Mr. Rajeev Chauhan, Ms. 
Lavina Babani, Mr. Parimal Desai, and Mr. Sanket Gandhi. And I commend 
its advisors: Dr. Nehal Shukla, the Honorable Tricia Pridemore, Ms. 
Carmen Rice, Mr. Kartik Bhatt, Ms. Missy Kendrick, Dr. Ranjankrishna 
Aiyer, Dr. Sumul Raval, Mr. Britt Hayes, and Mr. Milind Godbole. They 
give generously of their time, talent, and resources so that every 
member has genuine access to mentorship and leadership.
  Mr. Speaker, the 30 Under 30 Awards were only one expression of that 
mission. Through its Learn and Grow initiatives, Roots & Routes offers 
workshops, roundtables, and direct mentorship from experienced 
entrepreneurs and industry experts. Through its Network and Visibility 
programs, it builds powerful relationships across the Indo--US business 
community and gives members real visibility for their enterprises. 
Through its Advocacy and Trade work, it represents its members on 
issues that matter and helps them navigate Indo--US trade and expansion 
opportunities. And through its Community Impact efforts, it builds a 
supportive, family-like community doing purposeful work. Whether a 
member is starting a business, expanding an established company, or 
looking for ways to serve, Roots & Routes helps them find the people, 
information, and opportunities to move forward.
  Mr. Speaker, behind each of the thirty-four young honorees is a 
journey that began long before they were born. Somewhere in a village 
in Gujarat, a city in Punjab, a town in Kerala, or a neighborhood in 
Hyderabad, a mother and father made a decision that would change 
everything. They packed what little they could carry into a few 
suitcases. They said goodbye to parents they might never see again. 
They left behind the smell of their mother's kitchen, the sound of 
their own language in the streets, and everything familiar, and they 
crossed oceans to a land where they knew almost no one.
  They came with degrees that were not always recognized and with 
accents that were not always welcomed. Some who had been engineers and 
doctors at home started over behind motel desks and convenience store 
counters, working through the night, seven days a week, so their 
children would never have to. They missed weddings back home. They 
missed funerals. They called overseas on crackling phone lines and told 
their families, ``We are fine. The children are doing well.'' And then 
they went back to work.
  Mr. Speaker, on Sunday afternoon, those sacrifices walked across a 
stage in Columbus, Georgia. Those thirty-four rising Indo--US business 
leaders were the answer to every prayer whispered in a temple, a 
gurdwara, a church, or a mosque by parents and grandparents who 
believed America would keep its promise. They are building businesses, 
creating jobs, advancing technology, healing patients, and serving 
their communities. They are proof that the American Dream is not a 
relic of the past. It is alive, and it is being renewed by every 
generation willing to work for it.
  There is an ancient Indian teaching, Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam, which 
means ``the world is one family.'' Roots & Routes lives that teaching 
every day, building trust between communities that, together, make 
Georgia stronger.
  To the thirty-four honorees, I said then, and I say again today for 
them to never forget their roots. Remember the hands that worked so 
theirs could reach higher. Call their parents. Thank their grandparents 
while they still can. As they travel their routes, reach back and open 
the door for someone else, just as someone once opened it for them.
  I say to the mothers and fathers, the grandparents, and the elders 
who filled the room that their tears were not in vain. Their long 
nights were not wasted. Their children have become everything they've 
dreamed, and more. That honor belonged to them as well.
  Mr. Speaker, the great poet Rabindranath Tagore once prayed for a 
land ``where the mind is without fear and the head is held high.'' On 
Sunday, in Columbus, Georgia, we saw thirty-four young people standing 
with their heads held high, and we saw that prayer answered.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join my wife Vivian and me, along 
with the more than 765,000 people of Georgia's Second Congressional 
District in commending Chairman Dr. Piyush B. Patel, President Dr. 
Pravinchandra Patel, Founder Director Mr. Nandkishor Shah, and the 
officers, directors, advisors, and members of the Roots & Routes Indo--
US Chamber of Commerce for their vision and service; in congratulating 
the 2026 30 Under 30 honorees; in thanking the families who made their 
journeys possible; and in wishing these remarkable young leaders 
continued success as they shape the future of Georgia and our Nation.
```
