# TRIBUTE TO DR. PAUL KEARNS

**Speaking:** Durbin, Richard J. (D000563) [D-IL]

```
                       TRIBUTE TO DR. PAUL KEARNS

  Mr. DURBIN. Mr. President, if you find yourself driving down I-55 
from Chicago, you are certain to pass one of Illinois' great treasures. 
Tucked in the Waterfall Glen Forest Preserve is the campus of Argonne 
National Laboratory, one of our Nation's premier scientific research 
facilities. Every day, thousands of scientists and engineers gather 
here to conduct groundbreaking research that pushes the bounds of our 
understanding of the universe. With their work we will build better 
batteries, faster computers, stronger materials, and countless other 
wonders. Their achievements are the pride of Illinois, and it has been 
my great pleasure to support their work during my time in the Senate.
  But as I approach the end of my tenure in Congress, so too is Argonne 
preparing for a significant change. After 17 years of extraordinary 
service, Argonne's laboratory director Dr. Paul Kearns has announced 
his retirement. Dr. Kearns has served as the lab's director for the 
last decade, and he leaves behind a legacy of steady leadership, great 
ambition, and a genuine kindness. Dr. Kearns has paved the way for an 
era of collaboration and discovery for Argonne, and I would like to 
take a moment today to recognize his contributions to the scientific 
and research communities.
  Dr. Kearns came to the lab in 2010 to serve as COO, and since 
becoming director in 2017, he has guided the laboratory through a 
decade of transformation and scientific advancement. His leadership has 
reflected a deep understanding of the National Laboratories' unique 
responsibility: to undertake grand scientific challenges that go beyond 
the reach of individual institutions, to bring together expertise 
across disciplines and sectors, and to translate discovery into 
innovations to better society.
  Central to his tenure has been the modernization and expansion of 
world-class scientific facilities at Argonne. Under Dr. Kearns, Argonne 
completed the transformational upgrade of the Advanced Photon Source, 
equipping scientists with the world's brightest X-ray beam. His 
leadership also brought the Aurora exascale supercomputer into 
operation, positioning Argonne as home to one of the fastest computers 
in the world. During my visits to the lab these last 10 years, I have 
seen these projects move from concept to completion, all shepherded by 
Dr. Kearns' thoughtful leadership. These achievements have given 
researchers across the country new tools to enable breakthroughs in 
energy, materials, medicine, and other critical fields.
  Take COVID-19. In 2020, when the pandemic reached the United States, 
Argonne sprang into action. The X-ray beam of the Advanced Photon 
Source was used to determine the protein structure of the virus, 
helping to enable the rapid development of an effective vaccine. 
Likewise, Argonne's advanced computing and artificial intelligence 
tools were used to screen billions of potential drug molecules for 
treatment options. The lab even developed advanced modeling to predict 
the spread of COVID-19 throughout the country. Even in a time of 
national crisis, with the lab on lockdown like the rest of the world, 
Dr. Kearns leveraged the brilliant work of Argonne's scientists to help 
on a global scale.
  That has always been Dr. Kearns' way; as director, his instinct was 
to bring together the best people to face the biggest challenges. 
Throughout his career, Dr. Kearns recognized that America's scientific 
leadership depends not only on individual breakthroughs, but on the 
strength of the research enterprise that makes them possible. He 
championed collaboration among national laboratories, universities, 
Federal Agencies, industry, and even Members of Congress. With Dr. 
Kearns and his staff's guidance, I worked to restart the Senate 
National Labs Caucus, adding my colleagues Senator Marsha Blackburn of 
Tennessee and Senator Ben Ray Lujan of New Mexico as new cochairs. 
Together, we have grown support for the whole National Labs system. 
What is more, Dr. Kearns' service as chair of the National Laboratory 
Directors Council extended his leadership across the entire Department 
of Energy research system, where he fostered partnerships and healthy 
competition between all 17 of the labs.
  Equally important has been Dr. Kearns' commitment to the people and 
operations behind Argonne's scientific achievements. Dr. Kearns 
recognized that the laboratory's greatest strength lies in its people. 
Having served as the laboratory's chief operations officer before 
becoming director, he brought a strong appreciation for operational 
excellence, safety, and the responsibility to be a careful steward of 
taxpayer dollars. He fostered a culture of shared purpose and respect, 
recognizing the contributions of researchers, engineers, technicians, 
and professional staff alike.
  Dr. Kearns' legacy extends beyond any single scientific discovery, 
facility, or technological advancement. His impact lies in helping 
shape a more capable, connected, and forward-looking laboratory, united 
by a commitment to scientific excellence and public service. Thanks to 
his leadership, Argonne's future is bright. The lab is positioned 
better than ever to strengthen America's technological leadership and 
translate the promise of scientific discovery into lasting benefits for 
all.
  I ask my colleagues to join me in recognizing Dr. Paul K. Kearns for 
his distinguished leadership and decades of service to the Department 
of Energy's National Laboratory System. His contributions have 
strengthened Argonne

National Laboratory, benefited the State of Illinois, and advanced our 
Nation's enduring leadership in science and innovation.
  I extend my sincere congratulations, thanks, and best wishes to Dr. 
Kearns, his wife Lynn, their three children, and their five 
grandchildren upon his retirement.
```
