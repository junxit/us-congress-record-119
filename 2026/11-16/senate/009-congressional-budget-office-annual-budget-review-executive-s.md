# Congressional Budget Office Annual Budget Review (Executive Session)

**Speaking:** Grassley, Chuck (G000386) [R-IA], Thune, John (T000250) [R-SD]

```
            Congressional Budget Office Annual Budget Review

  Mr. GRASSLEY. Madam President, last week, the Congressional Budget 
Office released its annual budget review. So, of course, that is for 
fiscal 2026 year, and it paints a very bleak picture for our Nation.
  The 2026 deficit came in at a stunning $2 trillion. That represents 
6.2 percent of the Nation's gross domestic product.
  Now, let's put this in a historic context. Over the six decades 
spanning since the end of World War II, the deficit never once exceeded 
6 percent of GDP. Yet over the past 7 years, annual deficits have 
exceeded 6 percent of GDP five times.
  In other words, deficits of a size that were unheard of for most of 
the Nation's post-war World War II history seem to be commonplace 
today, and that is a sad situation for our Nation to be in.
  Such large and persistent deficits also affect the average Americans. 
It makes life less affordable. They have fanned inflation, driving up 
the cost of living.
  Persistent large deficits have also pushed up interest rates, raising 
borrowing costs for home mortgages, auto loans, business loans, and 
almost any loan that you want to talk about.
  Higher interest rates have also caused the cost of servicing the 
Nation's $40 trillion national debt to soar to unprecedented levels. In 
2026, interest rates on the national debt totaled $1.14 trillion, up 11 
percent over last year. Interest costs alone now exceed spending on the 
Department of national defense by $225 billion and even exceed Medicare 
expenses by $73 billion.
  Soaring interest payments mean fewer dollars are available to fund 
Federal programs and leave the Nation ill-equipped to respond to the 
next recession or national emergency. And then it affects our national 
defense as well.
  That is why former Joint Chiefs of Staff Chairman Admiral Michael 
Mullen famously declared that ``debt is the single biggest threat to 
[our] national security.''

[[Page S5672]]

  The fiscal year 2026 budget review reinforces for all of us what the 
CBO has told Congress repeatedly. The Nation is on an unsustainable 
fiscal path. The sooner we work together to right the fiscal ship the 
better our Nation will be.
  I yield the floor.
  I suggest the absence of a quorum.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The clerk will call the roll.
  The senior assistant bill clerk proceeded to call the roll.
  Mr. THUNE. Madam President, I ask unanimous consent that the order 
for the quorum call be rescinded.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Without objection, it is so ordered.
```
