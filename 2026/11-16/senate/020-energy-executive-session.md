# Energy (Executive Session)

**Speaking:** Markey, Edward J. (M000133) [D-MA], Wicker, Roger F. (W000437) [R-MS]

```
                                 Energy

  Mr. MARKEY. Madam President, I rise today to talk about the energy 
affordability crisis in America and the false solutions that this 
permitting bill that we are going to be considering on the floor of the 
Senate will be presenting as real solutions when ultimately they are 
not.
  Families are struggling. Electricity bills are just too high. Data 
centers are gobbling up our land, gobbling up our electricity, gobbling 
up our water. They are pumping pollution into the air. Our water 
supplies are under major stress from contamination from forever 
chemicals known as PFAS and also from droughts and the Trump 
administration's attacks on protections for our wetlands and for our 
creeks.
  And climate change is causing more and more harm to our communities 
with the super El Nino throwing weather, temperatures, and actual 
economies into chaos. Families want relief, and they want to have 
control over their communities and their lives.
  This permitting bill is getting billed as the solution. Cut the costs 
and time it takes to get stuff through the Federal permitting process, 
which they promise will create trickle-down benefits for Americans in a 
more resilient energy grid, lower prices, and, hopefully, more clean 
energy that gets built instead of polluting fossil fuels.
  And they are going to make it easier for developers of all stripes to 
build facilities of all kinds, from transmission wires carrying clean 
electricity to data centers doing computation for the latest artificial 
intelligence models.
  We are going to make it easier to build petrochemical plants next to 
your kid's school, make it easier to build a pipeline through your 
apple orchards, make it easier to build a new copper mine on your 
fishing grounds.
  Part of how this bill makes it easier to do all of those things is by 
making it harder for you, for your community, to do anything about it, 
now or forever.
  Let's talk about data centers. We just had a big election on data 
centers. There are 17 data centers getting proposed right now for 
construction on our public lands, covering more than 17,000 acres of 
public lands across six Western States. Federal environmental laws like 
the National Environmental Protection Act help us understand how 
development will affect our air, our water, and species that could be 
endangered as well as other users of public lands.
  But under this bill, if the Federal Government phones in the review 
process, say it doesn't consider how a data center could hurt the 
environment, communities wouldn't have any ability to stop the AI 
projects. At best, they could sue and get the courts to send the data 
center's permit back to the government for some edits, but they 
couldn't get the courts to throw out the permit entirely, no matter how 
many corners were cut or how much harm the project will do.
  This bill eliminates the ability to vacate permits under the National 
Environmental Policy Act and the National Historic Preservation Act. If 
those laws are violated by a project, that project will still go 
forward. You are just out of luck if this bill becomes the law.
  And under this bill, any data center that is less than two acres gets 
considered under a general permit, not a site-specific permit. This is 
a rubberstamp without any proper review.
  It doesn't matter the specific conditions of a community. It doesn't 
matter whether kids go to school near the diesel generators or if the 
community is already parched for water. The Trump administration has 
already rolled out the red carpet for data centers in communities 
across our country. They are exempting Big Tech from our clean air 
laws. They are exempting Big Tech from our clean water laws.
  Trump is giving Big Tech a big blank check for polluting. This bill 
would take many of Trump's Executive actions and write them into law, 
make them the law of our land, what he is doing. A new President 
wouldn't be able to bring sanity back. If the Democrats won in 2028, 
they wouldn't be able to bring the sanity back.
  A better Executive branch wouldn't be able to restore community power 
because this would become the law. This wouldn't just be an Executive 
order, this would become the law, promulgated by the U.S. Senate.
  We don't have to guess what data center companies will do to get 
their hands on our land and on our electricity. By the way, electricity 
rates, which are skyrocketing, they are already doing it right now.
  Entergy is trying to seize foreign tracks of land in Louisiana 
through

[[Page S5677]]

eminent domain so they can build transmission lines for Meta's, for 
Facebook's hyperscale data center. They are saying these land seizures 
are in the public interest. A data center developer in Pennsylvania is 
promising $10,000 to every family in town if they let them build a data 
center on 1,300 acres of their land. Families are still saying no, 
turning down $10,000 to protect their neighbors and environment from 
harm. That takes courage. That is happening all across our country.

  That is what happened on election day. It was just 2 weeks ago. We 
just had a referendum on this issue. These communities are saying they 
cannot be bought, but if we pass this bill, Congress is going to ensure 
that those actions--that election that we just had--are not going to be 
heard on the floor of this body because this Congress is going to be 
selling them out.
  The permitting bill sells out those communities for the promise of 
electricity bills that might go down by between $4 to $6 a month in 
2040. Can I say that again? Your bills don't go down until 2040, and it 
is only by $4 a month. Is that really worth it?
  We have pages out here right now who are 16 years old. They will be 
30 years old before they see the benefit of this bill. That is not what 
this election was about. They deserve to get the response right now. 
That is what we should be considering on the floor.
  Fifteen years from now, you might as well--$6 a month on your 
electricity bill right now. In exchange you lose your land, you lose 
your water, you lose your air, you lose your voice.
  Data centers are at the forefront of Americans' minds when we talk 
about development because they are getting planned in front yards 
across our country.
  We are projected to spend more than $10 trillion on data centers and 
AI infrastructure in the next 8 years. That is a higher percentage of 
our gross domestic product than we have spent on any kind of 
infrastructure in our history, including the expansion of our railroads 
in the late 1800s.
  Let me say that again. We are projected as a country to spend more 
than $10 trillion on data centers and AI infrastructure over the next 8 
years, which is higher than any other expenditure in the history of our 
Nation.
  We should be debating that right now on the floor of the Senate. Our 
country is already reckoning with the projected impacts of that massive 
investment.
  This bill doesn't just roll out the red carpet for data center 
developers, it puts them on a greased-up slide into towns across 
America. It shields them from any obligations to build and operate with 
public health or welfare in mind.
  Now, it is true that this bill would make it possible to have data 
centers pay more of their fair share and prevent them from jacking up 
electricity costs for ratepayers. That is a policy that I strongly 
support.
  This bill also includes language that would establish a data center 
community assistance program to give communities more resources to 
understand and address costs of development.
  And, fundamentally, the ability to power the data centers that are 
getting built with renewables rather than behind the metered gas plants 
would be a far better policy for the climate than the alternative, and 
the ability to build new transmission is one component of that 
solution.
  But those provisions only speak to part of the concerns that people 
are fairly raising as Big Tech and other big developers use their money 
and their political influence to run roughshod over their communities.
  Nearly every Republican in the Senate has voted for this bill so far. 
Democrats are being asked to accept massive rollbacks of Federal 
environmental protections--the National Environmental Policy Act, the 
1970 law which is the constitution for the environment in our country. 
It is the National Environmental Policy Act. The Clean Water Act would 
be rolled back. The Endangered Species Act would be rolled back.
  There would be attacks on Tribal sovereignty in exchange for 
beneficial transmission and modest data center pricing policies.
  With those rollbacks, States, Tribes, and communities are left 
without the levers they need to pause or halt an unwanted data center 
or gas plant. The administration could proceed with plans to build 
hyperscale data centers on our Federal public lands without the 
opportunity for meaningful environmental review or public intervention 
through the courts.
  And the bill is still a boon for the dirty energy that Big Tech is 
doubling down on to power their data centers. The number of proposed 
gas plants to power data centers doubled to 189,000 megawatts in the 
first of this year alone. How much is 189,000 megawatts? Think of one 
big nuclear power plant. Then think of 189 nuclear power plants. That 
is from the first of this year alone.
  Environmental law rollbacks in this bill would fast-track projects 
like the Portsmouth Powered Land Project, a 9,000-megawatt gas plant 
that would power a data center as the largest gas plant operating in 
the world and the country's largest source of power-sector pollution.
  And it enables the construction of the gas pipelines that can get 
these data centers with the fossil fuel they demand.
  We may see some benefits for clean energy from transmission build-out 
several years from now, but the devastation wreaked from massive 
natural gas build-out for data centers will be felt immediately.
  Under this bill, Big Tech gets the bill cheaper, faster, and without 
consequence. And communities will be forced to witness the degradation 
of their land and suffer from more cancer-causing pollution in exchange 
for the promise of marginal savings.
  In addition to the $5-a-month savings in 15 years, even modelers 
supporting this bill have found with modest to nonexistent climate and 
affordability benefits for our country. We are in a climate crisis 
right now. It is getting hotter and hotter and hotter. The storms are 
worse. The flooding is worse. The fires are more intense. It is 
happening all around the world. The United States is the leader.
  This bill, what does it say? It says, well, in a best-case scenario, 
this bill would bring economywide emissions down by 3.8 percent by 2040 
compared to business as usual.
  In a worst-case scenario under this bill, it won't do anything at 
all. The bill won't do anything about climate change. It won't do 
anything to ensure that the climate forecasts that have been made will 
not just lead us continually on the glide path to a climate crisis--
when these pages are 30 years old, a climate crisis. This bill doesn't 
do anything for them in 2040.
  An earlier Rhodium--a private company--Rhodium model of this bill 
found it could even increase economywide emissions by 1 percent--
increase them. That is not what young people want. Young people want 
solutions. Young people want solutions to the climate crisis. They want 
solutions to high-price energy. That is what they want.
  And none of these models factor in the climate bomb represented by 
supercharged natural gas exports, buying into the lie--that climate 
action only matters within our own national borders.
  If we start exporting--which we are already doing--massive amounts of 
natural gas off of our own soil and sending it overseas, that pollution 
that goes up is still making the planet warmer. It is being captured, 
and the planet will get warmer and warmer and warmer. That is not 
factored into this bill. That is not factored in.
  I am a wholehearted backer of clean energy, and I believe that solar 
and wind and storage--especially offshore wind--are the keys to an 
independent, affordable, and reliable energy future.
  The Trump administration has broken our energy system. Trump and his 
Cabinet have blocked 400 onshore wind projects and spent $4 billion to 
buy out and cancel 21,000 megawatts of offshore wind that would have 
brought a staggering amount of clean energy to our entire eastern 
seaboard. They have abused executive authorities to keep coal plants 
running, costing ratepayers more than $300 million in just 1 year.
  Trump is attacking clean energy jobs and clean energy projects. So 
this permitting bill, will it bring clean energy back? Well, let's 
look. Let's look at the supporters of this bill and their models 
because their models, unfortunately, when you look at it, the answer is 
not really.
  According to Rhodium, in the conservative case estimation, this bill

[[Page S5678]]

would get an additional 17,000 megawatts of clean energy built over the 
next 15 years.
  So for anyone that is listening, what is a megawatt? What is 1,000 
megawatts? Last year, we deployed 50,000 megawatts of solar in the 
United States--50,000 new megawatts. This bill says you get 1,000 more 
per year. But the price? The National Environmental Policy Act, Clean 
Water Act, Endangered Species Act, and we don't get the tax breaks for 
wind and solar back that Trump took off the books last year. Those tax 
breaks for wind and solar were supercharging the deployment of clean 
energy in our Nation. We don't get them back. We don't get those tax 
breaks back as part of this deal.
  And, honestly, if you think about it, that 1,000 megawatts a year--
because under current conditions, we are projected to build 70,000 
megawatts of wind and solar in storage just next year. So this bill 
would represent a potential 1.4 percent increase in clean energy 
projects.

  We make a lot of sacrifices to get that very small benefit, and the 
natural gas industry is just clinking their champagne glasses, just 
saying: This is great for us. We get permanent relief. We can build 
natural gas pipelines, polluting fossil fuel pipelines forever with no 
restrictions upon us. Wow, that is a good deal.
  So here is the permitting bill pitch: 15 years from now, we might 
save you the cost of a cup of coffee a month in your electricity bills. 
We might increase clean energy growth by a little over 1 percent per 
year. We might see no change in our climate emissions. And that is what 
the bill's supporters are saying.
  Those with concerns have a lot more to add. Tribes have been 
rightfully outraged by the attack on their sovereignty through the 
rollback of the National Historic Preservation Act. The National 
Association of Tribal Historic Preservation Officers have called this 
bill `` . . . an assimilation-era tactic of telling us how and we can 
participate, what matters to our cultures, and [what] harms we [should] 
accept.''
  I believe our Tribal leaders when they say this is not something that 
Tribal nations should have to bear. Yes, we might want transmission 
reform, but are we going to say to the Tribes all across our country: I 
am sorry. You have no rights anymore to stop them from coming through 
your sacred, historic Tribal areas?
  People working every day in our States to safeguard our water and 
everyone who depends on it have called out the massive rollbacks in the 
Clean Water Act protections. Eight State environmental departments, 
including Massachusetts, have stated that the bill ``significantly 
undermines State authority . . . crippling our ability--[crippling our 
ability]--to protect clean water in our States.''
  That means less protection against PFAS, against flooding, against 
pollution, and other dirty water threats.
  And the provisions that are meant to protect clean energy projects 
from Trump's blockade actually give him massive new powers to permit 
his favored fossil fuel projects and prevent future administrations 
from doing anything about it.
  This creates more opportunity for Trump to push offshore drilling in 
California--offshore drilling for oil, for natural gas--in places that 
don't want it or mining in the Boundary Waters or major new fossil gas 
export terminals in the next 2 years.
  And a future administration would not be able to do anything about it 
that in any way would affect ``the construction of operation at normal 
capacity of a project that has gotten its permits,'' meaning it is 
permitted. If the Democrats win the Presidency in 2028, that new 
President is not going to be able to affect this law. They are just 
going to continue to rumble right through areas of our country that 
otherwise would have had environmental protections.
  At best, this creates a massive amount of legal uncertainty as to 
what the Federal Government can and can't do, as well as whether 
projects like offshore wind farms have any recourse against Trump's 
vindictiveness.
  At worst, it gives Trump and his other administration officials 
unfettered power to shove projects down the throats of States and 
communities who do not want those projects.
  Last week, you might have heard Trump was asked in a press 
conference: If this bill passes, are you going to allow for offshore 
wind to be constructed, which you have been blocking?
  And he says: No, I won't. I don't like wind. I am not going to allow 
it to happen.
  Well, that is the plan we have in Maine, in Massachusetts, in New 
Jersey, in New York, in Virginia, in Delaware, in Maryland. That is our 
plan: offshore wind. He is not saying we can do it. And the natural gas 
industry is saying: We will do it for you. We will build pipelines for 
fossil fuels into your States in order to produce the electricity which 
you need.
  So if Trump is saying no in public, just last week, why don't we just 
take him at his word? Like Maya Angelou would say: When somebody tells 
you who they are, you should believe them.
  He is telling us he will not allow it to happen. So where is the 
bargain for us that want a clean energy future? You know, otherwise, we 
are just going to have States that want to go clean energy just have 
Trump's vindictiveness there for the next 2 years with no guarantee, by 
the way, that there is ever going to be a Democratic President after 
2028. You get no guarantees about the future.
  This law, however, will be there as a guarantee. It will be something 
that the new President will have to live with. And, at worst, it is 
going to give Trump and all of the rest of his fossil fuel Cabinet--
make that fossil fuel cartel that is disguised as a Cabinet--the 
ability to shove projects down the throats of States and communities 
who don't want them, who don't want those fossil fuel projects.
  So I believe we can find solutions to building clean power without 
taking power away from our Tribes, from our States, from our 
scientists. Tribes and our State clean water agencies aren't 
obstructions to progress; they are partners in progress. That is what 
we have constructed over the last 60 years in our country--a 
partnership with environmentalists and energy companies.
  And we last year produced 50,000 new megawatts of solar; that wasn't 
on the scoreboard just 5 years before. Ten thousand new megawatts of 
wind; that wasn't on the scoreboard. Ten thousand new megawatts of 
battery storage technology; that wasn't on the scoreboard. That is part 
of the new plan we put in place that Trump last year had all the 
Republicans vote to kill.
  Our progress will come from working with everyone to create those 
solutions--not by creating new enemies by establishing generational 
winners and losers with this permitting bill.
  There are good provisions in this bill, but they are buried beneath 
unnecessary attacks on communities and dangerous new powers for 
President Trump and his agenda.
  Don't permit Donald Trump to supercharge his crusade against our 
health and our bottom line. Don't permit data centers to get built 
under the cover of darkness. Don't permit this attack on Tribal 
sovereignty. Don't permit PFAS and pollution in our water. Don't permit 
this permitting bill to pass.
  Americans demanded better from us when they voted for change 2 weeks 
ago. They deserve better. This bill is not a bill for the future. It is 
a bill negotiated under the existing political structure in this 
institution.
  The American people voted for a new era, and that begins in January 
of 2027. We should be negotiating under those circumstances.
  And we can produce legislation. We can make sure that everyone gets 
what they need. But we can't allow for legislation to pass which locks 
us into an era which does not dramatically increase the amount of clean 
energy, which does not reduce emissions, and which allows for these 
companies to continue to build their pipelines, filled with fossil 
fuels, into areas that otherwise would have had wind and solar and 
battery storage technology producing the energy those regions needed.
  That is why I urge a ``no'' vote on this legislation. We can and we 
must do better.
  I yield the floor.
```
