# Ukraine (Executive Session)

**Speaking:** Wicker, Roger F. (W000437) [R-MS]

```
                                Ukraine

  Mr. WICKER. Madam President, having asked for that on behalf of the 
leadership, I now want to discuss with my colleagues the situation 
going on in Ukraine and specifically Vladimir Putin's illegal war on 
Ukraine and the Ukrainian people.
  I am wearing on my lapel, as I do most days, the U.S. flag and the 
Ukrainian flag to demonstrate my support and to emphasize to people who 
see me my view that it is in the best interest of America and of the 
American people for the United States and for this Senate and this 
Congress to support the effort in Ukraine to defend themselves and 
their people against Russia's aggression.
  Listen to these words:

       After getting to know and fully understand the Ukraine/
     Russia Military and Economic situation and, after seeing the 
     Economic trouble it is causing Russia, I think Ukraine, with 
     the support of the European Union, is in a position to fight 
     and WIN all of Ukraine back in its original form.

  Those are not my words; those are the words Donald J. Trump made a 
little over a year ago--precisely, on September 23, 2025. Those are the 
words of our Commander in Chief just a year ago or so.
  Let me read them again, along with all of President Trump's full 
statement:

       After getting to know and fully understand the Ukraine/
     Russia Military and Economic situation and, after seeing the 
     Economic trouble it is causing Russia, I think Ukraine, with 
     the support of the European Union, is in a position to fight 
     and WIN all of Ukraine back in its original form.

  I applaud the President and that remark.
  Continuing to quote from our Commander in Chief, Donald J. Trump:

       With time, patience, and the financial support of Europe 
     and, in particular, NATO, the original Borders from where 
     this War started, is very much an option. Why not? Russia has 
     been fighting aimlessly for three and a half years a War that 
     should have taken a Real Military Power less than a week to 
     win.

  These are the words of our Commander in Chief.
  I continue to quote him:

       This is not distinguishing Russia. In fact, it is very much 
     making them look like ``a paper tiger.'' When the people 
     living in Moscow, and all of the Great Cities, Towns, and 
     Districts all throughout Russia, find out what is really 
     going on with this War, the fact that it's almost impossible 
     for them to get Gasoline through the long lines that are 
     being formed, and all of the other things that are taking 
     place in their War Economy, where most of their money is 
     being spent on fighting Ukraine, which has Great Spirit, and 
     only getting better, Ukraine would be able to take back their 
     Country in its original form and, who knows, maybe even go 
     further than that!

  Our President said that a year ago.
  Continuing on with quoting our President:

       Putin and Russia are in BIG Economic trouble, and this is 
     the time for Ukraine to act.

  Further to quote our President:

       In any event, I wish both Countries well. We will continue 
     to supply weapons to NATO for NATO to do what they want with 
     them. Good luck to all!

  Well, let me say this: I do wish the people of both countries well. I 
have nothing but admiration for the Ukrainian people. After having 
visited them, as many Senators have over time, my admiration for them 
and what they are going through and the resolve they are showing is a 
matter of--something to admire.
  I have nothing but heartache and affection for the people of Russia. 
They are suffering under a war criminal, under a dictator who should 
not be entertained and feted in the palaces of this world and of 
democratic countries but should be in jail as a war criminal. But I do 
have affection for the Russian people, and they deserve better than 
what they are getting from their President.
  Russia's position has continued to deteriorate. In the year since 
President Trump made these remarks--the statement that our Commander in 
Chief made 13 months, 14 months ago is even truer today. Putin's 
continued losses vindicate President Trump's words. Russia has not 
distinguished itself; on the contrary, it has shamed its leadership.
  Putin is likely the most inept military leader of our century. 
Vladimir Putin is perhaps the most inept military leader of our 
century. Consider Russia's dismal rate of territorial gains. With all 
their power, last year, Russia gained 1,874 square miles of territory 
or about 0.8 percent of Ukraine's territory. That is not impressive. 
But it gets worse for Vladimir Putin, the war criminal and dictator. 
This year, Russia has managed a mere 46 square miles of territory 
gained in Ukraine. That is 0.02 percent of Ukrainian territory. What an 
accomplishment for this vast military power. It is less than half the 
land area of my State's capital city, Jackson.

  This summer, Ukraine's heroic Vivaldi counteroffensive blocked 
Russia's attempt to breach the ``fortress belt.'' This is the line of 
cities in Donetsk that are key to the defense of eastern Ukraine, and 
it was a key target of Vladimir Putin's war aims.
  As the President said, a real military power should have succeeded 
easily in this campaign. Instead, Putin has managed only 46 square 
miles since the start of this year.
  The trend is getting even worse. In September and October, Russia 
actually lost ground--the tide is turning--with a net loss of 31 square 
miles in September and a net loss of 10 square miles in October. The 
Ukrainians are pushing the Russians back.
  Both these gradual battlefield losses and meager battlefield gains 
have come at a terrible, tragic, growing cost for Russia's frontline 
troops. And the tragedy is for average Russian families, for young 
Russian men and women who have suffered at the lust for power and 
territory of their dictator.
  In October 2026, Russian forces experienced their highest monthly 
casualties of the entire war. It was the fourth month in a row that 
Russia exceeded 40,000 casualties in 1 month. Now, a casualty is more 
than an injury; a casualty is either a fatality or a war wound that 
takes the troop out of action. Those are casualties--there have been 
more injuries--40,000 in 1 month.
  In the past year, Vladimir Putin passed a grim milestone, and this is 
the truth: 1.5 million Russian casualties in his war--a tragedy for 
Russian moms and dads; an incredible, tragic, needless number. To put 
this in context, Russia's population is less than half ours--340 
million for the United States; 143 million only for Russia. That is 
like having more than 3 million American casualties. That is the rate.
  Can you imagine what a democratic representative republic would do to 
a President who had resulted gratuitously in that level of casualties?
  At some points this year, Russia's casualty rate exceeded the 
recruitment rate. They are losing more boys and girls than they are 
actually able to bring into the military. It is a shame on the 
leadership, the dictatorship of Russia. It is no wonder he has had to 
turn to bribing and tricking foreigners to replenish the ranks of his 
army.
  But Putin's troubles have been further compounded by Ukraine's own 
long-range missile campaign. Consider

[[Page S5676]]

Ukraine's effective strikes on Putin's energy supplies. I am glad they 
finally started. Yes, ma'am, I am glad they finally started. Russian 
energy sales fund Putin's war machine. It is Russia's energy sales that 
fund the deaths of Ukrainian boys and girls and 1.5 million casualties 
on Russia itself--Putin's energy supplies. Russian energy sales fund 
Putin's war machine. But Ukraine has managed to put 30 percent of 
Russia's oil refining capacity offline. Good for them. I hope Ukraine 
puts more of Russia's oil refining capacity offline.
  For years, Putin's terror campaign has felt distant to residents of 
Russia's major cities--St. Petersburg, Moscow. Ukraine's effective 
counter has made the war real even to them, to the sophisticated people 
of western Russia. They see the smoke plumes. They see the way Putin 
has to ration oil. They certainly do not appreciate sitting in long 
lines to fill up their vehicles.
  Russia's situation continues to deteriorate. Putin has reinforced the 
truth of President Trump's words, which I just quoted verbatim from the 
President. Putin and Russia are in big economic trouble, and I say 
bravo to that.
  I agreed with the President in September of last year, and I still 
agree with those words. President Trump has said that with time, 
patience, and assistance, Ukraine can win. I believe that. We and our 
European partners can give Ukraine all three.
  We will soon see the end of 5 years of this war--incredible. Within 
that time, Ukraine has proven steadfast and Russia has proven weak and 
unable to achieve its battlefield goals.
  Putin has made it clear that he, not Ukraine, is the obstacle to 
peace. For years, President Zelenskyy has agreed to cease-fires. He 
would agree to a cease-fire in place today, I firmly believe. Putin 
doesn't want peace. Putin wants the Donbas. Putin wants Crimea. But he 
can't have it. He is not entitled to it under international law. And he 
can't even get the Donbas after 4--almost 5--years of war.
  Instead of rushing into a bad deal, our President and Ukraine have 
been patient, and Ukraine has stewarded its patience well. They have 
capitalized on Putin's incompetence by taking the war to the Kremlin, 
and bravo for them.
  Finally, with assistance, Ukraine has been wearing Russia down. After 
nearly 5 years of war, it is clear that the only thing that will change 
Putin's calculations is strength.
  President Trump asked in his statement, which I quoted, ``Why not?'' 
Why can't Ukraine win back its territory? It is a good question. It is 
a result devoutly to be wished, and it is a prospect that stirs the 
heart of anyone who loves freedom and hates tyranny.
  Russia continues to send missiles that rain death on Ukrainian women 
and children. We ought to help Ukraine defend against those missiles. 
We and our European allies should increase sanctions on Russian energy 
and crack down on that shadow fleet--continue to do that. We should do 
what we can to expedite Ukraine's growing defense industry. And our 
European allies must continue doing so as well, and I applaud the 
President for making that a key part of his program and his success in 
this regard. Yes, our European allies are doing more, as they should. 
Together, we can increase the pressure on Putin and bring a faster, 
more just end to this war.

  The President wants peace. I want peace. And no one wants peace more 
than the Ukrainian people. Putin, the war criminal, wants territory. He 
wants conquest. As the President said, let's put Ukraine in a position 
to fight and win all of Ukraine back in its original form.
  And so I come back to how I started. President Trump's words last 
year are true. They are still absolutely true. The world will be safer 
and Americans will be safer if we help Ukraine do just that--win their 
victory.
  I yield the floor.
  (Mrs. BRITT assumed the Chair.)
  The PRESIDING OFFICER (Mrs. Hyde-Smith). The Senator from 
Massachusetts.
```
