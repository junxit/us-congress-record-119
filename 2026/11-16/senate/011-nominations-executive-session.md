# Nominations (Executive Session)

**Speaking:** Thune, John (T000250) [R-SD], Durbin, Richard J. (D000563) [D-IL]

```
                              Nominations

  Mr. THUNE. Madam President, on Thursday, the Senate confirmed Gregory 
Cook to be U.S. district court judge for the Northern District of 
Alabama. Judge Cook is the 57th Federal judge confirmed during this 
Congress, and later today, we will confirm our 58th.
  There will be more to come. Thanks to the diligent work of Chairman 
Grassley and the Judiciary Committee, we have a number of additional 
judges to confirm before the year is out, and we have already locked in 
floor votes for every available judicial nominee. In fact, if you look 
at this chart and the number that have been confirmed, the noms that 
are teed up, which we will process here shortly, there are zero 
remaining on the calendar. In other words, they have been recommended 
by the administration and gone through the process of the Senate 
Judiciary Committee, where they have been reported out to the floor for 
consideration. So 57 confirmed already and 6 that are teed up that we 
will be confirming here shortly. Then, hopefully, we will have more 
that we will be able to put on the calendar soon as the administration 
makes recommendations and the Judiciary Committee is able to process 
those.
  So we have locked votes in for every available judicial nominee. 
Every available judicial nominee has been locked in right now on the 
floor to be voted on.
  We will also confirm more than 100 additional Presidential nominees 
before the end of the year. Once again, our committees have done 
excellent work processing these nominations.
  Since this majority took over last January, we have confirmed over 
606 civilian--I should say we have confirmed 606 civilian nominees. 
That includes getting the President's Cabinet in place at the fastest 
pace in over 20 years, in half the time--half the time--it took to fill 
the Obama and first Trump administration Cabinets.
  We have consistently cleared the calendar of available nominees 
throughout our majority. As I said, we will confirm our 58th judicial 
nominee today.
  I will note that when it comes to judges, we are turning nominations 
into confirmations at the fastest pace since the Reagan administration. 
When this majority took over nearly 2 years ago, building this kind of 
record on nominations didn't look very likely. It was clear from the 
outset the Democrats were intent on a historic level of obstruction. 
They dragged out the process for virtually every nominee to come before 
this body. It took a year and a half before Democrats finally let a few 
nominees go by voice vote--something no other President has had to deal 
with. In fact, if you look at recent history, Presidents have had the 
vast majority of their nominees approved by voice vote or unanimous 
consent because historically, this institution has cooperated to try to 
make sure the administration has their people in place.
  If you look throughout history, 98 percent for Presidents George H. 
W. Bush and Clinton--98 percent of the nominations they made for 
positions in the executive branch of our government were confirmed 
either by voice vote or unanimous consent here on the floor of the 
Senate--98 percent; 90 percent for Presidents George W. Bush and Barack 
Obama.
  Well, then came President Trump's first administration, when 
Democrats introduced an unprecedented level of partisanship to the 
confirmation process, forcing time-consuming votes on nominees who 
would normally have gone by unanimous consent or voice.
  But even with this new level of partisanship, President Trump, in his 
first term, and President Biden each had more than half of their 
nominees approved by unanimous consent or voice vote here in the U.S. 
Senate.
  Well, then President Trump started his second term, and Democrats 
took it up a notch--quite a few notches, actually. As I said, until 
earlier this year, Democrats had not allowed a single--single--nominee 
to be confirmed by voice or unanimous consent. They also dragged out 
the process of nominees who went on to get bipartisan support in their 
confirmation vote, sometimes by large margins here on the floor.
  So, let's not mistake Democrats' obstruction for some sort of 
principled stand. This was partisan politics.
  The Republicans were determined to overcome Democrats' obstruction 
and ensure that the President got his nominees in place. That included 
taking action to restore the Senate's practice of confirming 
noncontroversial nominees in batches, the way things have traditionally 
been done around here. That has allowed us to clear the nominations 
calendar on several occasions. Most of all, we have just done a lot of 
work.
  To begin with, we had the longest continuous session in 15 years 
which allowed us to confirm the Cabinet, as I said earlier, at a 
historically quick pace. And when we turned to lower-level nominees, we 
continued to put in the work in committees and on the floor. We showed 
up for the votes, lots and lots of votes.
  In fact, last year, 2025, the first year, as a lot of these nominees 
were coming to the Senate for consideration, we cast more votes in 2025 
than any Senate in history with one exception going back 80 years. Lots 
and lots of votes.
  And we did the same when it came to confirming judges to the Federal 
bench. The results speak for themselves.
  We are going to continue our aggressive pace for the remainder of 
this year because when Democrats take control of the Senate next year, 
our job is going to get a lot harder. But there is time left on the 
clock for this Republican majority, and we intend to use it.
  I yield the floor.
  Madam President, I suggest the absence of a quorum.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The clerk will call the roll.
  The senior assistant bill clerk proceeded to call the roll.
  Mr. DURBIN. Madam President, I ask unanimous consent that the order 
for the quorum call be rescinded.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Without objection, it is so ordered.
  The Senator from Illinois.
```
