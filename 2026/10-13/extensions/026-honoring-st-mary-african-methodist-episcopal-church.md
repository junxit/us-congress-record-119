# HONORING ST. MARY AFRICAN METHODIST EPISCOPAL CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
          HONORING ST. MARY AFRICAN METHODIST EPISCOPAL CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, October 13, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to acknowledge St. Mary African 
Methodist Episcopal Church in Shreveport, Louisiana, a congregation 
whose history dates back to 1911 on Harts Island. The founders formally 
organized the church in 1912 in a three-room house on Freewater Street, 
then known by the congregation as City Hopewell. From the prayer 
meeting union formed within that small home, St. Mary A.M.E. Church 
emerged, guided by a devoted group of Christians and inspired by 
visiting ministers who recognized the congregation's need for a 
permanent place of worship.
  Among those present at the church's organization were Sister Carrie 
Jones, Brother Robert Knuckles, Brother Charlie Knuckles, Brother 
George Knuckles, Sister Ella Knuckles, Brother Kunt Holt, Sister Pearl 
Holt, Brother Elder Williams, Sister Sissy Washington, Brother Gus 
Campbell, the Houston family, and the pastor, Rev. George Jones. Under 
the pastorate of Rev. Jim Brown, the congregation received 
encouragement that strengthened its determination to press forward in 
faith.
  As the church grew, the Board of Trustees purchased a site at 1654 
Rex Street and erected a frame structure for worship. Seeking a more 
suitable location, the congregation later sold that property and moved 
the church to 1603 Rex Street under the pastorate of Rev. E.D. Wilson. 
While workers relocated the structure, services were held in the old 
Stoner Hill School. Over subsequent years, St. Mary continued improving 
its building through the leadership of dedicated pastors, including 
Rev. W.L. Debro, Rev. F.D. Murray, and Rev. T.H. Terry.
  Major improvements took place under Rev. Earl A. James, who led the 
first major renovation and encouraged members to honor past family 
members by purchasing shrubbery in their memory. The church also served 
the community through operating a day care center and hosting Project 
Head Start when organizers launched the program in 1965.
  Rev. Willie B. Johnson later led the second major renovation, 
including the present interior design of St. Mary, completed in 
February 1995. Under his leadership, the church also purchased the lot 
across the street. Additional improvements were made under Rev. Robert 
Shadrack Boyd and Rev. Cynthia Harris, including demolition of the 
dilapidated parsonage.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring St. Mary 
African Methodist Episcopal Church for more than one hundred years of 
worship, service, leadership, and resilience within the Shreveport 
community. Sustained by faith and strengthened by generations of 
dedicated members, St. Mary continues its mission of glorifying God and 
doing good for Shreveport as it enters its second century of ministry.
```
