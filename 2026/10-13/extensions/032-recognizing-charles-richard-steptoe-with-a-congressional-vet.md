# RECOGNIZING CHARLES RICHARD STEPTOE WITH A CONGRESSIONAL VETERAN COMMENDATION

**Speaking:** Sessions, Pete (S000250) [R-TX]

```
   RECOGNIZING CHARLES RICHARD STEPTOE WITH A CONGRESSIONAL VETERAN 
                              COMMENDATION

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. PETE SESSIONS

                                of texas

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, October 13, 2026

  Mr. SESSIONS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Charles Richard 
Steptoe of Groveton, Texas, as a recipient of the 2026 Congressional 
Veterans Commendation, honoring a veteran whose commitment to service 
has continued throughout his life.
  Richard served honorably in the United States Air Force from 1981 to 
1985 as a Security Specialist with the 314th Security Police Squadron. 
During his military career, he earned the Air Force Good Conduct Medal, 
Air Force Overseas Long Tour Ribbon, and Air Force Training Ribbon.
  Richard's commitment to service did not end when he left the 
military. Even during his time in the Air Force, he volunteered in 
support of Special Olympics, giving his personal time to help children 
with disabilities. His efforts were formally recognized by his 
commanding officer, demonstrating that his dedication to serving others 
began while he was still wearing the uniform.
  Following his military service, Richard devoted 32 years to law 
enforcement, serving the citizens of East Texas and continuing the 
tradition of protecting and helping others. What is particularly 
deserving of recognition, however, is that Richard has continued to 
serve his community long after completing his military and law-
enforcement careers--and he has done so without seeking compensation 
for some of these important responsibilities.
  For the past eight years, Richard has served as the Justice of the 
Peace for Trinity County Precinct 4, and he has also served as the 
sitting Judge for the City of Groveton.
  These are positions that carry significant responsibility and require 
commitment to the people of the community, Yet he has continued to give 
his time and experience to his neighbors. Except for his role as 
Justice of the Peace, he volunteers his time serving as the Judge for 
the City of Groveton and Trinity County Emergency Management 
Coordinator which he has served for the last 6 years.
  His willingness to serve without compensation is a powerful 
reflection of his character and his understanding of public service. 
For Richard, service is not about what he receives in return. It is 
about what he can give to the people who depend upon him.
  As County Judge Danny R. Martin has noted, Richard's work in 
emergency management is critical to Trinity County. He works to ensure 
that timely and accurate emergency information reaches as many 
residents as possible, coordinates with surrounding communities, 
assists with emergency preparedness, and works on evacuation planning. 
He has also devoted his time to navigating administrative requirements 
and researching solutions to help Trinity County prepare for 
emergencies and natural disasters. He performs this important work as a 
volunteer.
  When a community faces a crisis, emergency preparedness can make the 
difference between confusion and an organized response. He has 
willingly taken on that responsibility, using the experience he gained 
through his military and law-enforcement careers to help protect his 
fellow citizens.
  He has also continued his service as a member of the Pennington 
Volunteer Fire Department, once again putting himself in a position to 
respond when his neighbors need assistance.
  Richard's record therefore represents something larger than a list of 
positions held. It represents a lifetime of volunteering and service.
  He served our Nation in the United States Air Force. He spent decades 
protecting citizens through law enforcement. And after those careers 
were completed, he continued to volunteer his time as a judge, 
emergency management coordinator, firefighter, and community leader. 
Importantly, much of this continued service has been performed without 
compensation.
  Richard could have chosen to step away from public responsibilities 
after completing his military and law-enforcement careers. Instead, he 
chose to continue giving back to his community. That willingness to 
serve simply because his neighbors and community need someone willing 
to step forward is precisely the spirit we seek to honor when we 
recognize America's veterans.
  Mr. Speaker, Charles Richard Steptoe has demonstrated that the 
commitment to serve does not end when the uniform comes off. For him, 
it has been a lifelong commitment. His military service, decades of 
law-enforcement service, volunteer firefighting, judicial service, and 
emergency-management work have all been guided by the same principle: a 
willingness to put the needs of others before his own.
  On behalf of the people of the 17th Congressional District of Texas, 
I extend my sincere gratitude to Charles Richard Steptoe for his 
service to our Nation and for the countless hours he has continued to 
give to the people of East Texas.
  Mr. Speaker, I am proud to recognize Charles Richard Steptoe as a 
recipient of the 2026 Congressional Veterans Commendation and to thank 
him for a lifetime of service, sacrifice, and volunteerism.
```
