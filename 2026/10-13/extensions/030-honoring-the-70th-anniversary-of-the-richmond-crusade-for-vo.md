# HONORING THE 70TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE RICHMOND CRUSADE FOR VOTERS

**Speaking:** McClellan, Jennifer L. (M001227) [D-VA]

```
    HONORING THE 70TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE RICHMOND CRUSADE FOR VOTERS

                                 ______
                                 

                       HON. JENNIFER L. McCLELLAN

                              of virginia

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, October 13, 2026

  Ms. McCLELLAN. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize the 70th 
anniversary of the Richmond Crusade for Voters, one of the oldest civil 
rights organizations in Richmond, Virginia, and to honor its seven 
decades of work to empower voters and advance justice and equality in 
our community.
  The Richmond Crusade for Voters was founded in 1956 at a pivotal 
moment in Virginia's history. Just two years earlier, the Supreme Court 
issued its landmark decision in Brown v. Board of Education, declaring 
segregated public schools unconstitutional. Yet rather than comply with 
that decision, Virginia's leaders embarked on a campaign of Massive 
Resistance designed to preserve segregation and defy the promise of 
equal protection under the law.
  Black Richmonders understood that changing those laws and changing 
the leaders who wrote and enforced them required political power. They 
understood that the right to vote was not merely a right to be 
exercised on Election Day, but a tool to hold government accountable 
and create lasting change. It was in that spirit that the Richmond 
Crusade for Voters was born.
  From its earliest days, the Crusade worked to register and educate 
voters, increase political participation, evaluate candidates for 
public office, advocate for economic opportunity and minority-owned 
businesses and ensure that the voices of Black Richmonders were heard 
in the halls of government. Importantly, the Crusade carried out that 
mission as a bipartisan organization focused not on party labels, but 
on whether candidates will advance justice, opportunity and the 
interests of the communities they seek to serve. For 70 years, that 
mission has endured.
  Generations of Crusade members have helped Richmonders understand the 
power of their vote and the responsibility each of us has to 
participate in our democracy. They have registered voters, educated our 
neighbors about the issues affecting their lives, held elected 
officials accountable and helped ensure that communities once 
deliberately excluded from the political process have a meaningful 
voice in shaping their government.
  The Commonwealth has changed dramatically since 1956. As someone who 
has spent much of my career protecting and expanding the right to vote, 
I know progress did not happen by accident. It happened because people 
organized and demanded change. It happened because generations of 
Virginians refused to accept a democracy that did not include them. It 
happened because organizations like the Richmond Crusade for Voters 
understood that the promise of our democracy can only be fulfilled when 
people exercise their power within it.
  That work remains as important today as it was 70 years ago. The 
right to vote is foundational to every other right we enjoy, and our 
democracy is strongest when every eligible voter can make their voice 
heard, every community has a seat at the table, and every elected 
official is accountable to the people they serve. I thank the Crusade 
for 70 years of service to Richmond and the Commonwealth of Virginia 
and for its unwavering commitment to voter education, civic 
participation, economic opportunity, justice and equality.
  May its legacy inspire the next generation of Richmonders to make 
their voices heard and carry this important work forward for the next 
70 years and beyond.
```
