# HONORING STONER HILL BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                  HONORING STONER HILL BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, October 13, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Stoner Hill Baptist 
Church of Shreveport, Louisiana, an enduring institution of faith, 
fellowship, worship, and community service for more than a century. 
Established in 1914, the church traces its beginnings to a small prayer 
band that gathered in the home of Sister Anna Taylor under the 
spiritual leadership of Reverend Early Jones. From those humble 
gatherings emerged a congregation that became deeply rooted in the 
historic Stoner Hill community and has ministered to generations of 
families throughout Shreveport.
  The church's origins reflect the strong tradition of faith and 
community that characterized many African American congregations in the 
early twentieth century. Without a permanent sanctuary or extensive 
resources, its earliest members gathered because of a shared commitment 
to prayer, worship, Christian fellowship, and spiritual growth. As more 
individuals and families joined, the group outgrew its original meeting 
place and relocated to Old Lindsey Hall. The growing number of members 
and baptisms showed that a small prayer gathering was becoming an 
established body of believers. The congregation then formally organized 
as Stoner Hill Baptist Church and called Reverend Early Jones as its 
first pastor, laying the spiritual and organizational foundation on 
which future generations would build.
  From its earliest years, Stoner Hill Baptist Church has been closely 
connected to the community that shares its name. As the historic Stoner 
Hill neighborhood developed, the church became an important institution 
in the lives of residents and families, serving as a center for 
fellowship, Christian education, spiritual guidance, and service. Its 
role has extended well beyond Sunday worship, and its ministries and 
outreach have sought to respond to the spiritual and practical needs of 
people in the Stoner Hill neighborhood and throughout the greater 
Shreveport community.
  Through the decades, successive generations of pastors, ministers, 
deacons, church officers, ministry leaders, musicians, educators, 
volunteers, and members have strengthened the congregation and expanded 
its ability to serve. As membership grew, the church expanded its 
ministries and facilities to meet the changing needs of children, young 
people, adults, senior citizens, and families. Christian education has 
remained central to its mission, with biblical teaching, Sunday school, 
and ministry activities helping members of all ages deepen their faith 
and preparing individuals for leadership in the church and the broader 
community. A strong commitment to evangelism has likewise carried 
forward the spirit of the believers who first gathered to share their 
faith.
  For generations of families, Stoner Hill has been far more than a 
place of worship. It has been a spiritual home where children have been 
introduced to the faith, young people have received guidance and 
encouragement, adults have grown as leaders, and senior members have 
passed their wisdom and traditions to those who follow. Families have 
gathered there during some of life's most important moments and found 
support in times of difficulty. In this way, the church has carried 
forward the tradition of congregations that strengthen neighborhoods by 
providing leadership, stability, fellowship, mentorship, and 
opportunities for community engagement.
  Today, Dr. Don DeCarlo Taylor serves as pastor of Stoner Hill Baptist 
Church, continuing the tradition of pastoral leadership that began with 
Reverend Early Jones more than a century ago. Under Dr. Taylor's 
leadership, the congregation continues to build on its historic 
foundation by advancing its ministries, strengthening Christian 
fellowship, promoting spiritual development, and serving the 
surrounding community, while honoring the sacrifices of those who 
established the church and preparing new generations for Christian 
leadership and service.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring Stoner Hill 
Baptist Church and its congregation for more than 110 years of faith, 
perseverance, and service to the people of Shreveport. What began in 
1914 as a prayer band in the home of Sister Anna Taylor stands as a 
powerful example of what a small group of committed believers can 
accomplish across generations. May Stoner Hill continue its mission of 
worship, Christian education, evangelism, fellowship, and community 
service for years to come.
```
