# RECOGNIZING MASTER SERGEANT JAMES P. HOGG, U.S. ARMY, (RET.), WITH A CONGRESSIONAL VETERAN COMMENDATION

**Speaking:** Sessions, Pete (S000250) [R-TX]

```
 RECOGNIZING MASTER SERGEANT JAMES P. HOGG, U.S. ARMY, (RET.), WITH A 
                   CONGRESSIONAL VETERAN COMMENDATION

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. PETE SESSIONS

                                of texas

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, October 13, 2026

  Mr. SESSIONS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize and honor James 
P. Hogg of Nacogdoches, Texas, as a recipient of the 2026 Congressional 
Veteran Commendation. Jim served in the United States Army for 21 
years, retiring as Special Forces Master Sergeant in 2006.
  Jim's life reflects a remarkable commitment to service--first in 
uniform, and later through decades of dedication to his fellow 
veterans, their families, and communities across Deep East Texas. After 
graduating from high school in Livingston, Texas, he volunteered to 
serve in the United States Army. Over the course of 21 years of active-
duty service, he distinguished himself as a member of an exceptionally 
select group of soldiers who served as an active-duty airborne Ranger, 
and Special Forces soldier.
  His military career included multiple combat deployments. He served 
during Operation Desert Shield and Operation Desert Storm with the 3rd 
Battalion, 505th Parachute Infantry Regiment, 82nd Airborne Division. 
He later deployed in support of Operation Enduring Freedom and 
Operation Iraqi Freedom with the 5th Special Forces Group (Airborne).
  His service was recognized with numerous awards and distinctions, 
including the Bronze Star Medal with Valor device, Meritorious Service 
Medal, six Army Commendation Medals, and twelve Army Achievement 
Medals. His military qualifications include the Combat Infantryman 
Badge, Combat Medic Badge, Expert Infantry Badge, Master Parachutist 
Badge, Special Forces Tab, Ranger Tab, Drill Sergeant Badge, British 
Jump Wings, and Bahrain Jump Wings.
  Jim's commitment to service did not end when he retired from the Army 
in 2006. From 2007 to 2009, he volunteered with the Texas State Guard, 
serving as Command Sergeant Major of the 1st Battalion, 2nd Regiment, 
Civil affairs. During that time, he and his fellow volunteers responded 
to the needs of Texans along the Gulf Coast during disaster-relief 
operations following Hurricanes Dolly, Gustav, and Ike. Jim has also 
continued his service through veteran organizations. He is a lifetime 
member of Veterans of Foreign Wars Post 3893 in Nacogdoches, where he 
served as Post Service Officer, and he is a member and former Service 
Officer of American Legion Post 86.
  Perhaps one of the most significant chapters of Jim's service came 
through his work as the Nacogdoches County Veterans Service Officer, a 
position he held from January 2020 through June 2026. In that role, Jim 
worked tirelessly to help veterans and their families navigate the 
often-complicated process of obtaining the benefits they had earned 
through their service. Through extensive research, diligence, and 
personal commitment, he helped veterans pursue VA claims and connect 
with benefits and resources available to them.
  Yet his service went far beyond the formal responsibilities of his 
position. He took veterans to medical appointments in Nacogdoches, 
Lufkin, Tyler, Longview, and Henderson. He worked with local 
organizations to help homeless veterans find shelter and coordinated 
with the Department of Veterans Affairs and local government programs 
to help secure long-term housing.
  He worked with local food-bank volunteers to help veterans and their 
families who were experiencing financial hardship obtain food. He 
connected veterans seeking employment with employers in the community. 
And when veterans were struggling with the burdens of their military 
experiences, he worked to connect them with assistance and renewed 
hope. On some occasions, he personally provided financial and material 
assistance to veterans and their families from his own resources. These 
acts of kindness were often performed quietly, without recognition or 
expectation of praise. They demonstrate a simple principle that has 
guided Jim throughout his life: when a veteran or a veteran's family 
needs help, he believes they should not be turned away.
  Even after retiring as the Nacogdoches County Veterans Service 
Officer, Mr. Hogg has continued answering the call to serve. Veterans 
he previously assisted continue to reach out to him, and veterans he 
has never met have also sought his guidance after being referred to by 
friends or family members he previously helped. His continued 
willingness to serve is a testament to the character of this veteran 
and to the values he learned growing up in Texas and reinforced 
throughout his military career. Jim served on the Deep East Texas 
Congressional Veterans Commendation Committee form 2023 to 2025.
  Mr. Speaker, James P. Hogg has spent much of his life serving 
others--first by defending our Nation in uniform, then by responding to 
Texans in times of disaster, and finally by standing beside veterans 
and their families when they needed an advocate.
  His record of military service is distinguished. His record of 
community service is extensive. But perhaps most importantly, his 
record demonstrates a lasting commitment to the men and women who have 
worn the uniform of the United States.
  It is my privilege to recognize James Hogg as a 2026 Congressional 
Veteran Commendation recipient and to thank him for his more than two 
decades of military service and his continued dedication to veterans 
and the people of Deep East Texas.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring James Hogg 
for his service, sacrifice, and enduring commitment to others. I am 
proud to recognize him today.
```
