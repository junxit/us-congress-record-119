# HONORING LITTLE UNION BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                  HONORING LITTLE UNION BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                        Friday, October 9, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Little Union Baptist 
Church of Shreveport, Louisiana, which was established in 1892 with an 
original congregation of sixteen faithful members. For more than 130 
years, the church has served as a cornerstone of faith, spiritual 
growth, community service, and social justice. Throughout its history, 
Little Union has strengthened the African American community of 
Shreveport and has given generations of families in Northwest Louisiana 
a place of worship, fellowship, leadership, and empowerment.
  The church holds a particularly important place in the history of the 
Civil Rights Movement. Under the leadership of Reverend Claude Clifford 
McLain, who served as pastor for 32 years, Little Union became a 
prominent gathering place for civil rights advocacy and community 
organizing. The church hosted mass meetings for the National 
Association for the Advancement of Colored People and the Congress of 
Racial Equality, supported voter registration initiatives, and provided 
a meeting place for individuals working to advance racial equality and 
voting rights.
  Little Union also served as a headquarters for the Southern Christian 
Leadership Conference and welcomed prominent civil rights leaders, 
including Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr. In an era when Black citizens 
were too often denied the ballot and equal treatment under the law, the 
church opened its doors to those who were organizing, planning, and 
standing up for justice. These contributions established Little Union 
Baptist Church as an important institution in the struggle for justice 
and equal opportunity throughout the region.
  The church's legacy also includes connections to influential 
individuals in American civil rights history. The father of renowned 
attorney Johnnie Cochran, Jr. served as a deacon at Little Union 
Baptist Church, further reflecting the congregation's longstanding ties 
to leadership, advocacy, and public service. In recognition of its 
contributions to the Civil Rights Movement, Little Union Baptist Church 
has been designated as a site on the Louisiana Civil Rights Trail, 
preserving its historical significance for future generations.
  Today, Little Union Baptist Church continues its mission of faith, 
service, and community engagement under the leadership of Pastor C.E. 
McLain. The congregation remains committed to Christian education, 
youth development, spiritual enrichment, and outreach initiatives that 
support individuals and families throughout the Shreveport community. 
The church is also undertaking efforts to restore and preserve its 
historic sanctuary, ensuring that its architectural heritage and its 
contributions to civil rights history remain protected.
  As one of Shreveport's historic African American congregations, 
Little Union Baptist Church continues to honor its rich legacy while 
serving as a place of hope, unity, and spiritual guidance for future 
generations. Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring 
Little Union Baptist Church and its congregation for more than 130 
years of faith, service, and the pursuit of justice. May Little Union 
continue to inspire and serve the people of Shreveport for generations 
to come.
```
