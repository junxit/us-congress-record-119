# TRIBUTE TO MS. EDITH L. FRIERSON

**Speaking:** Clyburn, James E. (C000537) [D-SC]

```
                    TRIBUTE TO MS. EDITH L. FRIERSON

                                 ______
                                 

                         HON. JAMES E. CLYBURN

                           of south carolina

                    in the house of representatives

                        Friday, October 9, 2026

  Mr. CLYBURN. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life and enduring 
legacy of Ms. Edith Lila Frierson, a pioneering educator whose 
extraordinary devotion to the children of Wadmalaw Island transformed a 
school, strengthened a community, and opened doors of opportunity for 
generations. On October 24, 2026, the Wadmalaw Island community will 
commemorate one hundred years since Ms. Frierson first arrived on the 
island to educate its children. As a historical marker is unveiled in 
her honor, we celebrate a remarkable woman whose nearly half-century of 
service continues to inspire us today.
  Ms. Frierson was born on October 2, 1908, in Charleston, South 
Carolina, to Edward and Elizabeth Frierson. She was educated at Avery 
Teacher Training High School and South Carolina State College. Armed 
with an education and a calling to serve, Ms. Frierson made her way to 
Wadmalaw Island, where she would devote her professional life to 
ensuring that its children received the education and opportunities 
they deserved.
  For forty-eight years, Ms. Frierson served as both teacher and 
principal at the former Rockville Elementary School. Her work came 
during an era when educational opportunities for African American 
children in rural South Carolina were severely limited. Under her 
leadership, the school grew from a one-room schoolhouse into a modern 
educational facility, completed in 1954 as part of South Carolina's 
Equalization School program.
  Ms. Frierson was much more than an administrator. She was a teacher, 
mentor, advocate, and stabilizing force in the lives of countless 
children and their families. Her commitment to excellence and 
opportunity helped

[[Page E1057]]

shape generations of students and strengthened the fabric of Wadmalaw 
Island. She understood that education could change the trajectory of a 
child's life and, in turn, transform an entire community. In 1974, the 
Charleston County School District recognized her extraordinary 
contributions by renaming Rockville Elementary School in her honor. 
Today, Edith L. Frierson Elementary School remains Wadmalaw Island's 
only public school and stands as a lasting testament to her service.
  Ms. Frierson passed away on February 3, 1998, but her influence did 
not end with her passing. It lives in the former students she taught, 
the families she strengthened, those who have walked through the 
schoolhouse doors bearing her name, and those who will in the future.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask that you and our colleagues join me in honoring 
Ms. Edith Lila Frierson on this historic centennial. One hundred years 
after she first arrived on Wadmalaw Island, we remember a woman of 
perseverance, dignity, and unwavering commitment to public service. The 
historical marker being unveiled in her honor will remind future 
generations that one devoted educator can transform countless lives. 
Ms. Frierson's legacy is a living testament to the power of education, 
the importance of service, and the enduring difference one person can 
make.
```
