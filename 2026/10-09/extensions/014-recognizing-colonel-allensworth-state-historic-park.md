# RECOGNIZING COLONEL ALLENSWORTH STATE HISTORIC PARK

**Speaking:** Valadao, David G. (V000129) [R-CA]

```
          RECOGNIZING COLONEL ALLENSWORTH STATE HISTORIC PARK

                                 ______
                                 

                         HON. DAVID G. VALADAO

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                        Friday, October 9, 2026

  Mr. VALADAO. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize the Colonel 
Allensworth State Historic Park on its 50th anniversary and its 
historic impact on the Central Valley.
  The namesake of the historic park, Colonel Allen Allensworth, was 
born into slavery in Kentucky in 1842. He escaped, served in the

[[Page E1049]]

U.S. Navy during the Civil War, and became an ordained Baptist 
minister. As a minister, he saw the need for African American chaplains 
in the armed services and was appointed Chaplain of the 24th Army 
Regiment, one of the Army's renowned Buffalo Soldier Units. By 1906, 
Colonel Allensworth retired as the highest-ranking Black officer in the 
U.S. Army.
  By 1908, Colonel Allensworth and four other men with a similar vision 
established the town of Allensworth in Tulare County, the first 
California community founded, financed, and governed by African 
Americans. Families throughout the country moved to Allenstown seeking 
the opportunity to own land, build schools, and govern themselves. 
Within a few years, the town grew into a thriving community with a 
school district, library, church, farms, and several other flourishing 
businesses and public buildings.
  The town of Allensworth persevered through many hardships after its 
founding. Due to the devoted work of former Allensworth resident Ed 
Pope and several other volunteers, then-Governor Reagan authorized the 
California Department of Parks and Recreation to establish the park in 
1974. Just two years later, the department approved the plans, and the 
Colonel Allensworth State Historic Park officially opened to the 
public, celebrating the preservation of California's only town founded 
and governed by African Americans. Today, visitors can still explore 
several preserved buildings and homes, allowing guests to experience 
the spirit of Allensworth firsthand. The dedicated State Park staff, 
local volunteers, descendants of original settlers, and community 
leaders have led the ongoing preservation and revitalization of the 
park, ensuring that the community can enjoy the park for years to come.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues in the House of Representatives to 
join me in celebrating 50 years of the Colonel Allensworth State 
Historic Park and recognizing all who have kept its story alive. I wish 
them continued success in the years ahead.
```
