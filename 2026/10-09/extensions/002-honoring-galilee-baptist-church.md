# HONORING GALILEE BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                    HONORING GALILEE BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                        Friday, October 9, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Galilee Baptist 
Church of Shreveport, Louisiana, a historic institution of faith, 
community service, and African American leadership. Founded in 1877 by 
formerly enslaved African Americans, Galilee Baptist Church has served 
as a place of worship, fellowship, education, leadership development, 
and community advancement for nearly 150 years.
  From its earliest days, Galilee Baptist Church provided its members 
and neighbors with a foundation for spiritual growth and collective 
progress. During a period when African Americans faced significant 
social, political, and economic barriers, the church became an 
essential institution where families could worship freely, organize, 
develop leaders, support one another, and pursue greater equality and 
opportunity.
  Throughout its history, Galilee has remained committed to addressing 
both the spiritual and practical needs of the Shreveport community. 
Generations of pastors, ministers, deacons, ministry leaders, 
volunteers, and members have strengthened the congregation through 
their faith, perseverance, service, and dedication to improving the 
quality of life for those throughout Northwest Louisiana.
  Galilee Baptist Church also holds an important place in the history 
of the Civil Rights Movement in Shreveport and across the region. 
During the struggle for voting rights, equal treatment, educational 
opportunity, economic justice, and an end to racial segregation, the 
church served as a center of faith-based civic engagement. The visits 
of Reverend Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr., in 1958 and 1962 further 
demonstrate Galilee's significance within the broader movement for 
civil and human rights.
  Another defining chapter began in 1959 with the pastoral leadership 
of Reverend Dr. E. Edward Jones, Sr. For more than five decades, 
Reverend Jones provided extraordinary spiritual, civic, and community 
leadership. His advocacy for civil rights, education, economic 
opportunity, housing, and community development reflected the historic 
role of African American churches as both houses of worship and 
institutions dedicated to social progress.
  The development of ``Galilee City'' stands as a lasting example of 
this commitment to community advancement. Through initiatives connected 
to housing, economic development, education, and community empowerment, 
Galilee demonstrated that its ministry extended beyond the walls of the 
church and included tangible efforts to strengthen individuals, 
families, and neighborhoods.
  In 2022, Galilee Baptist Church entered another important chapter 
when Reverend Craig B. Collier, Sr., was called to serve as the 
church's twelfth pastor. Under his leadership, the congregation 
continues to honor the foundation established by its founders and 
former leaders while advancing its ministry through worship, Christian 
discipleship, outreach, youth engagement, leadership development, 
community involvement, and service.
  Mr. Speaker, I invite my colleagues to join me in honoring Reverend 
Collier and the faithful congregation of Galilee Baptist Church for 
nearly 150 years of unwavering faith, courageous leadership, and 
dedicated service. Galilee's enduring legacy reflects the strength of 
generations who transformed faith into action, confronted injustice 
with courage, and helped build a stronger Shreveport community. May 
Galilee Baptist Church continue to serve as a beacon of hope, 
leadership, fellowship, and service for generations to come.
```
