# HONORING ST. MARY BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                    HONORING ST. MARY BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                        Friday, October 9, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor St. Mary Baptist 
Church of Shreveport, Louisiana, an enduring institution of Christian 
faith, worship, fellowship, education, and community service for more 
than 125 years. Organized on July 30, 1899, the church grew from the 
faith and determination of a small group of believers who wanted a 
permanent congregation where individuals and families could worship 
together, grow spiritually, and serve their community.
  St. Mary was organized through the efforts of Reverend R. Lowe, J.W. 
Wells, John Kennedy, Sister Tena Ferguson, Sister Betsy Williams, and 
Sister Erna Ferguson. Their vision came in the closing years of the 
nineteenth century, when churches played an especially important role 
in the religious, educational, social, and community life of African 
Americans throughout the South. Before there was a sanctuary, the 
earliest members gathered for prayer meetings in one another's homes, 
showing that the strength of the congregation lay not in a building but 
in the shared faith and commitment of its people.
  As participation grew, the congregation established a ``brush 
harbor'' for worship, a simple and often temporary structure that gave 
believers a place to gather before a permanent church could be built. 
The congregation later moved into a frame building, and continued 
growth ultimately led to the present church structure. From private 
homes, to the brush harbor, to a frame church, and finally to a 
permanent sanctuary, each stage reflects the sacrifice and 
accomplishment of successive generations of members.
  Throughout its history, St. Mary has made worship the foundation of 
congregational life, offering preaching, prayer, music, biblical 
teaching, and Christian fellowship. Through Sunday school, Bible 
teaching, and ministry activities, the church has equipped members to 
understand Scripture and has passed its faith, history, and traditions 
from one generation to the next. For many families, St. Mary has been 
far more than a place attended on Sunday mornings. It has been a 
spiritual home where generations have celebrated baptisms, marriages, 
anniversaries, and educational achievements, and where members have 
found comfort, prayer, and fellowship in times of illness, grief, and 
hardship.
  The church's longevity is closely tied to its pastoral leadership. 
One of the most significant chapters began in 1967 with Reverend 
Richard Hardy Jr., who served for 47 years and provided spiritual 
guidance to multiple generations of members as the congregation and 
community experienced profound social, cultural, and economic change. 
In 2014, Reverend Hardy retired and was recognized as Pastor Emeritus. 
In December 2015, Reverend Melvin R. Bates, Sr. was called as Senior 
Pastor, becoming the tenth pastor of St. Mary Baptist Church. Under his 
leadership, the church continues to emphasize spiritual growth, 
discipleship, worship, education, fellowship, and service while 
reaching individuals and families in a changing community.
  Today, St. Mary's ministries serve people at every stage of life, 
including ministries for youth, men, women, music, education, 
transportation, and community fellowship. The youth ministry helps 
prepare a new generation to carry forward the church's faith and 
traditions, while the transportation ministry shows how practical 
service can remove barriers to participation in worship. Through 
fellowship and outreach, the church builds relationships, strengthens 
families, and contributes positively to the broader Shreveport 
community. Its stated goal of ``knowing Jesus and making Him known'' 
summarizes a mission of personal spiritual growth and of carrying the 
faith into the community through service, compassion, and outreach.
  St. Mary's endurance is a tribute to the generations of pastors, 
ministers, deacons, trustees, officers, teachers, musicians, ministry 
leaders, volunteers, families, and members who have sustained it. The 
congregation has ministered through the turn of the twentieth

[[Page E1058]]

century, two world wars, the Great Depression, the Civil Rights era, 
and major transformations in Shreveport, and in every era it has 
remained a place for worship, prayer, education, fellowship, and 
service. What began with prayer meetings among a small group of 
believers has become an enduring congregation that has served 
generations of families throughout the city.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring St. Mary 
Baptist Church and its congregation for more than 125 years of faith, 
perseverance, and service to the people of Shreveport. May St. Mary 
continue its mission of knowing Jesus and making Him known for 
generations to come.
```
