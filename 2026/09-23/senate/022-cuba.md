# CUBA

**Speaking:** Welch, Peter (W000800) [D-VT]

```
                                  CUBA

  Mr. WELCH. Madam President, in the midst of all the chaos and cruelty 
inflicted by the White House on the American people and around the 
world, I suspect not many Americans are aware that we are also carrying 
out an illegal blockade against Cuba, a country the size of Tennessee 
that poses no threat to the United States, but whose government we have 
tried to depose by one means or another for more than 60 years.
  There is plenty to not like about the Cuban Government, which has 
defended a failed economic model and repressive political system for 
decades. The Cuban people deserve freedom and opportunity. But that 
does not justify the United States imposing a naval and financial 
blockade against an impoverished country that has neither attacked us 
nor threatened to attack us.
  The consequences for the Cuban people have been devastating. Today, 
they are suffering from daily electrical blackouts that have made 
everyday life a virtual nightmare. Schools, hospitals, public 
transportation, food production, trash collection, and many other life 
necessities have been brought to a practical standstill. Ironically, 
even our own embassy has been impacted to the point that they are 
unable, or unwilling, to provide the necessary support for Members of 
Congress who have a legitimate interest in observing firsthand the 
consequences of the policy of collective punishment against the Cuban 
people being carried out by our government. According to an article in 
the New York Times on September 17, 2026:

       ``Thousands of containers filled with food, medicine and 
     other goods bound for Cuba have been stranded throughout the 
     Caribbean since two shipping giants stopped deliveries to 
     avoid tough U.S. sanctions, according to diplomats, Cuban 
     officials, merchants and others involved in the imports.
       ``The containers stranded in various ports include 
     humanitarian aid, from solar panels to medication to 
     colostomy bags. Dozens of containers of aid purchased by 
     different United Nations agencies are among them.
       U.S. sanctions have also hobbled shipments by air, and 
     forced the World Health Organization to scrap planned 
     deliveries of cancer drugs.''

  Secretary of State Rubio claims that ``if Cuba is a humanitarian 
disaster, it is because their regime is a disaster, because the 
economic model they follow doesn't work.'' That heartless statement 
ignores the fact that no such humanitarian crisis existed before the 
Trump administration launched what amounts to a unilateral policy of 
economic warfare against Cuba.
  This is unconscionable, it is immoral, and it is a clear violation of 
international law. If any country were to engage in such aggression 
against the United States, we would condemn it as an act of war.
  During the past year, Cuba's authorities have announced wide-ranging 
and long overdue economic reforms. While they are aspirational goals, 
they represent the kind of transformational reforms the United States 
has been calling for as long as I can remember. But has it made any 
difference to the Trump administration? To the contrary. The 
administration has only increased sanctions on Cuba, making it even 
harder to implement structural changes to the economy. It is apparent 
that nothing will satisfy the White House short of regime change--or 
regime collapse, with the consequent risk of social upheaval and mass 
migration. President Trump confirmed as much in his speech at the U.N. 
General Assembly earlier today, when he said that Cuba ``will fall.''
  Earlier this year, I spelled out steps that I and many others believe 
both governments can and should take to address the legitimate concerns 
of the other. Among other things, on the U.S. side, those steps include 
ending the failed economic embargo and removing Cuba from the list of 
state sponsors of terrorism, which has no factual or legal basis. On 
the Cuban side, it includes economic reforms to increase foreign 
investment and unshackle the private sector, and releasing prisoners 
whose only offense is criticizing Cuba's repressive one-party system. 
Back then, neither government showed interest in taking the initiative, 
and conditions for the Cuban people, and for Americans whose own 
government has prevented them from freely traveling to Cuba, have only 
gotten worse.
  But the Cuban Government has finally recognized that its antiquated 
centralized economic system needs to change, and they are trying to 
move in that direction. They have also released some political 
prisoners, including celebrated artist Luis Manuel Otero Alcantara, 
although he was forced into exile in the U.S. Others, including Maykel 
``Osorbo'' Castillo Perez, must be released.
  The Trump administration, which routinely insists that its policy is 
to support the Cuban people, should match its rhetoric with action. It 
should support economic reforms that have the potential to 
significantly improve life on the island. Ending the illegal blockade 
would be an obvious and necessary step. No legitimate U.S. interest is 
served by compounding the misery of the Cuban people by preventing 
other countries and companies from sending lifesaving fuel and supplies 
to Cuba.
```
