# REMEMBERING KAREN CLARK

**Speaking:** Klobuchar, Amy (K000367) [D-MN]

```
                        REMEMBERING KAREN CLARK

● Ms. KLOBUCHAR. Madam President, on behalf of myself and 
Senator Tina Smith of Minnesota, I rise today to honor Minnesota State 
Representative Karen Clark, who passed away in June after a lifetime of 
service to our State.
  Karen was raised on a farm near Edgerton, MN, where she first 
developed her love for the land and passion for protecting the earth--a 
commitment that would come to define her legacy as a legislator and the 
cofounder of an environmental organization. She earned her degree from 
the College of Saint Teresa and began working as a public health nurse, 
providing care to underserved communities. Karen was also known as a 
powerhouse community organizer fighting for civil rights. In 1980, she 
turned her focus to the State house and was elected as a State 
representative. Karen won that first race promising to be a fighter for 
social justice and economic justice. And for 38 years, she never 
wavered from that promise.
  Karen strengthened protections for survivors of domestic violence, 
got rid of BPA in baby bottles, stood up for the environment and 
workers, as well as for immigrants and refugees, and wrote the bill 
legalizing same-sex marriage in Minnesota. She did all of this as a 
trailblazer, as the first openly lesbian legislator to serve in the 
Minnesota State House.
  Karen was committed to doing the right thing, no matter how long it 
took and no matter the obstacles in her way. After an earlier setback 
in the fight for marriage equality, Karen did not give up. She did not 
lose hope. She did not lose sight of the fight, and we remember her 
words from that moment: ``The Minnesota I know is a place of inclusion, 
tolerance, and love.'' Thanks to Karen, Minnesota is a more inclusive 
place and warmer place all the more.

[[Page S4916]]

  There are many words that describe Karen: organizer, 
environmentalist, legislator, advocate, nurse. But above them all, we 
think of Karen as the best of Minnesota, as the very ideal of a servant 
leader. She will be dearly missed by her wife Dr. Jacquelyn Zita and 
all who benefited from her dedication, compassion, and service.●
```
