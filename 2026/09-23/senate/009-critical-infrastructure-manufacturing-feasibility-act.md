# CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE MANUFACTURING FEASIBILITY ACT

**Speaking:** Ernst, Joni (E000295) [R-IA]

**Measures:** H.R. 1721

```
         CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE MANUFACTURING FEASIBILITY ACT

  Ms. ERNST. Mr. President, I want to thank my colleague from Delaware 
for bringing up our Iowa State Cyclones and our University of Iowa 
Hawkeyes. We had the Cy-Hawk game just 2 weekends ago. Unfortunately, 
my Cyclones fell to the Iowa Hawkeyes. But God bless Coach Kirk Ferentz 
and his Iowa Hawkeyes. It was another great game for all of Iowa.
  Mr. President, I am here today to talk a little bit more about our 
national and economic security of the United States. This has really 
been the focus of my career--protecting that national and economic 
security.
  In 1940, the United States became the ``Arsenal of Democracy,'' and 
our country came together to quickly accelerate production of weapons, 
vehicles, food, and supplies that enabled the allies to defeat the axis 
powers.
  Americans in factories, farms, small businesses, and across the 
country did what was necessary to make what was needed.
  Instead of internalizing the lesson that our ability to make these 
critical items in the United States led to great success, we spent much 
of the last 50 years off-shoring our jobs and our manufacturing 
capabilities. This is something China, Russia, and other nefarious 
actors around the world have been all too happy to accommodate and to 
take on themselves.
  Today, we have only one active facility producing cannon barrels. 
Today, we rely 100 percent on foreign sources for 12 critical minerals 
essential to our military and our economy.
  During COVID, we saw what happened when supply chains are strained. 
There was a global shortage of microchips, which upended manufacturers' 
ability to make everything from cars to computers. At the same time, 
moms in the Midwest had to worry about finding baby formula due to 
supply chain issues at both the production and shipping levels.
  Put simply: We have lost the ability to make what we need right here 
at home.
  In the event of a conflict with one of our near-peer adversaries, 
supply chains will be challenged, and the United States will have to 
make what it needs.
  Thank goodness President Trump, his administration, and Congress have 
been taking steps to put us on the right track and bring back the 
manufacturing capabilities we need here at home.
  My bipartisan Critical Infrastructure Manufacturing Feasibility Act 
builds on these efforts. My bill, which has already passed the Senate 
unanimously, directs the Secretary of Commerce to identify critical 
goods that are currently being imported and find ways to help domestic 
producers manufacture them in the rural areas and shovel-ready 
industrial sites.
  Rural Iowans are ready to roll up their sleeves and build the 
factories and processing facilities we need to secure our supply 
chains.
  We can and need to make things here. But you can't fix 
vulnerabilities you can't see, which is why my bill gives Congress and 
the executive branch the visibility necessary to identify gaps and then 
close them.
  I am grateful to Representative Mariannette Miller-Meeks for 
introducing the House version of this bill, and for the House of 
Representatives passing the bill unanimously.
  Now it is time for the Senate to act and send this bill to the 
President's desk.
  Mr. President, notwithstanding rule XXII, I ask unanimous consent 
that the Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation be 
discharged from further consideration of H.R. 1721 and that the Senate 
proceed to its immediate consideration.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The clerk will report the bill by title.
  The legislative clerk read as follows:

       A bill (H.R. 1721) to direct the Secretary of Commerce to 
     conduct a study on the feasibility of manufacturing in the 
     United States products for critical infrastructure sectors, 
     and for other purposes.

  There being no objection, the committee was discharged, and the 
Senate proceeded to consider the bill.
  Ms. ERNST. Mr. President, I ask unanimous consent that the bill be 
considered read a third time and passed and that the motion to 
reconsider be considered made and laid upon the table.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Without objection, it is so ordered.
  The bill (H.R. 1721) was ordered to a third reading, was read the 
third time, and passed.
  I yield the floor.
```
