# TRIBUTE TO CINDY ROSENWALD

**Speaking:** Shaheen, Jeanne (S001181) [D-NH]

```
                       TRIBUTE TO CINDY ROSENWALD

  Mrs. SHAHEEN. Madam President, I rise today to pay tribute to Cindy 
Rosenwald and recognize her decades of public service. Cindy is 
currently wrapping up her final term in public office, bringing to a 
close a remarkable 22-year career in the New Hampshire Legislature. 
After 14 years in the New Hampshire House of Representatives and 8 
years in the New Hampshire Senate, she leaves a legacy of resilience, 
creative problem-solving, and genuine care for her constituents.
  Cindy spent her early years in Rye, NY, before earning a bachelor's 
degree from Harvard University and a master's degree from Rivier 
University. Following her education, she pursued a varied career in 
communications, healthcare, and teaching. She has held positions in 
hospital marketing and public relations and was formerly a vice 
president at Ted Bates Advertising, where she continued to work with 
healthcare and packaged food clients. Before entering the political 
arena, Cindy also worked in education, holding positions at multiple 
local universities. In addition to working as a writing instructor at 
her alma mater Rivier College, Cindy held positions at New Hampshire 
College--now Southern New Hampshire University--and the University of 
Massachusetts Lowell, where she eventually served as an adjunct 
professor.
  Fueled by her experience in the realm of social services, Cindy's 
transition into politics began in 2004 when she was elected to the New 
Hampshire House of Representatives, representing the Hillsborough 30th 
district. During her tenure in the House, she became known as an active 
participant in legislative matters and a fierce advocate for affordable 
housing, childcare, healthcare, and policies to help working families. 
She was notably a key voice in the fight to expand Medicaid under the 
Affordable Care Act, championing relief for Granite Staters who had 
gone without health insurance and put off needed care.
  In 2018, Cindy continued this work after winning election to 
represent New Hampshire's 13th district in the State senate, which 
encompasses six wards in Nashua, NH. As one of the legislature's most 
tireless advocates for mental health care, she was the prime sponsor of 
legislation to raise reimbursement rates for Medicaid providers for 
mental health and substance use disorder treatment. With a keen focus 
on the future, Cindy also turned her attention to growing New 
Hampshire's economy, leading the successful effort to bring municipal 
cash reserve investments back to the State. She truly embodies what it 
means to be a public servant, and her advocacy for the well-being of 
Granite Staters is both unwavering and unmatched.
  Over more than two decades of public service, Cindy has fostered 
genuine relationships built on trust, courage, and partnership. She 
will soon conclude her fourth term in the New Hampshire Senate, but her 
persistent advocacy will leave an unforgettable mark on us all. Many of 
Cindy's colleagues, past and present, regard her as a visionary leader 
who fought long and hard to make New Hampshire a healthier and more 
compassionate State. She sought meaningful change through legislation, 
and she strove to ensure that no Granite Staters fell through the 
cracks. Cindy's enduring commitment to public service is an inspiration 
to us all. On behalf of the people of New Hampshire, I ask my 
colleagues and all Americans to join me in thanking Cindy Rosenwald for 
her many years of service to New Hampshire and her fellow Granite 
Staters. We wish her all the best in the next chapter of her life.

                          ____________________
```
