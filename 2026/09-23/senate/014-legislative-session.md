# LEGISLATIVE SESSION

**Speaking:** Shaheen, Jeanne (S001181) [D-NH]

```
                          LEGISLATIVE SESSION

  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Under the previous order, the Senate will 
resume legislative session.
  The Senator from New Hampshire.


                                 China

  Mrs. SHAHEEN. Mr. President, well, one issue that I hope will be at 
the top of the agenda when President Trump meets with Xi Jinping on 
Thursday is China's support for Iran.
  Two months ago, Iranian ballistic missiles struck the housing area at 
the U.S. airbase in Jordan. That attack killed three American soldiers 
and wounded four more.
  United States officials told the Wall Street Journal--and we can see 
this mockup of that article--that Iran got high-resolution satellite 
imagery of that base from Chinese entities before the attack. 
Unfortunately, this wasn't a surprise because senior Trump officials 
had warned for months that Chinese companies were giving Iran imagery 
that could be used to target American personnel.
  In May, the administration sanctioned several Chinese companies over 
precisely that activity. Senate Foreign Relations Committee staff 
recently returned from the Middle East, where they saw the damage 
Iran's attacks have done to United States facilities.
  It is not only satellite imagery that Iran relies on to carry out 
attacks against Americans. China also provides a critical economic 
lifeline to Iran. According to the Treasury Department, 90 percent of 
Iran's oil exports go to China. That is billions of dollars in revenue 
for the Iranian regime, and much of that oil is sold in defiance of 
United States sanctions, through shadow fleets, front companies, and 
Chinese refineries. That money supports Iran's military, its weapons 
programs, and its terrorist proxies.
  China is also a source of the parts and materials that Iran needs to 
build its weapons. The United States Treasury Department has repeatedly 
sanctioned Chinese and Hong Kong companies for supplying components for 
Iran's ballistic missiles and attack drones. Those are the same types 
of weapons that Iran is using to attack American forces.
  This cannot be treated as a side issue. When President Trump sits 
across from President Xi at the White House, he should use his leverage 
to do three things: first, to stop Chinese companies from giving Iran 
satellite imagery and targeting support; second, stop Chinese networks 
from supplying parts for Iran's missiles and drones; and third, stop 
the purchase and trade of sanctioned Iranian oil.
  If we want to bring this war to an end, we need to cut off the 
outside support that allows Iran to keep fighting, and that starts with 
China. The administration should enforce our sanctions against the 
Chinese entities helping Iran, and if China doesn't stop, Congress 
should impose additional consequences.
  I hope the President will also think about the weapon that Congress 
gave him when we passed the Russia sanctions bill because it allows us 
to tariff the Chinese companies that are purchasing Russian oil and 
gas. China is the No. 1 purchaser of Russian oil and gas, so they are 
the top funder of the war in Ukraine as well as being one of the top 
funders of the war in Iran. So I hope the President will take this 
opportunity to use the tools that we have given him and to point out to 
President Xi that China needs to start behaving differently.
  I yield the floor.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from New Mexico.
```
