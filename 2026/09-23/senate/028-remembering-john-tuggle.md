**Speaking:** Capito, Shelley Moore (C001047) [R-WV]

                        REMEMBERING JOHN TUGGLE

● Mrs. CAPITO. Madam President, I rise today to pay tribute to 
John Tuggle, executive director of Region 4 Planning and Development 
Council, who departed this Earth suddenly on the evening of September 
18, 2026, much to the sorrow of myself, his family and friends, the 
community he called home, and the many people across the State that he 
mentored, encouraged, and supported throughout all of their endeavors.
  In 1971, the West Virginia Legislature created 11 planning and 
development councils, which are responsible for administering and 
facilitating Federal and State funding for local and regional projects 
including water, wastewater, transportation, broadband deployment, 
workforce development, housing, healthcare, and tourism, among other 
priorities. These councils are the unsung heroes of infrastructure in 
our State. Most West Virginians do not even know they exist. Their work 
is not publicized; they do not seek attention or expect praise. They do 
it because they love their communities and want to create a better 
quality of life for their neighbors.
  Region 4 is comprised of Fayette, Nicholas, Greenbrier, Pocahontas, 
and Webster Counties--five rural counties in our State that are among 
the most beautiful and scenic places we have to offer. Each of these 
counties have unique challenges that make public infrastructure costly 
and difficult. But no challenge was too daunting for John and his team 
of project specialists, financial managers, and GIS coordinators. If 
you have ever spent time around John and the staff of Region 4, you 
know they are more than colleagues; they are family. My heart is with 
them during this difficult time.
  Throughout my time serving in Congress, I have had many opportunities 
to work with John and his team, and I can say without hesitation that 
they are the very best. So often, elected officials get all the credit 
when Federal or State dollars get handed down to communities for 
projects. It is our names written in the headlines, but truly nothing 
can be accomplished without the boots on the ground required to submit 
applications, manage projects, and make them a reality. John was the 
epitome of boots on the ground, the one who built trust and made things 
happen, and we certainly have big shoes to fill with his passing.
  Most importantly, John was a family man and dedicated Christian. He 
was a loving husband, father, foster parent, mentor, neighbor, and 
friend. He was an active member of the United Methodist Temple in 
Beckley, WV, and a volunteer for the Annual Community Thanksgiving meal 
in his hometown of Oak Hill, where he was one of the first to arrive 
and one of the last to the leave while serving free hot meals to 
thousands of his neighbors. When the June 2016 floods struck much of 
John's region, he was instrumental in the recovery efforts, restoring 
vital infrastructure to communities and working with volunteer 
organizations to help rebuild the homes and lives of those impacted. 
John and his wife Romona were proud foster parents and remained in 
their children's lives even after they left his family's care.
  John Tuggle was the best of us. He was a gentle giant. Yes, he was 
impossible to miss even in a crowded room, but it was his warmth, 
kindness, knowledge, and commitment to helping others that made him 
larger than life. His spirit lives on through the many projects and 
people he helped shape. Charlie and I join countless others across West 
Virginia in prayer for his wife Romona, his family, and many 
friends.●
