# ORLANDO LETELIER AND RONNI KARPEN MOFFITT

**Speaking:** Welch, Peter (W000800) [D-VT]

```
               ORLANDO LETELIER AND RONNI KARPEN MOFFITT

  Mr. WELCH. Madam President, 50 years ago yesterday, on September 21, 
1976, Chile's former ambassador to the United States Orlando Letelier 
and his colleague Ronni Karpen Moffitt were murdered by a car bomb 
placed by agents of the Chilean secret police.

[[Page S4913]]

  Every year on the anniversary of that horrendous crime, people gather 
to pay tribute to Ambassador Letelier and Ronni Moffitt at Sheridan 
Circle, just 3 miles from the Capitol Building, where a small monument 
marks the spot where their lives were violently cut short. That 
monument is inscribed with the words ``Justice--Peace--Dignity''--
ideals they embraced in their professional and personal lives.
  For years, justice eluded Orlando Letelier and Ronni Moffitt and much 
has been written about the decades-long effort to identify the 
perpetrators and bring them to justice. I want to acknowledge and 
credit the Institute for Policy Studies where Letelier and Moffitt both 
worked, along with Peter Kornbluh and the National Security Archive for 
their unending efforts to obtain the declassification of documents that 
record that long arc of justice, and to keep the memory of Orlando 
Letelier and Ronni Moffitt and the ideals they stood for in the public 
consciousness for the past 50 years.
  Eventually, the FBI identified two DINA operatives, American-born 
Michael Townley and Maj. Armando Fernandez Larios, as leading suspects. 
The U.S. then sought the extradition of DINA's top two officers, Manuel 
Contreras and his deputy Pedro Espinoza, which Chile's dictator General 
Pinochet blocked. A CIA review of its own intelligence on the car 
bombing concluded that there was ``convincing evidence that President 
Pinochet personally ordered his intelligence chief to carry out the 
murder'' and that Pinochet ``decided to stonewall on the case to hide 
his involvement and, ultimately, to protect his hold on the 
presidency.''
  When Chile returned to democracy in 1990, the George H.W. Bush 
administration agreed to drop sanctions against the Pinochet government 
for protecting the DINA officers if the new government prosecuted 
Contreras and Espinoza. They were both found guilty of conspiring to 
assassinate Letelier and given short prison sentences of 7 and 6 years 
respectively. But Pinochet, still the head of the Chilean Army, 
remained immune from prosecution, as did all the other torturers and 
executioners from his cruel dictatorship.
  It was not until Pinochet was arrested in London in October 1998 
under the European Anti-Terrorism Convention that his immunity was 
lifted. Although the British released him after more than a year of 
house arrest, just days after his return to Santiago a courageous 
judge, Juan Guzman, filed papers in court to strip Pinochet of his 
immunity from prosecution. At the time he died in 2006 at the age of 
91, he faced indictments and investigations for corruption and human 
rights atrocities.
  In 2012, a Chilean court finally ordered that Ronni Moffitt's case be 
officially investigated. Fourteen years later, on June 15, 2026, 
Chile's magistrate for human rights, Judge Paola Plaza, ruled that 
three former DINA officials were guilty of killing Moffitt. Brig. Gen. 
Pedro Espinoza, DINA's former deputy director for operations, was 
sentenced to 15 years. Gen. Raul Iturriaga, a high-ranking officer in 
DINA's ``Exterior'' department, was also sentenced to 15 years, as was 
retired Brig. Gen. Jose Zara Holger, also a director in DINA's 
international operations division.
  Fifty years after the assassination, the Moffitt ruling is part of 
the final chapter in the effort to hold General Pinochet and his DINA 
accomplices accountable for an act of international terrorism in 
Washington, DC. This past July, The New York Times reported on ICE's 
detention--and then release--of one of those accomplices, Armando 
Fernandez Larios, who defected to the U.S. in 1987 and served just 5 
months in prison for his role in the assassination plot. Last October, 
in its quest to find immigrants with real criminal backgrounds, ICE 
detained Fernandez Larios and the Department of Homeland Security 
rightly labeled him ``the worst of the worst'' for his past crimes of 
homicide. But while hundreds of migrants who have no criminal history 
are summarily arrested and deported from this country every day, 
Fernandez Larios was quietly freed from the Krome Detention Center in 
Miami on March 19--a glaring example of the Trump administration's 
double standard in the administration of justice.
  Fernandez Larios should be deported for his part in the assassination 
of Orlando Letelier and Ronni Moffitt and the egregious human rights 
crimes he committed before coming to this country. The United States 
must not be a haven for foreigners who violate human rights or commit 
acts of terrorism.
  The Fernandez Larios case and the recent ruling in the case of Ronni 
Moffitt have refocused attention on the September 21 bombing a half-
century ago. I join those who honor the memory of Orlando Letelier and 
Ronni Moffitt and reaffirm that no matter how long it takes for the 
wheels of justice to turn, no one is above the law, especially those 
who abuse their positions of power to commit heinous crimes.

                          ____________________
```
