# ROMANCE SCAM PREVENTION ACT

**Speaking:** Blackburn, Marsha (B001243) [R-TN]

**Measures:** H.R. 2481

```
                      ROMANCE SCAM PREVENTION ACT

  Mrs. BLACKBURN. Mr. President, each year, Americans lose roughly $1.3 
billion to online romance scams. The victims are overwhelmingly senior 
citizens. They go online, thinking they are going to be able to find 
some companionship. They meet somebody. Often, this is on a dating 
site. They think that what they have made is a genuine connection 
because they are chatting online with this individual, but what is 
actually happening is they are communicating with a fraudster the 
entire time. Often, these victims--as I said, many of them are older. 
They are senior citizens. These victims will lose their entire 
lifesavings before they realize or before their friends and their 
family realize what has actually happened.
  Here is the thing that is so important to realize. These scam artists 
are not amateurs. In fact, they are often part of a transnational 
criminal organization that traffics individuals in developing countries 
with the promise of getting a high-paying job. With their passports 
stolen, these individuals, who are victims, are often transported to 
what are called fraud dens, and they are forced to pay back their debt 
to these transnational criminal organizations by working overtime to 
defraud our senior citizens here in the United States.
  It is really quite amazing. These are scam artists. They are part of 
transnational criminal organizations. They lure people into being 
trafficked.
  They promise those individuals high-paying jobs in other countries, 
and then they take their passports and put them to work in these fraud 
dens. And there, they are defrauding U.S. citizens.
  These scams are well financed, they are very sophisticated, and more 
often than not, they are using AI and other technology to dupe American 
consumers.
  Scams are proliferating in the virtual space. We are seeing much of 
this in online dating platforms, and we do think these online dating 
platforms have the ability and the responsibility to notify their users 
when they are interacting with a bad actor.
  By taking this simple step, the Romance Scam Prevention Act would 
protect our seniors from fraud and strike at the heart of one of the 
most lucrative financial streams for some of the most heinous criminal 
networks in the world.
  I want to thank Representative Valadao and his staff for their 
commitment to protecting our seniors and championing this important 
legislation, and I thank my colleagues here in the Senate who have also 
supported it.
  Notwithstanding rule XXII, I ask unanimous consent that the Senate 
proceed to the immediate consideration of Calendar No. 438, H.R. 2481.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The clerk will report the bill by title.
  The legislative clerk read as follows:

       A bill (H.R. 2481) to require online dating service 
     providers to provide fraud ban notifications to online dating 
     servicemembers, and for other purposes.

  There being no objection, the Senate proceeded to consider the bill.
  Mrs. BLACKBURN. I ask unanimous consent that the bill be considered 
read a third time and passed and that the motion to reconsider be 
considered made and laid upon the table.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Without objection, it is so ordered.
  The bill (H.R. 2481) was ordered to a third reading, was read the 
third time, and passed.
```
