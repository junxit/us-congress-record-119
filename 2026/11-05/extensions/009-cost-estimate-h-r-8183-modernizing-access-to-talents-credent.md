       H.R. 8183 would direct the Secretary of Labor to establish 
     a pilot program to provide grants for states to build 
     workforce longitudinal data systems, talent marketplaces, and 
     related tools. The bill would require the Secretary to 
     allocate to the pilot program between 5 percent and 10 
     percent of funding of the Dislocated Worker Assistance (DWA) 
     national reserve each fiscal year starting in 2027. The bill 
     also would require the Department of Labor (DOL) to provide 
     technical assistance to grantees and develop tools to assess 
     workers' skills.
       The authorization of Title I of the Workforce Innovation 
     and Opportunity Act (WIOA) of 2014--which includes DWA--
     expired after 2020, but funds have been provided in annual 
     appropriation acts since that expiration. For this analysis, 
     CBO estimates

[[Page E1159]]

     only the cost to implement the new activities specified in 
     the bill and not the cost of reauthorizing DWA.
       WIOA requires the Secretary to allocate 20 percent of the 
     amounts appropriated for DWA to the national reserve. In 
     2026, $1.4 billion was appropriated for DWA and $301 million 
     was allocated to the national reserve, slightly more than the 
     20 percent required by law. CBO's estimate of the 
     authorization for the pilot program reflects the assumption 
     that funding for DWA would be provided each year, along with 
     adjustments for inflation, and that 20 percent of the amount 
     appropriated would be allocated to the national reserve. 
     Further, CBO estimates that the Secretary would allocate 10 
     percent of the national reserve funding for the pilot 
     program. Thus, CBO estimates that the pilot program would 
     receive $29 million in 2027, rising to $33 million in 2031, 
     for a total of $155 million over the 2027-2031 period. Using 
     historical spending patterns, CBO estimates that outlays for 
     the pilot program would total $112 million over that period.
       On the basis of information from DOL about the cost of 
     similar activities, CBO estimates that, over the 2027-2031 
     period, DOL would require $10 million to administer the pilot 
     program and $14 million to provide technical assistance and 
     develop the skills assessment tools.
       In total, CBO estimates that implementing H.R. 8183 would 
     cost $136 million over the 2027-2031 period, assuming 
     appropriation of the estimated amounts.
