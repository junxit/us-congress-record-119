# RECOGNIZING THE CONTRIBUTIONS OF RICK SIMONSON

**Speaking:** Jayapal, Pramila (J000298) [D-WA]

```
             RECOGNIZING THE CONTRIBUTIONS OF RICK SIMONSON

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. PRAMILA JAYAPAL

                             of washington

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, November 5, 2026

  Ms. JAYAPAL. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize and honor Mr. 
Rick Simonson, Senior Book Buyer at Elliott Bay Book Company in 
Seattle, as he celebrates his fiftieth year with the bookstore. Since 
joining Elliott Bay in 1976, Mr. Simonson has become one of Seattle's 
most respected literary figures, known to generations of Washington 
readers and writers as a trusted guide to the books and voices of the 
Pacific Northwest, the Nation and the world.
  In 1984, Mr. Simonson established Seattle's first bookstore reading 
series. What began as a bold idea has grown into a tradition that has 
inspired thousands of literary events across our region. Today, Elliott 
Bay hosts more than 400 readings and events each year, most of them 
free and open to the public. The bookstore is a pillar of Seattle's 
literary, arts and small business communities. Mr. Simonson's graceful 
introductions and tireless devotion to readers have welcomed emerging 
Northwest poets and internationally acclaimed authors alike, and his 
work has helped establish Seattle as a destination for authors and book 
lovers.
  Mr. Simonson has also given his time and judgment to the wider 
literary community. He has served on the boards of Copper Canyon Press, 
the University of Washington Press, Seattle City of Literature, Seattle 
Arts & Lectures and the Central District Forum for Arts & Ideas. He has 
carried his expertise beyond our region, presenting at festivals and 
conferences across the United States and abroad. His work has also been 
instrumental in bringing major literary festivals, such as the Jaipur 
Literary Festival, to Seattle.
  Mr. Simonson is a resource to writers. He listens to their stories, 
sees what they have to offer and makes sure that diverse voices are 
uplifted. He has worked to ensure that a broad range of communities, 
writers and voices are heard and celebrated. Whether it's for emerging 
Northwest poets or internationally acclaimed authors alike. Mr. 
Simonson gives the same attention and devotion to making sure the world 
sees and reads their work. His decades of dedication have contributed 
immeasurably to Seattle's recognition as a UNESCO City of Literature. 
This designation recognizes our city's dozens of independent 
bookstores, thriving community of writers and literary calendar where 
readers can hear an author share their work on nearly any evening.
  Mr. Simonson's career is a reminder of why independent bookstores 
matter. In an era of rapid change for publishing and retail, he has 
built an enduring community where authors and readers come together 
through literature, memoir, history and storytelling. He has championed 
voices that have too often been overlooked. He understands that the 
true value of books lies in the ideas, experiences and humanity they 
allow us to share. In doing so, he has fostered greater understanding, 
empathy and compassion through the transformative power of 
storytelling.
  On behalf of Washington's 7th Congressional District, I extend my 
deepest gratitude

[[Page E1161]]

and congratulations to Mr. Rick for 50 years of extraordinary service 
to our community and our city. His legacy will continue to inspire 
booksellers, writers, readers and civic leaders for generations to 
come. I urge my colleagues to join me in honoring his remarkable 
contributions and reaffirming our commitment to sustaining vibrant, 
independent bookstores and literary communities across the United 
States.
```
