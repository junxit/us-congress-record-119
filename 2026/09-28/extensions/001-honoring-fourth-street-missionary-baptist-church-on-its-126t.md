# HONORING FOURTH STREET MISSIONARY BAPTIST CHURCH ON ITS 126TH ANNIVERSARY

**Speaking:** Bishop, Sanford D. (B000490) [D-GA]

```
     HONORING FOURTH STREET MISSIONARY BAPTIST CHURCH ON ITS 126TH 
                              ANNIVERSARY

                                 ______
                                 

                      HON. SANFORD D. BISHOP, JR.

                               of georgia

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 28, 2026

  Mr. BISHOP. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Fourth Street 
Missionary Baptist Church of Columbus, Georgia, as its congregation 
celebrates 126 years of faithful service to God and to the people of 
the Chattahoochee Valley. A worship service to celebrate this momentous 
accomplishment was held on Sunday, September 27, 2026, at 2:00 pm. For 
me, this is not simply a tribute to a church; it is a tribute to my 
church home--the place where I sat in the pews for 24 years, where I 
was fed, corrected, comforted, and sent.
  Mr. Speaker, every great story has humble beginnings, and Fourth 
Street's story began under a fig tree. In 1900, a few devoted men and 
women gathered beneath a fig tree on Third Avenue in Columbus to 
worship God under the leadership of the Reverends John Bellamy and 
Willis Carter. They called themselves Mt. Canaan Baptist Church. They 
had no building. They had no pews. They had no steeple, but what they 
had was faith.
  The very next year, in 1901, Reverend Bellamy led fifteen faithful 
souls to worship beneath a bush arbor on Fifth Street--open sky above, 
wooden benches below--as the New Mt. Canaan Baptist Church. Later that 
year, the Reverend Pearson was called as pastor, and under his 
leadership that littLe band of believers did something remarkable. They 
bought a tract of land for 50 dollars. 50 dollars, Mr. Speaker. For 
people only a generation removed from slavery, that was not a small 
sum. That was a sacrifice. On that land they raised a humble shelter 
for worship, and that shelter became Fourth Street Baptist Church.
  The prophet Micah wrote, ``But they shall sit every man under his 
vine and under his fig tree; and none shall make them afraid.'' Those 
first saints sat under their fig tree. And for 126 years, Mr. Speaker, 
no one has been able to make Fourth Street afraid.
  In 1961, the Lord sent Fourth Street a young preacher from the 
Pumpkin Bottom community of Phenix City, Alabama--the Reverend Johnny 
H. Flakes, Jr. He would remain its pastor for 51 years, while also 
shepherding Good Hope Missionary Baptist Church in Phenix City for 
fifty-three.
  Dr. Flakes was a preacher, a teacher, an evangelist, and--as anyone 
who ever heard him will tell you--a soloist who could sing the Spirit 
into a room. But he never believed the Gospel stopped at the church 
door. He was a leader in the NAACP, a pivotal force in establishing the 
Urban League of Columbus, and a cofounder of One Columbus, which worked 
to bridge the racial divide in our city. He chaired the Board of 
Trustees of his alma mater, American Baptist College in Nashville.
  And when hate tried to make Fourth Street afraid--when the church was 
firebombed--Dr. Flakes did not flee, and he did not falter. He stood. 
He stood for Fourth Street, and he stood for every other church that 
faced the same terror.
  With his beloved wife, Mother Robena Gaines Flakes, at his side for 
58 years, Dr. Flakes led Fourth Street's founding of Fourth Street 
Towers in 1972, bringing affordable housing to the families surrounding 
the church. The congregation raised its Christian Education Building in 
1977. And in 1992 it opened the Fourth Street Day Care, with Mrs. 
Flakes as director--the first day care center in the State of Georgia 
to receive the Center of Distinction Award.
  Mr. Speaker, I must speak personally now. For 24 years, Fourth Street 
Missionary Baptist Church was my church home, and Dr. Johnny H. Flakes, 
Jr. was my pastor. Before many people knew my name, Dr. Flakes knew me. 
Through trouble or triumph, he was always available, and he always gave 
me wise counsel. And Mrs. Flakes--Mrs. Flakes was like a mother to me.
  When I think about the man I have tried to become, in public life and 
in private faith, I cannot separate it from the lessons I learned in 
those pews on Fifth Street. When Dr. Flakes went home to be with the 
Lord on November 12, 2012, a part of my own heart went with him. But I 
have learned, Mr. Speaker, that when God calls a shepherd home, He does 
not leave the sheep without a shepherd.
  Dr. Flakes encouraged his children not to be carbon copies of him, 
but to glean from his wisdom and follow the lead of the Holy Spirit. 
His son has done exactly that.
  The Reverend Dr. Johnny H. Flakes, III, was once a football standout 
at Tuskegee University--the school's all-time return specialist and a 
member of its Athletic Hall of Fame. Mr. Speaker, a return specialist, 
is the one who catches what has been sent to him and carries it 
forward--through traffic, against opposition, toward the goal. And that 
is precisely what Pastor Flakes has done with the mantle his father 
placed in his hands.
  He earned his Master of Divinity from the Morehouse School of 
Religion at the Interdenominational Theological Center, a master's 
degree from the University of Iowa, and a Doctor of Ministry from 
Mercer University's McAfee School of Theology. He pastored Mount Gilead 
Missionary Baptist Church in Atlanta and served as co-pastor alongside 
his father before taking up the pulpit at Fourth Street. Today, with 
his wife, Jacqueline, beside him, he chairs Fourth Street Towers and 
the Fourth Street Daycare and Academy, and he serves our community 
through the Interdenominational Ministerial Alliance, the John Amos 
Center Advisory Board, Historic Columbus, and so much more.
  The Word of the Lord declares, ``The glory of this latter house shall 
be greater than of the former, saith the LORD of hosts: and in this 
place will I give peace.'' Under Pastor Flakes, the latter house is 
building on a glorious foundation.
  Mr. Speaker, 126 years ago, there was a fig tree. Then there was a 
bush arbor. Then there was a 50-dollar lot and a humble shelter. Today 
there stands a great church at 222 Fifth Street--with housing for 
families, a school for children, and a pulpit that has never stopped 
proclaiming the Gospel of Jesus Christ.
  ``Wherefore seeing we also are compassed about with so great a cloud 
of witnesses''--Reverend Bellamy, Reverend Carter, Reverend Pearson, 
those first fifteen members, Dr. Johnny H. Flakes, Jr., and generations 
of saints who have gone on before--I believe that cloud of witnesses is 
looking down on Fourth Street today. And I believe they are well 
pleased.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join my wife, Vivian, and I, 
along with the more than 765,000 people of Georgia's 2nd Congressional 
District, in congratulating Pastor J. H. Flakes, III, the officers, and 
the entire membership of Fourth Street Missionary Baptist Church on 126 
years of faith, sacrifice, and service. May God continue to bless this 
house--my house--for generations yet to come.
```
