# HONORING THE RETIREMENT OF AFSCME PRESIDENT LEE SAUNDERS

**Speaking:** Horsford, Steven (H001066) [D-NV]

```
        HONORING THE RETIREMENT OF AFSCME PRESIDENT LEE SAUNDERS

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. STEVEN HORSFORD

                               of nevada

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 28, 2026

  Mr. HORSFORD. Mr. Speaker, I rise today along with my colleagues, 
fellow Co-Chairs of the Congressional Labor Caucus, Representative 
Debbie Dingell, Representative Mark Pocan, and Representative Donald 
Norcross, to recognize our brother and friend, Lee Saunders, President 
of the American Federation of State, County and Municipal Employees 
(AFSCME), who retired in August 2026 after five decades in lifetime 
service to the labor movement. President Saunders is a staunch and 
unflinching labor leader who has served with distinction and stood up 
for working people across this country with unparalleled enthusiasm and 
grit. He has served as the President of AFSCME since 2012.
  Saunders grew up in Cleveland, Ohio, and was the son of a union bus 
driver and a community organizer. In 1975, he started working for the 
Ohio Bureau of Employment Services and joined the Ohio Civil Service 
Employees Association (OCSEA). He started his career with AFSCME in 
1978 as a labor economist and participated in numerous efforts for the 
union ranging from research and organizing to community outreach and 
building worker power. Saunders previously served as administrator of 
numerous AFSCME councils, including AFSCME District Council 37, and 
became AFSCME's Secretary-Treasurer in 2010.
  Saunders has always been laser-focused on grassroots mobilizing and 
giving a voice to working people. Beyond his work to support workers, 
he has been deeply involved in political and civil rights as a board 
member of the NAACP and treasurer of the Leadership Conference on Civil 
and Human Rights. His leadership reminds us not only that we have a 
duty to represent and fight for all workers, but that public services 
remain vital to every community in this country.
  Widely known as one of the labor movement's most savvy political 
strategists, President Saunders presided over a period of unprecedented 
growth at AFSCME during his 14 years as its first African American 
president. His dedication to our Nation's public service workers both 
in his role at ASFCME and at the AFL-CIO have made the labor movement 
stronger and more united.
  On behalf of myself and my fellow Congressional Labor Caucus Co-
Chairs, Representive Debbie Dingell, Representative Mark Pocan, and 
Representative Donald Norcross, we thank President Saunders for his 
tremendous leadership and recognize the difference it has made in the 
lives of ordinary workers.
```
