# HONORING FIRST LIEUTENANT JAMES CLAYTON FLOWERS

**Speaking:** Vasquez, Gabe (V000136) [D-NM]

```
            HONORING FIRST LIEUTENANT JAMES CLAYTON FLOWERS

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. GABE VASQUEZ

                             of new mexico

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 28, 2026

  Mr. VASQUEZ. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor First Lieutenant 
James Clayton Flowers, who is the oldest living United States veteran, 
a retired teacher, and a proud resident of New Mexico's 2nd 
Congressional District.
  Born on Christmas day in Dendron, Virginia, James was raised by his 
father, who was a farmer, and his mother, who was a teacher. His father 
taught him bricklaying before he entered the military in 1942, at 25 
years old. James remembers his parents were strongly anti-war, and his 
decision to join the military was a difficult choice.
  Shortly after James began his service in the Army Air Corps, he was 
chosen to become an officer. He completed Officer Candidate School at 
Fort Sill, Oklahoma, and was commissioned as a second lieutenant. 
During this time, he began pilot training but did not complete the 
program. Instead, the Army recognized his intelligence and outstanding 
academic record and appointed him an Academic Instructor with a 
specialty in Aerodynamics and Mechanics. In this role he taught cadets 
aviation intelligence, threat analysis, and security, and he became one 
of the first Black United States military pilots. James served at 
several key domestic military bases during World War II, including 
Godman Field, Kentucky, and Freeman Field, Indiana. He departed the 
military after 5 years of service.
  James is a member of the Tuskegee Airmen, which are the first Black 
aviators that served with the United States Army Air Forces during 
World War II. While stationed at Freeman Field, Indiana, he served with 
the 477th Bombardment Group. This was the first Black bombardment unit 
in Air Force history and became renowned for fighting against 
``separate but equal'' practices in the military. James remembers being 
one of the few black officers in the military at the time and once 
instructing

[[Page E993]]

an all-black intelligence squadron that was ecstatic to have him as 
their superior. Serving during this time was incredibly challenging as 
black servicemembers faced intense racism and discrimination, but his 
service tells a story of excellence, courage, and breaking systemic 
barriers.
  Following the war, James moved to New York City where he met his 
wife, Evelyn, and they got married in 1951. He became a middle school 
science teacher alongside Evelyn, who also taught in public schools. 
Teaching students and serving as a mentor for the next generation was 
one of James' greatest joys in life. James and Evelyn taught school and 
raised their children on the lower east side of Manhattan for 30 years 
before retiring in New Mexico in 1984. Evelyn passed away in 2008 at 88 
years old, and James reminisces on their happy and wonderful marriage. 
Together they had 3 kids, 6 grandchildren, and 3 great-grandchildren. 
James has lived a remarkable life, and he is most proud of his 
wonderful family and his work as a teacher.
  James is a strong man of faith and has been a member of the Unitarian 
Universalist Church of Las Cruces for over 40 years. During his 
membership with the church, he has served as the congregation's 
representative for the annual meeting of the Unitarian Universalist 
Association known as the General Assembly. James and his wife also 
acted as grandparents for children in the church without any living or 
nearby grandparents. Additionally, James taught students religious 
education during his tenure teaching.
  James is cherished in his community and loved by his neighbors, who 
appreciate his commitment, steadfastness, and strength. He is also 
loved by his caregiver, Lidia Macias, Dr. Sierra, and Home Kare Inc. of 
Dona Ana. He was awarded the New Mexico Medal of Merit from the New 
Mexico Army Air National Guard for ``exceptionally meritorious service 
for his lifelong commitment to service, sacrifice, and dedication to 
the continued success of our great Nation.''
  On behalf of the people of New Mexico's 2nd Congressional District, I 
would like to thank First Lieutenant James Clayton Flowers for his 
courageous service and sacrifice to our Nation, his incredible work as 
an educator, and for his ongoing dedication to our local communities. 
He exemplifies the best that New Mexico and our country have to offer, 
and we are truly grateful for his tireless service.
```
