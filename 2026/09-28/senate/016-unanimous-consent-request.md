# UNANIMOUS CONSENT REQUEST

**Speaking:** Merkley, Jeff (M001176) [D-OR], Lee, Mike (L000577) [R-UT]

```
                       UNANIMOUS CONSENT REQUEST

  Mr. MERKLEY. Mr. President, I come to the floor to propose that 
Democrats and Republicans join together to stop a monumentally horrific 
idea, an idea called Trump's triumphal arch. It distorts our history 
while destroying historic views. It disregards the Constitution's 
separation of powers. It disrupts air safety at DC's Ronald Reagan 
Airport, and it disrespects the sacrifices of our veterans and their 
families.
  How much worse could an idea possibly be?
  Then, when Trump encountered fierce pushback about this unfunded, 
unauthorized construction, he invoked a national security 
justification, saying the arch would house drones and snipers and 
ammunition. This is a replay of the justification that he used on his 
billion-dollar ballroom, gold-plated boondoggle. Those oversight 
procedures called an authorization by Congress and funding by Congress 
are absolutely inherent to our Constitution, and we should absolutely 
not be going forward or be allowing anything to go forward that so 
blatantly violates so much of what we stand for.
  Let's talk about why a memorial arch distorts our history.
  We are a Republic. We are not a monarchy. We are not an imperial 
regime. It is called a democratic republic. I would like the President 
of the United States to actually understand the difference. With this 
arch, he is imitating what the Roman Empire did in commemorating great 
military victories and leaders. The Arc de Triomphe, in

[[Page S5066]]

Paris, was commissioned in 1806 by Emperor Napoleon.
  While Trump may fancy himself a modern American emperor, his war in 
Iran is hardly a great military victory. It is quite the opposite. It 
is a towering, 250-foot-tall edifice, topped with golden statues--twice 
as tall as the nearby Lincoln Memorial that has such a place in the 
heart of every American, 100 feet taller than the Arc de Triomphe, and 
30 feet taller than the largest triumphal arch in the world--the 
Monumento a la Revolucion in Mexico City. Imitating emperors and 
authoritarians is not what America is about. Constructing things with 
no authorization and no funding is in violation of the separation of 
powers.
  Let's talk about disregarding the Constitution.
  Under Federal law, only Congress has the power to approve the 
placement of new monuments and memorials on Federal lands.
  It is written into law to ensure that memorials are the product of 
deliberation and consensus, accountability through the legislative 
process.
  But there has been no legislative process in this Congress or any 
prior Congress that said something about putting a massive arch, 
disrupting the connection between the Mall and the Lincoln Monument and 
the Arlington Cemetery. No such discussion has taken place. No 
congressional deliberation. No congressional authorization. No 
congressional funding.
  And yet Trump is planning to divert funds from other accounts while 
American families are struggling--struggling--because of the high cost 
of gas and diesel and groceries, fertilizer. Everyone is hurting, but 
Trump wants to build this massive monument to himself in violation of 
the very premises of our Constitution.
  And now he has this brilliant idea. He says he is the smartest man 
around. His brilliant idea is to launch drones into the air in the 
runway up to the Reagan Airport.
  Can someone please explain to him that launching drones into the air 
with oncoming jets is a bad idea.
  Just last year, 67 civilians were killed in a flash of light when a 
plane collided there with a military helicopter.
  In August of this year, the Presidential helicopter Marine One lifted 
off at the same time as a jet was taking off from the airport, and that 
gave a lot of people anxiety. They did not hit each other, thankfully, 
but close calls are scary as hell.
  And now Trump says the arch will house a large number of drones to be 
launched up in the air to counter national security threats. Launching 
drones directly into the approach of DC's airports is a reckless 
invitation for a collision.
  And Trump's proposed arch disrespects the sacrifices of our veterans 
and their families. Back when I was working for the Secretary of 
Defense, I traveled through Arlington Cemetery a lot. It is a sacred 
place. More than 400,000 military servicemembers are laid to rest there 
across the river. The Americans buried there died defending a Republic 
by and for the people, not defending a dictator or a sovereign, not 
defending an imperial regime. A triumphal arch in the tradition of 
dictators and authoritarians is an insult to their sacrifice defending 
our Republic.
  Veterans and their families have come forth and said: Please don't do 
this. The observation deck that Trump wants to build violates the 
solemnity of the cemetery's hallowed grounds. As one Gold Star mother 
put it--a Gold Star mother is someone who lost her son or daughter in 
battle--she said:

       We don't need tourists overlooking the funerals.

  So this arch, it distorts our very premise and soul of our country as 
a Republic. It disregards the separation of powers of our Constitution. 
It disrupts the air safety of DC's Reagan National Airport, and it 
disrespects the services and the sacrifices of our veterans buried at 
Arlington Cemetery.
  This legislation is very simple and cosponsored by several of my 
colleagues, and it simply says this: that this arch cannot be built 
without authorization in law.
  Mr. President, as if in legislative session, I ask unanimous consent 
that the Senate proceed to the immediate consideration of this bill at 
the desk and that the bill be considered read three times and passed 
and the motion to reconsider be considered made and laid upon the 
table.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Is there objection?
  The Senator from Utah.
  Mr. LEE. I object.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The objection is heard.
  Mr. MERKLEY. I would ask my colleague from Utah--and I appreciate his 
staying for this--if I reframe this, if I come back and say unanimous 
consent, if I come back tomorrow and say let's have debate for an hour 
on this and actually vote on it, would you object to that or would you 
approve that, let us debate it and consider it?
  Mr. LEE. I object.
  Mr. MERKLEY. Mr. President, I would like to think that at least, as 
those in the Senate carried the oath of our office to defend the 
prerogatives of our Constitution and separation of powers, we could at 
least debate such a bill rather than block it.


                       Unanimous Consent Request

  Mr. President, I have a second bill I would like to see us pass 
tonight by unanimous consent. To be summed up in a sentence, a memorial 
to honor a President of the United States, designated by an act of 
Congress, may not be demolished unless authorized by an act of 
Congress.
  This has, of course, to do with the President planning to demolish 
the Kennedy Center. Kennedy said:

       A nation reveals itself not only by the men it produces but 
     also by the men it honors, [and] the men it remembers.

  And those words have taken on special meaning this month. Following 
President Kennedy's assassination, Congress renamed the National 
Cultural Center the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts as a 
``living memorial'' to our slain President--a ``living memorial'' to 
our slain President.
  I was in second grade on the West Coast. The word came. Everything 
shut down immediately. The Nation wept. And this is the way we honored 
President Kennedy. It was a fitting memorial to a President who 
reminded us:

       The life of the arts, far from being an interruption, a 
     distraction, in the life of the nation, is close to the 
     center of a nation's purpose.

  But now we are in a chapter where President Trump wants to demolish 
this memorial. He wants to demolish this enormously incredible 
building, which hosts operas and plays and ballets, concerts, the 
Kennedy Center's Honors Program that is watched by millions of 
Americans every year celebrating our Nation's most revered artists.
  But the doors have been locked shut at the Kennedy Center. How did 
that happen? Last year, when President Trump won election, took office, 
he filled the Board of the Kennedy Center with loyalists. He installed 
himself as chairman. He renamed the institution after himself, not 
because he asked for legislation to do so but because, with his 
imperial mindset, he can put his name on anything he wants because he 
is the imperial ruler of our country.
  But we are not a nation with an imperial ruler, a man sitting on a 
throne to whom people must genuflect. We are a Republic. And there is a 
stark difference in the ways that the two Presidents' names were added 
to this building.
  In 1964, the U.S. Congress passed a law to remember President 
Kennedy's commitment to the arts. That was the legislation that made 
the Kennedy Center this living memorial.
  And then, in 2025, Donald Trump broke that law by renaming the 
Kennedy Center after himself. It is wrong--constitutionally, 
legislatively, morally.
  Morally, the Kennedy Center has been a place for generations where 
people of both parties go to celebrate the arts. They put aside their 
politics. They honor America's artists. They revel in incredible 
performances.
  Back in 1977, I dropped out of college for a year to be here, to be 
here during the first year of the Carter administration to see how a 
new administration would shape a change of course for our Nation.
  And during that year, I worked all these different odd jobs and 
volunteered for different groups. And I was volunteering for an 
environmental nonprofit. They were working on the Law of the Sea, a 
treaty that never has come before this Chamber almost a half century 
later, Law of the Sea. In

[[Page S5067]]

walks someone--they said: There is a concert tonight that pertains to 
the environment and the Law of the Sea. Would anybody like a free 
ticket?
  I said: Well, yes. Yes, I would. I would like to go.
  And I went that night. And I have never been in a venue like that. I 
was just kind of struck in awe. Lots of folks from Washington's elite 
were there--Senators and House Members--and sitting up in the box was 
President Carter. And Rostropovich played a cello concert. It was 
stunning. I mean, I had never heard a cello played except for the kind 
of slow, droning sounds of my sister when she was learning the cello. I 
mean, this was just amazing.
  And I have been there when President Trump was in the Presidential 
box. Politics set aside; American people rejoicing in the arts in this 
living memorial to President Kennedy, our President who was slain.
  Constitutionally, the separation of powers means no President can 
ignore laws passed by Congress, which is exactly what a Federal judge 
ruled on May 29 of this year. He said that the President cannot rename 
the Kennedy Center unless Congress approves it because Congress, by 
law, made the Kennedy Center a living memorial to the President--by 
law. And you can't undo the law unless you pass a new law.
  Immediately, Trump's handpicked Board tried to get around the judge's 
ruling by proposing to add the words: ``Restored and Renovated by 
President Donald J. Trump'' or by renaming the Kennedy Center's plaza, 
the President Trump Plaza. And the Federal judge, citing the law, on 
September 15 said:

       Simply put, defendants cannot install memorials for 
     President Trump or anyone or anything else at the Kennedy 
     Center without Congress' blessing.

  In other words, done by law.
  So it isn't about President Trump; it is about the principle that 
would apply to any President who was proposing breaking the law, and 
the President's court says you can't break the law. You want to do what 
you do? Go get a law that says you can do it.
  Apparently, that infuriated President Trump. A judge--how dare he 
tell him that he had to follow the law. After all, he is our King. He 
is our imperial leader. He is the ``on high'' who can do any damn thing 
he wants in this country. He can start wars when the Constitution says 
he can't start wars. He can spend money on projects where the 
Constitution says he can't spend money on projects.
  And, boy, did it infuriate him that a judge said he had to follow the 
law. How dare we have a judge stand in his way?
  So he called into the Kennedy Center Board meeting to scream at a 
Board member, Congresswomen Joyce Beatty. Beatty had filed the lawsuit 
defending the Kennedy Center.
  Minutes later, the Board voted to shut down the Kennedy Center for 2 
years because President Trump couldn't have his name on it.
  Oh, they said: Well, we will shut it down for renovations. You are 
shutting down the beating heart of our arts world that reverberates 
across America because the President can't put his name on it? What 
kind of children are serving on that Board? And what kind of childish 
tantrum was thrown by the President of the United States shutting down 
the Kennedy Center?
  Shortly afterward, Trump implied on Truth Social that the Kennedy 
Center would not reopen until he had his name on it.
  Let's be clear, this is an aging building. It needs maintenance. It 
needs to have old boilers replaced and electrical wiring updated and 
old seats modified and ceiling tiles, water damage fixed. There is a 
lot of work to do, standard types of maintenance. You don't shut down 
the building to do these types of maintenance. But he can't have his 
name on it. So he is shutting it down for 2 years.

  But the situation got a lot worse on September 16. Photojournalists 
captured images that show Trump looking at a large poster, talking 
about demolishing the Kennedy Center. The words said: ``Kennedy Center 
DEMOLISHED.''
  Trump believes that if he can't take over and name it after himself 
and swipe Kennedy's name off of it, no one can go to perform and no one 
can go to listen to someone perform. In fact, the building must not 
remain standing.
  So my bill tonight--this very short bill--simply says:

       A memorial to honor a President of the United States 
     designated by an Act of Congress may not be demolished unless 
     authorized by an Act of Congress.

  It is that simple. It is that straightforward--us standing up for the 
rule of law, the separation of powers, and the fact we are a Republic; 
and standing up for the fact that our Nation wept at the assassination 
of John Kennedy, and this is the memorial that we go to to remember 
him.
  So I ask, on behalf of myself and Senator Murkowski--she asked me to 
carry her spirit into this conversation tonight. So on her behalf and 
myself, and Senators Van Hollen and Reed and Whitehouse, the bill says: 
A memorial to honor a President designated by Congress may not be 
demolished unless the demolishment is authorized.
  President Kennedy said:

       I am certain that after the dust of centuries has passed 
     over our cities, we, too, will be remembered not for our 
     victories or our defeats in battle or politics, but for our 
     contribution to the human spirit.

  That is why we made the Kennedy Center the memorial to Kennedy, 
because of his belief in elevating the human spirit. Let us not let it 
be torn down.
  As if in legislative session, Mr. President, I ask unanimous consent 
that the Senate proceed to the immediate consideration of the bill at 
the desk that would prohibit the demolition of the Presidential 
memorials; that the bill be considered read three times and passed, and 
the motion to reconsider be considered made and laid upon the table.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Is there objection?
  Mr. LEE. I object.
  Mr. MERKLEY. Would my colleague object if I come back--
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Objection is heard. Objection is heard.
  Mr. MERKLEY. Thank you. I think I am recognized.
  Would my colleague from Utah object if I come back with a proposal 
that we debate this for a limited amount of time so that this Chamber 
can weigh in on defending our Constitution?
  Mr. LEE. I object.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Objection is heard.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from Utah.
```
