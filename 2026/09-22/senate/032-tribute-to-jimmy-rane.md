# TRIBUTE TO JIMMY RANE

**Speaking:** Britt, Katie Boyd (B001319) [R-AL]

```
                         TRIBUTE TO JIMMY RANE

● Mrs. BRITT. Mr. President, I am honored to recognize Jimmy 
Rane for his leadership in serving on the America250 Commission. As one 
of just 16 private citizens on this bipartisan commission, Jimmy's 
vision, patriotism, and direction helped ensure that our great Nation's 
semiquincentennial anniversary reflected both America's historic past 
and the promise of her future. At a time when our country can feel 
divided, the America250 celebrations gave us a chance to come together 
around our shared history founding principles. Jimmy was instrumental 
in reigniting that flame of allegiance as we look ahead to America's 
next 250 years.
  Jimmy understands the fulfillment of America's promises all too well. 
In 1907, his grandfather Giuseppe Reina journeyed to Ellis Island from 
his home of Cammarata, Sicily, in search of a better life for his young 
family. For years, he worked to build that life and instilled in his 
children, including Jimmy's father Tony, a deep sense of patriotism and 
pride in their adopted country. Jimmy's story--the grandson of an 
immigrant who arrived at Ellis Island now entrusted with the Nation's 
celebration of 250 years of independence--is the embodiment of the 
American Dream.
  Jimmy was born and raised in Abbeville, AL. His father, a World War 
II veteran, operated several hotels and restaurants and later served as 
the president of Abbeville's local chamber of commerce, teaching Jimmy 
the importance of hard work, drive, and giving back. Jimmy became a 
first-generation college graduate, earning his bachelor's degree in 
business administration from Auburn University, where he was a member 
of Theta Xi fraternity. He went on to earn his J.D. from the Cumberland 
School of Law at Samford University and began his career at a 
Birmingham law firm.
  Jimmy's path since then has been anything but conventional, but in 
every role he has poured in the same relentless drive to build the best 
possible product. After his wife's parents passed away, Jimmy returned 
to Abbeville to take over their lumber-treatment plant, turning the 
business from the brink of bankruptcy into Great Southern Wood, one of 
the largest companies in the industry, best known for its YellaWood 
brand. While running the company, Jimmy also opened his own law firm 
and served as a judge for Henry County. He later attended Harvard 
Business School's president management program, inspiring him to push 
Great Southern Wood to even greater heights.
  Instead of relocating Great Southern Wood to a larger market, Jimmy 
chose to keep the company's headquarters in Abbeville, bringing 
economic revitalization back to his hometown. He has since restored 
several of Abbeville's historical buildings, made a lasting impact on 
Auburn University through prodigious philanthropic efforts, and founded 
the Jimmy Rane Foundation, which has awarded over 600 scholarships over 
the past quarter century. Jimmy's greatest legacy is not simply his 
business success, but rather what he has chosen to do with it. His 
service and community pride are what made him an indispensable member 
of the America250 Commission.
  From an immigrant family's search for opportunity, to a young boy 
growing up in Abbeville, to an Auburn graduate, lawyer, entrepreneur, 
and now a leader in commemorating 250 years of American independence, 
Jimmy Rane's story is deeply and uniquely American. I am grateful for 
his service on the America250 Commission and for the time, energy, and 
patriotism he devoted to this historic undertaking. Alabama is honored 
to call him one of our own, and I am proud to recognize his indelible 
contributions to our State and to our Nation.●
```
