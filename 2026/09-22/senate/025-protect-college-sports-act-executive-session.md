# Protect College Sports Act (Executive Session)

**Speaking:** Cantwell, Maria (C000127) [D-WA], Cruz, Ted (C001098) [R-TX], Kelly, Mark (K000377) [D-AZ]

```
                       Protect College Sports Act

  Ms. CANTWELL. Mr. President, I come to the floor because we are going 
to be voting shortly on two things this afternoon: one on a judicial 
nominee and, second, on cloture on the underlying substitute of the 
Protect College Sports Act. I just wanted to make sure that we were 
talking to colleagues about changes that were in that underlying 
substitute that we have been able to make.
  I think earlier today, at 3 or 3:30, was the deadline for filing 
amendments. So we obviously have been working all weekend and 
processing amendments that were filed last week, and now we will work 
on amendments that are filed this week and, hopefully, make progress 
with our colleagues on a bipartisan basis.
  But as we continue to move through the legislative process on the 
Protect College Sports Act, we have sought to clarify and improve the 
bill in ways that make clear to our colleagues the intent of what the 
Senate Commerce Committee and now those here on the Senate floor have 
tried to achieve in this legislation.
  The substitute makes clear that medical coverage and healthcare and 
safety standards in the bill are a floor and not a ceiling. So it 
amends sections 106 and 107 to clarify that it cannot be construed to 
preclude provisions of additional medical coverage or establishment of 
additional health, wellness, and safety standards in the bill.
  It requires that the NCAA take more responsibility in school 
compliance with health, safety, and wellness standards; and I 
appreciate this very much because I think these actions do need to have 
someone who can continue to improve the accountability of our academic 
institutions and how they are performing or not performing. It requires 
a Commission that we put into

[[Page S4849]]

the legislation to also study further health, wellness, and safety 
standards and consider ways to improve them.
  It is making progress in a couple of other areas: the academic 
progress requirement, which I think is really important. This bill is 
to make sure that we still have student-athletes and they meet and 
achieve academic performance. So this requires an institution to comply 
with the uniform benchmarks established by the Intercollegiate Athletic 
Association or conference related to graduation rates and the academic 
process of student-athletes in order to be eligible for this retention 
fund.
  So we are putting in more safeguards and accountability. If these 
institutions want to continue to have additional resources, they are 
going to have to prove that they are meeting academic performance.
  It prevents the NCAA from weakening its standards for qualifying as a 
full-time student by cross-referencing terms and definitions.
  So, again, there are lots of eyeballs on this legislation, and it is 
good because we are making and perfecting and ensuring that the 
language does what we think it does; and, if not, we are improving it 
by making changes.
  The substitute also includes a role for the House and Senate 
Education and Labor Committees in the selection of Commission members. 
It does this by requiring the House and Senate leadership and the 
Senate Commerce and House Energy and Commerce leadership to work with 
the Senate HELP and House Ed and Workforce leadership in appointing the 
members of the Commission.
  Now, this legislation does try to stabilize the college environment 
today as it relates to sports, but it also says we need to have a big 
discussion about how this continues to play out in the future. And this 
Commission needs to be well represented by a variety of interests. So 
we are very happy to add both HELP and House Education and Workforce 
leadership to be part of that.
  And it requires the Commission to study how and whether students 
should get a financial stake in their own teams. Now, this is a novel 
idea but something I think the Commission should consider. One of the 
things I am very proud of in the underlying bill is the fact that we 
say to broadcast entities who bid on college sports--most of the time 
just to show football or just to show basketball--that if you are not 
using your rights for those same institutions to help support other 
programs--like, say women's volleyball or track and field--that you 
have to allow that institution to come up with other ways that they 
might enhance their revenue by doing streaming media.
  Now, we think this is really important because we think there are a 
lot of great--I am very proud of, right now, that, I think, our UW 
women's pole vaulter is No. 1 in the world right now. I think I just 
saw that headline. But why can't the University of Washington promote 
their track and field if, in fact, their football Huskies contract, 
which--I would just say, I saw a lot of Big Ten football this weekend, 
trying to engage and understand, but you didn't see anything about the 
Huskies other than if you tried to watch that game. Why? Because nobody 
on the east coast even reports about what happens to west coast 
football. After a certain time, they just don't report about it. This 
bill is about trying to empower these other sports--particularly that 
drive women and Olympic sports--to make their own revenue streams.
  So one of our colleagues here wanted an improvement to the bill that 
said that students should even think about how to get a bigger 
financial stake. I welcome it. I welcome that as part of a Commission 
requirement.
  We also did a few things to make sure that we are covering pending 
cases, that the underlying bill does not wipe out pending cases. And so 
we took language recommended by a variety of our athletes working on 
this prior to us even moving the bill out of committee, since the bill 
moved out of committee, and this, I think, helped us perfect this 
language to make sure that existing pending cases are not wiped out by 
our legislation.
  And it obviously also narrows the broad field of preemption 
provisions for eligibility to just those covered in this legislative 
section.
  So this legislation before us today continues to make improvements. 
As I said, I think there are about 15 or 20 amendments that were filed 
last week and maybe another 20-plus amendments filed today. So we are 
going to continue to work through our colleagues on that but continue 
to push forward on the very challenging environment that college 
education is facing and the huge amounts of add-on fees that they are 
adding to nonstudent-athletes to pay for athletics, taking money out of 
a general fund to pay for athletics--basically, taking out an endowment 
to pay for athletics. The majority of these are not even making money 
anymore for themselves.
  So we have to bring some order to the system, and I urge my 
colleagues to continue to improve the situation so that we are not 
balancing the books on behalf of larger education just for sports but 
that they have a system that will work for all of us.
  So I encourage my colleagues to continue the dialogue with us, and I 
hope that we can move forward on this important legislation.
  I yield the floor.
  I suggest the absence of a quorum.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The clerk will call the roll.
  The legislative clerk proceeded to call the roll.
  Mr. CRUZ. Mr. President, I ask unanimous consent that the order for 
the quorum call be rescinded.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Without objection, it is so ordered.
  Mr. CRUZ. Mr. President, I ask unanimous consent to speak for up to 5 
minutes prior to the scheduled rollcall vote.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Without objection, it is so ordered.
```
