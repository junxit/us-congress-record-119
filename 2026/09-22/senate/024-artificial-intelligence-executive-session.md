# Artificial Intelligence (Executive Session)

**Speaking:** Kelly, Mark (K000377) [D-AZ], Tuberville, Tommy (T000278) [R-AL]

```
                        Artificial Intelligence

  Mr. KELLY. Mr. President, I happen to be the last person to sit in 
the commander's seat of Space Shuttle Endeavour. It is hard to describe 
how it feels to be strapped to 4\1/2\ million pounds of fuel, waiting 
for three main engines and two solid rocket boosters to ignite 
underneath you.
  It gives you clarity because flying that thing--it is not one 
dangerous moment. It is continuous. There is always a catastrophic 
failure lurking around every corner.
  And when things go sideways on you and you are troubleshooting a 
cooling issue while managing an electrical failure, while mission 
control is telling you something that contradicts your instruments, it 
requires that you are keenly aware of what is happening.
  You figure out how to separate the signal from the noise. So you pay 
attention to the data, and you do your very best to understand what is 
happening with a very complicated machine. You ignore the noise, you 
evaluate the numbers, and you work the problem. And you listen when the 
alarms are telling you something isn't right.
  These last few weeks--as engineers, researchers, and CEOs have all 
been shouting about AI becoming a threat to humanity's very survival--I 
feel like I am back in that seat with the master alarm blaring and 
systems failing. And nobody is steering the ship.
  While the people inside the industry discuss catastrophic damage and 
the chance that AI ``will kill us all by the end of the decade,'' the 
White House is actively lobbying against regulation. Our President 
calls Americans' fears a ``hoax'' and a ``SICK conspiracy.'' He says 
that we don't need new guardrails, and Speaker Johnson says that 
Congress isn't qualified to make them. Instead, he wants a summit--
someday--but, in the meantime, we will revisit it after a 7-week 
recess.
  When the master alarm is flashing, you don't write a strongly worded 
letter. You don't form a committee. You definitely don't leave your 
post for a 7-week vacation. And you don't make some ridiculous comment, 
like what the President did, that we will have a--I think he said a 
little ``gear'' that will fix everything.
  What does that even mean?
  So here is what you do: You act. You step up, and you be the leader 
that the crew needs. You make smart decisions based on real data.
  I am not saying this isn't complicated. The United States has been--
and has to be--at the forefront of technology, as we have been for 
generations. AI is important to our national security, and it is 
important to a thriving economy, and we need to find a path forward 
that protects Americans and our national interests.
  I am also not saying we abandon innovation. AI could unlock benefits 
for the American people, from curing diseases to solving big 
engineering problems that unlock new industries and new jobs.
  But we can't expect corporations to help us out of the goodness of 
their hearts. That is why I am introducing the Make AI Work for 
Americans Act, to make sure workers aren't left behind and that Big 
Tech pays their fair share.
  These companies are getting rich off of a technology trained on the 
wealth of humanity's research, knowledge, and work--our ideas and 
discoveries.
  My bill would require them to give some of that wealth back to us by 
investing in the American worker through an AI Horizon Fund. The fund 
would use the money we make from holding the tech industry accountable 
and invest it in training workers, paid service-to-career 
opportunities, and support for those whose jobs just disappear.
  It will ensure Americans are prepared and have a choice about what 
their future looks like--and what it looks like in the next generation 
of the economy.
  We don't have to choose between leading on innovation and protecting 
workers. We can do both.
  That doesn't mean it will be easy. Our country is polarized. Those of 
us in the Senate don't agree on a lot of things, just like the 
Americans we represent have different perspectives and positions. But 
they agree on this: 80 percent of Americans want us to step in and 
regulate AI--80 percent.
  I hear it from Arizonans every day. They are worried. They wake up 
wondering if today is the day their job just gets automated away, 
wondering if their kids are going to have careers in the future--or 
have a future at all.
  Americans across the spectrum want us to step in--Republicans, 
Democrats, Independents, all of them. The tech industry itself, 
communities worried about data centers, families who just want to be 
able to sleep at night--all of those groups would like regulation.
  It seems that the only people who don't want guardrails happen to be 
working in the White House.
  So what is the holdup?
  A lot of folks seem to have forgotten what Congress is for. They seem 
to have lost faith that we can do hard things.
  Well, let me remind you: When our financial system was collapsing in 
1933 and F.D.R. asked for help, Congress passed the Emergency Banking 
Relief Act, and it did it in 1 day.
  The International Atomic Energy Agency, or the IAEA--the U.N. body 
that helps prevent the spread of nuclear weapons--was started right 
here in the United States, made possible by Congress.
  The Civil Rights Act, which reversed decades of racial 
disenfranchisement, was passed in just a few months, once Congress 
finally began listening to those fighting for justice.
  It is not a question of if we have the power to do big and hard 
things. It is if we have the courage.
  So to those in Washington who think Congress can't or shouldn't take 
on this problem, let me ask you: Why are you here?
  This is the job. We weren't elected to just raise money or to trade 
stocks or to rubberstamp a President's agenda. We were elected to make 
people's lives better, and that is what leaders do.
  If we act now on AI, we can avoid a catastrophe and shape a future 
with more opportunity, with more security, with more of the things we 
want and need.
  The alarms are sounding. The signals are clear. And nobody else is 
coming to the rescue. It has to be us.
  So let's be the leaders that this country elected. Let's get this 
done now. And as we say in the U.S. Navy, don't give up the ship.
  I yield the floor.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from Washington.
```
