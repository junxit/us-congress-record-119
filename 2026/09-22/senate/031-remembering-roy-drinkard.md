                        REMEMBERING ROY DRINKARD

● Mrs. BRITT. Mr. President, today I rise to remember the 
incredibly full and long life of Mr. Roy Drinkard, an Alabama and 
American legend. Throughout his 106 years on Earth, Roy's joy, 
kindness, and drive touched the lives of so many. His dedication to 
business strengthened our communities, especially his home of Cullman; 
his military service inspired many Americans across our Nation; and his 
love for others impacted all who had the honor of knowing him.
  A native of Falkville, AL, Roy grew up with three brothers and two 
sisters, all of whom learned the importance of hard work, public 
service, and a strong love for their community. Roy's father was a 
notable businessman who also served as the mayor of Falkville. Roy's 
own passion for business started from a young age when he began 
delivering newspapers at age 9. He attended St. Bernard Preparatory 
School and later graduated from Gumpton-Jones College of Mortuary 
Science.
  After his graduation, Roy served as a private first class for the 
U.S. Marine Corps during World War II. Roy was incredibly proud of his 
service to our country, and at the time of his death, he was the oldest 
living marine in the United States. His bravery, perseverance, and love 
for our Nation were driving forces behind his exceptional military 
service. Though his service was just the beginning of his career, Roy 
was a marine until his final breath; honorable, courageous, and 
committed.
  After his time in the Marines, Roy began working at the family 
funeral services business. He was a trained funeral director and 
mortician, and under his family's leadership, the business grew 
precipitously, serving families all across north Alabama. During the 
most difficult moments of someone's life, Roy lent a helping hand, a 
listening ear, and a space to grieve. It takes a special person to work 
in the funeral industry, and Roy was certainly that. His compassion and 
guidance brought consolation to so many.
  When Roy returned to Cullman, he purchased the historic Cullman home, 
the Borkenau. It became the backdrop to so many occasions, from family 
reunions to business meetings to holiday parties. In Cullman, Roy began 
working in the auto industry, operating the Drinkard Chevrolet Company 
dealership from 1955 until 1974. For Roy, car sales were never about 
the cars; they were about the people he met, the deals he created, and 
the problems he solved.
  Using his passion for people and business, Roy began Drinkard 
Development, a commercial real estate management company. Drinkard 
Development's early projects included Cullman's Town Square Shopping 
Center and King Edward Shopping Center. Soon, the business expanded 
into neighboring counties and even beyond Alabama into Tennessee, 
Kentucky, Louisiana, Ohio, and more. Soon, Roy and his partners became 
leading developers in the Nation, working as head developers for Kmart 
in 1981 and for Kroger in 1982. He worked with numerous prominent 
retailers including Walmart, Kroger, and AMC Theatres. After becoming 
an approved developer for the General Services Administration, he also 
helped to develop government facilities throughout the Southeast 
region. Through it all, Roy viewed Drinkard Development as more than 
just a job; it was a calling and one that gave him an even larger 
family.
  Roy's devotion to his Cullman community extended far beyond Drinkard 
Development. Roy was among the original members of the Flying Fifty, 
business and civic leaders known for traveling throughout the country 
to attract new industry and investment to the area. He devoted nearly 
three decades to leading the Cullman Industrial Development Board and 
held numerous other local leadership positions, including president of 
the Cullman County Chamber of Commerce and chairman of the Cullman 
Planning Commission. His work helped create jobs, strengthen the local 
economy, and shape the community he loved. His commitment to service 
was also evident through his nearly 84 years as a Freemason and through 
his longtime membership in First Baptist Church of Cullman.
  Expanding access to education was another cornerstone of Roy's public 
service. He served as a director of what is now Wallace State Community 
College and later chaired the Wallace State Foundation, helping expand 
opportunities for students through scholarships, donating land, and 
other initiatives. In 2000, then-Governor Don Siegelman appointed Roy 
to the Troy University Board of Trustees, beginning more than 25 years 
of service to a university he loved deeply. He remained devoted to 
Troy, its students and leaders, and its football program, continuing to 
serve on the board even through his 100th birthday.
  Yet the accomplishments that filled Roy's resume never overshadowed 
what mattered most to him: family and friendship. He treasured fishing 
trips with his daughters, grandchildren, and great-grandchildren, 
annual gatherings at Buck Island, visits to New Orleans, weekly 
``Pawpaw's Night,'' and countless conversations around a fire or on a 
porch. He kept up with the milestones of his great-grandchildren, 
celebrated their accomplishments, and was never

[[Page S4853]]

too busy for a phone call or a story. At age 100, he even joined his 
great-grandson Julius's fraternity, another reminder of his curiosity, 
humor, and determination to remain connected to the generations that 
followed him.
  Roy often summed up the wisdom of his 106 years with the simple 
command from the Gospel of John: to love one another. That principle 
captured the heart of a man who measured his life not only by the 
businesses he built, the communities he strengthened, and the 
institutions he served, but most importantly by the people he loved and 
the relationships he carried with him across generations. He leaves 
behind his daughters, grandchildren, great-grandchildren, and great-
great-granddaughter, along with countless relatives, friends, 
colleagues, and members of his Drinkard Development family. Roy 
Drinkard's extraordinary life was marked by more than a century of 
accomplishments; his most profound impact is reflected in the 
remarkable number of people who knew and loved him.
  I am incredibly proud to honor the incredible life of Mr. Roy 
Drinkard, whose life serves as a reminder to greet each day with joy, 
love, and humor. His legacy and service to our great Nation will live 
on in Cullman, across our great State of Alabama, and throughout his 
family, friends, and colleagues.●
