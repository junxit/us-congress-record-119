# China (Executive Session)

**Speaking:** Wicker, Roger F. (W000437) [R-MS], Budd, Ted (B001305) [R-NC]

```
                                 China

  Mr. President, this week, President Xi Jinping will visit the United 
States for bilateral meetings at a pivotal moment for United States-
China relations. During his two terms in office, President Trump has 
been clear that under his leadership, the United States must engage 
with China given the reality of our economic relationship, but we can't 
forget that China is also a competitor and an adversary.
  The Chinese Communist Party's malign influence on the world stage is

[[Page S4843]]

growing rapidly, and American citizens are paying the price. America's 
economy, our fundamental values, and our security--both at home and 
abroad--have been challenged by the CCP.
  I have long believed that a whole-of-government approach is necessary 
to address this, and I am grateful that President Donald Trump is at 
the helm for these crucial negotiations.
  China is our most consequential geopolitical competitor, and we 
cannot afford to ignore the CCP's ambitions to reorganize global order 
at the expense of the United States or to underestimate the CCP's 
growing and rapidly progressing hard and soft power influence around 
the world to execute this goal.
  When President Xi enters the White House later this week, I hope that 
discussions between our two nations include America's most fundamental 
values and responsibilities, including the protection of American 
citizens abroad and reiterating a zero tolerance for violating the God-
given right to religious freedom.
  Let me be clear, there is no excuse for the CCP to unjustly target 
American citizens abroad or their families, harass religious groups, or 
carry out a broad-sweeping war on faith in China.
  To this day, eight leaders of Zion Church remain behind bars in China 
because of China's attack on religious freedom. These detainments are 
egregious, and they can't be ignored. China's persecution of Christians 
and other religious groups must also not go unaddressed. And that is 
why, during my time in the Senate, I have sought to implement strict 
sanctions on any CCP official who is responsible for or directly 
carries out violations of religious freedom.
  Under President Trump's leadership, we have already proven to China 
that the United States has zero tolerance for religious persecution, 
and we have seen some notable results.
  At the United States-China summit this past May, President Trump 
personally raised the case of Zion Church pastor Ezra Jin with 
President Xi, and 2 months later, Pastor Jin was released and returned 
here to U.S. soil, where he was reunited with his family after years of 
unjust detention.
  Now, while we are deeply grateful for Pastor Jin's return, we must 
never stop fighting to bring home the numerous pastors still unjustly 
detained and to defend the freedom to worship around the world.
  China's pattern of wrongfully detaining Americans or relatives of 
American citizens is not solely limited to religious crackdowns. I also 
want to highlight the bipartisan effort of Senator Markey and I have 
taken to bring home both Dr. Youlin Chen and Mr. Min Zin, American 
academics who are currently being held in brutal and inhumane 
conditions.
  I am grateful for this body's bicameral, bipartisan support in 
condemning the People's Republic of China's repeated pattern of 
arbitrary or wrongful detention of U.S. citizens.
  Here at home, China's influence and key role in the illicit drug 
supply chain have resulted in the loss of far too many American lives 
and the destruction of families and stolen livelihoods.
  I have met with far too many families changed forever by the impacts 
of the drug epidemic. Through buying up American farmland, malign 
actors have brought the production of dangerous hemp-derived products 
into our communities, and our children are paying the price.
  And by partnering with transnational criminal organizations, Chinese-
connected companies have intentionally made highly addictive, 
intoxicating substances--like fentanyl or illicit vapes--readily 
accessible to Americans.
  But make no mistake, China is aware of the consequences of its 
actions, and that is why Beijing has no problem extending law 
enforcement and counternarcotics cooperation to other nations to 
mitigate these outcomes. But China products are still flooding American 
markets in what looks more and more like a reverse opium war, and it 
needs to end.
  Just last week, the Trump administration listed the PRC as 1 of 23 
major drug-transit and producing countries. And we have rightfully 
planted the flag, and we must now demand action to dismantle this 
criminal enterprise that is preying on American people. Ahead of his 
meetings, President Trump is right to raise this issue directly with 
President Xi.
  The American people cannot be blind to China's efforts to threaten 
our national security, especially since the PRC's growing economic and 
strategic relationship with adversaries like Russia, Iran, and North 
Korea have had sweeping consequences.
  Earlier this year, at the United States-China summit in Beijing, 
President Trump and President Xi agreed that Iran can never possess a 
nuclear weapon, and the Strait of Hormuz must remain open for the free 
flow of global energy.
  But if Beijing wants to maintain a productive relationship with 
Washington, it must recognize the consequences of continuing to partner 
with adversarial nations that fundamentally defy U.S. interests on the 
world stage.
  Recent reports suggest that China provided Iran with intelligence, 
including satellite imagery that led to the death of three American 
servicemembers. A PRC state-owned firm has reportedly supplied Russia 
with materials for kamikaze drones, violating sanctions. The PRC has 
reportedly supplied Iran with air defense equipment, among other goods, 
bypassing more sanctions. This behavior cannot continue.

  America's national security must remain at the center of every 
discussion shaping the future of United States-China relations, 
including the race to develop technologies that will define this next 
century.
  We are in a race with China to develop the next generation of 
advanced manufacturing technology, quantum computing, space travel, and 
satellite technology and--perhaps most consequentially--artificial 
intelligence.
  China's cyber threats to the U.S. Government have been well 
documented and damaging to our government and private sector even 
before the rise of artificial intelligence.
  The AI race is not simply about who builds the most powerful model, 
it is about who is going to set the standards and safeguards and norms 
that will govern one of the most consequential technologies of our 
time.
  Beijing's national AI strategy calls for China to reach world-leading 
levels in artificial intelligence by 2030 and surpass the United 
States, and that reality should alarm all of us--especially given that 
these models are being developed with fundamentally different political 
systems.
  China is developing and deploying AI within its authoritarian system 
that employs censorship and state surveillance. In dramatic contrast, 
our models are being developed under constitutional principles that 
promote free speech, value individualism, and encourage private 
enterprise.
  As the Senator from North Carolina, a State with a growing tech 
industry, thriving pharmaceutical manufacturing, a pivotal financial 
sector, and a critical agricultural industry, my State represents many 
of the successes that the PRC aspires to. Failure to maintain American 
dominance in these industries threatens to leave our nation vulnerable 
and decimate the economic growth and development that has benefited 
North Carolina and many others.
  I rise today to urge this body and our entire Federal Government to 
engage with China with their eyes wide open and without compromising 
our values, our security, or our strategic national interests, not 
because we expect our differences to just disappear but because those 
differences are too consequential to ignore.
  We must continue to invest strategically in our national defense and 
prioritize AI engineering that also promotes safety. And as a nation, 
the United States is the most uniquely positioned to hold China 
accountable for exporting deadly drugs, evading sanctions in support of 
Iran and Russia, and for waging war on religion. So in that vein, 
Congress cannot rest in its vigilance to contain the China threat.
  I yield the floor.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from Mississippi.
  Mr. WICKER. Mr. President, Chinese President Xi Jinping arrives in 
town tomorrow for a highly anticipated state visit. Quite frankly, had 
the White House asked me for advice, I would have suggested the 
President not

[[Page S4844]]

invite Xi Jinping to Washington for such a lavish welcome here in the 
United States of America based on all of the troubling issues we have 
with President Xi and the Chinese Communist Party. But in light of the 
fact that the Chinese Communist President is coming to the United 
States, I want to take a moment to suggest a list of topics that the 
President might mention during the summit.
  As we know, a proper state visit requires a proper state dinner. 
Again, I would not have scheduled such a thing. But as guests converse 
over the meal, the President might inquire about the purposes of 
China's massive military buildup or about China's clear support for 
Iran.
  This summer, it was all over the press that China gave Iran satellite 
imagery intelligence, and that intelligence led to the death of 
American troops. So I would certainly suggest that the President try to 
get an answer to that.
  While President Trump and President Xi are talking, perhaps Mr. Trump 
could bring up the Uighur people. For nearly a decade, Xi Jinping has 
consigned more than a million Uighurs to concentration camps in China's 
Xinjiang region. I hope our President will take this visit as an 
opportunity to advocate for the Uighur people. The fact that they 
remain in prison by the millions is a grave injustice and a reminder of 
Xi Jinping's many human rights abuses.
  Then, after asking about the Uighur people, the President should 
mention China's persecution of all manner of religious groups, to 
include Tibetan Buddhists, Christians, and those practicing Falun Gong. 
In July, President Trump successfully advocated for the release of 
Pastor Ezra Jin. Why not suggest releasing all imprisoned Christians 
and allowing all people of whatever faith to worship freely?
  Then President Trump might ask why China openly coerces Japan, 
Taiwan, and the Philippines. The Chinese dictator works hard to 
intimidate America's allies in the East China Sea, the Taiwan Strait, 
and the Second Thomas Shoal. China also bullies, jails, and otherwise 
silences dissidents.
  When meeting with Xi in May, President Trump advocated for Jimmy Lai, 
the Hong Kong newspaper publisher Xi has kept locked up in solitary 
confinement. Why has Mr. Lai been locked up? His crime was speaking 
freely in Hong Kong. I hope Mr. Trump will again ask for the release of 
the frail, elderly Mr. Lai and all those imprisoned by the Chinese 
Communist Party in Hong Kong and elsewhere.
  Perhaps over the salad course of the dinner, our President could ask 
Xi Jinping about the Chinese navy's harassment of Filipino fishermen 
and sailors and about China's abuse of its neighbors. Perhaps he could 
ask Mr. Xi why he invents military disputes and then resolves them by 
military force--or attempts to resolve them.
  Then I hope the President will inquire about Xi Jinping's 
interference in the internal affairs of nations around the world. 
Perhaps he could bring up Chinese exploitation of African countries and 
Latin American nations with debt-trap diplomacy and election meddling.
  While the servers are switching from chardonnay to cabernet at the 
fancy state dinner, perhaps the President could turn to Xi's so-called 
reunification of China with Taiwan, his wish that Taiwan become a part 
of mainland China. In the strong Chinese leader's view of the future, 
should the freely elected Government of Taiwan expect the same 
treatment that the people of Hong Kong have experienced--the loss of 
their freedom that the world has seen over the past three decades since 
reunification with Hong Kong.
  I know the President intends to discuss trade and artificial 
intelligence with the Chinese leadership--and rightfully so. Perhaps 
some progress will be made.
  But, Mr. President, during every minute of dialogue, our Commander in 
Chief should keep in mind that his guest is a brutal, unelected, and 
oppressive dictator who seeks to dominate his neighbors and whose 
massive military arsenal is aimed directly at the United States of 
America.
  I yield the floor.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from Washington.
```
