# China (Executive Session)

**Speaking:** Durbin, Richard J. (D000563) [D-IL]

```
                                 China

  Mr. President, tomorrow Chinese President Xi Jinping will visit 
Washington and meet with President Trump. The meeting between the 
leaders of our two countries is important and timely. Both nations have 
a great deal to discuss, including in areas of shared concern and 
disagreement.
  I met regularly with the Chinese Ambassador during my time in the 
Senate, and while we often--often--disagree, I welcome our continued 
engagements as constructive and helpful in avoiding conflict between 
the two great powers.
  Certainly, our nations have many areas of shared concern, including 
the dangers of AI research, North Korea's pursuit of nuclear weapons, 
climate change and energy supplies, global economic stability, and 
reduced tensions in and around the South Pacific.
  And we undoubtedly have points of continued disagreement on issues 
such as the future of Taiwan, Chinese harassment of allies, the war in 
Ukraine, economic espionage, and stemming fentanyl precursors from 
being exported by China, and of course, basic political freedoms and 
human rights.
  Earlier this year, the Senate unanimously passed a resolution I led 
with Senator Ted Cruz, a Republican from Texas, calling for the release 
of several notable political prisoners being held in China.
  Not long after, and with the assistance of President Trump, Pastor 
Ezra Jin was released after 266 days in a Chinese prison for the crime 
of operating a church.
  I had the pleasure of hosting Pastor Jin and his daughter Grace, who 
was tireless in her effort to plead her father's case. She came to the 
Capitol after his release for a moving discussion and meeting. The 
pastor reminded us that at least eight of his congregants remain in 
prison.
  I welcome President Xi's release of Pastor Jin and call on him to 
similarly release these remaining congregants, as well as the following 
mentioned in our Senate resolution. They are up here, four of them: Dr. 
Gulshan Abbas, Jimmy Lai, Gao Quanfu and his wife Pang Yu, and Ekpar 
Asat. They are all noted on this poster.
  President Trump has a chance to secure the release of these 
detainees, who have already suffered so much, and I hope he will 
separately make the request to Xi during his visit. Such a move by Xi 
would be a most welcome gesture, particularly since there is no reason 
to fear these people. They have done nothing but exercise peaceful 
freedom of religion and expression.
  And to those still detained and their heartbroken families, I want to 
remind them that they are not forgotten.


                      Wedding of Donald Trump, Jr.

  Finally, today I received a letter from my colleague from Utah  John 
Curtis, a Republican from Utah. He is from Provo. He is formerly the 
mayor. We have gone together on trips. Our families know one another, 
and I like John. Our politics notwithstanding, there are many things we 
agree on. He sent a letter raising a question as to whether there has 
been a violation of ethical standards at the highest level of our 
government.
  Now, John showed real courage in doing this. As a Republican, to say 
anything critical of the Trump administration is a risk. But he has 
been a brave man, and he is a man of principle.
  What he called on is for Chairman Chuck Grassley of Iowa and myself, 
as ranking Democratic member of the Senate Judiciary Committee, to have 
an investigation and a hearing of Donald Trump, Jr.'s recent wedding, 
where he received lavish gifts from a Russian oligarch close to 
Vladimir Putin. This sort of thing cannot be allowed to happen without 
some response from Congress, and Senator Curtis is right: The Senate 
Judiciary Committee is the place to start.
  I know some will argue that we only have a few months left in this 
year. But let me tell you something: We are not overworking ourselves 
during those few months. We have enough resources and staff and talent, 
on a bipartisan basis, to call Donald Trump, Jr., before the committee 
for a deposition to answer some basic questions about who paid for his 
wedding ceremony.
  Now, in fairness, Senator Curtis also asked the same of Hunter Biden. 
To be fair to Hunter Biden, who has already responded to say he is 
willing to appear wherever we wish, he has already been subject to a 
sworn deposition at length and has answered the questions that were 
propounded to him. But there has been no such effort made by the Trump 
family to explain the largess of gifts being given to members of his 
family, not to mention the $1.4 billion which he was given--the 
President was given--in income in his first year of the second term.
  These are legitimate questions about corruption. There was a time 
when both parties would agree that it was unacceptable for this to 
happen, regardless of whether the origin was Democrat or Republican. 
That still applies.
  Senator Curtis is right. I stand by his request of the Senate 
Judiciary Committee to respond to this request for deposition and 
investigation.
  We may not be able to finish it this year, but shame on us if we 
won't even consider the issue, as timely as it is.
  It is important that this Senate function to uphold the ethical 
standards of our government, and this is a classic example at the 
highest levels of our government.
  I yield the floor.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from North Carolina.
```
