# COMMEMORATING THE SENECA NATION OF INDIANS AND THE SIGNING OF THE 1842 BUFFALO CREEK TREATY

**Speaking:** Kennedy, Timothy M. (K000402) [D-NY]

```
COMMEMORATING THE SENECA NATION OF INDIANS AND THE SIGNING OF THE 1842 
                          BUFFALO CREEK TREATY

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. TIMOTHY M. KENNEDY

                              of new york

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, August 20, 2026

  Mr. KENNEDY of New York. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize the 
Seneca Nation of Indians as they commemorate the signing of the 1842 
Buffalo Creek Treaty. Signed in a partnership agreement between the 
United States and the Seneca Nation of Indians, the Buffalo Creek 
Treaty ensured that the culture and heritage of the Seneca Nation 
remain on their rightful land in the Allegany and Cattaraugus 
Reservations.
  Signed nearly 184 years ago in Western New York, the 1842 Buffalo 
Creek Treaty restored the Seneca Nation's possession of Cattaraugus and 
Allegany territories and reaffirmed the nation's right to self-govern 
and protect their land. Through unwavering unity and leadership, Seneca 
Nation leaders worked to ensure that the Indigenous population of 
Western New York was not displaced and that their culture and tradition 
survived and preserved. This treaty marked a defining moment in the 
preservation of Seneca sovereignty and identity. The dedication of the 
Seneca people to their heritage, community, and future ensures the 
well-being, prosperity, and sovereignty of future generations.
  Today, as we commemorate 184 years since the signing of the Buffalo 
Creek Treaty, we look toward the future with hope and acknowledge the 
struggles and sacrifices of the past. The 1842 Buffalo Creek Treaty 
remains a lasting symbol of sovereignty, resilience, and cultural 
respect. As the Seneca people continue to strengthen their communities 
and protect their heritage, we recognize their enduring legacy of 
perseverance and leadership and celebrate their many contributions to 
Western New York and the United States.
```
