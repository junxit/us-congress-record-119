# HONORING THE LIFE AND SERVICE OF ARMY SPECIALIST DAVID A. WILKEY, JR.

**Speaking:** Bergman, Jack (B001301) [R-MI]

```
 HONORING THE LIFE AND SERVICE OF ARMY SPECIALIST DAVID A. WILKEY, JR.

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. JACK BERGMAN

                              of michigan

                    in the house of representatives

                       Friday, September 4, 2026

  Mr. BERGMAN. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life and service 
of Army Specialist David Anthony Wilkey, Jr., a native of Michigan's 
Upper Peninsula, who made the ultimate sacrifice in service to our 
Nation during Operation Iraqi Freedom.
  Specialist Wilkey was born in Norway, Michigan, and grew up nearby in 
Wilson. As a teenager, he moved to Elkhart, Indiana. After graduating 
from Jimtown High School in 2003, David worked alongside his father at 
Plastic Components Inc. before answering the call to serve and 
enlisting in the United States Army. He was assigned to the 1st 
Battalion, 28th Infantry Regiment, 4th Infantry Brigade, 1st Infantry 
Division, based at Fort Riley, Kansas, and deployed to Iraq in support 
of Operation Iraqi Freedom.
  On June 17, 2007, while traveling with a Humvee convoy in Baghdad, an 
improvised explosive device detonated near David's unit, leaving him 
with wounds that would claim his life the following day. On June 18, 
2007, at just 22 years old, Specialist Wilkey gave his life in defense 
of our Nation and in service to his fellow Soldiers.
  Those who knew David remember him as someone who was always willing 
to help others. He was the kind of person who was always ready to lend 
a hand, smooth out rough edges, and make a friend wherever he went. He 
enjoyed hunting, fishing, camping, traveling, sports, repairing things, 
shopping, and poetry. Most importantly, he was a devoted husband, 
father, son, and friend who remained deeply connected to his loved ones 
even while serving thousands of miles from home.
  David was ultimately laid to rest in Powers, Michigan, near the Upper 
Peninsula community where he grew up. Though his military service took 
him far from home, he returned to the place that helped shape him and 
where his memory remains part of the community which he called home.
  Mr. Speaker, may we always remember Specialist David A. Wilkey, Jr., 
honor his service, and never forget that the freedoms we enjoy were 
defended by brave Americans like him.
```
