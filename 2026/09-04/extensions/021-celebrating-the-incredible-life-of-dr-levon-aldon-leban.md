# CELEBRATING THE INCREDIBLE LIFE OF DR. LEVON ALDON LeBAN

**Speaking:** Carter, Troy A. (C001125) [D-LA]

```
        CELEBRATING THE INCREDIBLE LIFE OF DR. LEVON ALDON LeBAN

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. TROY A. CARTER

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                       Friday, September 4, 2026

  Mr. CARTER of Louisiana. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to celebrate and 
honor the life of a remarkable humanitarian, community servant, human 
rights activist, and my fellow Kappa Alpha Psi brother, someone I 
considered to be a dear friend, the late Dr. Levon Aldon LeBan. His 
commitment to social justice transformed Louisiana and had a profound 
impact on this entire Nation. A United States Army veteran, minister, 
and retired educator, he served the New Orleans Parish Schools for over 
20 years, with multiple accolades--including ``Teacher of the Year.'' 
He was not a man who loved power, but he deeply understood the power of 
LOVE, and his light impacted those both near and far.
  A seventh-generation Louisianan, Dr. LeBan was born in New Orleans, 
Louisiana, and is a graduate of the public school system. He completed 
undergraduate studies at Southern University and A&M College and 
Southern University at New Orleans, earning a B.S. and B.A. degree, 
respectively. He earned a Master of Science degree from Prairie View 
A&M University in Texas, a Master of Social Work degree from Southern 
University at New Orleans, and a Master of Divinity from Bethel Bible 
College. He also earned a Doctor of Theology degree from the American 
College and Theological Seminary. He received a Doctor of Divinity and 
a Doctor of Ministry, honoris causa. He completed additional studies at 
the University of New Orleans and the University of Maryland, College 
Park. While at Maryland, he participated in a study with the U.S. 
Agency for International Development to examine socio-economic systems 
in Puerto Rico, USA. Twice selected as a Fulbright Scholar, Dr. LeBan 
studied in Zimbabwe in 1995 and the Republic of South Africa in 2000. 
His travels in Africa also included Swaziland, Zambia, and Mozambique.
  In 1979, he was employed as a Researcher by Texas A&M University in 
Waller County and served for over twenty years with Orleans Parish 
Schools. He was elected ``Teacher of the Year'' at Washington High, 
Cohen High, and Walker Middle Schools. He also taught at San Jacinto 
College (Texas), the University of Maryland, and Tulane University 
Graduate School of Social Work. As a member of the American Association 
of Suicidology, Dr. LeBan supervised the crisis hotline for the 
Volunteer and Information Agency for several years, then founded the 
Crisis Institute of Mediation, Intervention & Training--a 501(c)(3) 
nonprofit organization. He has written over 80 journal articles and 
papers, many of which were featured in the ``Africa Rising'' section of 
a New Orleans publication, the Louisiana Weekly. Dr. LeBan also 
published: ''Masonic Silk Stocking--History of a Louisiana Lodge,'' and 
``History of the New Orleans Chapter of Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity, 
Inc.'' He served as the Historian for the Southwestern Province of 
Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity, Incorporated, covering New Mexico, Texas, 
Arkansas, Mississippi, and Louisiana. Dr. LeBan most recently served as 
the President of the Louisiana Retired Teachers Association (LRTA) for 
District 1 (Orleans, Plaquemines, and St. Bernard Parishes). In 2024, 
received the prestigious LRTA ``Name of Fame'' Medallion in Houma, 
Louisiana.
  As a citizen soldier, Dr. LeBan served five years in the United 
States Army. In 1986, he was selected as ``Soldier of the Year'' for 
the 122nd Army Reserve Command (Oklahoma, Arkansas, Louisiana, and 
Texas). He served several years as an instructor with the 4152nd USAR 
School and was awarded the Army Commendation Medal and the Army 
Achievement Medal with Four Oak-leaf Clusters. He served as the first 
African American President of the Alliance for Good Government, Orleans 
Chapter.
  In 1983, he was licensed and ordained as a minister of the gospel and 
served seven years as Senior Pastor of ``Walk in the Light'' 
Interdenominational Church and three years as Associate Pastor of 
``Jesus Never Fails'' Ministry of New Orleans. He is the first African 
American to serve as the Disaster Spiritual Care Louisiana Regional 
Lead for the American Red Cross. Dr. LeBan also serves as a Chaplain 
with the Association. He is a member of the National Voluntary 
Organizations Active in Disasters (VOAD) Emotional and Spiritual Care 
Committee and the National Disaster Spiritual Care Leadership Cadre.
  Though an accomplished researcher and skilled writer, he supervised a 
crisis hotline in our city, established intervention strategies for 
handling crisis situations, and led spiritual care for the American Red 
Cross in Louisiana--aiding victims of natural disasters. Dr. LeBan has 
completed numerous humanitarian deployments with the American Red 
Cross, assisting individuals and families impacted by disasters 
throughout Louisiana since 2016. Some of his humanitarian efforts 
around the country include Wildfires in Maui, Hawaii; Typhoon in Guam, 
United States of America; Atmospheric Floods in San Jose, California; 
Hurricane Michael in Macon, Georgia; Oil Rig Tragedy in Port Fourchon, 
Louisiana; Tornado in Little Rock, Arkansas; Atmospheric Floods in 
Vallejo, California; Hurricane Fiona in San Juan, Puerto Rico; Tornado 
in Mayfield, Kentucky; Flash Flooding in Central Tennessee; Floods in 
Eastern Kentucky, Texas; Mass shooting in Uvalde, Texas: Tornado in 
Southern Kentucky; Hurricane Harvey in Baton Rouge, Louisiana and 
Houston, Texas; Floods in Milwaukee, Wisconsin; and Tornadoes in the 
Metropolitan New Orleans area. He currently serves as the first African 
American Louisiana Lead for Disaster Spiritual Care and Community 
Engagement/Partnerships with the American Red Cross.
  A life member of the NAACP since 1979, he has held various offices at 
the local and state levels and was thrice elected the Louisiana State 
Conference Vice President. Dr. LeBan was currently serving as the 5th 
elected President of the New Orleans Chapter of the Southern Christian 
Leadership Conference (SCLC)--the oldest affiliate of SCLC in the 
Nation at the time of his passing. In 2019, the SCLC New Orleans 
Chapter was selected as the Interlebal SCLC Chapter of the Year, please 
see hyperlink below: https://sclcnola.org.
 From the military to the educational system, his fraternity, 
philanthropy, and ministry, Levon's life was one of service to his 
community. I thank Dr. LeBan's family for letting him be a part of our 
lives. I thank Dr. LeBan for his extraordinary contributions. His 
legacy will live forever and continue to light our paths toward a more 
just society. We salute the life and legacy of this dear man of God and 
send our deepest condolences to his family.

                          ____________________
```
