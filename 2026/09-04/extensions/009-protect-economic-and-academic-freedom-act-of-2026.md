# PROTECT ECONOMIC AND ACADEMIC FREEDOM ACT OF 2026

**Speaking:** McCollum, Betty (M001143) [D-MN]

**Measures:** H.R. 4795

```
           PROTECT ECONOMIC AND ACADEMIC FREEDOM ACT OF 2026

                                 ______
                                 

                               speech of

                          HON. BETTY McCOLLUM

                              of minnesota

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, September 3, 2026

  Ms. McCOLLUM. Mr. Speaker, I rise today in opposition to H.R. 4795, 
the Protect Economic and Academic Freedom Act of 2025. This bill would 
condition federal funding based on a university's choices regarding 
investments, procurement, partnerships, and other institutional 
activities in Israel and the West Bank.
  Peaceful dissent and the protesting of injustice are the right of all 
Americans guaranteed by the Constitution. It is called freedom of 
speech. H.R. 4795 would infringe on that right to free speech. In a 
political environment where the President of the United States is 
canceling federal grants for academic institutions that do not align 
with his views or his policies, H.R. 4795 would give President Trump 
one more tool to infringe on that right to free speech and retaliate 
against our colleges and universities where he sees fit.
  My Republican colleagues are advancing this bill to punish academic 
institutions that divest from Israeli companies or end partnerships 
with Israeli universities. But this bill will

[[Page E869]]

not punish the institutions. It will punish the students at those 
institutions who rely on federal student loans or Pell Grants to afford 
college. In my District and across the country, many low- and middle-
income students rely on financial aid to afford a college education. 
Congress should be working to make higher education more affordable for 
students, not less affordable.
  Rather than considering H.R. 4795, the House should take up 
legislation that would actually protect students on college campuses 
and condemn all forms of hate. Threats, harassment, and hate based on 
race, religion, ethnicity, sexual orientation, gender, or any other 
identity have no place in our communities, on our college campuses, or 
in our society. But this bill does not even attempt to address the 
recent rise in antisemitism, nor does it address the rise in 
Islamophobia. Instead, it does real harm to Jewish students by 
conflating antisemitism with criticism of the state of Israel.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to oppose the bill.

                          ____________________
```
