# RECOGNIZING JOHN H. WATSON

**Speaking:** Palmer, Gary J. (P000609) [R-AL]

```
                       RECOGNIZING JOHN H. WATSON

  (Mr. PALMER asked and was given permission to address the House for 1 
minute and to revise and extend his remarks.)
  Mr. PALMER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize the life and 
legacy of my friend John Watson of Dothan, Alabama.
  John was born February 12, 1938, in the small rural community of 
Skipperville. He was the first in his family to attend college, and he 
earned a degree in mechanical engineering from Auburn University. It 
should be noted that the University of Alabama offered him a football 
scholarship, which he declined because Auburn had a superior 
engineering school.
  He was a co-op student, working at the Redstone Arsenal, and he was 
in the ROTC program at Auburn. He entered the United States Army Corps 
of Engineers as a second lieutenant, and he served during the Berlin 
crisis.
  He returned to Dothan and took a job as an engineer for Smith's Inc. 
In 1966, he and two other employees bought 47 percent interest in the 
company from James M. Smith, and 4 years later, they bought the rest of 
the company, a transaction financed by Mr. Smith.
  While John was an excellent engineer, he was also an astute 
businessman, building or owning numerous other businesses, including 
Aladan, which became the largest latex glove producer in the United 
States, and USA Yeast, a state-of-the-art baker's yeast plant producing 
one-seventh of total U.S. yeast demand.
  His success as an engineer and businessman was recognized when he was 
inducted into the Alabama Business Hall of Fame in 2004 and the Alabama 
Engineering Hall of Fame in 2008.
  He was a man of faith. He served as an elder in the Evergreen 
Presbyterian Church, and he served his community and State on numerous 
boards, including the chairman of the Alabama Ethics Commission.
  He was a devoted supporter of his alma mater, Auburn University, 
where he and his wife established the John H. and Gail P. Watson 
Scholarship Endowment. He was a major supporter of Auburn athletics. 
Auburn University honored John by naming their premier volleyball 
facility the John H. Watson Fieldhouse.
  He was a faithful husband to his wife, Gail, and devoted father and 
grandfather to his two children and grandchildren.
  Mr. Speaker, I am very grateful to have had John as a friend. I miss 
him. I think of him often. I am humbled to honor his legacy. In the 
spirit of our friendship, I conclude with: War Eagle.
```
