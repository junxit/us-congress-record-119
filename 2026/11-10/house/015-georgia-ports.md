# GEORGIA PORTS

**Speaking:** Carter, Earl L. "Buddy" (C001103) [R-GA]

```
                             GEORGIA PORTS

  (Mr. CARTER of Georgia was recognized to address the House for 5 
minutes.)
  Mr. CARTER of Georgia. Madam Speaker, I rise today to recognize the 
great economic impact of the Georgia ports. Georgia ports' economic 
impact grew by 4 percent in 2025, an increase of $7 billion compared to 
the previous year. The ports now generate $181 billion in total annual 
sales and supports over 605,000 full-time and part-time jobs across the 
State of Georgia.
  Port-related activity accounts for 12 percent of Georgia's total 
economic output. Twelve percent means nearly one in every eight jobs in 
Georgia is tied to our ports. Between 2021 and 2025, employment 
supported by port activity grew by more than 44,500 jobs, yielding $44 
billion in income for Georgia workers.
  The Georgia Ports Authority continues to invest in the future through 
a $5 billion infrastructure plan, including new berths at the Port of 
Savannah and in Brunswick. These ports remain a vital engine of 
opportunity for the entire First District and our State.


                       Congratulating Jack Ganter

  Mr. CARTER of Georgia. Madam Speaker, I rise today to congratulate a 
former member of Team Carter, Jack Ganter, as he begins an exciting new 
chapter of public service at the Department of Health and Human 
Services.
  Jack previously served as my legislative director, where he was a 
trusted adviser and played an important role in advancing our work on 
behalf of Georgia's First Congressional District.
  Throughout his time on Team Carter, Jack brought tremendous 
knowledge, sound judgment, and a strong commitment to public service to 
everything he did. I am proud to see him continue that service in his 
new role working with the House of Representatives at HHS.
  Having worked alongside Jack, I know firsthand the dedication and 
professionalism he will bring to this position and the important work 
ahead.
  I thank Jack for his service work to Georgia's First District. I 
congratulate him on this well-deserved opportunity and wish him the 
best for continued success.


                        Valdosta State Sigma Chi

  Mr. CARTER of Georgia. Madam Speaker, I rise today to recognize the 
Lambda Mu Chapter of Sigma Chi fraternity at Valdosta State University 
as it celebrates its 15th anniversary. Founded on October 11, 2011, 
Lambda Mu has spent the past 15 years building a tradition of 
friendship, leadership, service, and scholarship.
  Over the years, the chapter has raised tens of thousands of dollars 
for the Huntsman Cancer Foundation through Derby Days, provided food to 
Second Harvest Food Bank, and invested in student life at Valdosta 
State University. These efforts embody the founding principles of Sigma 
Chi: friendship, justice, and learning.
  The chapter has also received numerous honors and recognition over 
the years, including recognition from fellow Sigma Chi and former 
Senator Saxby Chambliss when the chapter was founded.
  I congratulate the brothers, alumni, and friends of Lambda Mu on 15 
years of excellence, brotherhood, and service.
  In Hoc, and go Blazers.


          Recognizing St. Marys, the Prettiest Town in Georgia

  Mr. CARTER of Georgia. Madam Speaker, I rise today to recognize St. 
Marys, Georgia, for being named ``the prettiest town in Georgia'' by 
Architectural Digest.
  St. Marys, a great representation of Georgia in this contest, is in 
the most southeastern corner of the State. The town is proudly known 
for serving as the gateway city to Cumberland Island, Georgia's largest 
barrier island, home to famous architecture consisting of the Dungeness 
Ruins and the Greyfield Inn mansion.
  St. Marys is recognized for its beautiful downtown oak-lined streets 
draped in Spanish moss, 19th century churches, and grand historic homes 
showcasing classic Southern design.
  Some of the notable architecture in the community consists of Orange 
Hall, an 1830 Greek Revival mansion; and the First Presbyterian Church, 
which houses a bell cast by Paul Revere's foundry.
  Madam Speaker, it is a great honor to represent the town of St. 
Marys, and I could not be prouder of the rich history this place holds.

  I would like to congratulate the following hospitals: Emory 
University, Piedmont Atlanta, Emory St. Joseph's,
```
