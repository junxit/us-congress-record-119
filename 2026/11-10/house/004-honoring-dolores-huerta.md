# HONORING DOLORES HUERTA

**Speaking:** Garcia, Jesus G. "Chuy" (G000586) [D-IL]

```
                        HONORING DOLORES HUERTA

  (Mr. Garcia of Illinois was recognized to address the House for 5 
minutes.)
  Mr. GARCIA of Illinois. Mr. Speaker, today I rise in recognition of a 
trailblazing feminist, a giant of the labor and civil rights movement, 
and an icon in the Mexican-American history of this country, Dolores 
Huerta.
  For over seven decades, Dolores Huerta fought tirelessly for the 
voiceless--organizing marginalized communities for economic, political, 
and social justice.
  In 1962, she helped found what is known as the United Farm Workers, 
serving as the vice president until 1999 and playing a central role as 
the union's lead negotiator, securing labor contracts and legislative 
victories.
  It is where she first gave us the rallying cry that echoes in our 
hearts forever, ``Si, se puede,'' meaning ``Yes, we can,'' because, for 
Dolores, no obstacle was too great and no hope was out of reach.
  And through the Dolores Huerta Foundation, she trained grassroots 
leaders and inspired generations to stand up and speak out.
  I am forever grateful for her friendship and for her support when I 
ran for mayor of Chicago in 2015. Walking the campaign trail with 
Dolores was a learning experience. She was persuasive and could lighten 
up every conversation with voters.
  When the painful revelations of the abuse she endured at the hands of 
Cesar Chavez came to light, our hearts broke for the quiet burden she 
carried for decades.
  Yet, in true Dolores fashion, she refused to let pain define her. 
Instead, she continued to transform heartache into a powerful, 
unstoppable force for change.
  Dolores Huerta will be remembered for her unbreakable spirit and 
tireless fight for working people.
  Mr. Speaker, may my dear friend rest in power.


                     Honoring 40 Years of the ICIRR

  Mr. GARCIA of Illinois. Mr. Speaker, I stand today to honor the work 
that the Illinois Coalition for Immigrant and Refugee Rights, ICIRR, 
has done for 40 years.
  Since 1986, ICIRR has been dedicated to promoting the rights of 
immigrants and refugees and to bring them into full participation in 
civic, cultural, social, and political life.
  ICIRR has advocated and helped pass pro-immigrant legislation in 
Illinois, establishing the first permanent line item in the State 
budget dedicated to immigrant services in the country.
  It has helped pass legislation to provide in-State tuition for 
undocumented students, created scholarships for immigrant youth 
pursuing higher education, and helped create the Governor's Office of 
New Americans in the Governor's office and the city of Chicago.
  More than 100,000 immigrants have been able to prepare applications 
for citizenship thanks to ICIRR's workshops. More than half a million 
individuals and families have had access to safety-net services thanks 
to the organization's focus on the well-being of people, including many 
of my constituents.
  And ICIRR successfully fought the opening of seven for-profit 
immigration prisons in Illinois and Indiana.
  During Operation Midway Blitz, when our communities were being 
terrorized by Federal immigration agents, ICIRR trained community 
members on how to defend their rights and served as a central 
organizing and rapid response force, setting a national example to 
resist Trump's fascism.
  My team and I have been honored to have ICIRR as a key partner, 
organizing citizenship workshops, Know Your Rights trainings, and 
standing for immigrants in the face of Trump's cruel policies.
  For 40 years, ICIRR and their work have strengthened our community 
with the vision for how to ensure a more equitable society rooted in 
dignity and respect for all.
  Mr. Speaker, I thank ICIRR for their vital work.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Members are reminded to refrain from 
engaging in personalities toward the President.
```
