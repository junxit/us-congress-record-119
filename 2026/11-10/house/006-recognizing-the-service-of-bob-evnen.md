# RECOGNIZING THE SERVICE OF BOB EVNEN

**Speaking:** Bacon, Don (B001298) [R-NE]

```
                  RECOGNIZING THE SERVICE OF BOB EVNEN

  (Mr. Bacon of Nebraska was recognized to address the House for 5 
minutes.)
  Mr. BACON. Mr. Speaker, I rise to recognize and honor a dedicated 
public servant who has led our State with integrity, vision, and 
effectiveness: Nebraska's 27th Secretary of State, Bob Evnen.
  Since taking office in January 2019, Secretary Evnen has worked to 
strengthen our elections, support our economy, and represent Nebraska 
around the world.
  On elections, Bob authored a bill instituting Nebraska's voter ID 
law, expanded hand-counted postelection audits of every Statewide 
election, and opened testing processes to public observers to increase 
transparency. He has also worked to secure ballot drop boxes, all with 
the goal of giving Nebraskans confidence in their elections.
  Secretary Evnen also served as a leader in Nebraska's business 
community. By modernizing State filing systems, cutting red tape, and 
eliminating unnecessary fees, his office made it easier and less 
expensive for entrepreneurs to start, operate, and grow their 
businesses in Nebraska.
  As Nebraska's Ambassador abroad, Bob showcased our State's world-
class agricultural products, especially our premium corn and beef. His 
trade missions to Taiwan, Israel, Bulgaria, Egypt, and beyond opened 
international markets for our farmers and ranchers.
  Whether securing our elections, promoting our agriculture, 
modernizing State services, or serving on the Board of Pardons, Bob 
brought the same diligence to the office that marked his 40-year legal 
career and his service on the State of Board Education.
  Along with his wife, Debra; his children; and his grandchildren, 
Secretary Evnen embodies Nebraska values. On behalf of our State, I 
thank Secretary Evnen for his outstanding service and commitment to 
Nebraska.


               Honoring Lieutenant Colonel Michael DeBolt

  Mr. BACON. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor and celebrate a 
lifetime of exemplary service, leadership, and selflessness as we 
recognize Lieutenant Colonel Michael DeBolt. For over four decades, 
Michael has embodied the very best of Nebraska and the United States, 
leaving an indelible mark on our Nation's military, the State of 
Nebraska, and the city of Omaha.
  Michael's military journey began in Omaha, graduating as a JROTC 
Battalion Commander from North High School before earning his 
commission through Creighton University's ROTC program. He went on to 
dedicate 30 years of distinguished service as an Army military police 
and human resources officer. From commanders' roles in the Army 
Reserves to serving as the deputy commander of J15 at U.S. Strategic 
Command at Offutt Air Force Base, his military career was defined by 
steady leadership and deep operational expertise. His exceptional 
service earned him numerous decorations, including the Defense 
Meritorious Service Medal and the Military Outstanding Volunteer 
Service Medal.
  But, for Michael, retirement from the uniform in 2011 was simply the 
beginning of a second chapter of service as he went on to help shape 
the next generation of leaders. For more than a decade, he served as 
the senior Army instructor at Benson High School and the director of 
Army instruction for Omaha's Public Schools.
  Through these roles, as well as his work as an adjunct professor at 
Bellevue University, he has mentored thousands of young Nebraskans, 
instilling in them the same values of discipline, integrity, and civic 
duty that guided his own life.
  He has spent over 14 years as a mentor with the TeamMates Program, 
coached and directed State competitions for the Nebraska Special 
Olympics, and spearheaded crucial fundraising efforts for families 
battling Huntington's disease.
  He has also led and spoken at numerous veterans events across Omaha, 
honoring those who served in Nebraska and the greater metro area. His 
tireless volunteerism has earned him the Governor's Points of Light 
Award and, most recently, the Daughters of the American Revolution 
Outstanding Veteran Volunteer Award for Nebraska.
  Whether leading soldiers in uniform, guiding students in the 
classroom, or volunteering his time to ensure that no neighbor is left 
behind, Michael has spent his entire life answering the call to serve.
  I ask my colleagues to join me in thanking Lieutenant Colonel Michael 
DeBolt for his 30 years of military service, his 15 years of 
educational and community leadership, and his enduring legacy of 
altruism. Nebraska is a better and stronger place because of him.
```
