  Mr. GRIFFITH. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may consume.
  The Rules Committee met last night and favorably reported a rule 
providing for consideration of one measure, the Retirement Fairness for 
Charities and Educational Institutions Act of 2025.
  House Resolution 1650 provides for consideration of H.R. 1013, the 
Retirement Fairness for Charities and Educational Institutions Act of 
2025, under a closed rule. The rule provides for 1 hour of general 
debate equally divided and controlled by the chair and ranking minority 
member of the Committee on Financial Services or their respective 
designees and provides for one motion to recommit.
  H.R. 1013 is a bill trying to make retirement more secure for a huge 
swath of the population who work at nonprofit organizations, including 
at hospitals and public schools.
  Millions of public schoolteachers, nurses, some first responders, and 
charity employees depend on section 403(b) tax-sheltered annuity plans 
for their retirement. These 403(b) plans were set up to provide a way 
for nonprofits to offer retirement benefits and compete with 401(k) 
plans and private pensions.
  However, 403(b) plans have been left behind and have had more 
restrictive rules around which investments those institutionally 
sponsored plan managers are allowed to make. The bill allows these 
plans the option of investing in collective investment trusts if they 
so choose.
  Collective investment trusts are similar to mutual funds but have a 
lower cost to its investors. To clarify, these products are sold to 
retirement plans and not to individual retail investors. These types of 
investment decisions are routinely made under the 401(k) plans, and 
there is no reason for nonprofit workers to be hindered in their 
retirement returns only because these plans aren't available to them.
  The types of investments that we are talking about under this bill 
are already an option for 401(k) beneficiaries and for Federal employee 
retirement accounts.
  I have heard some Members on the other side of the aisle act like 
each retirement plan is forced to invest in collective investment 
trusts. That is not so. I would also remind them that each plan manager 
still has to apply fiduciary rules to whatever investment decisions 
they make for plan beneficiaries.
  I get the concern. My mother, who has since passed away, was a public 
schoolteacher. She was a single parent who slept on a couch in our 
front room so that my sister and I could each have our own bedrooms, so 
I understand the importance of protecting the retirement of teachers 
and nonprofit employees. This bill does that.
  What this bill looks to do is to allow for more competition in the 
retirement investment market, which will hopefully lead to more plans 
investing in pools with lower fees and higher returns that ultimately 
make it more likely for retirees to get bigger payouts.
  I understand the skepticism when we are looking at a distinct class 
of retirees. I would emphasize again, there are rules around how 
retirement plan managers are required to evaluate investments for each 
plan.
  Really, what Republicans are aiming to do with this bill is to 
increase retirement parity and expand access to retirement benefits to 
people who work for nonprofits or who are schoolteachers.
  I hope all Members of this House support the passage of this rule and 
the underlying bill. I urge a ``yes'' vote. Mr. Speaker, I reserve the 
balance of my time.
