# HONORING MICHELLE MORALES

**Speaking:** Ramirez, Delia C. (R000617) [D-IL]

```
                       HONORING MICHELLE MORALES

  (Mrs. Ramirez of Illinois was recognized to address the House for 5 
minutes.)
  Mrs. RAMIREZ. Mr. Speaker, I rise to honor the life and memory of 
Michelle Morales, a fearless Latina who in her lifetime built 
coalitions across our differences to defend our neighbors, demand 
racial and education justice, resist displacement, fight 
gentrification, and work to decolonize our communities, our movements, 
and our organizations.
  A first-generation U.S.-born Puerto Rican, Michelle used her platform 
to uplift the interconnected struggles between her loved ones in Puerto 
Rico and her neighbors in Chicago.
  Over her powerful career, Michelle advocated for community 
development, education and economic justice, Puerto Rican independence, 
and against the rapid gentrification of the Humboldt Park neighborhood. 
She amplified youth voices and fought the systemic marginalization of 
youth of color, centering the development, opportunity, and growth of 
our young people.
  As an organizer against colonialism and for human rights, Michelle 
co-coordinated an international campaign to secure the release of the 
Puerto Rican political prisoners held in U.S. prisons. Thanks to 
Michelle's efforts, Edwin Cortes, Ida Luz Rodriguez, Elizam Escobar, 
Alberto Rodriguez, Alejandrina Torres, Ricardo Jimenez, Carmen 
Valentin, Carlos Alberto Torres, Juan Segarra-Palmer, Antonio Camacho-
Negron, Luis Rosa, Dylcia Pagan, Adolfo Matos, Alicia Rodriguez, and 
Oscar Lopez Rivera returned to their loved ones after years of torture 
and inhumane treatment.

  She served as the president of the Woods Fund Chicago, where she 
developed opportunities, secured grants, and partnered with communities 
to fight structural racism and economic injustice.
  Through their programs, Michelle ensured that those closest to the 
problem and most affected by poverty and racial inequity led efforts to 
address the issues that impacted them most.
  Michelle was the embodiment of the belief that in community that ``We 
have each other''; ``nos cuidamos.'' We keep each other, and we love 
each other.
  While she may not physically be with us any longer, the seeds that 
Michelle planted and the gardens that she watered will flourish for 
years to come. ``Her memory will keep guiding us''; ``Su memoria nos 
seguira guiando.''
  That is why, on behalf of Illinois' Third Congressional District, I 
commend and honor Michelle Morales for her life of advocacy for more 
fair, more just, more sovereign, more loving communities. Her memory 
will live on. ``May she rest in power and peace''; ``Descansa en paz,'' 
``my dear friend''; ``mi querida amiga,'' Michelle.


                   Recognizing Miguel Angel Rodriguez

  Mrs. RAMIREZ. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Miguel Angel 
Rodriguez, a multidisciplinary artist, social impact leader, and 
community and cultural steward serving Illinois' Third Congressional 
District.
  Earlier this summer, Albany Park Theater Project announced that after 
5 years of shared leadership, Miguel Angel would step into the role of 
executive artistic director, becoming the organization's sole chief 
executive.
  His leadership is an exceptional reflection of the work of APTP 
because Miguel Angel was an alum of the program. He first joined as a 
freshman in high school at Roosevelt High School while growing up in 
Chicago's Albany Park neighborhood. His 25 years with APTP included 
roles as a youth ensemble member, teaching artist, director, and 
organizational leader.

  Today, Miguel leads the organization's artistic and organizational 
vision, creating original, youth ensemble-devised theater rooted in the 
lived experiences of immigrant, working-class, and first-generation 
communities. I call him a community and cultural steward because at the 
heart of Miguel Angel's work with APTP is a simple idea: that a young 
person's first experience with theater can happen in their own 
neighborhood, performed by young people who look like them and centered 
on stories that feel familiar to their lives and their communities.
  The proud child of immigrants, Miguel Angel's artistry roots us in 
community, showcases the power of storytelling, and reminds us that the 
transformational power of art can forge human connection and practice 
radical empathy.
  Mr. Speaker, on behalf of Illinois' Third Congressional District, I 
am proud to commend Miguel for the ways that he has nurtured the 
creativity and leadership of young people across our district using the 
arts to celebrate community and to celebrate culture and the stories of 
Albany Park. It is because of him that today he is entered into the 
permanent Congressional Record. I congratulate Miguel Angel.
```
