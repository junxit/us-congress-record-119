# HONORING THE LIFE OF RICHARD ASARO

**Speaking:** Lawler, Michael (L000599) [R-NY]

```
                   HONORING THE LIFE OF RICHARD ASARO

  (Mr. Lawler of New York was recognized to address the House for 5 
minutes.)
  Mr. LAWLER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life of Richard 
Asaro, a lifelong resident of Mount Kisco who passed away this past 
October at the age of 69.
  For nearly seven decades, Richard called Mount Kisco home and 
dedicated his life to his family, his business, and the community he 
loved. Since 1982, he owned and operated Mount Kisco Refrigeration, 
building a family business that continues today through his son.
  Richard's commitment to his community extended well beyond his work. 
For more than 48 years, he served as a volunteer firefighter with Union 
Hook and Ladder Company, where he also served as president. A proud 
Eagle Scout, Richard lived by the principle of always being ready to 
help others, and he never hesitated to lend a hand to someone in need.
  Above all, Richard will be remembered for his generosity, his 
devotion to his family, and the countless ways he gave back to the town 
he loved.
  My thoughts and prayers are with his wife, Nancy; his children and 
grandson; and all who knew and loved him. May he rest in peace.


        Honoring the Life and Legacy of Hugh ``Chaim'' Jacobson

  Mr. LAWLER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life and legacy of 
Hugh ``Chaim'' Jacobson, a devoted public servant, volunteer 
firefighter, and beloved member of the Monsey community, who recently 
passed away at the age of 46 after a courageous battle with cancer.
  For nearly three decades, Chaim dedicated himself to protecting his 
neighbors as a member of the Monsey Fire Department's Brewer Fire 
Engine Company No. 1. Inspired by his late father's commitment to 
service, Chaim followed in his footsteps and rose through the ranks, 
serving as lieutenant, captain, second assistant chief, and first 
assistant chief.
  Chaim was known for his calm leadership, commitment to firefighter 
safety, and dedication to his community. He took great pride in the 
unity of his fire company, where firefighters from all backgrounds 
served together as one family.
  His dedication to protecting others and his community will always be 
remembered, and I extend my deepest condolences to Chaim's family, 
friends, the entire Monsey Fire Department, and all who had the 
opportunity to know him. May his memory be a blessing, and may he rest 
in peace.


                 Honoring the Life of Duke Alan Searles

  Mr. LAWLER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life of Duke Alan 
Searles, a beloved husband, father, veteran, and dedicated community 
servant.
  Duke proudly served in the United States Army during the Vietnam war, 
but his commitment to serving others did not end when he returned home. 
For 25 years, he volunteered at the Montrose VA Food Pantry, giving his 
time to fellow veterans and earning recognition for his dedication, 
including induction into the Veterans Hall of Fame.
  Duke also made a lasting difference in the lives of our youth in his 
community, serving as PTA president and directing a youth group. At 
home, he and his wife, Ellen, raised 2 sons and welcomed 10 foster 
children, opening their hearts and homes to children who needed the 
support.
  Whether he was helping a neighbor, cheering on his favorite football 
team, or enjoying the love of jazz, Duke always brought positivity and 
generosity to everything he did. He will be sorely missed by all of our 
veterans and our community.


                   Honoring the Life of Michael Shine

  Mr. LAWLER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life of Michael 
Shine, who passed away peacefully surrounded by his family on October 
25 at the age of 98.
  Born in County Roscommon, Ireland, Michael immigrated to New York at 
the age of 21, beginning a life defined by hard work, service, and 
devotion to family. He proudly served as a sergeant in the United 
States Army during the Korean war, earning the Bronze Star, and later 
served with the New York City Police Department.
  Michael went on to build a successful career as a self-employed 
contractor, becoming well known throughout Rockland County for his 
craftsmanship.
  No matter how far life took him, he never lost his connection to his 
Irish roots, making more than 100 trips back to Ireland to remain close 
to the family and heritage he loved. Michael's life was a testament to 
the importance of family, service, and remembering where you came from.
  My thoughts are with his family, his children, grandchildren, great-
grandchildren, and all who knew and loved him. He will be deeply 
missed.
```
