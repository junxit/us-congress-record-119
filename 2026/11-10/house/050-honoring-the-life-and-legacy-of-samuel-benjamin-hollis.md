         HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF SAMUEL BENJAMIN HOLLIS

  (Under the Speaker's announced policy of January 3, 2025, Mr. Carter 
of Georgia was recognized for 60 minutes as the designee of the 
majority leader.)
  Mr. CARTER of Georgia. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Samuel 
Benjamin Hollis of Okatie, South Carolina. Hollis is a decorated World 
War II veteran who passed away July 7 at the age of 100.
  Born in Mayfield, Georgia, Sam answered the call to serve at just 17, 
entering the United States Army immediately after high school. He 
served honorably as a sergeant in the European theater from 1943 to 
1945.
  After the war, Sam earned degrees from the University of Georgia and 
the Woodrow Wilson School of Law and became a certified public 
accountant.
  Sam devoted his career to public service as regional counsel for the 
United States Government, earning respect for his integrity and wisdom.
  Sam is survived by his wife, Mary; his daughters, Mel Evans and 
Kathryn Hollis Peters; and many other loving relatives.
  Sam lived through a century of change, helping shape history through 
his service to God, country, and family.


           Honoring the Life and Legacy of Sidney Ray Howell

  Mr. CARTER of Georgia. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life 
and the legacy of Sidney Ray Howell of St. Marys, Georgia, who passed 
away on September 2 at the age of 71.
  Sidney was a lifelong resident of St. Marys and dedicated nearly 
three decades of his working life to the Gilman Paper Company, but his 
commitment to his community went far beyond his career.
  Sidney proudly served two terms on the St. Marys City Council, where 
he worked to preserve and improve the community he loved. One of his 
proudest accomplishments was making the motion to purchase St. Marys 
Waterfront Park, a decision that continues to benefit generations of 
families.
  Sidney was a devoted father and grandfather, a proud American, and a 
loyal friend. He enjoyed hunting, camping, cheering on the Georgia 
Bulldogs, and spending time with those he loved, including his beloved 
dog, Dinky.
  Sidney Howell leaves behind a legacy of service, family, friendship, 
and love for his hometown. His community is better because of his 
dedication, and he will be deeply missed by his family, friends, and 
the people of St. Marys.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring the life of 
Sidney Ray Howell and extending our deepest condolences to his family 
and his loved ones.
