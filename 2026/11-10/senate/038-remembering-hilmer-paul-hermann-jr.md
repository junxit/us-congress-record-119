# REMEMBERING HILMER "PAUL" HERMANN, JR.

**Speaking:** Cassidy, Bill (C001075) [R-LA]

```
                REMEMBERING HILMER ``PAUL'' HERMANN, JR.

● Mr. CASSIDY. Madam President, I rise to pay tribute to Hilmer 
Hermann, Jr., known to his family, friends, and colleagues as Paul. Of 
all the people my staff and I have worked with during my time in the 
U.S. Senate, I can think of few others who were as generous, thorough, 
and patriotic as Paul.
  He was born in New Orleans on December 28, 1956, and passed away on 
September 23, 2026. The first thing to know about Paul is that he was a 
veteran. He signed up for the U.S. Air Force in 1976 and became a 
logistics manager supporting the Air Force's Deputy Commander of 
Resources. He served for nearly 17 years until taking medical 
retirement in 1993.
  Upon retirement from the Air Force, Paul already devoted a 
substantial portion of his life to serving our country. He could have 
ended his public service there. However, he became involved with 
Disabled American Veterans after one of their representatives 
successfully helped him file a disability claim with the U.S. 
Department of Veterans Affairs. Paul believed in paying it forward and 
so began working professionally for DAV.
  During his employment with DAV, he worked as a national service 
officer and later as their supervisor in New Orleans from 2002 to 2007. 
In this work, he helped other veterans and their families receive the 
benefits they earned from the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. This 
included helping veterans facing desperate circumstances before, 
during, and after Hurricane Katrina. He also spent time in Nashville 
working for DAV, including as acting supervisor for their office there.
  Despite retiring in 2009, Paul continued to serve veterans, including 
as Louisiana department commander and later department adjutant for 
DAV. This is when my staff and I became acquainted with Paul and his 
passion for veterans.
  My casework team is committed to helping veterans but relies on 
community partners to help veterans fill out VA disability claims and 
applications for other forms of assistance. Paul was available whenever 
our team needed to connect a veteran with someone who could help them 
file for assistance from the VA. In one case, my veterans' outreach 
coordinator was meeting with a veteran in my office who needed 
assistance with the VA. During that meeting, Paul called him about an 
unrelated matter. When informed about the veteran in my office, Paul 
spoke with her and offered to help her. In another case, a veteran who 
was clearly struggling with trauma visited my office requesting help 
with the VA. When my staff asked Paul to assist him with a disability 
claim, Paul quickly reached out. Communicating with this veteran was 
not easy, but Paul committed to helping him.
  Whenever there was a veterans' event or resource fair that would 
benefit from my office's presence, Paul let us know about it. 
Additionally, when advocating for his fellow veterans, he was 
respectful of me and my team but assertive in his requests. During a 
2025 field hearing of the Senate Committee on Veterans' Affairs that I 
convened on improving mental health care for veterans, Paul delivered 
some of the hearing's most powerful testimony. He spoke knowledgeably 
about turnover among VA mental health providers, the lack of mental 
health care services for veterans in rural communities, and the need 
for suicide prevention and trauma-informed care training for providers 
who serve veterans. He also proposed practical solutions which 
demonstrated a deep understanding of the problems and his determination 
to solve them.
  When Paul was stationed at Charleston Air Force Base in South 
Carolina, he married Joan Gondron. Together they raised two children 
Paul and Jean-Edward and were blessed with six grandchildren. I extend 
my deepest condolences to Joan, their children and grandchildren, and 
everyone who knew and loved Paul. They can take pride in the legacy he 
leaves behind and in his impact on the lives of so many veterans.
  We rely upon unsung heroes who avoid public attention but steadfastly 
serve their neighbors for decades. They make it easier to provide for 
those who need a helping hand, including our veterans. Paul was one of 
those heroes. At our field hearing, Paul said that veterans need to 
know they matter and that they are not just numbers. He devoted his 
life to that cause, helping countless veterans and their families. Our 
community and our country are better because of Paul.
  I honor Paul Hermann for his extraordinary commitment to our country 
and his fellow veterans. May his example inspire others to serve those 
who have served us.●
```
