                                 China

  Mr. SCHUMER. Mr. President, despite talking a big game, Trump 
continues to show a troubling resistance to confront China on AI and to 
protect American national security.
  The press reports that Trump was briefed more than a year ago that 
Nvidia's CEO was having an affair with a suspected Chinese spy, and 
Trump didn't do anything--anything--about it. Can you believe that? One 
of our most important companies that is involved in national security 
and the CEO might be having an affair with a Chinese spy and Trump did 
nothing about it? What a dereliction of duty. He is supposed to protect 
America, not China, not his friends.
  Trump has been busy trying to defend Jensen Huang when he ought to be 
defending our national security. It is that simple. It is obvious.
  Instead, Trump invited Nvidia's CEO to join him on a visit to 
Beijing, took his advice on granting the PRC access to some of our most 
advanced AI chips, and accepted a $25 million donation from the company 
for his golden ballroom.
  I have been saying this for years: Our cutting-edge AI chips are too 
valuable to fall into the hands of potential adversaries.
  This alarming situation makes it clear that Congress must now act to 
pass critical bipartisan legislation like the AI OVERWATCH Act, the 
MATCH Act, and Chip Security Act to cut off China's access to advanced 
American technology before it is too late.
  Myself and colleagues have been pushing on this for quite a while, 
and I urge my Republican colleagues to come together with us to pass 
this legislation while we still can. And I call on the Trump 
administration to brief the Senate and House Intelligence Committees 
immediately on the PRC's efforts to spy on Nvidia and other U.S. AI 
companies.
  We also need answers on Nvidia's influence campaign and how it 
affected

the Trump administration's national security decisions, including the 
loosening--loosening--of key export controls.
  We need a full accounting for Trump's failure to address foreign 
espionage in the American tech industry--full accounting.

lead our brave men and women in uniform. The Senate needs to put

  This morning, Pete Hegseth had the audacity to declare a ``historic 
military victory'' in Iran. Can you believe it? What the hell is he

  A gallon of gas costing more than $4 a gallon is not a historic 
victory.
  Iran and its proxies still taking potshots at passing ships 8 months

  Trying to hide American casualty numbers from the public certainly 
doesn't scream ``historic victory.''
  Imagine justifying your disaster by arguing that the Vietnam war was 
worse and doing so just a day before Veterans Day. Well, that is just 
what Hegseth did. The guy has no sense, no morality, no decency, no 
honor.
  The administration could not be any more clueless about how unpopular 
Trump's failed war is with the American people.
  Hegseth fancies himself as a Patton, but he is nothing more than a 
deranged pundit. He has to go. Hegseth is already trying to hide behind 
our troops, falsely claiming that Congress disapproves of our 
courageous servicemembers, even though we have fought to bring them 
home for months.
  Let's be clear, the failure of this war falls solely on the shoulders 
of the Trump administration's disastrous leadership, not on our brave 
servicemembers who have served us so well.

       If [Congress wants] to talk about [Trump's] foreign policy 
     and national security, let's do it--I'll see you there.

  Well, Pete, we will be seeing you soon. And you better come prepared 
to defend one of the worst foreign policy records in American history.


                               Inflation

  The biggest surge in costs in October was food prices. I am talking 
about eggs, fish, beef. It is crazy and very difficult for the American

Trump's illegal war, his costly tariffs, and his nonsensical trade war 
with Canada. Trump ran the economy into the ground, and Republicans 
were more than happy to help him do it.
  Senate Democrats are dedicating every second of every day to stopping 
the high costs of groceries, gas, electricity, healthcare, and more 
from spiraling out of control.
  And, Republicans, if you dare to stand in the way, the American 
people will hand you another withering rebuke on election day of 2028.

                                  Gaza

  Mr. SCHATZ. Mr. President, tens of thousands of Palestinians have 
been displaced yet again after heavy rain flooded more than 70,000 
tents in Gaza this weekend. Four people, including three children and 
one woman, are dead. Nearly everyone in the area needs help.
  This is a humanitarian emergency unfolding in realtime, and the 
Israeli Government's ongoing blockade of fuel and the destruction of 
equipment has made getting help to people much more difficult.
  This is not a partisan accusation. Even the President's Board of 
Peace called the Israeli Government's response insufficient, adding 
that ``[delays have] . . . exacerbated the consequences of a 
foreseeable humanitarian challenge.''
  ``[Delays have] . . . exacerbated the consequences of a foreseeable 
humanitarian challenge.''
  This isn't from me. This isn't from the Democrats. This is from the 
President's Board of Peace.
  And winter is just beginning in Gaza, and things will get worse 
without urgent action. The Israeli Government must immediately allow 
rescue equipment, building materials, and winter supplies into Gaza.
  The suffering happening right now among women and children and 
families who have had their lives turned upside down again and again is 
not inevitable. These people need and deserve help because they are 
people.
  There are a lot of challenges when it comes to Israel and Palestine 
that are complicated and fraught and contentious, but we should all be 
able to agree that repairing torn tents and helping people to dry out 
and providing warmth to freezing children is a basic human obligation. 
We don't need to agree about everything else before we help these 
people. We should help them because they are people.


                                Iran War

  Mr. President, tens of millions of Americans all across the country 
and from every part of the political spectrum sent a very, very clear 
message last week: Enough is enough. We need to end this war.
  It is raising prices for everyone everywhere, and it is not making us 
any safer. This war has been a disaster on every level, and it needs to 
end now.
  Mr. President, $2,000--that is how much this war has cost American 
households already. Gas is just shy of four bucks a gallon nationally, 
and it is even higher in Hawaii at about $5.63. Diesel is more than six 
bucks after reaching a record high in September. Food prices are up 
because of fuel and fertilizer costs, and that includes beef, seafood, 
fruits, vegetables, dairy. Everything is up. The President may have 
started this reckless adventure all on his own, but it is the rest of 
us getting crushed at the pump and at the grocery store and everywhere 
we go.
  Now his argument is that the short-term economic pain is worth the 
long-term security gain, except for this: We are in a weaker position 
today than we were at the start of the war. Just consider the 
President's own stated objectives. He wanted regime change, but the 
same regime is in place, only younger and more radical.
  He wanted to wipe out Iran's nuclear program, only to turn around and 
say ``I don't care'' about Iran's stockpile of highly enriched uranium.
  He wanted to obliterate Iran's missiles, and yet the missiles keep 
falling.
  He wanted the Strait of Hormuz to be open and free, which was the 
case before the war, but now we have destroyers and aircraft carriers 
babysitting commercial ships every time they cross the strait. That is 
neither open nor free.
  And here is the thing. These disastrous consequences were not only 
foreseeable, they were foreseen. Every President over the past few 
decades has been pitched specifically by Prime Minister Netanyahu to go 
to war with Iran, and every President of both parties has wisely 
rejected it. And that is what makes the consequences of this war 
especially frustrating for so many Americans on both sides of the 
aisle. When Donald Trump said no new wars, we believed him. This wasn't 
Joe Biden, the former chair of the Senate Foreign Relations Committee. 
This wasn't George W. Bush. This was the guy who campaigned not once, 
but twice, against endless wars in the Middle East, and yet here we 
are.
  Mr. President, 10 months in, 23 American servicemembers are dead. 
Thousands of Iranian civilians, including women and children, have been 
killed. People here at home and all around the world are having to 
choose between feeding their families and heating their homes and 
seeing a doctor because of acute shortages and skyrocketing prices.
  Enough is enough. There is broad recognition from the President's 
son, from the Vice President, on down that this is not working. So 
let's end it, and let's end it together. Because every day that this 
war goes on, there is more pain and suffering for literally no reason 
at all.
  I yield the floor.
