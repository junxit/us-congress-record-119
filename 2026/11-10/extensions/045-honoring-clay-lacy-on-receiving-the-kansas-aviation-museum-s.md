# HONORING CLAY LACY ON RECEIVING THE KANSAS AVIATION MUSEUM'S LIFETIME ACHIEVEMENT AWARD

**Speaking:** Estes, Ron (E000298) [R-KS]

```
 HONORING CLAY LACY ON RECEIVING THE KANSAS AVIATION MUSEUM'S LIFETIME 
                           ACHIEVEMENT AWARD

                                 ______
                                 

                             HON. RON ESTES

                               of kansas

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 10, 2026

  Mr. ESTES. Mr. Speaker, I rise to honor Clay Lacy, a native of 
Wichita, and one of the most accomplished aviators in American history. 
Tonight, the Kansas Aviation Museum will present him with the Lifetime 
Achievement Award in recognition for his extraordinary contributions to 
aviation around the world.
  Nearly a century ago, in 1928, Wichita was designated as the Air 
Capital of the World, thanks to aviation pioneers like Clyde Cessna, 
Olive Ann Beech and Lloyd Stearman. Clay grew up on farmland outside 
the Air Capital during the Great Depression, and it didn't take long 
for him to find his passion for flight. He took his first flight on an 
airplane at age 8. By age 12, he was working at a local airport in 
exchange for flying time, and by 16, he was a certified flight 
instructor.
  At 19, Clay joined United Airlines as a DC-3 copilot. He flew for 
United for more than 41 years and retired in 1992 as the airline's most 
senior pilot. He also served his country in the California Air National 
Guard.
  Clay's leadership helped usher in a new era for the American business 
aviation industry. In 1964, the first Learjet to touch down at Van Nuys 
Airport was flown by Clay. Four years later, he founded Clay Lacy 
Aviation, the first executive jet charter company in the western United 
States.
  His impact on American aviation extends beyond the cockpit. Clay 
helped develop Astrovision, an air-to-air camera system that changed 
how aircraft are filmed. His camera system on planes captured aerial 
footage for more than 2,000 projects, including the films Top Gun and 
The Right Stuff. In 1988, he flew a Boeing 747SP named Friendship One 
around the world in just under 37 hours, raising more than $500,000 for 
children's charities.
  For more than seven decades, Clay has logged over 55,000 flight hours 
on hundreds of different aircraft types. He was enshrined in the 
National Aviation Hall of Fame in 2010 and inducted into the Kansas 
Aviation Hall of Fame in 2011. Through the Clay Lacy Foundation, he has 
also helped young people pursue careers in aviation.
  Clay has truly lived a life that advanced the aviation industry, and 
his story began in the Air Capital of the World.
  Mr. Speaker, on behalf of the people of Kansas' 4th Congressional 
District, I want to

[[Page E1190]]

congratulate Clay Lacy on his well-deserved Lifetime Achievement Award 
and thank him for a lifetime of contributions to American aviation.
```
