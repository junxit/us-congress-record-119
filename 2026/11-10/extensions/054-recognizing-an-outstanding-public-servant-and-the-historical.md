# RECOGNIZING AN OUTSTANDING PUBLIC SERVANT AND THE HISTORICAL CAREER OF RETIRED CRIMINAL JUDGE MIRIAM WALTZER

**Speaking:** Carter, Troy A. (C001125) [D-LA]

```
RECOGNIZING AN OUTSTANDING PUBLIC SERVANT AND THE HISTORICAL CAREER OF 
                 RETIRED CRIMINAL JUDGE MIRIAM WALTZER

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. TROY A. CARTER

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 10, 2026

  Mr. CARTER of Louisiana. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor a 
remarkable and resilient individual, retired criminal judge Miriam 
Waltzer. Born in Germany, she endured the tumult of World War II, 
finding refuge in a convent during the Holocaust. Her life took a turn 
for the better when she met an American soldier, leading her to the 
United States. Her journey continued when she married Bruce Waltzer, a 
civil rights lawyer, and she embarked on a path toward justice.
  Even though she had not completed high school, given her husband's 
background, she gravitated toward a career in justice. Mrs. Waltzer 
earned her undergraduate degree from Loyola University, New Orleans, 
and her Juris Doctorate from Loyola School of Law, where she was one of 
only five women in her law school class.
  After graduating from Law School, Judge Waltzer was the first woman 
elected Judge in the Criminal District Court for the Parish of Orleans, 
Section ``A,'' in 1982. She served there until 1992. She was then 
elected to the Fourth Circuit Court of Appeal, Division ``B.'' She 
retired from that position in 2002.
  Judge Miriam Waltzer, a product of oppression, was driven to improve 
the lives of those in need. As a judge in Criminal Court, she 
spearheaded programs to empower system-involved youth, fostering their 
connections to the community and promoting their success in school and 
life. She championed legislative reforms and advocated for the rights 
and protection of youthful victims. Judge Waltzer was more than a 
symbol of authority. She was a catalyst for change, advocating for 
education over imprisonment for young offenders, and demonstrating that 
toughness and kindness can coexist.
  Judge Waltzer has been honored numerous times, both professionally 
and civically, for her

[[Page E1192]]

many endeavors to benefit her profession and the beloved City of New 
Orleans, which she called home and its children. During her tenure, 
Judge Waltzer recognized the shifting demographics of defendants, with 
many becoming younger and less educated. She saw an opportunity to make 
a difference in their lives, offering probation with education as a key 
component. Her belief in second chances was palpable, and it resonated 
deeply with me.
  I am honored to document the incredible career of retired Judge 
Miriam Waltzer as the Orleans Parish Criminal Court celebrates the 
official unveiling of the portrait and plaque of Judge Miriam Waltzer 
that will forever be on display in Section A of the Orleans Parish 
Criminal Court building.
  I am also honored to have witnessed her courageous efforts during my 
career as a member of the Louisiana House of Representatives, New 
Orleans City Council, and Louisiana State Senate. I am often reminded 
of all the programs she created in the court system as a Member of 
Congress. So many people's lives are forever changed because of the 
fight she endured during her lifetime.
```
