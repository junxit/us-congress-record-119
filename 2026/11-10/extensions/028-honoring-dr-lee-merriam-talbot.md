# HONORING DR. LEE MERRIAM TALBOT

**Speaking:** Thompson, Mike (T000460) [D-CA]

```
                    HONORING DR. LEE MERRIAM TALBOT

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. MIKE THOMPSON

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 10, 2026

  Mr. THOMPSON of California. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Dr. 
Lee Merriam Talbot, a world renowned environmentalist who dedicated his 
life and career to conservation.
  Dr. Talbot was born in 1930 in New Bedford, Massachusetts. Dr. Talbot 
holds an Associate of Arts degree, a Bachelor of Arts in liberal arts 
and wildlife ecology, a Master of Science in vertebrate ecology, and a 
Ph.D. in geography and ecology, all from the University of California, 
Berkeley.
  Dr. Talbot was one of the most influential wildlife conservation 
scientists and environmental policy architects of the 20th century. Dr. 
Talbot helped advance the scientific idea that ecosystems must be 
protected as whole systems, not simply individual species. This later 
became central to the study of modern conservation biology. He was the 
primary architect and author of numerous environmentally focused 
objectives including the landmark Endangered Species Act (ESA), the 
Marine Mammal Protection Act (MMPA), the Convention on Trade of 
Endangered Species (CITES), and the World Heritage Convention. Dr. 
Talbot advised on environmental issues in over 130 countries, working 
closely with the World Wildlife Fund, United Nations, and the 
International Union for Conservation of Nature. Through these efforts, 
Dr. Talbot became internationally known for his major ecological 
fieldwork and research that resulted in the creation of the Serengeti 
National Park, the Masai Mara National Reserve, and many more World 
Heritage Sites.
  Dr. Talbot was the Senior Scientist and Director of International 
Affairs of the Council on Environmental Quality (CEQ) in the West Wing 
of the White House for President Nixon, President Ford, and President 
Carter. In this position, he placed endangered species high on the 
Nation's political and environmental agenda. His efforts resulted in 
the Endangered Species Act, one of the most important environmental 
pieces of legislation in United States history, getting signed into law 
by President Nixon on December 28, 1973. Thanks to Dr. Talbot, the ESA 
has helped prevent the extinction of species such as the Bald Eagle, 
Gray Whale, Peregrine Falcon, California Condor, American Alligator, 
and countless other animals.
  Dr. Talbot was also a beloved professor at George Mason University, 
where he taught environmental science, public policy, conversation 
biology, and international affairs for nearly 30 years. Dr. Talbot 
served in the United States Marine Corps during the Korean War. He was 
also a Resident Ecologist at the Smithsonian Institution and a 
championship racecar driver for seven decades. His last race took place 
when he was 87 years old, at Laguna Seca raceway in Monterey, 
California, one of the largest and most prestigious motor sports events 
in the world. At this race, Dr. Talbot received the prestigious Spirit 
of Monterey Award.
  Dr. Talbot's father, Murrell Williams Talbot, a noted ecologist, was 
closely tied to the early conservation movement in California. Dr. 
Talbot's wife, Martha ``Marty'' Hayne Talbot, spent much of her youth 
growing up in St. Helena. Dr. Talbot and his wife strongly encouraged 
organically farming their Napa Valley Zinfandel vineyard before this 
was widely practiced in the valley. Their children, Lawrence Hayne 
Talbot and Russell Merriam Talbot, took responsibility for farming 
their family vineyards in 2008. To this day, the Talbot family vineyard 
is one of the oldest family-held vineyard properties in Napa.
  Mr. Speaker, Dr. Talbot was described as a kind, funny, and humble 
person. Dr. Talbot's legacy of being one of the major conservation 
figures of the 20th century will live on in his former students, the 
environmental laws he guided into reality, and the wildlife and wild 
places he fiercely protected throughout his life. Therefore, it is 
fitting and proper that we honor him here today.
```
