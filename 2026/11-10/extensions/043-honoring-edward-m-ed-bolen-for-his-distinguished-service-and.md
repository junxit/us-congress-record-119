# HONORING EDWARD M. "ED" BOLEN FOR HIS DISTINGUISHED SERVICE AND LEADERSHIP IN AMERICAN AVIATION

**Speaking:** Graves, Sam (G000546) [R-MO]

```
   HONORING EDWARD M. ``ED'' BOLEN FOR HIS DISTINGUISHED SERVICE AND 
                    LEADERSHIP IN AMERICAN AVIATION

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. SAM GRAVES

                              of missouri

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 10, 2026

  Mr. GRAVES. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor my friend, Edward M. 
``Ed'' Bolen, following the announcement of his retirement as President 
and Chief Executive Officer of the National Business Aviation 
Association (NBAA), and to honor his more than four decades of service 
to American aviation.
  As Chairman of the House Committee on Transportation and 
Infrastructure, a fellow pilot, and a longtime advocate for general 
aviation, I have had the privilege of working alongside Ed on many of 
the most important aviation issues facing our Nation. Throughout his 
career, he has been a tireless champion for a safe, accessible, and 
innovative aviation system that keeps America at the forefront of the 
world.
  Ed's contributions began long before he took the helm at NBAA in 
2004. As legislative director to Senator Nancy Kassebaum, he played an 
instrumental role in the passage of the General Aviation Revitalization 
Act of 1994, landmark legislation that helped revive American general 
aviation manufacturing and protect thousands of jobs. He went on to 
serve as senior vice president and general counsel of the General 
Aviation Manufacturers Association, becoming its President and CEO in 
1996. He also served as Chairman of the

[[Page E1189]]

FAA Management Advisory Council and was appointed by President George 
W. Bush to the Commission on the Future of the U.S. Aerospace Industry.
  When Ed joined NBAA in 2004, he brought an exceptional understanding 
of aviation, Congress, and the policies that sustain the business 
aviation industry. Over the next 22 years, he became one of aviation's 
most respected voices and a trusted resource to lawmakers on both sides 
of the aisle.
  Ed has been a consistent and effective advocate on issues ranging 
from FAA reauthorization and air traffic control modernization to 
airport access, aviation safety, workforce development, and emerging 
technologies. He has testified before Congress numerous times, ensuring 
that the perspectives of business aviation operators, manufacturers, 
and the communities they serve were heard. He also understood that our 
national airspace is a vital resource that must remain accessible to 
general aviation, small businesses, rural communities, and those who 
depend on aviation in times of emergency.
  I am particularly grateful for Ed's partnership during consideration 
of the bipartisan FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024. He worked closely 
with Congress to advance policies that strengthen aviation safety, 
support our workforce, modernize infrastructure, and prepare our 
industry for the future. The legislation included a priority of mine--
the first dedicated General Aviation title in an FAA reauthorization 
bill--a significant milestone for an industry Ed has spent his career 
championing. His leadership helped ensure that general aviation had a 
strong voice throughout that process.
  Beyond the legislative accomplishments, Ed has always understood that 
business aviation is about much more than aircraft. It supports 
American jobs, connects businesses and communities, strengthens our 
economy, and provides critical services during natural disasters and 
other emergencies. As a pilot, I appreciate that Ed has never lost 
sight of the people, communities, and freedom that make aviation so 
important to our country.
  I am especially grateful for Ed's friendship and his willingness to 
work together, even when the issues were difficult. He has always 
brought candor, professionalism, and a genuine passion for aviation to 
our conversations. His credibility on Capitol Hill was earned over 
decades of hard work, strong relationships, and a reputation for doing 
what is right for the industry.
  Mr. Speaker, few individuals have shaped American aviation from as 
many perspectives as Ed Bolen. His legacy will live on in the laws he 
helped enact, the industry he helped strengthen, and the countless 
people whose careers and opportunities have benefited from his 
leadership.
  I am proud to call Ed a friend. I thank him for his extraordinary 
service to American aviation and our Nation, and I ask my colleagues to 
join me in congratulating him on a remarkable career and wishing him 
and his family all the best.

                          ____________________
```
