# HONORING THE LIFE OF JERE "MIKE" RICHARDSON

**Speaking:** Courtney, Joe (C001069) [D-CT]

```
             HONORING THE LIFE OF JERE ``MIKE'' RICHARDSON

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. JOE COURTNEY

                             of connecticut

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 10, 2026

  Mr. COURTNEY. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life and legacy 
of an extraordinary Connecticut community member, Jere ``Mike'' 
Richardson, who passed away on October 12, 2026, at 86 years old.
  Throughout his life, Mr. Richardson devoted his time to the 
engagement and betterment of his country and community. For example, he 
honorably served our Nation in the United States Army and the federal 
Environmental Protection Agency, and on the local level as a member of 
both the Lyme Land Trust and the Old Lyme VFW. His attainment of 
excellence in those endeavors made an indelible positive impact, and he 
will be deeply missed by those whose lives he touched.
  Mike was born in Plentywood, Montana to a family employed by and 
serving in the war effort in the 1940s. At an early age, Mike decided 
to step up and serve our country in the United States Army and was 
nominated and accepted into the United States Military Academy in West 
Point, New York. He graduated first in his class in 1964, which given 
the high level of West Point's student body, is proof of his 
exceptionalism.
  While serving in the Army, Mike received his master's degree in 
engineering from the University of Illinois and was stationed in 
Thailand where he aided the Royal Thai Air Force in the development and 
construction of their runways.
  After completing his military service, Mike continued to expand his 
impactful and wide-ranging career. As an engineer in Seattle, he 
contributed to the creation of the first tidal aquarium in the world. 
After earning his law degree from the University of Washington, Mike 
moved to Washington, D.C., where he returned to public service at the 
Environmental Protection Agency, establishing the enduring, 
groundbreaking concept of ``you pollute, you pay,'' forever altering in 
a positive direction the approach to environmental regulation in the 
United States. Mr. Richardson then went on to a new career as a lawyer 
for Pfizer in New England, where he retired as the vice president of 
Environmental Health and Safety.
  Mike Richardson made immeasurable contributions to his local 
community and to the Nation as a whole. It is a privilege to share some 
of his many accomplishments, and to honor his service to our country. I 
had the honor and pleasure to know him and his amazing wife Faye 
Richardson over a number of years, and benefited greatly from their 
thoughtful, well-informed perspectives on many issues and challenges 
our country has confronted during my time in Congress.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask the House to join me in expressing our condolences 
to Mike's wife and high school sweetheart, Faye; his sons, Tom and 
Pete, and their wives, Heather and Dass; and to his nine grandchildren 
for their loss. His legacy of deep intelligence, kindness, and 
integrity will long live amongst all of us who knew him and be an 
example of a life well lived.
```
