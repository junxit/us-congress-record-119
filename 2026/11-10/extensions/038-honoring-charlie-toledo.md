# HONORING CHARLIE TOLEDO

**Speaking:** Thompson, Mike (T000460) [D-CA]

```
                        HONORING CHARLIE TOLEDO

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. MIKE THOMPSON

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                       Tuesday, November 10, 2026

  Mr. THOMPSON of California. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor 
Charlie Toledo, in recognition of her over 40 years of expertise and 
advocacy in social justice.
  Charlie Toledo was born in 1950 in Albuquerque, New Mexico, as a twin 
in a family of 10 children. She attended a Catholic high school with 
deep philosophies which taught her how to question, research, and work 
hard. After college, she began fighting against the draft in Orange 
County. She was a lifelong learner and continued to study, work, and 
research throughout her life.
  Charlie worked as the Executive Director and founder of the Suscol 
Intertribal Council from 1992, located in Napa County. She went on to 
work on the National Water Board in San Francisco and became the Chair 
of the Women's Intercultural Network. She additionally worked on the 
Advisory Committee for the California Rural Indian Health Board (CRIHB) 
and assisted in the movement to reclaim tobacco as medicine. Charlie's 
contributions to the community include her extensive experience as a 
community organizer in regional, statewide, national, and international 
forums. Since 1982, she has worked in alternative healthcare fields, as 
well as background in mediation for individuals, families, and 
organizations. She would speak out politically for water, forest, and 
soil management when it was unsafe to do so as an Indigenous woman.
  In her over three decades of public service, Charlie created and 
taught an historical trauma education model to educate people about our 
Indigenous community. She also facilitated a community mural in 
remembrance of the Indigenous people of Napa. She has been an avid 
component of the cultural exchange with Mexico and the Tuspan people 
and has been an organic gardener since 1978. Charlie was a midwife 
before it was legal to have home births, advocating for a more humane 
birthing system in the State of California.
  Charlie Toledo leaves behind a legacy of Indigenous leadership, 
humanitarian advocacy, and deep cultural reconnection that continues to 
inspire and guide communities worldwide.
  Mr. Speaker, Charlie Toledo has dedicated her life to protecting the 
Indigenous people and underrepresented communities in California and 
the United States of America. She has transformed the lives of those 
around her through her service and mentorship to others. Therefore, it 
is fitting and proper that we honor her here today.
```
