# REMEMBERING B. LEE MALLORY, III

**Speaking:** Cohen, Steve (C001068) [D-TN]

```
                    REMEMBERING B. LEE MALLORY, III

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. STEVE COHEN

                              of tennessee

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 29, 2026

  Mr. COHEN. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to pay tribute to a Memphis 
civic and business leader, environmental steward and Renaissance man, 
B. Lee Mallory, III, who passed at his home on October 15. Born Barton 
Lee Mallory, III, he was known as ``B. Lee.'' After graduating from 
Pomfret School and Yale University, where he played football, he served 
four years as an officer in the Navy. He returned to Memphis with his 
California bride, Susan, to join his family's logistics and 
distribution business where he worked for more than 40 years. He also 
received an MBA from the then-Memphis State University. He was also a 
very good friend and flew some of my friends to Washington for my 
swearing in in 2007. And he was a supporter while most of his pals at 
the country club, hunt club and polo club were not. He used to send me 
post cards, encouraging me to continue to fight against the status quo 
and speak the truth. The post cards were very anti-Washington-
politician, but he was all for me. B. Lee served on many boards of 
directors, including as Chairman of the Memphis Chamber of Commerce, 
Chairman of the International Warehouse Logistics Association, and 
Chair of the Tennessee Nature Conservancy. He also served on the boards 
of LeMoyne-Owen College, Agricenter International, the Overton Park 
Conservancy and Shelby Farms Park Conservancy. In 2004, he was 
appointed Honorary Consul of Canada for the Mid-South. An avid 
outdoorsman, B. Lee was a member of the Memphis Country Club, Five 
Lakes Outing Club, the Egyptians, and the Wolf River Society. His love 
for outdoor adventure--including fishing for trout, sailing, hiking and 
camping--was shared with his fellow residents of Memphis, Tenants 
Harbor, Maine, and Bozeman, Montana. His ashes will be scattered in the 
Yellowstone River. I extend my sincere condolences to Susan, his wife 
of 61 years; his daughters Emily Mallory and Shelby Herrod; Shelby's 
husband Coleman and their children Lucy and Wilkes; his brother Howie 
Mallory and sister-in-law Nora Berko; nephews Treat Macdonald and 
Linden Mallory; and nieces Mirte and Eliana Mallory. He was preceded in 
death by his sister Clare Macdonald, brother-in-law Blair Macdonald, 
and niece Verena Mallory. B. Lee led an exemplary life of service to 
his community and will be missed.
```
