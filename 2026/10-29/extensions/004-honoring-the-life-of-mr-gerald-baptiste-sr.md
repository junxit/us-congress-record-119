# HONORING THE LIFE OF MR. GERALD BAPTISTE, SR.

```
             HONORING THE LIFE OF MR. GERALD BAPTISTE, SR.

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. LATEEFAH SIMON

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 29, 2026

  Ms. SIMON. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the passing of a pillar 
of the disability rights and Independent Living movement in the Bay 
Area and across the country, Mr. Gerald Baptiste, Sr.
  Mr. Baptiste was born on February 10, 1934, in Beaumont, Texas. A 
talented athlete and gifted student, Mr. Baptiste graduated from 
Charlton-Pollard High School and earned a scholarship to Prairie View 
A&M University. Mr. Baptiste studied Sociology, Political Science, and 
History, served in ROTC and became a proud member of Phi Beta Sigma 
Fraternity, Inc. Post-graduation, Mr. Baptiste served as a First 
Lieutenant with the U.S. Army. Upon completing his 5-year term of 
service in 1960, Mr. Baptiste settled in California.
  In 1964, Mr. Baptiste suddenly began experiencing vision loss. Unsure 
of where to turn for help, Mr. Baptiste initially attempted to conceal 
his disability. For 10 years, Mr. Baptiste searched in vain to locate 
resources that could assist him in his day-to-day life. His search 
would eventually lead him to Berkeley's Center for Independent Living 
(CIL).
  In 1979, Mr. Baptiste joined the CIL's Blind Services Department. 
Initially intending to work temporarily, Mr. Baptiste quickly realized 
that CIL could provide the help he was seeking. Mr. Baptiste sunk 
himself into the work, rising to the role of Deputy Director. He 
championed programs to strengthen CIL's services to underserved 
communities, particularly disabled Black Americans. Mr. Baptiste was 
also an early advocate for accessible public infrastructure. His 
tireless work resulted in Berkeley installing its first curb cut, a 
piece of infrastructure Mr. Baptiste would coin ``the concrete slab 
heard around the world.'' In his later years, Mr. Baptiste played a key 
role in securing the land that would house the Ed Roberts Campus, an 
iconic piece of accessible architecture and a permanent home for a 
wealth of disability-focused nonprofits, including CIL.
  While always maintaining a firm foundation in local communities, Mr. 
Baptiste became a nationally recognized leader in the disability rights 
movement. He was instrumental in coordinating advocacy efforts 
throughout the western United States in support of the Americans with 
Disabilities Act and joined then-President George H.W. Bush in the 
White House to sign the ADA into law. Mr. Baptiste was so well-
respected that four different presidents extended him invitations to 
occupy government posts.
  At home, Mr. Baptiste co-founded the Channing Bonar Neighborhood 
Association and Channing Bonar Youth Association, fighting for 
neighborhood improvements and youth activities in West Berkeley. He 
also co-founded the West Berkeley Community Development Corporation, 
Black Property Owners Association, and the Africa African American 
Disability Project. A man of deep faith, Mr. Baptiste was also an 
active member of St. Paul African Methodist Episcopal Church for over 
45 years, including 15 years on its Trustee Board. Mr. Baptiste also 
served on several other committees and boards, including Berkeley Youth 
Alternatives, the California Foundation for Independent Living Centers, 
the Berkeley Mayor's Commission on Disability, and others. Among his 
many awards, Mr. Baptiste was honored by the President's Committee on 
Employment of People with Disabilities, the Alameda County Board of 
Supervisors, and the Mayor of Berkeley. In 2019, Mr. Baptiste was 
presented with CIL's Lifetime Achievement award.
  Mr. Baptiste passed away peacefully in his sleep on October 12, 2026 
at the age of 92. He is survived by his 3 children, 8 grandchildren, 
and a vast extended family. Despite the barriers he faced, Mr. Baptiste 
never let an obstacle derail him in his pursuit of community 
empowerment, human dignity, and the right to belong.
  I thank Mr. Baptiste for his profoundly positive impact on our 
country and extend my sincerest condolences to his family. His legacy 
will be remembered in the generations he inspired, the love he 
cultivated, and the doors he forced open. Rest in power, Mr. Baptiste.

                          ____________________
```
