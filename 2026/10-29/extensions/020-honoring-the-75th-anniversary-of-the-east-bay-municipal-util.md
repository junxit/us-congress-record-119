# HONORING THE 75TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE EAST BAY MUNICIPAL UTILITY DISTRICT'S WASTEWATER TREATMENT OPERATIONS

```
    HONORING THE 75TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE EAST BAY MUNICIPAL UTILITY 
               DISTRICT'S WASTEWATER TREATMENT OPERATIONS

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. LATEEFAH SIMON

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 29, 2026

  Ms. SIMON. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize and honor the 75th 
anniversary of the East Bay Municipal Utility District's (EBMUD) 
wastewater treatment program in the East Bay.
  EBMUD was first established in 1923 under the Municipal Utility 
District Act with the stated mission of supplying the population of the 
East Bay with safe and reliable water service. In 1944, East Bay 
residents voted to establish Special District 1, thereby expanding 
EBMUD's duties to include regional wastewater collection, treatment, 
and disposal services to protect both public health and the waters of 
the San Francisco Bay.
  On November 1, 1951, EBMUD celebrated the opening of its state-of-
the-art wastewater collection and treatment plant in West Oakland, 
marking the first coordinated effort to

[[Page E1139]]

handle sewage generated by East Bay communities. EBMUD has continued to 
be at the forefront of innovative approaches to wastewater treatment 
and collection. In 1972, EBMUD established a program to work with 
industrial and commercial customers to prevent organic pollutants, 
heavy metals, and other harmful toxins from entering the wastewater 
system, reducing toxic pollutant discharge into the Bay by over 90%. In 
the 1980s, EBMUD led a collaborative effort with the federal government 
and local municipalities to isolate and treat wet weather flows, thus 
minimizing the amount of untreated sewage entering the Bay during storm 
events. EBMUD continues to work hard to improve wet weather management 
in the East Bay, collaborating closely with private owners to ensure 
that private sewer laterals are well-maintained and leak-free. EBMUD 
has also demonstrated a long-standing commitment to expanding the use 
of recycled water, investing in projects like the East Bayshore 
Recycled Water Program to strengthen regional water supply reliability, 
reduce discharge into the Bay, and advance a more sustainable future. 
EBMUD has been a national model for other utilities, developing unique 
outreach and service programs to residents and businesses to assist in 
pollution prevention, including California's ``No Drugs Down the 
Drain'' campaign, a nationally recognized mercury reduction program. 
EBMUD also led a regional fat, oil, and grease (FOG) program to reduce 
the incidence of sewer overflows.
  In recent years, EMBUD's work proved critical to the Bay Area's 
response to the COVID-19 pandemic. EBMUD utilized wastewater 
surveillance data to monitor virus levels, demonstrating the utility's 
commitment to public health. EBMUD continues to be a national leader in 
wastewater treatment and management, exploring new ways to mitigate 
nutrient discharge into San Francisco Bay, leading regional efforts to 
optimize wastewater treatment, building new capital improvements, and 
helping the Bay Area adapt to a changing regulatory environment, 
including the increasing impacts of sea level rise and climate change.
  For 75 years, EBMUD and its dedicated staff members have worked 
tirelessly to protect public health and the environment, pioneer 
innovative technologies, and ensure reliable service for hundreds of 
thousands of East Bay residents. EBMUD has proven to be a responsible 
and exemplary public agency and essential service provider. I 
congratulate the East Bay Municipal Utility District, its leadership, 
and its staff on their incredible work, and wish them a bright future 
in the next 75 years to come.
```
