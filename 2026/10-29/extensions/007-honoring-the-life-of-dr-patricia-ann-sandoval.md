# HONORING THE LIFE OF DR. PATRICIA ANN SANDOVAL

**Speaking:** Ruiz, Raul (R000599) [D-CA]

```
             HONORING THE LIFE OF DR. PATRICIA ANN SANDOVAL

                                 ______
                                 

                             HON. RAUL RUIZ

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 29, 2026

  Mr. RUIZ. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life of Dr. Patricia 
Ann (Greco) Sandoval of

[[Page E1135]]

Indian Wells, California. Born May 6, 1959, at Tachikawa Air Base in 
Tokyo, Japan, she was a physical therapist, professor, and devoted 
mother. She passed away on July 4, 2026, after a long battle with 
breast cancer.
  Patricia spent much of her life healing others. It was never just her 
profession. It was who she was. She cared for people, for animals, and 
for anything that found its way into her hands needing help.
  Her life resisted easy description. The daughter of Italian and 
Japanese parents, she grew up as a military dependent. From her early 
teens to her early twenties, she worked as a high fashion model in 
Japan and across Asia. She learned to weld, taught aerobics, and was a 
gifted dancer, from rumba and cha-cha to tap and modern, and even belly 
dancing with a sword on her head. She was also an artist and a singer.
  She was smart from the start, finishing in the top ten percent of her 
class at Yokota High School in Japan. That is where she met Lt. Col. 
Alfredo A. Sandoval, who would be her partner for fifty years. She 
became the number one graduate of her undergraduate physical therapy 
program at Texas Tech. She went on to earn her Doctorate in Physical 
Therapy from Boston University as a Distinguished Graduate and taught 
in its doctoral program for many years.
  Patricia was also a respected hospital executive. For ten years, she 
led physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech, and all allied 
health programs at Desert Hospital, now Desert Regional Medical Center, 
and JFK Memorial Hospital. She was greatly admired there. Early in her 
career, she also bought a building and opened her own physical therapy 
practice.
  Later, she left the workforce to home school her grandchildren, and 
by Alfredo's account, she was a very tough principal.
  Her daughters, Rachel, an ER and trauma nurse, Ariana, a teacher, and 
Simone, a law school graduate, were at the center of her world, along 
with her grandchildren, Aki, Vincent, Xavier, and Luca. When Ariana was 
left paralyzed and in a coma after a devastating car accident, Patricia 
drew on everything she knew as a physical therapist and everything she 
was as a mother and devoted herself to Ariana's recovery.
  Alfredo remembers her patience, her positive outlook, and her 
tenacity. She always looked for solutions that left everyone feeling 
heard, working toward consensus with a smile and a reassuring word. 
``She was my vector, my compass,'' he says. ``She just knew which steps 
to take.'' He adds that it was always a blessing to be with such a nice 
and beautiful woman, and that everyone noticed.
  Patricia was a loving sister to Joe, Julie, Alex, and Angie, children 
of Yukie and Joseph K. Greco. What will be missed most is the certainty 
that when someone she loved needed her, she was there, and somehow, she 
knew what to do.
  I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring Dr. Patricia Ann (Greco) 
Sandoval and extending our deepest condolences to her family, 
especially her husband Alfredo, her daughters, and her grandchildren.
```
