# HONORING THE 90TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE YOUNG PEOPLE'S SYMPHONY ORCHESTRA

```
 HONORING THE 90TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE YOUNG PEOPLE'S SYMPHONY ORCHESTRA

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. LATEEFAH SIMON

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 29, 2026

  Ms. SIMON. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize the Young People's 
Symphony Orchestra of Berkeley, California, as it celebrates its 90th 
anniversary. For nine decades, YPSO has provided talented young 
musicians from across the San Francisco Bay Area with the opportunity 
to perform orchestral music at the highest level. I am proud to count 
many of YPSO's musicians and their families as my constituents.

[[Page E1136]]

  YPSO was founded in Berkeley in 1936 by pioneering female conductor 
Jessica Marcelli at a time when conducting opportunities for women were 
exceedingly rare. In the decades since, YPSO has grown into a veritable 
Bay Area institution. YPSO currently brings together 83 musicians 
between the ages of 11 and 18 from communities across the Bay. Guided 
by its mission to ``encourage young people to become exemplary 
musicians and young musicians to become exemplary people,'' YPSO 
provides an experience that extends beyond full-orchestra rehearsals 
and concerts. YPSO musicians gain opportunities to explore chamber 
music, touring, and community performances, giving young musicians the 
chance to develop both as performers and as active participants in 
their communities. The youth-led YPSO Student Council, founded in 2025, 
provides opportunities for students to develop their leadership skills 
and put their voices into practice. YPSO also extends this spirit of 
active engagement and community-focus through their direct support of 
other youth music organizations and their provision of free concert 
admission to all students. YPSO additionally provides needs-based 
scholarships to ensure that all young musicians have access to 
orchestral training, regardless of their financial circumstances.
  For the past four decades, YPSO Music Director and Conductor David 
Ramadanoff has worked tirelessly to ensure that the creative, 
accessible, and youth-focused vision of Ms. Marcelli is carried 
forward. A graduate of the Cleveland Institute of Music, he studied at 
the Juilliard School and with Leornard Bernstein and Seiji Ozawa, under 
whom he served as Assistant Conductor of the San Francisco Symphony. At 
YPSO, he has inspired young musicians to challenge themselves through 
ambitious orchestral repertoire.
  The incredible work and ceaseless drive of YPSO leadership and its 
young musicians is evident in YPSO's long list of accomplishments. In 
recent years, YPSO musicians have toured the globe and given 
performances in distinguished concerts halls from Carnegie Hall in New 
York to the Sydney Opera House in Australia. In 2022, YPSO toured 
Central Europe, performing at the Musikverein in Vienna and Dvorak Hall 
in Prague. In 2024, YPSO musicians performed at Fenice Sakai in Sakai, 
Japan--Berkeley's sister city--and at the Tokyo Metropolitan Theatre. 
Musicians also participated in musical and cultural exchanges with 
Japanese youth. In 2026, YPSO toured Finland and Sweden, performing at 
Sibelius Hall in Lahti and the Helsinki Music Centre. In May of 2026, 
YPSO presented its America 250 Festival. Supported in part by a grant 
from the National Endowment for the Arts, the festival featured chamber 
and orchestral works by American composers. YPSO musicians were not 
only afforded the opportunity to perform these great works, but also to 
research the composers and works themselves, sharing what they learned 
with visiting audiences. The festival highlighted the important role of 
a new generation in carrying America's musical traditions forward.
  YPSO's longevity is a testament not only to its talented musicians, 
but also to the families, faculty, volunteers, board members, donors, 
and community supporters who have sustained the orchestra since 1936. 
Over nine decades, YPSO has offered unparalleled opportunities for 
young musicians, many of whom have gone on to accomplished careers in 
music and other fields and have left the program with a lifelong love 
of music and a dedication to making and supporting art in their own 
communities.
  I commend YPSO's extraordinary contributions to music education and 
arts in the Bay Area over the past 90 years, and wish the orchestra, 
its students, alumni, families, volunteers, and artistic leadership 
continued success as it begins its next chapter.
```
