# RECOGNIZING LA COLONIA MEXICANA

```
                    RECOGNIZING LA COLONIA MEXICANA

                                 ______
                                 

                         HON. DAVID G. VALADAO

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 29, 2026

  Mr. VALADAO. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize La Colonia 
Mexicana as it celebrates 100 years of its vibrant history, resilience, 
and lasting impact on Kern County. Historically known as the Mexican 
Colony, this unincorporated community south of Shafter has endured for 
a century thanks to generations residents who have worked tirelessly to 
build a stronger community for their families and neighbors. In the 
early 1920s, Mexican families from California, Texas, and Mexico 
settled in the region south of Shafter. By 1926, Juan ``Big John'' 
Rodriguez negotiated with property owners Jacob and John Kliewer to 
help acquire the land for the community. Soon after, the Mexican Colony 
was established, giving families a place to establish roots and build a 
future together.
  La Colonia's growth was made possible by residents who stepped up to 
serve their neighbors and improve their community. Beyond the 
significant contributions of Juan Rodriguez, leaders such as Henry 
Rodriguez, Lewis Segura, and Dora Hernandez played an important role in 
establishing Self-Help Enterprises in Kern County, helping bring 
approximately 14 homes to the colony. Decades later, families continue 
to live in these homes, connecting the community's early history with 
the present. Critical infrastructure was also only possible through the 
efforts of residents who advocated for and installed a water system, 
sewer services, drainage, street improvements, sidewalks, and 
streetlighting.
  La Colonia's success is also defined by its perseverance in the face 
of adversity. Despite discrimination, segregation, and economic 
hardship, the colony continued to thrive. Agriculture provided a vital 
source of income for local families, who worked hard to harvest a wide 
variety of crops for the community, including grapes, potatoes, garlic, 
onions, cotton, and more. During difficult times, neighbors looked out 
for one another by sharing food and other essential resources. 
Organizations such as the Comision Honorifica, local churches, and the 
Burbank Community Water Association also played an important role in 
supporting families and strengthening the community.
  Today, La Colonia Mexicana remains home to descendants of the 
families who first settled there a century ago. Their history is a 
testament to the determination, hard work, and community spirit that 
have sustained the community through generations.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues in the House of Representatives to 
join me in recognizing La Colonia Mexicana on its 100th anniversary and 
celebrating the residents who have helped shape its history. I wish the 
community continued success in the decades to come.
```
