# HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF JANET HOWELL

```
              HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF JANET HOWELL

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. JAMES R. WALKINSHAW

                              of virginia

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 29, 2026

  Mr. WALKINSHAW. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life and 
legacy of former Virginia State Senator Janet Howell, who passed away 
on August 2, 2026, at the age of 82. For more than three decades, Janet 
dedicated her life to public service, representing Northern Virginia 
with distinction and leaving a lasting mark on the Commonwealth. She 
was a trailblazer, a dedicated advocate for children and families, and 
a beloved member of the Reston community.
  First elected to the Virginia Senate in 1991, Janet represented 
portions of Fairfax and Arlington Counties for 32 years. She broke 
barriers throughout her career, becoming the first woman to serve on 
the Senate Finance Committee and later the first woman to chair the 
Senate Finance and Appropriations Committee. In these roles, she helped 
shape Virginia's budget priorities, securing investments in public 
education, health care, transportation, and essential services. Her 
leadership opened doors for generations of women in public office and 
helped ensure Northern Virginia had a strong voice in Richmond.
  Janet's legislative accomplishments reflected her deep commitment to 
protecting vulnerable Virginians. She led efforts to strengthen 
domestic violence laws, protect children from sexual predators, 
safeguard genetic privacy, and reform Virginia's mental health system 
following the tragedy at Virginia Tech. A former public school teacher, 
she understood the importance of education and worked throughout her 
career to expand opportunities for students and families. Her advocacy 
earned widespread recognition, including the American Academy of 
Pediatrics' National Child Advocate of the Year award.
  Long before her election to the Senate, Janet was an active leader in 
Reston, serving as president of the Reston Community Association, a PTA 
leader, and chair of the Virginia State Board of Social Services. 
Despite her considerable influence in Richmond, she never lost sight of 
the community she called home. She understood that public service 
begins with listening to neighbors, responding to their needs, and 
working to improve their daily lives. Her commitment to Reston and 
Fairfax County remained a defining part of her life.
  Janet will be remembered not only for the laws she helped pass and 
the barriers she broke, but also for her mentorship, integrity, and 
dedication to those she served. Her legacy lives on in stronger 
protections for families, expanded opportunities for children, and the 
many public servants who followed her example. I extend my deepest 
condolences to her husband, Hunt, their sons, Eric and Brian, their 
grandchildren, and all who knew and loved her.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring the 
extraordinary life of Janet Howell and recognizing her decades of 
service to Northern Virginia and the Commonwealth. Her contributions 
will continue to benefit our community for generations to come.
```
