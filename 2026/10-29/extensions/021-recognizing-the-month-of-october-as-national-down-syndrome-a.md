# RECOGNIZING THE MONTH OF OCTOBER AS NATIONAL DOWN SYNDROME AWARENESS MONTH

**Speaking:** Moore, Barry (M001212) [R-AL]

```
 RECOGNIZING THE MONTH OF OCTOBER AS NATIONAL DOWN SYNDROME AWARENESS 
                                 MONTH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. BARRY MOORE

                               of alabama

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 29, 2026

  Mr. MOORE of Alabama. Mr. Speaker, I rise to recognize and celebrate 
the Month of October as National Down Syndrome Awareness Month. This 
month serves to celebrate the lives and countless accomplishments and 
contributions of Alabamians with Down syndrome, and to memorialize the 
caregivers, educators, and community that enables these individuals to 
thrive.
  Every life is precious and created in God's image, with dignity, 
worth, and purpose in this world. Down Syndrome Awareness Month is an 
opportunity to celebrate the lives of those living with disabilities, 
and to cherish the joy and blessings they bring to their community and 
the lives around them.
  I want to recognize the advocacy groups throughout the State of 
Alabama who have dedicated their lives to promoting awareness to Down 
syndrome. The Coastal Alabama Down Syndrome Society (CADSS), based in 
Mobile, for its work supporting individuals with Down syndrome and 
their families throughout southwest Alabama, including Mobile, Baldwin, 
Washington, Clarke, and surrounding counties. FRIENDS, an organization 
serving individuals with Down syndrome and their families in Dothan and 
the Wiregrass region, for its contributions to family support, 
advocacy, awareness, and community connections. Finally, Down Syndrome 
Alabama, which provides statewide advocacy, education, and family 
connections.
  Down syndrome is a condition that encompasses an additional full or 
partial copy of chromosome 21. This condition has varying effects on 
life expectancy and quality of life, but advances in medical care have 
contributed to expanded opportunities for those with Down syndrome. 
While a diagnosis is lifelong, it has not hindered individuals from 
developing careers, building relationships, and becoming pillars of 
their community.
  I also want to acknowledge the important work of national 
organizations, including the National Down Syndrome Congress, National 
Down Syndrome Society, and Global Down Syndrome Foundation, along with 
the affiliate-support network Down Syndrome Affiliates in Action.
```
