# CELEBRATING ONTARIO INTERNATIONAL AIRPORT'S FIRST DECADE OF LOCAL CONTROL

```
   CELEBRATING ONTARIO INTERNATIONAL AIRPORT'S FIRST DECADE OF LOCAL 
                                CONTROL

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. NORMA J. TORRES

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 29, 2026

  Mrs. TORRES of California. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor my 
favorite airport, Ontario International Airport (ONT), as it celebrates 
its ten year anniversary of being operated by local authorities.
  ONT was established prior to World War II, and after the war, the 
City of Ontario acquired the airport. In October of 1967, the City of 
Los Angeles entered a Joint Powers Agreement with the City of Ontario, 
allowing the Los Angeles Department of Airports to operate, manage, and 
control ONT. In July of 1985, the City of Los Angeles took control of 
ONT.
  For nearly 40 years, ONT operated as a part of a multi-airport 
portfolio under the remote management of Los Angeles World Airports 
(LAWA). Under LAWA's management, ONT's purpose was to limit costs and 
operate under the status quo.
  It was clear that ONT and the Inland Empire needed change to grow and 
thrive. As a State Assemblywoman, I co-authored legislation that 
proposed transferring authority for ONT from LAWA to local control. In 
2012, the City of Ontario and the County of San Bernardino joined 
forces to form the Ontario International Airport Authority (OIAA). The 
OIAA is a joint power authority formed with the purpose of returning 
ONT to local stewardship and operating as a key economic asset to not 
only the Inland Empire, but to the entire Southern California region.
  The transfer of ONT became reality due to the OIAA's collaboration 
with the City of Ontario, the County of San Bernardino, and the Federal 
Aviation Administration; together, they worked diligently to overcome 
unprecedented intergovernmental challenges and reach an agreement with 
LAWA. As a new Member of Congress, I worked with my colleagues on both 
sides of the aisle to secure passage of federal legislation to ensure 
Federal Aviation Administration approval for the transfer.
  On November 1, 2016, a Settlement Agreement between the City of 
Ontario and the City of Los Angeles formally transferred control of 
Ontario International Airport to the OIAA. This transfer marked the 
airport's return to local control after nearly four decades.
  Under local control, ONT immediately changed its trajectory, turning 
from decline to one of the fastest growing airports in North America. 
Local control allowed it to expand its roster of domestic and 
international carriers, attract major new investment from freight 
leaders, and earn recognition as one of the best airports across the 
country. ONT serves as a critical economic generator for the Inland 
Empire and Southern California, with a 78 percent increase in economic 
output, 72 percent hike in jobs supported by airport activity, and tax 
revenues jumping from $490 million to $820 million annually since its 
return to local control.
  In the past decade, passenger numbers have doubled to more than 7 
million, and ONT now supports more than 24,000 jobs, contributes more 
than $5 billion to the region's economy, and is known as one of the 
nation's busiest cargo airports. In the past year, I have proudly 
secured $33 million in federal funding for ONT to ensure that our 
region can continue to flourish as the airport grows.
  For the past year, ONT has celebrated its first decade under local 
control through pop-up events at the airport and in communities 
throughout Southern California, partnerships with local businesses, 
anniversary signage and swag, educational videos and blogs, social 
media campaigns, and more.
  For a historic ten years under local control, it is my honor to 
recognize Ontario International Airport. The determination of San 
Bernardino County, the City of Ontario, and the entire Inland Empire 
community to reclaim their airport has improved our communities and 
created new jobs and opportunities for thousands of people. ONT's 
contributions to the Inland Empire and beyond is worthy of the highest 
commendation.
```
