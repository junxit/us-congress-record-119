# INTRODUCTION OF THE ASAL SAYAS NATIONAL STRATEGY ON YOUNG ADULT CANCERS ACT

**Speaking:** Carson, Andre (C001072) [D-IN]

```
INTRODUCTION OF THE ASAL SAYAS NATIONAL STRATEGY ON YOUNG ADULT CANCERS 
                                  ACT

                                  _____
                                 

                           HON. ANDRE CARSON

                               of indiana

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, November 12, 2026

  Mr. CARSON. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to introduce the Asal Sayas 
National Strategy on Young Adult Cancers Act, legislation that would 
direct the National Institutes of Health (NIH) to develop a 
comprehensive national strategy to address the alarming rise of cancer 
among young adults ages 18 to 49.
  This legislation is named in honor of Asal Sayas, a former member of 
my congressional staff whose experience with young adult cancer 
inspired this bill. Asal's story reflects the reality faced by far too 
many young Americans who confront a devastating diagnosis while 
navigating a healthcare system that is often inadequately equipped to 
recognize, diagnose and treat cancers in their age group.
  Young adult cancer is an urgent and growing public health concern. 
Since 1990, cancer incidence among individuals ages 18 to 49 has risen 
by 79 percent globally. In the United States, approximately 200,000 
young adults are diagnosed with cancer each year, and roughly 30,000 
lose their lives. Despite these troubling figures, young adults 
experience an average delay of seven months between the onset of 
symptoms and treatment, compared to just one month for adults over 50.
  These delays are not simply statistics. They represent missed 
opportunities for early detection, timely treatment, and potentially 
lifesaving care. Outdated screening guidelines, overlooked symptoms, 
and gaps in provider awareness continue to leave young adults 
vulnerable. Beyond the devastating human toll, young adult cancers 
impose an estimated $50 billion to $80 billion in annual economic costs 
through medical expenses, lost productivity, and caregiver 
responsibilities. These are parents, workers, students, and caregivers 
whose lives and futures are being disrupted by a disease that demands 
greater national attention.
  The Asal Sayas National Strategy on Young Adult Cancers Act takes a 
comprehensive approach to addressing this growing crisis. The 
legislation directs the NIH to develop a national strategy focused on 
expanding public and provider education, advancing research, improving 
access to screening and early detection, reducing insurance barriers, 
and supporting innovative treatments. The bill also calls for the 
establishment of a National Centers of Excellence for Young Adult 
Cancers program through the National Cancer Institute, creating 
dedicated hubs for research and clinical care. Additionally, it would 
establish an online federal clearinghouse to connect patients, 
caregivers, and healthcare providers with critical resources, treatment 
information, and ongoing clinical trials.
  To ensure accountability and coordination, the legislation would 
establish a Federal Coordinating Committee on Young Adult Cancers, 
bringing together more than 20 federal agencies and departments, 
alongside researchers and patient advocates, to oversee implementation 
and report progress to Congress.

[[Page E1207]]

  Mr. Speaker, no young adult should have to fight for a timely 
diagnosis or navigate a cancer diagnosis without the resources and 
support they deserve. By strengthening research, improving early 
detection, and coordinating our Nation's response, we can work toward a 
future where young adults facing cancer have a better chance at 
survival and a healthier future.
  This legislation honors Asal Sayas and the countless young Americans 
whose lives have been affected by cancer. I urge my colleagues on both 
sides of the aisle to join me in advancing the Asal Sayas National 
Strategy on Young Adult Cancers Act and reaffirming our commitment to 
ensuring that no generation is overlooked in our Nation's fight against 
cancer.
```
