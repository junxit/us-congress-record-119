# HONORING THE LIFE OF JOSEPH NIEL SPILLANE

**Speaking:** Courtney, Joe (C001069) [D-CT]

```
               HONORING THE LIFE OF JOSEPH NIEL SPILLANE

                                  _____
                                 

                           HON. JOE COURTNEY

                             of connecticut

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, November 12, 2026

  Mr. COURTNEY. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life and legacy 
of an exemplary individual, Mr. Joseph Niel Spillane of Mystic, 
Connecticut, who passed away on September 18, 2026, at 102 years old.
  Niel truly embodied the title of ``public servant.'' His epic journey 
included distinguished military and civilian work for the United States 
Armed Forces, elected office and chairmanships of numerous local 
commissions, and participation in eastern Connecticut community 
organizations and his beloved church. His generosity and desire to help 
others knew no bounds and was a driving impetus clearly reflected in 
all aspects of his life.
  At the start of his impressive career, Niel received undergraduate 
degrees in both Naval Architecture and Marine Engineering and began his 
graduate studies in Environmental Engineering. However, before he could 
receive his master's degree, he joined the United States Army. His 
military service was expansive, yielding a Bronze Star Medal and a 
Presidential Unit Citation after his participation in World War II, 
where he landed at Normandy and saw heavy combat in the Battle of the 
Hedgerows and the Battle of the Bulge. His Army unit was one of the 
first cohorts of United States troops to cross into Germany during the 
final push that defeated the Nazi's murderous regime. After risking so 
much in World War II, he nonetheless once again entered active-duty 
service during the Korean War as an officer in the United States Navy 
and went on to achieve the rank of Lieutenant Commander in the United 
States Navy Reserve.
  After the completion of his military service, Niel utilized his naval 
education to pursue a 25-year career at General Dynamics Electric Boat, 
a cornerstone of eastern Connecticut's economy and community. His 
tenure at EB, where he rose to the position of Assistant to the 
President for Manufacturing, helped yield the USS Nautilus, the first 
nuclear-powered submarine in the world, a creation now instrumental in 
the military success of the United States. He then went on to offer his 
expertise as the Vice President of ShipTech International, which 
consulted on shipbuilding operations across the country. Of particular 
interest, in the late 1950s, Niel was instrumental in the historic 
effort by the United States Navy to assist our ally Great Britain in 
standing up its own fleet of nuclear-powered submarines. His work 
enabling the transfer of such sensitive technology is a highly relevant 
and powerful example in 2026, as our Nation is engaged in a similar 
naval enterprise with our ally Australia.
  Niel was drawn back to public service after his time in the private 
sector and was welcomed with open arms by his Connecticut community. He 
was elected to serve as a Representative Town Meeting member in both 
Waterford and Groton, Connecticut, on the Waterford Town Council, and 
finally as Mayor of Groton. This repertoire of service was joined as 
well by his chairmanship and vice chairmanship of five different local 
commissions and boards. It is clear by the trust his neighbors placed 
in him, that Niel's desire to serve others was more than a passion, it 
was a calling.
  On July 26, 2024, alongside the University of Connecticut, I had the 
honor of memorializing Niel's extraordinary, fascinating and historic 
career through the Library of Congress' Veterans History Project. 
During our two hours together, Niel shared stories never publicly told, 
recounting both the highlights and lowlights of his service. Captivated 
by his words, the audience listened to his authentic perspective on 
service and sacrifice. During his recounting of being a direct 
participant in some of the most consequential events of the 20th 
century, his down-to-earth modesty and decency shone through, which 
made his retelling even more powerful.
  In his retirement Niel expressed his creativity as a woodcarver and a 
caroler, bringing joy to his town by way of music and the iconic and 
beautiful ``Welcome to Historic Mystic'' signs that mark the entrances 
to the community.
  Whether at the Veteran's coffeehouse or SubVets, every opportunity to 
see Niel allowed for the rediscovery of long forgotten moments of our 
region's history. It is a privilege to share just a few highlights from 
a very full and honorable life of service to others and to our Nation 
as a whole. I ask that the House join me in extending our condolences 
to his children, Nancy Spillane and Jamie Spillane; to his six 
grandchildren; and to his five great-grandchildren.
```
