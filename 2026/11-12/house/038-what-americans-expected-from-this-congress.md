# WHAT AMERICANS EXPECTED FROM THIS CONGRESS

**Speaking:** Grothman, Glenn (G000576) [R-WI]

```
               WHAT AMERICANS EXPECTED FROM THIS CONGRESS

  (Under the Speaker's announced policy of January 3, 2025, Mr. 
Grothman of Wisconsin was recognized for 30 minutes.)
  Mr. GROTHMAN. Mr. Speaker, obviously, we have had a very significant 
election since I last spoke from this podium. I have to make a few 
comments on it, and, largely, the comments will be concerned with what 
wasn't done or what wasn't debated over the last 8 months.
  To kind of follow up on what Representative Roy said, I think there 
are certain things the Republican voters and Independent voters 
expected to see get done that sadly were not done.
  The first thing I have--and some of it is I think we have failed to 
explain to the voters why our position is as it is, and not only why it 
is as it is, but why our position has to succeed or we are done as a 
country.
  I am going to lead off with immigration. When I left the floor about 
an hour ago, a reporter followed me. And her narrative, which I think 
the mainstream media wants us to follow, is that the Republicans lost 
because they were too tough on immigration, because we were enforcing 
our immigration law and Steve Miller--who I think is doing a fantastic 
job, in particular--was enforcing our immigration law.

  I think what happens is people see pictures of somebody being 
arrested or somebody who is being removed from this country, and they 
just have--they are just sympathetic, and they don't look at the bigger 
picture.
  I think too many Republicans have to point out that right now, about 
one-sixth of the people in this country are foreign-born. That is the 
all-time record, at least the all-time record going back well into the 
19th century.
  Would that change people's minds on immigration, the fact that 
something had to be done? I believe it changed last year. But prior to 
that time, about

[[Page H6218]]

800,000 Americans every year were being sworn in as new citizens, 
800,000. When I was a child, that number was around 200,000.
  So we are also very high, by historical standards, in the number of 
people who become new citizens every year. Now, does it matter who we 
let in this country?
  John Adams, when talking about our Constitution, said our 
Constitution was fit for a moral and religious nation and totally unfit 
for any other kind of person.
  There are too many people out there, led by President Biden--who did 
this without any criticism that I can see from the Democratic Party--
who want to let in an unlimited number of people here. It should be 
obvious to all Americans that that cannot be the law.
  There are people, including Republicans, who believe that if somebody 
comes here and they don't break the law, they can stay forever.
  Okay. Now, under President Biden, he was allowing something on the 
order--we don't know exactly how many, but certainly near 3 million 
people in this country every year who probably shouldn't have been let 
in this country. And nobody that I can remember from the Democratic 
Party recognizes this as a problem.
  It is a huge problem. America would permanently change. We cannot 
allow everybody in this world who feels they would be better off in 
America--which means absolutely anybody around the world--stay here if 
you come here and don't break the law for a year.
  But way too many people think that this is a possible viewpoint when 
we deal with the immigration problem. They keep saying that we need new 
laws and immigration reform. I think we all can think of things we 
would like to change, but the overall problem is not that we need a lot 
of new laws or reform.
  Like I said, right now we are letting about 800,000 people be sworn 
in as new citizens every year. What do these people want? Do they want 
that number to be higher? We are already at a record number of people 
coming here.
  So I think we have to think more clearly about immigration laws, and 
we have to educate the public why immigration laws are right. And, by 
the way, it is one of the only issues that after the Republicans did or 
didn't do things, that the public felt they trusted Republicans more 
on.
  And why does the mainstream press say our number one problem is we 
need immigration reform? Because they know very well that immigration 
reform will probably result in raising that one-sixth of Americans that 
are foreign-born.
  Now, in a related area, one of our leadership Members asked us the 
other day--we didn't hear anybody who wanted other bills up here in 
August, September, and October. I am going to mention one bill that I 
think would have shown the difference between the parties that was not 
brought to the floor, and that is no welfare benefits for people who 
are here illegally. That seems kind of commonsense. But you'll notice--
or even for people who are noncitizens, no welfare benefits for people 
who are noncitizens.

  You will notice that that bill was never brought to the floor of the 
U.S. Congress.
  Why is that important? First of all, we are $40 trillion in debt, so 
any bill that reduces spending should be something that we like. More 
importantly than that, when we give welfare benefits to people who are 
here legally or illegally but aren't citizens, we are putting on the 
path to citizenship people who are using those welfare benefits.
  We all know people who are abusing these benefits and are living a 
good lifestyle on these benefits. If we tell people that you can come 
here from other countries and eventually stay here, and we have people 
who are comfortable living on low-income housing or on government 
health insurance, we are attracting people who are not who we want to 
have become permanent citizens.
  So, it should have been automatic among Republicans, with our $40 
trillion in debt, that none of that would go to people who are not 
already citizens. For whatever reason, that is one of the bills--and I 
think I know the reasons and their justifications--that didn't find its 
way to the floor to be passed.
  That is a problem. I would assume all Americans would understand why, 
if we have a limited number of slots to come in here, we can't accept 
people who are going to be living what I will call the welfare 
lifestyle.
  The whole world wants to come in here. Our goal should be, at a 
minimum, when we pick who the new 600,000, 700,000, or 800,000 people 
who are going to be on the path to American citizenship, at a minimum, 
we ought to be making sure that those people aren't living the welfare 
lifestyle. We are not doing that.
  For whatever reason, we couldn't bring to the floor--and I think this 
bill may have cleared the reconciliation requirements, as well. For 
whatever reason, that bill or bills like it were not brought to the 
floor.
  I think if they were brought to the floor, it would be one more time 
to show the public the stark difference between the Republican and 
Democratic Parties. More people would have voted Republican, and more 
people would have seen why it was dangerous to give the Democrats a 
majority of this Chamber or the other Chamber.
  I am going to one more time address another problem that I spoke 
about frequently earlier in this session, but I don't think we can 
speak about it enough. Even though I have gotten kind of nods that I am 
in the right from both the White House and Republican legislative 
leadership, these things did not move.
  It is a difficult problem, but it is a problem every American ought 
to be aware of and every American ought to be expecting their 
Congressman or U.S. Senator to do something to solve that problem.
  Right now, the difference between the way our government treats a 
mother and father who are not married with children and a mother and 
father who are married with children is stark. Right now, our 
government overwhelmingly favors a situation in which the mother and 
father of the child are not married.
  Robert Rector, who has been an expert in this area for over 30 years 
and was involved in the Gingrich-Clinton welfare reforms, has pointed 
out that it is very easy to have a married couple lose $25,000 after 
taxes.
  Think about that: Right now, our welfare system is set up to say 
that, if you have a child and don't get married, we will give you 
$25,000 a year, and you are going to get nothing if you are married.
  In the 1950s, before the Great Society, which was put in place by 
Lyndon Johnson--who, in my opinion, is the worst President in this 
country's history--about 4 percent of the children born in this country 
did not have a mother and father at home. We are now over 40 percent.

  What effect do you think that had? There are many, many wonderful men 
and women who are single, and I am aware of them, and they are doing a 
fantastic job of raising fantastic children. But we all know this makes 
things more difficult. We all know putting the burden on these people, 
or the financial situation--putting more stress on the married couple 
should not be considered fair.
  I will read a few of these programs which, the way they are built, 
penalize the couple that is married. I will rattle them off.
  SSI old-age assistance; the earned income tax credit, which is a 
program designed to punish married couples; the child credit; the TANF; 
the Title IV-E Foster Care program; adoption assistance; general 
assistance to Indians; refugee assistance; Assets for Independence; 
Medicaid; SCHIP; Indian Health Service; Consolidated Health Centers; 
maternal and child care; Healthy Start; medical assistance to refugees; 
school breakfast; WIC; school lunch; food stamps; Child and Adult Care 
Food Program; needy families; Emergency Food Assistance Program; 
Commodity Supplemental Food Program; Summer Food Service Program; 
Senior Farmers Market Nutrition Program; Section 8 housing; public 
housing; HOME Investment Partnerships Program; Special Milk Program; 
homeless assistance; Rural Housing Insurance Fund; Rural Housing 
Service; housing for the elderly; Native American housing block grants; 
Weatherization Assistance Program; Universal Service Fund; Pell Grant 
Program; title I grants to local education authorities;

[[Page H6219]]

special programs for the disadvantaged; Federal Supplemental Education 
Opportunity Grants; GEAR UP; Education for Homeless Children and 
Youths; 21st Century Community Learning Centers; aid for graduate and 
professional study for the disadvantaged of minorities; TANF work and 
training programs; migrant work programs; Job Corps programs; senior 
companion program; foster grandparents program; Native American 
training programs; title XX social services block grants; Community 
Services Block Grants; and legal services block grants.
  Again and again, every program that is allowed to go unchanged or 
added around here year after year is designed to say that if you want 
to take advantage of this program, make sure you don't get married and, 
for sure, don't have a man in the household. AmeriCorps is another one.
  I know that there are a lot of Members who object to this, and I know 
both Republican leadership and leadership in the executive branch have 
recognized this as a problem.
  Obviously, it is a difficult problem to solve, but it is a problem. 
America, I don't believe, can be the country it once was when we 
continue that hatred for families with a man in the house. That is 
where we are right now.
  First of all, I will ask the White House, perhaps with the help of 
Vice President Vance, to point out that this is a problem and put 
together a list of people on a commission or a committee to recommend 
what we are going to do to solve this problem.
  I don't know whether they can solve it entirely. It is going to be 
very difficult to establish a system in which we don't penalize people 
a degree for not having a man in the house, but it is a priority. There 
are so many wonderful single parents, but we know statistically that it 
is more difficult on the kids. We know, statistically, men behave a lot 
more responsibly if they are in a family with children to take care of.
  If more men were in families with children to take care of, I think 
the crime rates among men and the drug abuse among men would fall.

                              {time}  1400

  I ask the new Democratic leadership, first of all, to not add to 
these horrible marriage penalties about $25,000 to $30,000 a year. I 
beg the Democrats when they take control over here, don't start 
increasing these programs and further disincentivize people to get 
married.
  Secondly, though, I ask the President to highlight the problem. At 
the executive office's request, his request or the Vice President's 
request or the staff's request, we have given him a list of about 20 
different people to choose from who would be happy to be on a committee 
or happy to be on a commission trying to solve this problem.
  I don't know what we can expect from Republican leadership here, but 
recognizing the problem would be a good start in the right direction. 
There are three issues.
  Let me say something else. Even talking about the problem, I think 
would have helped us electorally because it would have shown that the 
Republicans recognize that responsible people who are getting married 
and having children should not be demonized, and that is what is going 
on in our society today. They are being demonized and penalized with 
every government giveaway program, and it is time we make an effort to 
stop it.
  Those are three little things for people to think about.
  Again, I think on immigration, the Republicans have done a horrible 
job of not explaining how many people have come here legally, how easy 
it is to become a citizen legally, and the huge number of people in 
this country who are not citizens and are foreign born right now.
  I mean, does anybody think that that one-sixth number, one-sixth of 
the people in America today are born in another country, does anybody 
think that number has to be higher, and if it should be higher, it 
should be marginally higher?
  Does anybody think it would have been too controversial for the 
legislature here to say no welfare benefits for people who are here 
illegally? It wasn't taken care of.
  Finally, does anybody think that we should continue to ignore the 
fact that we have huge financial benefits for people who don't get 
married? We continue to ignore the problem. So many other problems out 
here stem from the Federal Government's policy of we want to do all we 
can to make sure the children are raised without a married couple and 
particularly raised without a man in the house.
  I will give you one more anecdote, by the way, because I think we 
have a little more time on this. Let me look at one of my experiences 
with one of the programs, Pell grants. Pell grants are a kind of 
scholarship for poor people who go to college. I have addressed this 
marriage-hatred problem well back into the time when I was in the State 
legislature.
  I talked about Pell grants and the fact that if a child is a member 
of a family with a mother and father at home, and they are both 
working, it is almost impossible to get a Pell grant. However, if they 
are with a single parent, the parents are separated, never married, 
divorced, what have you, it quickly becomes much easier to get a free 
scholarship.
  When I talked about it one time, I talked to a gal who was in the 
room--she was a bartendress--and I asked her what she thought of my 
speech. This was, I believe, in Green Bay, Wisconsin, I am not sure. In 
Green Bay, Wisconsin, this gal told me: Well, me and my husband, we got 
married before we had children, but none of my friends are getting 
married. They get free college.
  I am aware that Pell grants frequently isn't all the way to free 
college, but it is a good-sized scholarship. Does anybody think it is a 
good idea to have the young people in this country think they shouldn't 
get married because they want free college? However, that is where we 
are right now. It really is.
  In any event, there are three things for our leadership to think 
about, three things for my colleagues to think about, and hopefully we 
will try to address these problems over the next 2 years, mindful of 
the fact that there are powerful people who some Members of the 
Democratic Party look up to: People like Karl Marx and people like Kate 
Millett, a famous feminist from the 1960s who was overtly anti-male. 
There are people who the Democratic Party looks up to who think it is 
great to destroy the American family, and I hope the Democratic Party 
stops respecting these people and gets on board to where they should 
be, and that is not punishing couples who get married before they have 
children.
  Mr. Speaker, I yield back the balance of my time.
```
