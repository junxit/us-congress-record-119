                        ADDRESSING VOTERS' NEEDS

  (Under the Speaker's announced policy of January 3, 2025, Mr. Roy of 
Texas was recognized for 30 minutes.)
  Mr. ROY. Mr. Speaker, I thank my friend from Arizona, who is now 
leaving the floor. I thank him for his service and, again, hope to be 
down here on the floor with him again.
  Look, I thank the American people. The American people sent a loud 
message last week that this town is not getting it right. The election 
didn't go as I would have preferred in a number of respects. There are 
a lot of good people in this Chamber who are going to be going home and 
will no longer be serving in this institution, at least not in the 
120th Congress.
  I will tell you that I do not direct my ire or my frustration at the 
people, because I think the people were trying to send a loud message, 
as they have been doing now for several cycles, that this town is not 
doing what it is supposed to do.
  I have given speeches on this before. I have tried to work across the 
aisle and work within the Conference, as Republicans, to actually 
address the concerns and the needs of the American people and to do our 
job, in a republican form of government, to actually follow the 
Constitution, to actually address unlimited spending and the national 
debt of $42 trillion, to actually secure the border of the United 
States, to actually put in place permanent reform, to actually stand up 
for the hardworking men and women of this country who actually make 
this world work.
  Look, I have something here that I want to read--and I am going to 
paraphrase; it was tweeted on election day--that captures part of the 
problem. This individual tweeted out the following:

       Every GOP strategist, consultant, and candidate should be 
     forced to participate in a role-playing exercise this 
     morning.
       Your name is Bob. You're a 49-year-old blue-collar worker 
     in Ohio. You've always struggled to make ends meet. The good 
     jobs were all shipped to China, and the few remaining jobs 
     pay a pittance wage, undercut by endless illegal labor.
       You're a three-time Trump voter because you believed he 
     would finally Make America Great Again.
       You put your son through Ohio State. He graduated cum laude 
     in computer science. You couldn't have been prouder. After 
     months of searching, he landed an entry-level job at 
     Microsoft--beneath his qualifications but a good start for a 
     smart kid.
       Then, he got laid off. That same month, Microsoft applied 
     for 150,000 more H-1B visas because it can't find qualified 
     Americans.
       Your son has spent 6 months back in his childhood bedroom, 
     sending hundreds of applications without landing an 
     interview.
       On TV, Trump boasts about the greatest economy in history 
     and how many women and minorities are finding jobs.
       You decide a father-son steak night might cheer him up.
       Gas is $4.50 a gallon. Filling your truck costs $100. At 
     Kroger, ribeyes are $30 a pound. You settle for hamburger, 
     even though 70/30 ground beef still costs $10-plus.

       At checkout, some person is screaming at the cashier 
     because her EBT will only cover most of the filet mignon and 
     lobster in her cart.
       You think to yourself how the Iran war has made everything 
     expensive, but at least we're stopping their nuclear bomb. 
     You weren't even sure where Iran was until you googled it 
     after we first bombed them in March.
       After dinner, you take your son to the county fair. Half 
     the people aren't speaking English. Virtually none of the 
     crowd looks like you. You're forced to leave early after a 
     ``teen takeover'' turns violent. A foreign-born judge 
     pledges none of them will go to jail.
       Back home, you turn on the TV. Vivek Ramaswamy is lecturing 
     the crowd about American mediocrity and why Ohio needs more 
     foreign talent.
       Your son grows upset. You change the channel.
       Now a Republican surrogate, the daughter of a Governor or 
     the wife of an elderly billionaire, explains that young White 
     men are the real welfare queens. They'd rather play video 
     games in their parents' basements than work.
       Your son says nothing. He goes back to his childhood 
     bedroom. You turn off the TV.
       Then, your neighbor starts blasting reggaeton and cracking 
     open Modelos. He brought his cousin's family from Mexico to 
     help with all the minority-business contracts his 
     construction firm keeps winning.
       You sit alone in your living room.
       You worked hard, paid taxes, obeyed the law, and raised 
     your son to do everything right. You voted Republican again 
     and again because you believed somebody in Washington would 
     finally fight for you.
       Instead, your wages are stagnant. Groceries are 
     unaffordable. Your community is unrecognizable. Your country 
     is fighting another foreign war.
       And upstairs, the son you sacrificed everything for is 
     lying awake in the bedroom that he slept in at 12, wondering 
     what the heck he's supposed to do with his life now, and 
     whether--I am going to paraphrase--he can achieve the 
     American Dream.
       Every time you turn on the TV, the Republicans you elected 
     are either congratulating themselves or explaining why you 
     and your son are the problem.
       For 10 years, they told you that America's future depended 
     on your vote. Now, you aren't even sure if America has a 
     future worth defending.
       The Republican Party needs to answer one simple question 
     this morning--and that was on election day. You've spent a 
     decade demanding Bob's loyalty. You've given him stagnant 
     wages, unaffordable groceries, another foreign war, and a son 
     who did everything right but can't find work.
       And when they complain, you call them lazy. What possible 
     reason have you given Bob and his son to vote Republican? Do 
     you honestly think you deserve their vote?

  Now, you don't have to agree with this individual. You can think this 
individual is coming from a limited perspective in Ohio versus some 
other part of the country, that it is just one person.
  I am here to tell you that he is speaking for an entire Nation of 
people who are tired of a town that they rightly perceive as being in 
the hip pocket of too much of the oligarchy, too many of the 
billionaires and ruling class, or--as my friend Jeff Sessions used to 
say in the Senate--the masters of the universe, the people who make all 
the decisions.
  People are sick of it, and they are right to be sick of it. They have 
been listening to people give speech after speech, including me, about 
these things and wondering when they are going to get their country 
back, when they are going to be able to achieve the American Dream, 
whether their kids and their grandkids can achieve the American Dream, 
and afford it and afford to live, whether they will like living and 
living in a community of people that they can share life with, laugh 
with, go to concerts with, go to sporting events with, whether they 
will have a community of people of like minds without wondering whether 
they are going to get shot, whether the next cartel violence is going 
to come, or whether there is going to be a whole new community from 
another country, with another language, coming into their community and 
changing it overnight.

  I have colleagues laughing it off saying: Oh, don't worry, it is not

  Well, you are an idiot, too. You are not opening your eyes to what

  With all due respect to the President and with all due respect to 
every vote we have taken on the floor of the House on war powers put

  Congress wasn't put into the position as it is supposed to be in the

and our treasure into Iran for our national security interests, even if 
the case is strong to do it, even if cutting the head of the snake in 
Iran is worth it, even if knocking out the proxy terrorists is worth 
it, even if taking out their nuclear capability and conventional 
capability is worth it. It might be, but you have to earn it. You have 
to explain it. You can't decide it by edict from the other end of 
Pennsylvania Avenue.
  We don't work that way. You can't come down here, pass the big, 
beautiful bill which had good tax cuts in it, good spending restraint 
in it, good repeals of subsidies to align myself with my friend from 
Arizona how subsidies distort prices and make energy more expensive. We 
passed a great bill, didn't campaign on it, immediately turned toward 
our own political interests in passing redistricting plans across the 
country, including Texas, spent our time trying to focus on political 
power rather than focusing on the people who send us here to represent 
them. That is what happened, and it pisses me off--the arrogance, the 
arrogance of the powers that be, the masters of the universe who tell 
you that is what you have to do.
  When you pursue political power over the people, you lose, and that 
is what happened. That is what happened. It is not just the concerns 
about the money floating around and AI and data centers, and it is not 
just the concern that Calley is raising about MAHA, whatever is going 
on at the Kennedy Center and the grift, whatever is happening with

numerous people, you go down the line, and as I said putting at the top 
of the ticket of the biggest Republican State

  The Founders gave us a system that does put the people first, and 
when politicians forget that, they rightly get sent home. I will be 
leaving the House come January. I am going to stay in the fight because

  Well, we have to decide what it is we are fighting for. I believe in

  No one stood on the wall at the Alamo shouting: I want 3.9 percent

  The people, the people right now are not in love with either party. 
Democrats right now think they are all fired up, that they are on some 
sort of wave--no sir. Republicans think they have all the answers. Oh,

  It is the people who are tired of seeing corporate crony government

  We are a people, a people entrusted over this Earth that God gave us.

conservatives. We are supposed to care for communities, care for our

  That is not who we are. Mom-and-pop shops can't compete with the

  The American people are sick of it. They just want their community

  Do you know the best time we have had as a people in the last decade
