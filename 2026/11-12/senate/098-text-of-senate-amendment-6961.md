# Text of Senate Amendment 6961

**Speaking:** Heinrich, Martin (H001046) [D-NM]

```
  SA 6961. Mr. HEINRICH (for Mr. King) submitted an amendment intended 
to be proposed to amendment SA 6883 proposed by Mr. Thune (for Mrs. 
Capito (for herself, Mr. Lee, Mr. Whitehouse, and Mr. Heinrich)) to the 
bill H.R. 2347, to amend the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 to exclude 
from gross income any damages, other than punitive damages, received on 
account of any sexual acts or sexual contact; which was ordered to lie 
on the table; as follows:

        At the end of subtitle B of title II of division B of the 
     amendment, add the following:

     SEC. 2216. TEMPORARY EXPEDITED AUTHORIZATIONS FOR LOW-
                   CONFLICT WIND ENERGY PROJECTS ON PRIVATE LAND.

       (a) Definitions.--In this section:
       (1) Administrator.--The term ``Administrator'' means the 
     Administrator of the Federal Aviation Administration.
       (2) Aircraft detection lighting system.--The term 
     ``aircraft detection lighting system'' means a sensor-based 
     system that activates obstruction lighting only when an 
     aircraft is detected nearby, as described in Federal Aviation 
     Administration Advisory Circular 70/7460-1.
       (3) Application.--The term ``application'' includes a 
     notice, preconstruction notification, request, and any other 
     submission provided by a project sponsor to a Federal agency 
     for the purpose of obtaining from the agency a covered 
     authorization.
       (4) Bird and bat conservation strategy.--The term ``bird 
     and bat conservation strategy'' means a project-specific 
     strategy to avoid, minimize, and compensate for impacts to 
     birds and bats, as described in the Land-Based Wind Energy 
     Guidelines.
       (5) Covered authorization.--
       (A) In general.--The term ``covered authorization'' means 
     an authorization, determination, approval, verification, 
     concurrence, or permit described in any of paragraphs (1) 
     through (4) of subsection (e).
       (B) Exclusion.--The term ``covered authorization'' does not 
     include a general permit described in subsection (e)(5).
       (6) Director.--The term ``Director'' means the Director of 
     the United States Fish and Wildlife Service.
       (7) Eligible project.--The term ``eligible project'' means 
     a wind energy project that meets the applicable criteria 
     described in subsection (c).
       (8) Federal authorization or permit.--The term ``Federal 
     authorization or permit'' has the meaning given the term in 
     section 1401(a).
       (9) Habitat conservation plan.--The term ``habitat 
     conservation plan'' means a conservation plan described in 
     section 10(a)(2)(A) of the Endangered Species Act of 1973 (16 
     U.S.C. 1539(a)(2)(A)).
       (10) IPaC species screening.--The term ``IPaC species 
     screening'' means a screening for a listed species or a 
     designated critical habitat using the Information for 
     Planning and Consultation system of the United States Fish 
     and Wildlife Service (or any successor system).
       (11) Land-based wind energy guidelines.--The term ``Land-
     Based Wind Energy Guidelines'' means the voluntary Land-Based 
     Wind Energy Guidelines issued by the United States Fish and 
     Wildlife Service in March 2012 (as in effect on the date of 
     enactment of this Act).
       (12) Listed.--The term ``listed'', with respect to a 
     species of animals or plants, means that the species is 
     listed as an endangered species or a threatened species under 
     section 4 of the Endangered Species Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 
     1533).
       (13) Local field office.--The term ``local field office'', 
     with respect to a project, means the field office of the 
     United States Fish and Wildlife Service with jurisdiction 
     over the area in which the project is located.
       (14) Nationwide permit.--The term ``nationwide permit'' 
     means a general permit issued on a nationwide basis under 
     section 404(e) of the Federal Water Pollution Control Act (33 
     U.S.C. 1344(e)).
       (15) Project sponsor.--The term ``project sponsor'' means 
     an entity, including any private, public, or public-private 
     entity, seeking an authorization for a wind energy project.
       (16) Remedy.--The term ``remedy'' means a result described 
     in subsection (e) that applies in a case in which a Federal 
     agency fails to provide a response by the applicable deadline 
     under this section.

[[Page S5642]]

       (17) Single and complete project; special aquatic sites.--
     The terms ``single and complete project'' and ``special 
     aquatic sites'' have the meanings given those terms in 
     section 330.2 of title 33, Code of Federal Regulations (or a 
     successor regulation).
       (18) Standard conservation measure.--The term ``standard 
     conservation measure'' means any measure described in 
     subsection (g)(4), including any applicable supplement to 
     such a measure under subsection (h).
       (19) Standard crossing design.--The term ``standard 
     crossing design'' means a water crossing design described in 
     subsection (c)(2)(C).
       (20) Standard marking and lighting.--The term ``standard 
     marking and lighting'' means full obstruction marking and 
     lighting in accordance with Federal Aviation Administration 
     Advisory Circular 70/7460-1.
       (21) Wind energy project.--The term ``wind energy project'' 
     means a land-based wind energy generation facility on 
     privately owned land, including any new construction, 
     repowering, or expansion of such a facility, and any 
     associated facilities, on privately owned land.
       (b) Requirement to Act.--
       (1) In general.--A Federal agency shall act on a complete 
     application for a covered authorization for an eligible 
     project by the applicable deadline under subsection (e).
       (2) Failure to act.--Except as otherwise expressly provided 
     in this section, if a Federal agency fails to issue a timely, 
     reasoned written determination with respect to a complete 
     application for a covered authorization in accordance with 
     paragraph (1), the applicable remedy shall take effect, 
     without further agency action, on receipt by the agency of 
     notice under subsection (g)(1)(A)(i).
       (3) No regulations.--No regulations shall be required to 
     implement this section.
       (4) Effect.--Nothing in this section authorizes any project 
     or activity requiring an approval not covered by this 
     section.
       (c) Eligible Project Criteria.--A wind energy project shall 
     be considered to be an eligible project if the project 
     sponsor certifies that the wind energy project meets each of 
     the following criteria:
       (1) Land and siting.--
       (A) Facilities and roads.--All turbines, substations, 
     operations and maintenance facilities, and access roads are 
     located entirely on privately owned land.
       (B) No federal interest.--The wind energy project involves 
     no Federal land, Federal mineral estate, Federal funding, or 
     Federal right-of-way.
       (C) Federal nexus.--The only Federal nexuses of the wind 
     energy project are 1 or more of the following:
       (i) Review by the Federal Aviation Administration under 
     section 44718 of title 49, United States Code, including any 
     necessary--

       (I) coordination with the Department of Defense under 
     section 183a of title 10, United States Code; and
       (II) authorization by the Federal Communications 
     Commission.

       (ii) Discrete crossings of waters of the United States by a 
     road, collection line, or transmission line requiring 
     authorization under section 404 of the Federal Water 
     Pollution Control Act (33 U.S.C. 1344).
       (iii) Consultation under section 7, or authorization under 
     section 10, of the Endangered Species Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 
     1536, 1539) arising from--

       (I) a crossing described in clause (ii); or
       (II) construction or operation of the wind energy project.

       (iv) Registration for, or renewal of, a general permit for 
     incidental take of eagles under section 22.250 of title 50, 
     Code of Federal Regulations (as in effect on the date of 
     enactment of this Act), solely as provided in subsection 
     (e)(5).
       (D) Protected areas.--No component of the wind energy 
     project is located within a unit of the National Wildlife 
     Refuge System, a designated critical habitat, a component of 
     the National Wild and Scenic Rivers System, or a unit of the 
     National Park System, or on land held in trust by the United 
     States for the benefit of an Indian Tribe.
       (E) Surveys and studies.--The project sponsor has completed 
     the site-specific avian use and species surveys and studies 
     recommended in the Land-Based Wind Energy Guidelines, except 
     for any such study that is inapplicable to the wind energy 
     project, as determined by a qualified wildlife professional 
     based on site-specific conditions, with a supporting 
     explanation included in the application for the covered 
     authorization.
       (F) Participation.--
       (i) In general.--The project sponsor has provided to each 
     relevant unit of local government, affected community, and 
     landowner--

       (I) written notice of the proposed wind energy project; and
       (II) a reasonable opportunity to provide input regarding 
     the wind energy project.

       (ii) Failure to respond.--A failure by a recipient to 
     respond to a written notice under clause (i) shall not--

       (I) affect the eligibility of the applicable wind energy 
     project; or
       (II) delay any remedy with respect to the wind energy 
     project.

       (2) Waters.--
       (A) Nationwide permit.--Each single and complete project 
     crossing waters of the United States qualifies for a 
     nationwide permit, including with respect to applicable 
     acreage and linear-foot limitations.
       (B) Effect on special aquatic sites and navigable waters.--
     The wind energy project does not affect any special aquatic 
     sites or any navigable waters for which an individual permit 
     under section 10 of the Act of March 3, 1899 (commonly known 
     as the ``Rivers and Harbors Act of 1899'') (30 Stat. 1151, 
     chapter 425; 33 U.S.C. 403), would be required.
       (C) Standard crossing design.--Each water crossing of the 
     wind energy project uses a standard crossing design, 
     including bottomless culverts or bridges for roads and 
     horizontal directional drilling or boring for collection and 
     transmission lines, where feasible.
       (3) Species.--
       (A) Screening.--The wind energy project has completed an 
     IPaC species screening.
       (B) Effect.--Either--
       (i) no listed species or designated critical habitat is 
     present at any crossing of the wind energy project; or
       (ii) the project sponsor commits to implement the standard 
     conservation measures for each listed species present at such 
     a crossing, including seasonal work windows, buffers, erosion 
     and sediment controls, and any applicable compensatory 
     mitigation requirements.
       (C) Bird and bat conservation strategies and monitoring.--
     The project sponsor--
       (i) has prepared or will prepare a bird and bat 
     conservation strategy; and
       (ii) will conduct not less than 1 year of post-construction 
     monitoring, in accordance with the Land-Based Wind Energy 
     Guidelines.
       (4) Aviation.--
       (A) Exclusion areas.--
       (i) In general.--No turbine of the wind energy project is 
     or will be located within an airport or military-airspace 
     exclusion area in which construction is expressly prohibited 
     by Federal law in effect on the date of enactment of this 
     Act, including any applicable airport-protection surface or 
     exclusion area established under Federal aviation law.
       (ii) Identification.--The project sponsor identifies any 
     applicable airport or military-airspace siting restriction in 
     the notice submitted with respect to the wind energy project 
     under part 77 of title 14, Code of Federal Regulations (or 
     successor regulations).
       (iii) Proximity.--For purposes of this subsection, 
     proximity to an airport, military training route, or airspace 
     designated under part 73 of title 14, Code of Federal 
     Regulations (or successor regulations), without an applicable 
     prohibition on construction shall not make a wind energy 
     project ineligible.
       (iv) Effect.--Nothing in this subparagraph constitutes a 
     determination under section 44718 of title 49, United States 
     Code.
       (B) Marking and lighting.--The project sponsor commits to 
     comply with the applicable requirements of subsection (g)(2).
       (5) Process.--
       (A) Historic properties.--A cultural resources screening 
     identifies no historic property listed or eligible for 
     listing in the National Register of Historic Places within 
     the area of potential effects of any Department of the Army 
     authorization sought for the wind energy project, or a 
     responsible Federal agency has completed consultation under 
     section 306108 of title 54, United States Code, with respect 
     to each affected historic property.
       (B) Attestation.--The project sponsor--
       (i) certifies the truthfulness of each submission under 
     this section;
       (ii) executes an enforceable compliance undertaking 
     document; and
       (iii) documents compliance with any applicable 
     decommissioning or mitigation financial assurance 
     requirements, including any financial assurances required 
     under an applicable habitat conservation plan.
       (C) Approvals.--
       (i) In general.--The project sponsor obtains all applicable 
     State and local approvals before carrying out any activity 
     for which such an approval is required.
       (ii) Phases.--An approval described in clause (i) for a 
     subsequent phase of a wind energy project shall not be 
     required in order to obtain a remedy for an earlier phase of 
     the wind energy project.
       (d) Authorizations.--
       (1) In general.--A project sponsor may seek only such 
     covered authorizations for an eligible project as are 
     required to carry out the eligible project.
       (2) Limitations.--
       (A) Secretary of the army.--No authorization from the 
     Secretary of the Army shall be required with respect to an 
     eligible project unless the eligible project involves 1 or 
     more activities subject to--
       (i) section 404 of the Federal Water Pollution Control Act 
     (33 U.S.C. 1344); or
       (ii) section 10 of the Act of March 3, 1899 (commonly known 
     as the ``Rivers and Harbors Act of 1899'') (30 Stat. 1151, 
     chapter 425; 33 U.S.C. 403).
       (B) FAA and fcc.--No approval from the Administrator or the 
     Federal Communications Commission for an aircraft detection 
     lighting system shall be required with respect to an eligible 
     project unless the eligible project proposes to use an 
     aircraft detection lighting system.
       (e) Review Periods and Remedies.--
       (1) Federal aviation administration.--
       (A) Determination of no hazard.--
       (i) In general.--For each turbine and meteorological tower 
     of an eligible project, the Administrator shall issue a 
     determination under section 44718 of title 49, United States 
     Code, not later than 15 days after the earlier of--

[[Page S5643]]

       (I) the date of receipt of a finding or notice of no 
     unacceptable risk under subparagraph (C); and
       (II) the date of expiration of the applicable review period 
     under subparagraph (C), including any extension agreed to by 
     the project sponsor.

       (ii) Failure to act.--If the Administrator fails to issue a 
     determination described in clause (i) by the applicable 
     deadline under that clause, unless the Secretary of Defense 
     has issued a timely finding of unacceptable risk under 
     subparagraph (C), the applicable turbine or meteorological 
     tower--

       (I) shall, on receipt by the Administrator of notice under 
     subsection (g)(1)(A)(i), be deemed to have received an 
     interim determination of no hazard; and
       (II) may be constructed and operated with standard marking 
     and lighting, subject to other applicable law.

       (iii) Subsequent adverse determinations.--

       (I) In general.--Any subsequent adverse determination 
     relating to a turbine or meteorological tower described in 
     clause (i) shall--

       (aa) apply prospectively; and
       (bb) identify each specific hazard and practicable 
     mitigation measures.

       (II) Limitations.--The Administrator--

       (aa) shall provide a reasonable opportunity for compliance 
     with the mitigation measures described in subclause (I)(bb); 
     and
       (bb) may not require removal or cessation of an existing 
     turbine or meteorological tower except as necessary to 
     address an imminent and specific danger to air navigation.
       (iv) Effect.--Nothing in this subparagraph grants any 
     airspace authorization required under any other provision of 
     law (including regulations).
       (B) Aircraft detection lighting systems.--
       (i) In general.--The Administrator shall act on a complete 
     application for approval of an aircraft detection lighting 
     system not later than 90 days after the date of receipt of 
     the application, including any required coordination with the 
     Secretary of Defense.
       (ii) Failure to act.--Failure by the Administrator to act 
     in accordance with clause (i) shall not--

       (I) constitute approval of an aircraft detection lighting 
     system; or
       (II) prevent construction or operation of an aircraft 
     detection lighting system with standard marking and lighting.

       (C) Department of defense coordination.--Notwithstanding 
     provisions to the contrary in subsections (c)(1) and (e) of 
     section 183a of title 10, United States Code, and subsections 
     (f) and (g) of section 44718 of title 49, United States Code, 
     the following shall apply:
       (i) Referral.--Not later than 5 days after the date on 
     which the Administrator receives from a project sponsor a 
     complete application for an eligible project, the 
     Administrator shall transmit to the Military Aviation and 
     Installation Assurance Siting Clearinghouse a copy of the 
     application, together with any supporting information 
     necessary for review by the Secretary of Defense under this 
     subparagraph.
       (ii) Initial review.--Not later than 45 days after the date 
     on which the Administrator receives from a project sponsor a 
     complete application for an eligible project, the Secretary 
     of Defense shall notify the Administrator and the project 
     sponsor, in writing, that--

       (I) the applicable eligible project presents no 
     unacceptable risk to national security;
       (II) a specifically identified potential adverse impact of 
     the eligible project may be resolved through 1 or more 
     identified mitigation measures; or
       (III) the Secretary of Defense has made a finding of 
     unacceptable risk with respect to the eligible project, in 
     accordance with section 183a(e) of title 10, United States 
     Code.

       (iii) Mitigation.--

       (I) Negotiation.--If the Secretary of Defense provides 
     notice under clause (ii)(II), the Secretary of Defense and 
     the project sponsor shall have not more than 45 additional 
     days to negotiate the mitigation measures described in that 
     clause, unless the project sponsor agrees in writing to an 
     extension.
       (II) Execution and notice.--If mitigation is agreed to 
     under subclause (I), the Secretary of Defense shall--

       (aa) execute the mitigation agreement; and
       (bb) not later than 10 days after the date of execution, 
     transmit to the Administrator the resulting findings.

       (III) Failure to resolve.--The Secretary of Defense may 
     issue a finding of unacceptable risk under section 183a(e) of 
     title 10, United States Code, with respect to an eligible 
     project during the applicable mitigation period under this 
     clause if an identified risk cannot be resolved through 
     mitigation.

       (iv) Failure to act.--

       (I) In general.--If the Secretary of Defense fails to 
     provide notice by the applicable deadline under clause (ii), 
     or fails to execute a mitigation agreement or issue a finding 
     of unacceptable risk by the applicable deadline under clause 
     (iii), the project sponsor may provide to the Secretary of 
     Defense a notice under subsection (g)(1)(A)(i).
       (II) Effect of notice.--On receipt of a notice under 
     subclause (I)--

       (aa) the Secretary of Defense shall be deemed to have made 
     a finding of no unacceptable risk for purposes of section 
     183a(e) of title 10, United States Code, and subsections (f) 
     and (g) of section 44718 of title 49, United States Code; and
       (bb) the Administrator shall proceed without further action 
     required by the Secretary of Defense.

       (III) Treatment.--A timely finding of unacceptable risk by 
     the Secretary of Defense pursuant to this subparagraph shall 
     not be deemed to be a finding of no unacceptable risk under 
     subclause (II)(aa).

       (v) National security.--Nothing in this subparagraph--

       (I) affects the implementation or effect of section 
     183a(e)(1)(B) of title 10, United States Code; or
       (II) limits any action necessary to address a subsequently 
     identified imminent and specific threat to national security.

       (2) Federal communications commission.--
       (A) In general.--Not later than 60 days after the date of 
     receipt of a complete application for a radio authorization 
     or antenna structure registration or modification required 
     for an aircraft detection lighting system involved in an 
     eligible project, the Federal Communications Commission shall 
     approve or deny the authorization, registration, or 
     modification.
       (B) Failure to act.--
       (i) In general.--Subject to clause (ii), failure by the 
     Federal Communications Commission to act in accordance with 
     subparagraph (A) shall not--

       (I) be considered to be approval by the Federal 
     Communications Commission; or
       (II) waive an authorization otherwise required to be 
     obtained from the Federal Communications Commission for an 
     eligible project.

       (ii) Limitation.--The absence of an authorization described 
     in subparagraph (A) shall not prevent or delay the 
     construction or operation of the applicable eligible project 
     using standard marking and lighting, subject to otherwise 
     applicable law.
       (3) Department of the army.--
       (A) Nationwide permit preconstruction notifications.--
       (i) In general.--The Secretary of the Army, acting through 
     the Chief of Engineers (referred to in this paragraph as the 
     ``Secretary''), shall issue a determination, in writing 
     (including conducting any required consultation), with 
     respect to a complete preconstruction notification under an 
     applicable nationwide permit not later than, as applicable--

       (I) 45 days after the date of receipt of the notification; 
     or
       (II) 60 days after the date of receipt of the notification 
     in any case in which consultation under section 7 of the 
     Endangered Species Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 1536) or section 
     306108 of title 54, United States Code, is required.

       (ii) Failure to act.--Notwithstanding part 330 of title 33, 
     Code of Federal Regulations (or successor regulations), on a 
     failure by the Secretary to issue a determination in 
     accordance with clause (i) and receipt by the Secretary of 
     notice under subsection (g)(1)(A)(i), the verification that 
     is the subject of that determination shall be deemed to be 
     issued for any activity carried out in accordance with, as 
     applicable--

       (I) a nationwide permit;
       (II) any regional conditions; and
       (III) subsection (g).

       (iii) Endangered species act requirements.--Notwithstanding 
     section 330.4(f) of title 33, Code of Federal Regulations (or 
     a successor regulation), or a nationwide permit condition 
     requiring separate written confirmation of the Chief of 
     Engineers under section 7 of the Endangered Species Act of 
     1973 (16 U.S.C. 1536), work may proceed under a deemed 
     verification under clause (ii) if the requirements of section 
     7 of the Endangered Species Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 1536) are 
     satisfied in accordance with--

       (I) subparagraph (A) or (B) of paragraph (4); or
       (II) another applicable provision of law.

       (iv) Other requirements.--

       (I) Treatment.--A deemed verification under clause (ii) 
     shall not be considered to constitute--

       (aa) a certification under section 401 of the Federal Water 
     Pollution Control Act (33 U.S.C. 1341); or
       (bb) completion of consultation required under section 
     306108 of title 54, United States Code.

       (II) Requirement.--A certification or consultation 
     requirement described in subclause (I) shall be independently 
     satisfied or waived under applicable law before any work 
     described in clause (iii) commences.

       (B) Approved jurisdictional determinations.--
       (i) In general.--The Secretary shall issue a verification 
     with respect to a complete application for an approved 
     jurisdictional determination relating to an eligible project 
     not later than 45 days after the date of receipt of the 
     application.
       (ii) Failure to act.--On failure by the Secretary to issue 
     a verification described in clause (i) by the applicable 
     deadline under that clause and receipt by the Secretary of 
     notice under subsection (g)(1)(A)(i), a delineation described 
     in subsection (g)(3)(D) shall be deemed to be accepted for 
     purposes of the eligible project, without determining 
     jurisdiction over any other affected property.
       (4) United states fish and wildlife service.--
       (A) Informal consultation.--
       (i) In general.--The Director shall issue a written 
     determination with respect to a complete application for 
     concurrence in a determination by a Federal agency under 
     section 7(a)(2) of the Endangered Species Act of 1973 (16 
     U.S.C. 1536(a)(2)) that an eligible

[[Page S5644]]

     project is not likely to adversely affect a listed species or 
     designated critical habitat not later than 30 days after the 
     date of receipt of the application.
       (ii) Failure to act.--On failure by the Director to issue a 
     written determination described in clause (i) by the 
     applicable deadline under that clause and receipt by the 
     Director of notice under subsection (g)(1)(A)(i)--

       (I) concurrence of the Director shall be deemed to be 
     provided for purposes of section 7(a)(2) of the Endangered 
     Species Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 1536(a)(2)) solely for the 
     eligible project and effects of the eligible project 
     described in the request; but
       (II) no incidental take not otherwise authorized by law 
     shall be considered to be authorized, subject to subsection 
     (g).

       (B) Programmatic formal consultation.--
       (i) In general.--The Director shall issue a written 
     determination with respect to a complete request to confirm 
     that a crossing of an eligible project meets the express 
     coverage criteria of an existing programmatic biological 
     opinion issued under section 7(b) of the Endangered Species 
     Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 1536(b)) not later than 60 days after 
     the date of receipt of the request.
       (ii) Failure to act.--On failure by the Director to issue a 
     written determination described in clause (i) by the 
     applicable deadline under that clause and receipt by the 
     Director of notice under subsection (g)(1)(A)(i), coverage 
     shall be deemed to be confirmed by the Director only to the 
     extent that the applicable crossing meets the criteria 
     referred to in clause (i).
       (iii) Incidental take statement.--Section 7(o)(2) of the 
     Endangered Species Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 1536(o)(2)) shall 
     apply only to a take involved in an eligible project that is 
     within the scope and limits, and subject to the terms, of an 
     existing incidental take statement.
       (C) Incidental take permits for construction.--
       (i) In general.--The Director shall issue a written 
     determination with respect to a complete application for a 
     permit under section 10(a)(1)(B) of the Endangered Species 
     Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 1539(a)(1)(B)) for a take incidental 
     to the construction of an eligible project not later than 180 
     days after the date of receipt of the application.
       (ii) Failure to act.--On failure by the Director to issue a 
     written determination described in clause (i) by the 
     applicable deadline under that clause and receipt by the 
     Director of notice under subsection (g)(1)(A)(i), an 
     incidental take during the construction of the applicable 
     eligible project shall be authorized by operation of 
     subparagraph (E), solely within the limits, and subject to 
     the conditions, described in that subparagraph and subsection 
     (g).
       (iii) Effect.--Nothing in this subparagraph authorizes 
     construction that requires an approval under any other 
     provision of law (including regulations).
       (D) Incidental take permits for operations affecting listed 
     bats.--
       (i) In general.--The Director shall issue a written 
     determination with respect to a complete application for a 
     permit under section 10(a)(1)(B) of the Endangered Species 
     Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 1539(a)(1)(B)) for a take of listed 
     bats incidental to the operation of an eligible project not 
     later than 180 days after the date of receipt of the 
     application.
       (ii) Failure to act.--On failure by the Director to issue a 
     written determination described in clause (i) by the 
     applicable deadline under that clause and receipt by the 
     Director of notice under subsection (g)(1)(A)(i), an 
     incidental take of listed bats during the operation of the 
     applicable eligible project shall be authorized by operation 
     of subparagraph (E), solely within the limits, and subject to 
     the conditions, described in that subparagraph and subsection 
     (g).
       (E) Statutory incidental take authorization.--
       (i) Operation of law.--

       (I) In general.--Notwithstanding paragraphs (1) and (2) of 
     section 10(a) of the Endangered Species Act of 1973 (16 
     U.S.C. 1539(a)), if the Director fails to issue a written 
     determination by an applicable deadline under subparagraph 
     (C) or (D), the incidental take described in the relevant 
     complete application and the accompanying habitat 
     conservation plan shall be considered to be authorized by 
     operation of this section on receipt by the Director of 
     notice under subsection (g)(1)(A)(i), without any further 
     agency action, finding, consultation, or approval.
       (II) Effect.--An authorization under this clause shall have 
     the force and effect of a permit issued under section 
     10(a)(1)(B) of the Endangered Species Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 
     1539(a)(1)(B)).

       (ii) Limitations.--An authorization under clause (i)--

       (I) extends only to the applicable listed species, 
     activities, geographic area, term, and quantified incidental 
     take expressly identified in the complete application and 
     accompanying habitat conservation plan;
       (II) is subject to all applicable avoidance, minimization, 
     mitigation, monitoring, reporting, funding, and other 
     enforceable conditions of that habitat conservation plan and 
     subsection (g); and
       (III) does not authorize any take beyond the limits 
     described in subclause (I).

       (iii) Complete applications.--

       (I) In general.--To be eligible to be considered to be 
     complete under this subparagraph, an application shall 
     include a habitat conservation plan containing the 
     information described in subparagraph (A) of section 10(a)(2) 
     of the Endangered Species Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 1539(a)(2)) 
     and evidence supporting each finding described in 
     subparagraph (B) of that section, including assurances of 
     adequate funding.
       (II) Incompleteness.--A materially incomplete application 
     shall not qualify for an authorization under clause (i), 
     subject to the completeness procedures described in 
     subsection (f).

       (iv) Public participation.--

       (I) In general.--Not later than 30 days after the date of 
     receipt of a complete application, the Director shall--

       (aa) publish the application and the accompanying habitat 
     conservation plan; and
       (bb) provide not less than 30 days for written public 
     comment regarding that application and habitat conservation 
     plan.

       (II) Action by project sponsor.--If the Director fails to 
     publish an application or habitat conservation plan by the 
     applicable deadline under subclause (I), the project sponsor 
     may publish the applicable materials and provide an 
     opportunity for comment through a publicly accessible website 
     and submission to the Director of relevant notice.
       (III) Response.--A project sponsor shall submit to the 
     Director a response to any material comments under this 
     clause before the applicable authorization takes effect.
       (IV) No extension.--Failure by the Director to publish, 
     review, or respond to any comments under this clause shall 
     not extend the 180-day period described in subparagraph 
     (C)(i) or (D)(i), as applicable.
       (V) Requirement.--An authorization under clause (i) may not 
     take effect until--

       (aa) the applicable comment period under this clause 
     concludes; and
       (bb) the project sponsor submits a response to those 
     comments under subclause (III).
       (v) Environmental review and consultation.--

       (I) In general.--An authorization under clause (i)--

       (aa) shall be conferred directly by this section; and
       (bb) notwithstanding the National Environmental Policy Act 
     of 1969 (42 U.S.C. 4321 et seq.) and section 7 of the 
     Endangered Species Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 1536), shall not 
     require an environmental assessment, environmental impact 
     statement, finding of no significant impact, record of 
     decision, biological opinion, or further consultation as a 
     condition of validity or effectiveness.

       (II) Application.--This clause--

       (aa) applies solely to a statutory incidental take 
     authorization under clause (i); and
       (bb) does not exempt any Federal agency action or 
     authorization from any otherwise applicable requirement.
       (vi) Enforcement.--

       (I) In general.--The Director may enforce the terms, take 
     limits, and conditions of an authorization under clause (i) 
     to the same extent as if the authorization were a permit 
     issued under section 10(a)(1)(B) of the Endangered Species 
     Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 1539(a)(1)(B)), subject to subsection 
     (g)(1)(D).
       (II) Treatment.--No subsequent agency documentation or 
     acknowledgment shall be required to establish an 
     authorization under clause (i).

       (5) Eagle general permits.--
       (A) In general.--An eligible project that satisfies the 
     eligibility criteria for a general permit for incidental take 
     of eagles associated with wind energy operations under 
     sections 22.210 and 22.250 of title 50, Code of Federal 
     Regulations (as in effect on the date of enactment of this 
     Act), shall remain eligible to register for and renew such a 
     permit, notwithstanding any subsequent change in those 
     eligibility criteria or a general suspension of new 
     registrations under section 22.210(e) of that title (as so in 
     effect), if the eligible project continues to satisfy the 
     criteria in effect on that date of enactment.
       (B) Requirements.--The registration, issuance, renewal, and 
     compliance of an eligible project described in subparagraph 
     (A) shall remain subject to the general-permit requirements 
     in effect on the date of enactment of this Act, including 
     with respect to certifications, fees, conditions, and 
     enforcement.
       (C) Effect.--Nothing in this paragraph--
       (i) authorizes an eagle take; or
       (ii) requires an eligible project to seek an eagle take 
     permit.
       (f) Complete Applications and Review Periods.--
       (1) Commencement of review period.--
       (A) In general.--Except as provided in subsection (e)(1), 
     for any determination or coordination with the Secretary of 
     Defense required under subsection (e), each applicable review 
     period under that subsection shall be considered to begin on 
     the date on which the applicable Federal agency receives a 
     complete application for a covered authorization for an 
     eligible project.
       (B) Determinations of no hazard.--The 15-day period 
     described in clause (i) of subsection (e)(1)(A) shall be 
     considered to begin on the applicable date described in 
     subclause (I) or (II) of that clause, without regard to the 
     date on which notice under subsection (g)(1)(A)(i) is 
     provided.
       (2) Completeness of applications.--
       (A) In general.--An application shall be considered to be 
     complete for purposes of this subsection if the application 
     contains the information required under the publicly 
     available requirements of the applicable agency, as in effect 
     on the date of enactment of this Act.
       (B) Notice of deficiency.--

[[Page S5645]]

       (i) In general.--Not later than 15 days after the date of 
     receipt of a complete application, an agency may issue 1 
     written notice identifying any specific missing information 
     reasonably necessary with respect to the covered 
     authorization that is the subject of the application.
       (ii) Completeness.--An application described in clause (i) 
     shall be--

       (I) absent a notice under that clause, deemed to be 
     complete on the date of submission; or
       (II) if a notice under that clause is issued by an agency, 
     complete on submission to the agency of the information 
     identified in the notice.

       (C) Agency obligations.--An agency--
       (i) may not extend a review period under this section 
     through--

       (I) successive requests for information under subparagraph 
     (B)(i); or
       (II) requests for information beyond the applicable 
     requirements published by the agency; and

       (ii) shall bear the burden of identifying, in a timely 
     written notice under subparagraph (B)(i), any deficiency in 
     an application.
       (3) Treatment of certain agency actions.--
       (A) In general.--A request by an agency for additional 
     information, a referral by an agency, or provision by an 
     agency of a notice that review remains pending shall not be 
     considered to be a determination by the agency for purposes 
     of this section.
       (B) Adverse determinations.--
       (i) In general.--A written denial or adverse determination 
     issued by an agency by an applicable deadline under this 
     section shall identify--

       (I) the statutory or regulatory grounds for the denial or 
     determination;
       (II) the material facts supporting those grounds; and
       (III) where legally available, specific measures that would 
     permit approval by the agency.

       (ii) Treatment.--A denial by an agency under this section--

       (I) shall not be deemed to be an approval; and
       (II) remains subject to otherwise applicable administrative 
     and judicial review.

       (4) Conditions required.--A remedy shall apply with respect 
     to an eligible project only if the eligible project complies 
     with the applicable conditions described in subsection (g).
       (g) Conditions for Remedies.--To receive a remedy under 
     this subsection, an eligible project shall comply with the 
     following conditions, as applicable:
       (1) All federal agencies.--
       (A) Notice and effect of failure to act.--
       (i) In general.--If a Federal agency fails to issue a 
     written determination by an applicable deadline under 
     subsection (e), the project sponsor may submit to the office 
     of the agency that is designated to receive covered 
     authorization applications a written notice of the missed 
     deadline, including by electronic submission.
       (ii) Remedy.--On receipt of a notice under clause (i), the 
     applicable remedy described in subsection (e) shall take 
     effect by operation of law, without further agency action, 
     determination, acknowledgment, or approval, subject to the 
     conditions and limitations of this section.
       (iii) Prohibition.--An agency may not delay or withhold a 
     remedy by declining to receive or acknowledge a notice under 
     clause (i).
       (iv) Effect of certain actions.--A request for additional 
     information, referral, or notice of continuing review by an 
     agency shall not--

       (I) extend a deadline under this section; or
       (II) prevent a remedy from taking effect.

       (B) Build as filed.--
       (i) In general.--A remedy shall be provided for an eligible 
     project activity only to the extent that the activity is 
     described in an application for a covered authorization under 
     this section.
       (ii) Material changes.--A material change affecting the 
     eligibility or environmental effects of an eligible project 
     shall--

       (I) require the submission of an updated application; and
       (II) cause the review period for the applicable covered 
     authorization to recommence.

       (C) Conditions.--The eligible project shall comply with--
       (i) the applicable conservation measures described in 
     paragraph (4); and
       (ii) for any activity requiring a nationwide permit, the 
     applicable general and regional conditions of the nationwide 
     permit, including conditions relating to any certification 
     under section 401 of the Federal Water Pollution Control Act 
     (33 U.S.C. 1341), in effect on the date of submission of an 
     application for a covered authorization for the eligible 
     project.
       (D) Oversight.--
       (i) In general.--An agency may inspect and prospectively 
     suspend or revoke a remedy for a documented material 
     violation or newly discovered material fact affecting the 
     eligibility of an eligible project, subject to the condition 
     that the agency shall provide to the project sponsor--

       (I) a written notice identifying the grounds for the 
     suspension or revocation; and
       (II) a reasonable opportunity to cure, to the extent 
     practicable.

       (ii) Immediate action.--Notwithstanding clause (i), an 
     agency may take immediate action pursuant to that clause in 
     order to prevent imminent harm to human safety or a listed 
     species.
       (iii) Limitations.--A suspension under this subparagraph--

       (I) shall be limited to the applicable activity; and
       (II) shall not be broader than necessary.

       (iv) Effect.--Agency oversight under this subparagraph 
     shall not extend, or reestablish the availability of, a 
     remedy solely because the agency failed to act by an 
     applicable deadline under this section.
       (E) Records.--The project sponsor shall--
       (i) maintain a record of all filings, notices, and 
     compliance records under this section; and
       (ii) make those items available to a Federal agency on 
     request.
       (2) FAA and fcc.--
       (A) Standard marking and lighting.--
       (i) In general.--The eligible project shall use standard 
     marking and lighting until--

       (I) the applicable aircraft detection lighting system is 
     approved by the Administrator;
       (II) all Federal Communications Commission authorizations 
     required for the operation of the aircraft detection lighting 
     system are obtained; and
       (III) the aircraft detection lighting system commences 
     operation.

       (ii) Prohibition.--An eligible project may not operate an 
     aircraft detection lighting system before each approval and 
     authorization described in clause (i) is obtained.
       (B) Notice of construction.--The project sponsor shall file 
     each notice of construction required by the Administrator 
     with respect to an applicable aircraft detection lighting 
     system.
       (3) Department of the army.--
       (A) Nationwide permits.--All work on an eligible project 
     shall remain within the terms, including acreage and linear-
     foot limitations, of the applicable nationwide permit.
       (B) Crossing designs; erosion and sediment controls.--The 
     eligible project shall use standard crossing designs and 
     erosion and sediment controls.
       (C) Special aquatic sites.--No work on an eligible project 
     shall occur in special aquatic sites.
       (D) Delineations.--A delineation submitted with a request 
     for an approved jurisdictional determination under this 
     section shall be prepared--
       (i) in accordance with the 1987 Corps of Engineers Wetlands 
     Delineation Manual and any applicable regional supplement to 
     that manual;
       (ii) by a qualified professional wetland scientist; and
       (iii) with the field survey completed within the 
     appropriate season.
       (4) United states fish and wildlife service.--
       (A) Conservation measures.--
       (i) In general.--The eligible project shall implement the 
     standard conservation measures, including avoidance, seasonal 
     work windows, buffers, and trenchless methods, at all 
     crossings.
       (ii) Additional measures.--In addition to the standard 
     conservation measures, an eligible project relying on a 
     remedy under subparagraph (C)(ii) or (D)(ii) of subsection 
     (e)(4) shall implement the measures included in the 
     applicable habitat conservation plan.
       (B) Listed bats.--If listed bats may be present onsite of 
     an eligible project, the eligible project--
       (i) shall limit tree removal to the minimum required for 
     construction, and mark clearing limits in the field before 
     clearing;
       (ii)(I) shall avoid tree removal in suitable habitat during 
     the pup season (May 1 through July 31), or during such other 
     dates as the local field office may establish; and
       (II) if avoidance described in subclause (I) is not 
     possible, shall--

       (aa) conduct visual emergence surveys of trees 9 inches or 
     more in diameter at breast height; and
       (bb) delay removal of such trees if listed bats are 
     observed;

       (iii) shall not remove any documented roost tree, or any 
     tree located within 150 feet of a documented maternity roost;
       (iv) shall schedule bridge and culvert work--

       (I) outside the pup season described in clause (ii)(I); and
       (II) during the winter season, when listed bats are known 
     to use a structure, as established in cooperation with the 
     local field office;

       (v) shall use downward-facing, full cut-off lighting, and 
     direct temporary lighting away from suitable habitat, except 
     for lighting required under paragraph (2);
       (vi) shall ensure that fueling and other spill-risk 
     activities are conducted not less than 300 feet from any 
     caves, sinkholes, losing streams, and springs in karst areas; 
     and
       (vii) during nighttime hours in the autumn migration season 
     specified in the bird and bat conservation strategy or 
     habitat conservation plan submitted with a complete 
     application, when ambient temperature exceeds 50 degrees 
     Fahrenheit, shall feather turbine blades so that rotor speed 
     does not exceed 3 revolutions per minute at wind speeds below 
     the cut-in speed, which shall be not less than 3, and not 
     more than 4, meters per second, as specified in the bird and 
     bat conservation strategy or habitat conservation plan, 
     subject to the conditions that--

       (I) the specified season and cut-in speed shall apply 
     without further approval by the local field office under this 
     section;

       (II) a facility operating on the date of enactment of this 
     Act shall implement this clause to the extent feasible; and
       (III) any more-protective condition contained in an 
     applicable habitat conservation plan shall control.

       (C) Listed plants.--If a listed plant may be present onsite 
     of an eligible project, the eligible project shall--
       (i) mark listed plant areas and maintain a 50-foot buffer 
     from those areas;
       (ii) avoid ground disturbance, vegetation removal, and 
     hydrologic changes in suitable listed plant habitat; and
       (iii) clean equipment before entering the eligible project 
     site.
       (D) Other listed species.--
       (i) In general.--For any other listed species identified in 
     an IPaC species screening, the project sponsor shall 
     implement applicable species-specific conservation measures 
     published as of the date of enactment of this Act or 
     established in an applicable conservation strategy or habitat 
     conservation plan submitted with the applicable complete 
     application.
       (ii) Deemed authorizations.--A deemed take authorization 
     under subparagraph (C)(ii) or (D)(ii) of subsection (e)(4) 
     shall extend to a listed species described in clause (i) only 
     to the extent that the habitat conservation plan submitted 
     with the applicable complete application--

       (I) specifies measures in accordance with section 
     10(a)(2)(A) of the Endangered Species Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. 
     1539(a)(2)(A)); and
       (II) expressly identifies the listed species and any 
     authorized take of that species.

       (iii) Treatment.--No additional approval by a local field 
     office or subsequent publication of any measure shall be 
     required solely to invoke a remedy under this section.
       (E) Monitoring.--The eligible project shall complete not 
     less than 1 year of post-construction monitoring in 
     accordance with the Land-Based Wind Energy Guidelines.
       (F) Stop work.--
       (i) In general.--If a listed species is encountered during 
     construction of the eligible project in circumstances not 
     addressed by the applicable conservation measures or habitat 
     conservation plan--

       (I) the project sponsor shall--

       (aa) suspend work only at the affected location;
       (bb) notify the United States Fish and Wildlife Service of 
     the encounter; and
       (cc) implement applicable avoidance measures;

       (II) any activities unaffected by the encounter may 
     continue; and
       (III) an activity affected by the encounter may resume when 
     the activity can proceed in compliance with the applicable 
     covered authorization and conservation measures.

       (ii) Effect.--Nothing in this subparagraph authorizes an 
     otherwise prohibited take.
```
