# Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (Executive Session)

**Speaking:** Thune, John (T000250) [R-SD], Barrasso, John (B001261) [R-WY]

```
             Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act

  Mr. THUNE. Mr. President, in January of 2026, part of the key sewer 
lines

[[Page S5584]]

for the customers in the Washington, DC, area collapsed.
  The resulting sewage spill was, the Washington Post reported, ``one 
of the largest spills of untreated wastewater in U.S. history. Enough 
raw sewage to fill 364 Olympic-sized swimming pools flushed into the 
Potomac River north of the nation's capital, exacting an ecological 
toll that scientists are still trying to calculate.''
  Why do I bring this up? Well, I will tell you, because DC Water had 
first identified weaknesses in the pipe and proposed repairs a full 7 
years earlier--7 years earlier--but had been prevented from starting 
that by a Federal permitting process that dragged on and on and on 
until the pipe suffered a catastrophic failure.
  It is an excellent illustration of the need for permitting reform in 
this country. Too often everything from critical repairs to important 
new energy projects get bogged down in Federal bureaucracy and are 
delayed for not months but years at a time.
  And the Washington, DC, sewage spill is an outstanding example of the 
potential consequences.
  Now, it is important to ensure that serious environmental concerns 
are addressed before projects are undertaken, and it is essential to 
ensure that the concerns of local stakeholders, in particular local 
landowners, are listened to and that they have a voice in the approval 
process.
  But the Federal permitting process has gone way beyond that to 
include years of senseless delays, often caused by endless litigation 
from environmental groups or from Agencies that are set on studying a 
project literally to death.
  We have seen those delays happen on projects with everything from 
drinking water and irrigation to building affordable energy sources and 
better roads and bridges.
  And like in the case of the DC sewage spill, these delays can even 
apply to projects where we are simply replacing something that has been 
there for many years.
  These delays all add costs, and those costs are ultimately paid by 
families through things like higher energy costs, higher gas and diesel 
prices, and higher taxes.
  The legislation that we are considering today would address that. The 
Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act will streamline and 
reform the permitting process to ensure that projects are approved or 
rejected in a timely fashion.
  This is particularly good news on the energy front. Needless to say, 
Americans have been dealing with an elevated cost of living since the 
early days of the Biden administration, and when it comes to 
affordability, energy prices affect pretty much everything. We all know 
that.
  High energy costs don't just mean higher gas and electric bills, they 
mean higher costs for almost everything. And one of the best ways to 
keep prices of all kinds down is to expand domestic energy production, 
everything from oil and gas to solar and wind to hydropower.
  Too often, however, as I said, new energy projects get bogged down in 
Federal Government bureaucracy and end up in limbo for years.
  Streamlining bureaucracy, by contrast, like the Bipartisan American 
Affordability and Jobs Act will do, will speed up key projects and 
bring more jobs and more affordable prices along with it.
  We have talked about what this bill would do for energy projects, but 
there is another kind of project on a lot of Americans' minds right 
now, and that is data center construction.
  Communities in my State of South Dakota and around our country are 
concerned about the movement of data centers into their communities, in 
particular, about what their construction could mean for energy prices.
  And I am pleased to report that the Bipartisan American Affordability 
and Jobs Act would enact new protections for local communities. For 
starters, it would require that the data centers shoulder the energy 
costs associated with their operation and construction, whether that is 
day-to-day running costs or the cost of additional infrastructure.
  But the legislation doesn't stop there. It also provides technical 
assistance to communities to help them understand and explore the 
impact a proposed data center would have on their locality--everything 
from the economic impact to the energy costs and infrastructure 
requirements that would be associated with the project.
  There are a number of other good elements in this bill. I am always 
particularly invested in what legislation can do to help farmers and 
ranchers, which is the livelihood of our economy in South Dakota. And I 
am very pleased that this bill will extend Clean Water Act permits for 
farmers from 5 to 10 years, significantly reducing the frequency with 
which farmers have to engage with government bureaucracy with that 
process.
  This legislation will also provide for faster grazing-related reviews 
which will make a real difference to ranchers.
  If there is one thing Americans across our country said in the 
election last week, it is that they are hurting economically.
  This bill is one important way that we can help. Reforming the 
permitting process will help advance the kind of projects that will 
create new jobs, grow our economy, and increase wages, and more 
American energy development means a more reliable and affordable energy 
supply for American families.
  I want to thank Chairmen Shelley Moore Capito--and by chairmen, I 
should say Shelley Moore Capito and Mike Lee for their tremendous work 
on this legislation.
  This is a bipartisan bill, and President Trump strongly endorsed this 
legislation on Truth Social earlier this week.
  I look forward to swift Senate passage, and I hope the House will 
follow suit and get this legislation to the President's desk in the 
very near future.
  I yield the floor.
  I suggest the absence of a quorum.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The clerk will call the roll.
  The senior assistant executive clerk proceeded to call the roll.
  Mr. BARRASSO. Mr. President, I ask unanimous consent that the order 
for the quorum call be rescinded.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Without objection, it is so ordered.
  The majority whip.
```
