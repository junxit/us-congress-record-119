# COPD AWARENESS MONTH

**Speaking:** Crapo, Mike (C000880) [R-ID]

```
                          COPD AWARENESS MONTH

  Mr. CRAPO. Madam President, I join in recognizing November as Chronic 
Obstructive Pulmonary Disease Awareness Month and drawing attention to 
the importance of broadening understanding about this disease affecting 
far too many Americans.
  The U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention reports chronic 
obstructive pulmonary disease, or COPD, was the fifth leading cause of 
death in the United States in 2023, claiming 141,733 American lives. 
Thirty million Americans live with COPD, according to the COPD Action 
Alliance, while more than 16 million have been diagnosed, based on 
National Institutes of Health data.
  COPD is debilitating. It robs a person of breath and, with it, the 
ability to walk across a room, climb a flight of stairs, or play with a 
grandchild in the yard. It is also preventable and treatable and far 
more manageable when caught early. Awareness matters.
  The COPD Action Alliance also reports the disease impacts some 
Americans more than others: Rural Americans, including many in Idaho, 
are about twice as likely to have COPD as those living in large cities, 
and they often must travel the farthest to receive adequate care; 
veterans carry a disproportionate burden as well, as more than 1 
million veterans live with COPD, about 25 percent of the veteran 
population; and first responders, who breathe in smoke, chemicals and 
debris while protecting our communities, face an elevated risk as well.
  I support sustained investment in COPD research to better align these 
efforts with research into other diseases such as cancer and 
Alzheimer's. In January 2004, I formed the Congressional COPD Caucus to 
raise awareness of COPD and the threat it poses to our families and 
communities. I continue to cochair this caucus focused on improving 
outcomes by educating people about the disease and its challenges and 
leading numerous initiatives on behalf of patients experiencing COPD 
and other lung diseases.
  COPD Awareness Month is an opportunity to reverse the alarming 
impacts of COPD and advance the goals of preventive health. Helping 
more Americans recognize the warning signs, such as a chronic cough, 
shortness of breath during everyday activities, and wheezing or chest 
tightness, can help catch the disease early, reduce severity, and save 
money. Early diagnosis saves lives and money by keeping patients out of 
the emergency room and in their homes with their families.
  To Idahoans and all Americans living with COPD, your voices are heard 
in the U.S. Senate, and COPD is a disease Congress must not overlook.
```
