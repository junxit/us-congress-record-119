# Legislative Agenda (Executive Session)

**Speaking:** Barrasso, John (B001261) [R-WY]

```
                           Legislative Agenda

  Mr. BARRASSO. Mr. President, in just 52 days, the 119th Congress will 
end, and Senate Republicans are going to make sure that every second 
counts.
  The American people sent a clear message last Tuesday, and 
Republicans heard it very clearly. We know that we have to work quite 
hard--and will continue to work--to regain their trust, and we are 
going to begin by delivering results.
  We are going to do it right here in the U.S. Senate. The work has 
already begun. Republicans are still in the majority. President Trump 
is still in the White House. And in the next 52 days, we have important 
work to complete.
  We will waste no time. We will confirm dozens of conservative Federal 
judges to the courts. We will confirm many qualified nominees to 
positions in the Federal Government. We will pass historic permitting 
legislation and reform. We will fund the government.
  Some of this work is bipartisan. Much of it will require long hours. 
Republicans are ready for both. We welcome bipartisanship when it 
serves the national interest. Bipartisanship can never come at the 
expense of conservative values. We will never apologize for our 
conservative convictions.
  So let's begin with the judges. In the coming days, we will have two 
dozen Federal judges to confirm. Federal judges, as you know, serve 
lifetime appointments. Their decisions will shape our courts and our 
country for generations to come. There will be judges who will uphold 
the Constitution--those are the judges who have been nominated--and 
they will apply the laws as written.
  Under Chairman Grassley, the Senate Judiciary Committee has advanced 
judges at the fastest pace since Ronald Reagan. Now it is time to 
finish the work. If that means long days, we are here to work. If it 
means late nights, we are here to work. We will leave no vacancy 
behind.

[[Page S5585]]

  The same commitment applies to nominees across the Federal 
Government. Our committees are working to approve nominees under their 
jurisdiction. We will complete a final nominations package before the 
end of the year and will get them all confirmed. President Trump 
deserves to have his team in place. Senate Republicans will deliver.
  Next, Republicans will deliver to lower costs. American families are 
feeling the pain of high prices. They need relief. Fixing our country's 
permitting system is one of the best ways to provide that relief.
  America is ready to build, and we have the ability to build. We have 
the money available to build. We need to get Washington, DC, out of the 
way.
  Many energy projects that would help lower costs remain frozen and 
have been for years. They are waiting for the approval from the Federal 
Government. Even after being approved, lengthy lawsuits can drive up 
costs, create uncertainty, and cause needed projects not to be 
constructed.
  Senator Capito of West Virginia and Senator Lee of Utah have worked 
with their Democrat counterparts in the Senate to write a bipartisan 
bill designed to make it easier to build. Their legislation streamlines 
environmental reviews. It also reins in out-of-control judicial review 
and abusive lawsuits.
  These are commonsense reforms. Building better, faster, cheaper, and 
smarter is the right path to lower energy costs for American families.
  It is the responsible path both to respond to our Nation's energy 
needs and to respect the environment. That is why this week, 73 
Senators voted in favor of cutting through the government redtape that 
slows down our Nation's progress. You get to 73, you are talking about 
a lot of Republicans who support it and a lot of Democrats who support 
it. And President Trump says he will sign it. Congress needs to pass it 
and do it now.
  But what I hear is that Democrats in the House and Democrats who will 
be coming to the House in January are not so sure. Let me remind the 
Democrats and specifically the current minority leader in the Democrat 
House of what even the New York Times had to say in an editorial on 
November 12, posted today on their website.
  It said:

       The Senate this week is debating a bill on a subject that 
     is more important than it sounds.

  Even the New York Times admits what we are doing here is more 
important than it sounds.

       [P]ermitting reform.

  They go on to say:

       This bill would make it easier to build solar energy farms, 
     battery storage, power lines and more. As a result, it [will] 
     reduce energy prices.

  That is what Republicans and Democrats on this floor have been saying 
for the last several weeks.

       The status quo is expensive, dirty and unstable. The 
     country needs to build a lot of big, new things to make its 
     future cheaper and cleaner.

  There is a lot to this editorial that the House of Representatives 
and the incoming Democrats ought to pay attention to, which is why it 
is important for Democrats not just in this body but across the hall in 
the House to get in line and get this done and get those prices down. 
It is critical, and I am happy to see such bipartisan work being done 
right here in the U.S. Senate.
```
