# Text of Senate Amendment 6953

**Speaking:** Bennet, Michael F. (B001267) [D-CO]

```
  SA 6953. Mr. BENNET submitted an amendment intended to be proposed to 
amendment SA 6883 proposed by Mr. Thune (for Mrs. Capito (for herself, 
Mr. Lee, Mr. Whitehouse, and Mr. Heinrich)) to the bill H.R. 2347, to 
amend the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 to exclude from gross income 
any damages, other than punitive damages, received on account of any 
sexual acts or sexual contact; which was ordered to lie on the table; 
as follows:
       Beginning on page 74 of the amendment, strike line 20 and 
     all that follows through page 76, line 14, and insert the 
     following:
       ``(5) Remedy.--
       ``(A) In general.--Notwithstanding any other provision of 
     law, if a court determines that an authorization does not 
     comply with the requirements of this Act, the court may, in 
     order to redress that violation--
       ``(i) remand the applicable authorization to the agency--

       ``(I) taking into account the seriousness of the alleged 
     legal deficiency, including the likelihood that the 
     deficiency may be remedied by the agency on remand and that 
     the agency may reach the same decision consistent with 
     applicable law; and
       ``(II) with--

       ``(aa) specific instructions to correct errors or 
     deficiencies found by the court; and
       ``(bb) a reasonable schedule and deadline for the agency to 
     act on remand, which shall not exceed 180 days after the date 
     on which the order of the court was entered; or
       ``(ii) issue a preliminary injunction, in accordance with 
     subparagraph (B).
       ``(B) Preliminary injunctions.--
       ``(i) In general.--Subject to clause (ii), a court may 
     issue a preliminary injunction for the applicable 
     authorization under subparagraph (A)(ii) when--

       ``(I) there is a substantial likelihood the authorization 
     violates a Federal law other than--

       ``(aa) this Act; or
       ``(bb) division A of subtitle III of title 54, United 
     States Code;

       ``(II) necessary to prevent irreparable harm to the 
     plaintiff;
       ``(III) the balance of equities favors the plaintiff; and
       ``(IV) a preliminary injunction is in the public interest.

       ``(ii) Disruptive consequences.--In issuing a preliminary 
     injunction under clause (i), the court shall take into 
     account the potential disruptive consequences of issuing such 
     injunction, including impacts on public health and safety, 
     energy reliability, economic activity, ongoing projects, and 
     the public interest.
       ``(iii) Requirements.--A preliminary injunction issued by a 
     court under clause (i) shall last no longer than the time the 
     court finds is reasonably necessary--

       ``(I) for the correction of the violation described in 
     clause (i)(I); and
       ``(II) to prevent the irreparable harm described in clause 
     (i)(II).

       On page 76, line 18 of the amendment, strike ``(5)(A)'' and 
     insert ``(5)(A)(i)''.
       On page 76, line 24 of the amendment, strike ``(5)(A)'' and 
     insert ``(5)(A)(i)''.
       Beginning on page 483 of the amendment, strike line 21 and 
     all that follows through page 485, line 18, and insert the 
     following:
       ``(C) Remedy.--
       ``(i) In general.--Notwithstanding any other provision of 
     law, if a court determines that an agency action does not 
     comply with the requirements of this division, the court may, 
     in order to redress that violation--

       ``(I) remand the applicable agency action to the Federal 
     agency--

       ``(aa) taking into account the seriousness of the alleged 
     legal deficiency, including the likelihood that the 
     deficiency may be remedied by the agency on remand and that 
     the agency may reach the same decision consistent with 
     applicable law; and
       ``(bb) with--
       ``(AA) specific instructions to correct errors or 
     deficiencies found by the court; and
       ``(BB) a reasonable schedule and deadline for the agency to 
     act on remand, which shall not exceed 180 days after the date 
     on which the order of the court was entered; or

       ``(II) issue a preliminary injunction, in accordance with 
     clause (ii).

       ``(ii) Preliminary injunctions.--

       ``(I) In general.--Subject to subclause (II), a court may 
     issue a preliminary injunction for the applicable agency 
     action when--

       ``(aa) there is a substantial likelihood the agency action 
     violates a Federal law (other than this division or the 
     National Environmental Policy Act of 1969 (42 U.S.C. 4321 et 
     seq.));
       ``(bb) necessary to prevent irreparable harm to the 
     plaintiff;
       ``(cc) the balance of equities favors the plaintiff; and
       ``(dd) a preliminary injunction is in the public interest.

       ``(II) Disruptive consequences.--In issuing a preliminary 
     injunction under subclause (I), the court shall take into 
     account the potential disruptive consequences of issuing such 
     injunction, including impacts on public health and safety, 
     energy reliability, economic activity, ongoing projects, and 
     the public interest.
       ``(III) Requirements.--The effective period of a 
     preliminary injunction issued by a court under subclause (I) 
     shall be not longer than the time the court finds is 
     reasonably necessary--

       ``(aa) for the correction of the violation described in 
     subclause (I)(aa); and
       ``(bb) to prevent the irreparable harm described in 
     subclause (I)(bb).
```
