# Introductory Statement on S. 5585

**Speaking:** Padilla, Alex (P000145) [D-CA]

**Measures:** S. 5585

```
      By Mr. PADILLA (for himself and Mr. Scott of South Carolina):
  S. 5585. A bill to require a pilot program to support members of the 
Armed Forces and military families transitioning to civilian life, and 
for other purposes; to the Committee on Veterans' Affairs.
  Mr. PADILLA. Mr. President, I rise to introduce the Building 
Readiness and Integration for Dependents Going to Civilian Environments 
Act of 2026 or the BRIDGE Act of 2026. This legislation seeks to ensure 
that servicemembers and military families have the support they need to 
successfully transition from military service to civilian life.
  Each year, thousands of servicemembers leave the Armed Forces and 
face significant challenges navigating employment, healthcare, 
education, benefits, and community reintegration. While the Department 
of Defense's Transition Assistance Program provides important 
preseparation services, many military families still struggle to access 
personalized and coordinated support during this major life 
transistion.
  The BRIDGE Act of 2026 would establish a 3-year pilot program at four 
geographically diverse military installations to provide nonclinical, 
community-based transition support for servicemembers and their 
families. This legislation recognizes that transition is not just an 
individual experience but a whole-family process that requires tailored 
support and long-term stability.
  Under this act, eligible servicemembers within 1 year of separation 
and their families would receive individualized transition assistance, 
including peer-led support groups, resilience workshops, spouse 
employment assistance, skills training and credentialing, benefits 
navigation, and reintegration support. Participants would also be 
paired with dedicated transition coordinators to help develop 
personalized transition plans and connect families with existing 
Federal, State, nonprofit, and community resources.
  This bill would further prioritize servicemembers facing accelerated 
or involuntary separations and require participating organizations to 
track outcomes related to employment, financial stability, resilience, 
and family well-being. In addition, the legislation would direct the 
Department of Defense and Department of Veterans Affairs to coordinate 
services to ensure a seamless postseparation transition for 
servicemembers and their families.
  The BRIDGE Act of 2026 is an important step toward strengthening 
military family readiness and improving long-term outcomes for veterans 
and their loved ones. Servicemembers and military families sacrifice 
greatly in service to our Nation, and they deserve support that 
reflects the full scope of the transition challenges they face.
  I would like to thank Senator Scott of South Carolina for coleading 
this bill with me. I hope our colleagues will join us in supporting 
this effort to strengthen transition support for servicemembers and 
military families.
  I look forward to working with my colleagues to pass the BRIDGE Act 
of 2026 as swiftly as possible.
```
