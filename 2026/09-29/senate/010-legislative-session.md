build out our State's workforce in behavioral healthcare, and it comes

  Mr. SCHATZ. Madam President, you know, this is about a couple of very 
simple questions. The first question is, Humans in control of 
technology or not? That is the first question.
  Even the mechanisms that we are accustomed to, for instance, in 
aviation, where you say ``Before this plane can take off, we have to 
certify to its safety,'' don't really get at the fundamental problem 
because what we have is technology that is equivalent to a plane being 
able to take off on its own while it is still being built. So the 
question is first, Humans in control or not?
  The second question is, If we agree that humans should be in control, 
should this be voluntary or not? Do we trust--not any individual CEO or 
board of directors, but do we trust that companies that are rolling 
toward the biggest IPOs in human history will voluntarily regulate 
themselves as opposed to maximize the value of that IPO, maximize 
shareholder profit?
  We have traditionally tried to strike the right balance, and 
Democrats and Republicans don't always see the sort of level of 
regulation necessary on an eye-to-eye basis, but I think at some level, 
there are 100 of us who believe that when it comes to serious risk to 
public safety, that those should not be voluntary safety regimes. They 
should be mandatory. They should be Federal. It shouldn't be every
