# TRIBUTE TO CLAY LACY

**Speaking:** Moran, Jerry (M000934) [R-KS]

```
                          TRIBUTE TO CLAY LACY

● Mr. MORAN. Madam President, today, I want to recognize Clay 
Lacy, a Kansan whose distinguished career of dedication, innovation and 
service has made him one of the most accomplished and influential 
aviators in the history of American flight.
  Clay Lacy grew up on a farm near Wichita--the Air Capital of the 
World--and from an early age, he knew he wanted to fly. Inspired by his 
grandmother and early aviation mentors, Clay pursued his passion with 
dedication. He learned to fly by the age of 12 and went on to earn his 
flight instructor certificate. In 1952, he joined United Airlines as a 
copilot. Just 1 year later, he joined the California Air National 
Guard, serving our Nation as a military aviator flying the F-86 Sabre 
fighter jet.
  Over a career that spanned more than seven decades, Clay has 
accumulated more than 55,000 flight hours and flown hundreds of 
different aircraft. In addition to his service as a United Airlines 
captain and military aviator, Clay distinguished himself as a test 
pilot, air racer, and entrepreneur. In 1968, he founded Clay Lacy 
Aviation, the first executive jet management company on the West Coast, 
helping establish a new standard for business aviation.

[[Page S5161]]

  Clay's spirit of innovation extended far beyond his work as a pilot. 
His passion for aviation inspired him to pioneer new techniques in 
aerial cinematography. Through Astrovision, an air-to-air camera system 
he developed, Clay revolutionized the way aircraft were filmed and 
photographed, contributing to some of the most iconic aviation imagery 
featured in motion pictures, television, and advertising.
  Just as importantly, Clay has used his success to create 
opportunities for others. Through his philanthropy and the work of the 
Clay Lacy Foundation, he has supported aviation education and expanded 
educational opportunities for young people across the country.
  Clay's extraordinary contributions to aviation have been recognized 
with numerous honors. In 2010, he was inducted into the National 
Aviation Hall of Fame, followed by his induction into the Kansas 
Aviation Hall of Fame in 2011. He has also received the Federal 
Aviation Administration's Wright Brothers Master Pilot Award, the 
United Airlines Historical Foundation's William S. Arnott Legacy Award, 
the National Business Aviation Association's Meritorious Service to 
Aviation Award, and many other distinguished honors.
  On November 10, 2026, Clay will receive the Kansas Aviation Museum 
Lifetime Achievement Award in recognition of his storied career, his 
contributions to the aviation industry, and the lasting legacy of a 
Wichita-born aviator whose influence has reached around the globe. Clay 
Lacy has devoted his life to advancing the aviation industry he has 
loved and cherished since he was a young boy. His remarkable career 
reflects not only a passion for flight, but also a commitment to 
innovation, service, mentorship, and giving back to the next 
generation.
  I thank Clay for his steadfast service to the aviation industry, our 
Nation, and the State of Kansas. His extraordinary contributions will 
continue to inspire generations of aviators and Kansans for years to 
come.●
```
