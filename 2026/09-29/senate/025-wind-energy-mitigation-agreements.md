# WIND ENERGY MITIGATION AGREEMENTS

**Speaking:** Grassley, Chuck (G000386) [R-IA]

```
                   WIND ENERGY MITIGATION AGREEMENTS

  Mr. GRASSLEY. Madam President, for over a year, the Department of 
Defense has stopped signing wind energy mitigation agreements through 
its Siting Clearinghouse, which is negatively impacting energy projects 
in Iowa. The reason the Pentagon Office of Energy, Installations, and 
Environment has provided for halting mitigation agreements is that the 
Department is reevaluating its process, analyzing threats to national 
security.
  I agree that the Department of Defense must ensure projects do not 
put national security at risk. That said, the Department has been 
``reevaluating the process'' for over a year. We have the most capable 
military and intelligence in the world. The Pentagon has the ability to 
identify and mitigate risks in a timelier fashion--especially given 
that wind energy projects are nothing new, nor are these agreements. 
Given the current administration's hostility to wind energy, it makes 
me wonder if this reevaluation is being used as an excuse to hamstring 
these projects.
  In addition to the capabilities the Pentagon has, not all wind energy 
projects pose the same concerns to national security based on the 
location of the project. For example, Iowa is a landlocked State. The 
projects underway in Iowa are not going to have the same level of 
national security concern as, say, a project where there are several 
major Active-Duty military installations, global shipping ports, and 
international border access points.
  I have raised concerns with the Pentagon and administration officials 
and have spoken directly with Secretary Hegseth about the importance of 
these projects for Iowa. I stressed that the mitigation agreement 
process must restart in a timely manner. Unfortunately, I have not been 
given an answer from the Pentagon on June, July, and September 2026 
inquiries. This is unacceptable.
  Congress established the Siting Clearinghouse through the fiscal year 
2011 National Defense Authorization Act, to evaluate and explore 
impacts and mitigation options related to alternative energy production 
to preserve military missions. Since its establishment, energy projects 
have been able to work with the Siting Clearinghouse to mitigate any 
concerns raised and agree on the most appropriate path forward.
  Wind energy is extremely important in Iowa. Iowa is a top leader in 
wind power capacity and share of total electricity generation. Delays 
in new wind energy projects mean holdups in energy generation and 
independence, which can lower energy prices for consumers. 
Unfortunately, the Department of Defense does not seem to recognize the 
importance of timely action.
  I am supportive of provisions in the fiscal year 2027 National 
Defense Authorization Act that would put tighter timelines on the 
Pentagon for completing these agreements. It is important that wind 
energy projects can continue and that the bureaucratic redtape blocking 
Iowa wind projects is disposed of soon.

                          ____________________
```
