# TRIBUTE TO BOB QUICK

**Speaking:** McConnell, Mitch (M000355) [R-KY]

```
                          TRIBUTE TO BOB QUICK

● Mr. McCONNELL. Madam President, I rise today to pay tribute to 
a true leader of business in central Kentucky, president and CEO of 
Commerce Lexington, Bob Quick. Bob marked 25 years of service to the 
organization earlier this year and retired at the end of July. Bob 
deserves significant recognition for the positive change he has 
implemented in the Lexington business community, so I ask my colleagues 
to please join me in honoring and celebrating his more than 25 years of 
dedicated service.
  Before coming to Lexington in 2001, Bob had previously served as 
executive vice president of the Iowa City Area Chamber of Commerce and 
also president of the Evansville, Indiana Chamber of Commerce. Already 
a seasoned chamber veteran, he was impressed by what Lexington offered 
him and his family, and he knew he could find a way to make a 
difference as well.
  Bob immediately got to work. Under his leadership, he oversaw the 
2004 merger of Lexington's economic and workforce development 
organizations, streamlining the process for promoting business 
development in the region. Commerce Lexington, Inc., was the chamber's 
new name, and its founding ushered in a new age for the city and its 
future success in economic development and growth. Since then, Bob has 
successfully led nearly 2,000 members of the Lexington chamber in its 
efforts to shape business policy. His accomplishments have not gone 
unnoticed.
  Commerce Lex has received numerous awards during Bob's leadership. 
These include being named the Chamber of the Year and Economic 
Development Organization of the Year. Bob's work has also led to the 
Greater Lexington Region being named a top place for business and 
industry. His leadership in promoting greater regional collaboration 
through trust and relationship building is one of his lasting legacies. 
His involvement in those efforts led to the creation of the regional 
public policy group, improvements in regional infrastructure in central 
Kentucky, elevated regional marketing efforts and events like the 
Greater Lexington Washington, DC, Fly-In and the Regional Summit--all 
integral to advancing the region's economic success over the years.
  Bob is a driven professional, and his impact continues to put 
Lexington in a better position, but it is clear that where Bob 
distinguishes himself most is through his character. He is a positive 
force in the lives of so many and all those around him benefit from his 
presence and steady leadership. Often quick to credit others, Bob's 
peers praise him for leading with grace and dignity. They have seen how 
well he connects with people and always seeks to collaborate with local 
leaders on important projects.
  I have had the pleasure of visiting with Bob during numerous 
occasions back home in the Commonwealth and in Washington, DC. It is 
evident that he cares deeply for the Lexington community and has 
dedicated a lot of effort to making sure the city punches above its 
weight, which supports the Commonwealth as a whole. There is no doubt 
Bob has succeeded in this goal, and the greater Lexington community 
continues to grow thanks to his work.
  In addition to his role at Commerce Lexington, Bob has served central 
Kentucky as a board member for several other organizations, including 
the Lexington Industrial Foundation, Bluegrass Sports Commission, and 
Downtown Lexington Partnership Board. He has also helped represent 
Lexington's interests on a wider scale through regional organizations 
focused on sustainability, business competition, and policy groups. 
There are few areas where Bob is not actively involved in making 
positive change.
  I know Bob's wife Julie and his entire family are so proud of him for 
his hard work and dedicated service, and I am glad they are a staple of 
the Lexington community.
  There are two types of leaders: those who seek to make a point and 
those who actively make a difference. I am certain Bob is a difference-
maker. Lexington is better off thanks to Bob Quick and his hard work in 
the community. Once again, I would ask my colleagues to join me in 
congratulating him on a successful career and thanking him for his 25 
years of service to Commerce Lexington.●

                          ____________________
```
