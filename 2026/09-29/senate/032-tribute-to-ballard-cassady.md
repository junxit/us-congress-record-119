# TRIBUTE TO BALLARD CASSADY

**Speaking:** McConnell, Mitch (M000355) [R-KY]

```
                       TRIBUTE TO BALLARD CASSADY

● Mr. McCONNELL. Madam President, I rise today to pay tribute to 
a friend who has led the Kentucky Bankers Association for more than 40 
years, president and CEO Ballard Cassady. Ballard stepped down at the 
end of July, and his career and leadership at the helm of this 
organization deserve significant praise. I ask my colleagues to please 
join me in honoring Ballard and applauding his four decades of service 
to KBA and to the people of Kentucky.
  Growing up in Pikeville, Ballard learned the value and importance of 
hard work from his first jobs picking strawberries and bailing hay. He 
also learned from his father that acquiring a good education could help 
him make a living. So he went off to Transylvania University and got 
his bachelor's degree in business administration. With a solidified 
interest in business, Ballard then decided to go straight through and 
earn his master's in business administration from Vanderbilt. After 
several years leading Citizens Bank of Pikeville and a stint in public 
service, Ballard took on the role of president at KBA. To those who 
know him, it is no surprise that Ballard assumed such a prestigious 
role so early in his career. Since he started in 1986, he has 
represented Kentucky's banks exceptionally well.
  Ballard's steadfast leadership has been marked by his comprehensive 
understanding of KBA's member banks and communicating their priorities 
to others. As a former banker himself, he recognizes how much these 
organizations do for their region, especially in rural counties. There 
is no doubt he shows up to work every day ready to fight for their 
concerns. And I am so glad he has been there representing them along 
the way.
  Ballard often says that local banks are the backbone of their 
communities. I couldn't agree more. From the pandemic to historic 
flooding and devastating tornadoes, Kentucky has seen more than its 
fair share of natural disasters in the last several years. When these 
misfortunes strike, banks are part of the frontline of recovery, 
immediately working to distribute loans and Federal funds to help 
businesses get back up on their feet. If it weren't for these 
institutions, recovery would take much longer--time that hard-working 
Kentuckians can't waste. Thanks to Ballard's community-oriented 
leadership, the Commonwealth's banks have stepped up to the plate time 
and again to take care of their communities during difficult times. And 
Ballard is vocal and proud of the work they have done.
  While his advocacy work at KBA has been top notch, Ballard is also a 
great person and a fixture throughout the Commonwealth. His involvement 
includes service on the board of trustees for the Wisconsin Graduate 
School of Banking and the Louisiana State University Banking School of 
the South, where he focuses his efforts in education.
  I have enjoyed visiting with him over the years in Washington, DC, 
and also back home in Kentucky. We share a firm belief in the power of 
free enterprise, and Ballard knows that communities flourish when 
capital has less restrictions. I am so glad KBA has had Ballard on 
their side for the last four decades. There is no doubt they are better 
off thanks to his tireless efforts and his resounding support for the 
services they provide to Kentuckians.
  Ballard, while the KBA will be in good hands upon your departure, 
know that your service and devotion to the community leaves a lasting 
legacy. I know that your wife Marcia, children Molly, Cash, and Jacob, 
and all of your grandchildren are so proud of you. Thank you for your 
dedication to businesses throughout the Commonwealth,

[[Page S5162]]

and I wish you well in this next chapter of your life.●

                          ____________________
```
