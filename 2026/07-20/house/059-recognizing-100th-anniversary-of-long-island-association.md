  Mr. LaLOTA. Mr. Speaker, I ask unanimous consent that all Members may

  Mr. LaLOTA. Mr. Speaker, tonight, I am honored to host a Special 
Order in recognition of the 100th anniversary of the Long Island 
Association.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize an institution that has helped 
shape Long Island's economy, strengthened its communities, and 
championed its future for the past century.
  For 100 years, the Long Island Association has served as the leading 
voice of Long Island's business community, bringing together employers, 
entrepreneurs, nonprofit organizations, educators, labor leaders, and 
public officials around a shared mission: ensuring Long Island remains 
one of the best places in America to live, work, raise a family, and 
build a business.
  Founded in 1926 as the Long Island Chamber of Commerce, the 
organization emerged during a period of tremendous growth with an 
ambitious mission: to promote Long Island's unique strengths and expand 
economic opportunity throughout the region.
  That mission has endured, even as Long Island has evolved from a 
collection of agricultural communities and seasonal destinations into 
one of the Nation's most dynamic regional economies.
  Today, Long Island is home to world-class manufacturers, innovative 
life science companies, internationally recognized research 
institutions, thriving small businesses, and vibrant downtowns.
  Throughout that transformation, the Long Island Association has been 
a steadfast advocate for policies that encourage investment, 
innovation, and job creation.
  Whether advancing critical infrastructure projects, strengthening 
workforce development, or helping businesses navigate extraordinary 
challenges--from the aftermath of September 11 to Superstorm Sandy to 
the COVID-19 pandemic--the LIA has consistently demonstrated thoughtful 
leadership and an unwavering commitment to our region.
  Its work continues today as Long Island confronts many of the 
challenges that will define our next century, including affordability, 
housing, infrastructure, energy reliability, and maintaining a business 
climate that allows employers of every size to succeed. New York's 
exceptionally high tax burden only makes those challenges more 
difficult.
  Meeting those challenges is essential, not only for today's economy 
but for ensuring that future generations can continue to build 
successful careers and rewarding lives on Long Island.
  Mr. Speaker, I will also recognize the outstanding leadership of the 
Long Island Association, who are joining us in the gallery this 
evening:
  Chairman Lawrence J. Waldman; Acting President and Chief Executive 
Officer Stacey Sikes; and members of the board of directors, including 
Billy Haugland, Katherine Heaviside, and Luis Vazquez.
  Their leadership and the dedication of countless other board members,

[[Page H4674]]

staff, volunteers, and business leaders over the past century have 
positioned the Long Island Association for continued success in the 
years ahead.

  As someone privileged to represent New York's First Congressional 
District, I know that Long Island's greatest asset has always been its 
people--the entrepreneurs, workers, educators, researchers, veterans, 
community leaders, and innovators whose talent and determination make

  Mr. Speaker, I yield to the gentlewoman from New York (Ms. Gillen).
  Ms. GILLEN. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to celebrate the 100th 
anniversary of the Long Island Association. For a century, it has been 
an institution at the heart of Long Island's economy and business 
community.
  I thank Chairman Waldman, Acting President Sikes, and the 
association's board members who traveled to Washington this week. It is 
a privilege to reaffirm our shared commitment to prosperity and 
opportunity for every Long Islander.
  Long Island's businesses and industries contribute more than $200 
billion to our Nation's economy each year. We are an economic 
powerhouse, driving innovation and discoveries in national security, 
medicine, and next-generation technologies.
  Giving Long Island's businesses a strong base with no limits on their 
success is how we bolster our local economy and our way of life.
  Core to its mission, the Long Island Association has been a crucial 
bridge between businesses and local and Federal government leaders. 
That partnership is foundational to the success of every entrepreneur, 
industry, and family-owned business that built Long Island.
  Our work here in Washington is first and foremost to deliver for our 
communities through bipartisanship and common sense. Doing so through 
pro-business and pro-worker policies gives our region the room to grow 
and stay competitive.
  The Long Island Association partnership has been instrumental in 
helping meet the challenges that we face. We must make Long Island more 
affordable to keep attracting world-class talent and give companies the 
chance to invest in themselves.
  That starts with lowering the high cost and taxes our families and 
businesses pay, including fully and permanently eliminating the SALT 
cap and undoing harmful tariffs. It means making housing more 
affordable and ensuring every young professional and hardworking family 
has a place to call home.
  We must meet our energy needs with an all-of-the-above strategy and 
guarantee our infrastructure can support a growing economy. We must cut 
Federal red tape and invest in our industries to fuel greater 
opportunity across our region.
  I am proud to have worked with the association and with my Long 
Island colleagues across the aisle to tackle these challenges head on. 
I remain committed to being a trusted voice in Washington for Long 
Island's businesses and workers.
  As we celebrate the Long Island Association's 100 years of 
excellence, I look forward to our continued partnership. 
Congratulations on this historic milestone and the beginning of another 
remarkable century of service.

  Mr. SUOZZI. Mr. Speaker, I am so happy to be joining with my 
colleagues in the spirit of bipartisanship to celebrate the 100th 
anniversary of the Long Island Association.
  It has been a long journey, where over 100 years, a few name changes, 
under different leadership, and over a transition from farmland to 
suburbia to one of the densest, most productive regions in the Nation, 
the Long Island Association has brought together the best minds in 
business, labor, education, government, and the not-for-profit 
community to lay out a vision to move our community forward.
  The Long Island Association has helped lead the way to make Long 
Island one of the most powerful regional economies in the United States 
of America.
  Now, the Long Island Association has not done everything right. In 
preparing for this speech, I learned that the Long Island Association, 
along with none other than Robert Moses, proposed a bridge from 
Connecticut to Long Island. It might have been a good idea for business 
and traffic, but it would have ruined my beaches.
  I have been in public life for over 30 years, first elected as mayor 
in 1993. As a result, I had the opportunity to be friendly with each 
leader of the Long Island Association during that time, from Jim 
Larocca to Matt Crosson to Kevin Law to Matt Cohen, and now the 
extraordinary Stacey Sikes.
  I worked together with Stacey when I was Nassau County Executive. I 
saw firsthand her intelligence, work ethic, integrity, and her ability 
to bring people together. She, together with our chairman, Larry 
Waldman, and so many other good people on the board, we realize the 
Long Island Association is in very good hands.
  For a century, the Long Island Association has been in the room for 
nearly every major conversation about our region's future. It helped 
advocate for the creation of Jones Beach State Park. It advocated for 
the Long Island Expressway and the Grand Central Parkway.
  It was there for the growth of Levittown and the merger of North 
Shore University Hospital and the Glen Cove Hospital, which laid the 
foundation for what is now Northwell Health.

  The Long Island Association has supported the growth of research and 
innovation and has brought together leaders to address the workforce 
development, housing, clean water, energy, and the stewardship of our 
natural resources like the Long Island Sound.
  The association knows what businesses and workers need. It knows 
where Long Island commerce is pulling ahead and where it is falling 
behind. It understands the challenges we are facing when it comes to 
housing, transportation, workforce shortages, and energy reliability. 
It knows who needs to be in the room to ensure Long Island stays 
competitive in a rapidly changing economy.
  We need the Long Island Association now more than ever. In our 
contemporary world, with the division between Democrats and Republicans 
and a fractured media environment, it is hard to know who to trust. We 
need the Long Island Association to continue to bring us together in 
search of a common vision to make Long Island the best place to live, 
work, and enjoy our lives.
  The challenges we face today are different than they were 100 years 
ago, but the need for people to work together to cross party lines, to 
cross from business to labor, from elected officials to the not-for-
profit community and education is the same need we have always had.
  We must work together to build upon the progress that has been made 
to increase the SALT deduction and make life more affordable for Long 
Islanders.
  We must work together to build more housing in downtowns so that we 
can expand the tax base and create an environment that is attractive 
and affordable to young people, and thereby expand our workforce and 
create cool downtowns.
  We must work together to ensure that our energy is sustainable, 
reliable, and affordable. We must work together to improve our 
infrastructure, reduce traffic, and hasten our commutes.
  We must work together to protect the environment and to keep our 
communities safe so that we can maintain one of the greatest qualities 
of life of any region in the Nation.
  Working together, Democrats and Republicans, in league with our 
partners represented by the Long Island Association, we know that Long 
Island's best days are ahead.
  Congratulations on an extraordinary first century, and here is to the 
next 100 years.

              Recognizing Ken Eppler, Outstanding Hoosier

  Mr. MESSMER. Mr. Speaker, I rise to recognize an outstanding Hoosier 
whose dedication to our country did not end when he took off the 
uniform.
  This week, I will have the honor of welcoming Evansville Army veteran 
Ken Eppler and his lovely wife, Cathy, to my congressional office here 
at the United States Capitol.
  Following his service to our Nation, Ken found great happiness in his 
incredible talent as a painter. He graciously lent me one of his 
beautiful paintings, The Enduring Spirit to display here in the Halls 
of Congress in honor of America's 250th anniversary.
  Ken served our Nation with honor in the United States Army, earning 
both Airborne and Air Assault qualifications before serving with the 
101st Airborne Division during the Gulf war.
  He later continued his service in the Indiana National Guard and has 
made us all proud as a defender of our country and a wonderful member 
of our community. His commitment to our Nation is reflected not only in 
his military service, but also in the remarkable artwork he continues 
to create.
  The Enduring Spirit depicts three colonial children standing together 
playing the fife and drum while proudly holding an American 250 flag. 
To me, this patriotic painting is a powerful reminder that the ideals 
of freedom and love of country are passed from one generation down to 
the next. It is a privilege to display his incredible artwork for my 
colleagues and all who visit Washington, especially those Hoosiers 
coming to our Nation's Capital.
  Ken has said that he hopes his artwork inspires students to love our 
country and encourages young people to learn more about our Nation's 
great history. I cannot think of a better message to share with the 
myriads of people who travel to Washington and visit our country's 
Capital.
  Indiana's Eighth District is proud to call Ken Eppler one of our own, 
and I am honored to display his beautiful artwork which I think 
reflects the very best of Hoosier and American patriotism.
