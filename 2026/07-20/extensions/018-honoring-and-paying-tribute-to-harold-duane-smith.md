# HONORING AND PAYING TRIBUTE TO HAROLD DUANE SMITH

```
           HONORING AND PAYING TRIBUTE TO HAROLD DUANE SMITH

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. MATT VAN EPPS

                              of tennessee

                    in the house of representatives

                         Monday, July 20, 2026

  Mr. VAN EPPS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor and pay tribute to a 
remarkable hero from Houston County, Tennessee, who answered the call 
of duty during a critical chapter in our Nation's history. As the years 
pass, the profound debt we owe to our Korean War veterans only grows.
  Of the 6.8 million Americans who served during the Korean War era, 
fewer than 300,000 remain with us today. This generation, now largely 
in their nineties, represents a rapidly dwindling connection to a 
pivotal struggle for global freedom. It is our sacred obligation to 
ensure their stories are told and their sacrifices are never forgotten.
  Today, I ask my colleagues to join me in recognizing the 
extraordinary service, sacrifice, and lifelong dedication of Harold 
Duane Smith. He represents the very best of Tennessee's Volunteer 
Spirit and the enduring resilience of the American servicemember.
  Mr. Harold Duane Smith was born in Flint, Michigan, on May 14, 1935. 
He began his distinguished military career when he enlisted in the 
United States Navy on January 21, 1955. For more than two decades, he 
served his country with honor and distinction before retiring from 
active duty on February 1, 1976.
  A testament to the daily perils of naval service, Mr. Smith recalls 
an incident during the Vietnam War while serving aboard the USS Kitty 
Hawk (CVA-63) in 1966 or 1967. A weapon-loading crew accidentally 
dropped a Mark 82 (Mk 82) 500-pound general-purpose bomb onto the 
flight deck, crushing its nose fuse. Displaying extraordinary composure 
under immense pressure, Mr. Smith successfully disarmed the damaged 
weapon, preventing what could have been a catastrophic explosion that 
threatened the ship and her crew.
  Mr. Smith's commitment to national security did not end with his 
retirement from the Navy. On the very day he retired from active duty, 
he began civilian service with the Nuclear Weapons Laboratory, working 
in Albuquerque, New Mexico, and Tonopah, Nevada. For the next twenty 
years, until January 31, 1996, he served as an Explosive Ordnance 
Disposal technician, testing weapons systems and ensuring the safety 
and readiness of our Nation's strategic deterrent. His more than forty 
years of combined military and civilian service stand as a remarkable 
testament to his patriotism. Mr. Smith currently resides in Cumberland 
City, Tennessee.
  Please join me in offering our deepest gratitude for his faithful 
service and proudly preserve their legacies in the United States 
Congress.
```
