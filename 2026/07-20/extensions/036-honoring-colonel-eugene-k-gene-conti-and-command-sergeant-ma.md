# HONORING COLONEL EUGENE K. "GENE" CONTI AND COMMAND SERGEANT MAJOR FRANK ROSSI

```
 HONORING COLONEL EUGENE K. ``GENE'' CONTI AND COMMAND SERGEANT MAJOR 
                              FRANK ROSSI

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. MATT VAN EPPS

                              of tennessee

                    in the house of representatives

                         Monday, July 20, 2026

  Mr. VAN EPPS. Mr. Speaker. I rise today to honor two distinguished 
veterans and residents of Houston County, Tennessee, whose combined 
lifetimes of service embody the very best of our Nation's Armed Forces: 
Colonel Eugene K. ``Gene'' Conti, U.S. Marine Corps, Retired. and 
Command Sergeant Major Frank Rossi, U.S. Army, Retired.
  Colonel Eugene K. ``Gene'' Conti began a distinguished 26-year career 
in the United States Marine Corps after earning his commission through 
the Platoon Leaders Class in 1977. After receiving his wings as a Naval 
Aviator in September 1979, he flew CH-46 Sea Knight helicopters with 
the 2nd Marine Aircraft Wing. He served on numerous operational 
deployments, including multiple Mediterranean tours and a critical NATO 
support mission. Colonel Conti later trained the next generation of 
Marine aviators as an instructor with Marine

[[Page E712]]

Helicopter Training Squadron 204 before managing complex aviation 
logistics as an Assistant Program Manager at the Naval Air Systems 
Command. He went on to command Marine Medium Helicopter Squadron 264, 
leading Marines during operations in Liberia and deployments with the 
26th Marine Expeditionary Unit. After earning master's degrees from 
both the University of Southern California and the National Defense 
University, he concluded his active-duty career as Executive Officer of 
the Naval Aviation Depot at Cherry Point. He retired in 2003 after 
earning the Meritorious Service Medal and the Navy Commendation Medal.
  Command Sergeant Major Frank Rossi matched that standard of 
excellence through 33 years of distinguished service in the United 
States Army from 1985 to 2018. He rose to the rank of Command Sergeant 
Major, where his leadership strengthened major military installations 
around the world. He served in Germany during the final years of the 
Cold War and later trained soldiers at Fort Jackson, Fort Moore, and 
Fort Liberty. During Operation Desert Shield and Operation Desert 
Storm, he deployed to liberate Kuwait. He later completed two combat 
deployments to Afghanistan in support of Operation Enduring Freedom. 
Throughout his career, he strengthened joint-force operations and 
earned some of our military's highest honors, including the Legion of 
Merit, the Defense Meritorious Service Medal, and the Joint Service 
Commendation Medal.
  Mr. Speaker, these men did not end their commitment to service when 
they retired from active duty. Today, both call Houston County, 
Tennessee, home, where they continue to strengthen their community. 
Colonel Conti serves as the dedicated Quartermaster for VFW Post 9689, 
while Command Sergeant Major Rossi continues to mentor fellow veterans 
and community members and preserves our Nation's military heritage. It 
is an honor to represent Tennessee's 7th Congressional District, where 
heroes like Colonel Conti and Command Sergeant Major Rossi continue to 
serve. I ask my colleagues to join me in expressing our deepest 
gratitude for their combined 59 years of unwavering service in defense 
of American freedom.
```
