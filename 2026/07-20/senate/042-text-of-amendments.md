# TEXT OF AMENDMENTS

**Measures:** S. 4784

```
                           TEXT OF AMENDMENTS

  SA 6703. Mr. SCHUMER submitted an amendment intended to be proposed 
by him to the bill S. 4784, to authorize appropriations for fiscal year 
2027 for military activities of the Department of Defense, for military 
construction, and for defense activities of the Department of Energy, 
to prescribe military personnel strengths for such fiscal year, and for 
other purposes; which was ordered to lie on the table; as follows:

       At the end of subtitle E of title X, add the following:

     SEC. 1049A. NATIONAL INTELLIGENCE ESTIMATE WITH RESPECT TO 
                   CYBER AND PHYSICAL THREATS TO THE ELECTRIC 
                   GRID.

       (a) Findings.--Congress finds the following:
       (1) The electric grid of the United States--the commercial 
     electric power generation, transmission, and distribution 
     system comprising power lines and other infrastructure--
     delivers the electricity that is essential for modern life. 
     As a result, the reliability of the electric grid, ability to 
     meet the electricity demand of consumers at all times, is not 
     a luxury. Such reliability is fundamental to the health and 
     safety of the people of the United States and the continued 
     strength of the economy of the United States, making the 
     reliability of the electric grid a paramount issue of 
     national interest.
       (2) The annual report on worldwide threats issued by the 
     Director of National Intelligence pursuant to section 108B of 
     the National Security Act of 1947 (50 U.S.C. 3043b) (commonly 
     known as the ``Annual Threat Assessment'') has consistently 
     found that state and non-state cyber actors pose the most 
     significant threats to the critical infrastructure of the 
     United States.
       (3) The 2025 Annual Threat Assessment found that foreign 
     adversaries are intent on attacking ``critical 
     infrastructure, disrupting utility company business networks 
     or manipulating poorly secured control systems''.
       (4) The 2026 Annual Threat Assessment states that ``cyber 
     actors from China, Russia, Iran, North Korea, and ransomware 
     groups will continue to pose critical threats to U.S. 
     networks and critical infrastructure''.
       (5) According to the annual report issued by U.S.-China 
     Economic and Security Review Commission, foreign adversaries, 
     like the Chinese state-backed cyber actor Volt Typhoon, have 
     infiltrated the power system of the United States to collect 
     operational insights into the electric grid, identify 
     chokepoints with outsized control over grid operations, and 
     pre-position cyber assets for operational disruptions.
       (6) Components of the power system produced by the People's 
     Republic of China, particularly internet-connected devices 
     and advanced wireless communications equipment, and 
     commercial satellite communications terminals create 
     vulnerabilities for the electric grid.
       (7) Grid distribution systems increasingly use operational 
     technology and industrial control systems that allow remote 
     access via public and private advanced wireless networks for 
     easier maintenance and more detailed data collection.
       (8) The fragmented nature of regulatory authority over the 
     electric grid of the United States poses challenges to 
     creating and implementing effective, uniform cybersecurity 
     regulations.
       (9) The Government Accountability Office has warned that 
     the electric grid of the United States is heavily reliant on 
     space-based infrastructure, particularly Global Navigation 
     Satellite Systems (GNSS), such as the Global Positioning 
     System (GPS), for the precise timing and synchronization of 
     power flows. The disruption, degradation, or spoofing of such 
     signals by foreign adversaries and the compromise of 
     commercial satellite communications used for remote grid 
     management, present critical vulnerabilities to the stability 
     of the electric grid.
       (10) A study issued by the Comptroller General of the 
     United States found that although the Department of Energy 
     has developed plans and an assessment to implement a Federal 
     strategy for addressing security risks to the electric grid, 
     such efforts have fallen short because the Federal Government 
     has not conducted a complete assessment of all the 
     cybersecurity risks to the grid.
       (b) Requirement.--Not later than 1 year after the date of 
     the enactment of this Act, the Director of National 
     Intelligence, acting through the National Intelligence 
     Council, shall produce and submit to the appropriate 
     committees of Congress a national intelligence estimate 
     addressing the risks and national security implications of 
     foreign adversaries targeting the electric grid of the United 
     States with cyber and physical attacks.
       (c) Elements.--The national intelligence estimate required 
     by subsection (b) shall include the following:
       (1) A comprehensive risk assessment of known cyber and 
     physical security threats and vulnerabilities to the electric 
     grid of the United States, including the risk posed by state-
     backed actors in the People's Republic of China, the Russian 
     Federation, the Islamic Republic of Iran, and the Democratic 
     People's Republic of Korea, and foreign non-state actors, 
     such as ransomware groups.
       (2) An assessment of how varying lengths of electricity 
     blackouts caused by cyber and physical attacks would impact--
       (A) national security, including with respect to the loss 
     of access to commercial internet and advanced wireless 
     telecommunications;
       (B) satellite communications infrastructure used in 
     security operations; and
       (C) the total economic activity, health, and other critical 
     activities of the United States.
       (3) An assessment of the efficacy of policies to strengthen 
     supply chain risk management requirements for the electric 
     grid, including--
       (A) identifying foreign-origin components within high- and 
     medium-impact bulk electric system and protected cyber 
     assets, including semiconductors, advanced wireless network 
     equipment, satellite receiver systems, precision timing 
     hardware, and other technology components; and
       (B) developing requirements to prohibit the installation of 
     or mitigate the foreign cyber risk posed by such components.
       (4) An assessment of the efficacy of policies to support 
     the adoption of nationwide cyber and physical security 
     standards and tools to protect the electric grid from foreign 
     state-backed cyber actors.
       (5) An assessment of the efficacy of policies to support 
     the expansion of the electric grid in a safe and secure 
     manner.
       (d) Submission to Congress.--
       (1) Form.--The national intelligence estimate shall be 
     submitted in classified form
       (2) Public availability.--Consistent with the protection of 
     intelligence sources and methods, at the same time as the 
     Director submits to the appropriate committees of Congress 
     the national intelligence estimate under subsection (b), the 
     Director shall make publicly available on an internet website 
     of the intelligence community an unclassified version of the 
     key findings of such national intelligence estimate.
       (e) Appropriate Committee of Congress Defined.--In this 
     section, the term ``appropriate committee of Congress'' 
     means--
       (1) the Select Committee on Intelligence, the Committee on 
     Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs, the Committee on 
     Armed Services, and the Committee on Energy and Natural 
     Resources of the Senate; and
       (2) the Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence, the 
     Committee on Homeland Security, the Committee on Armed 
     Services, and the Committee on Energy and Commerce of the 
     House of Representatives.
```
