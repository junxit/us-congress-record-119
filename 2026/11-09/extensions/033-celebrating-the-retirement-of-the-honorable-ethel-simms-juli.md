# CELEBRATING THE RETIREMENT OF THE HONORABLE ETHEL SIMMS JULIEN

**Speaking:** Carter, Troy A. (C001125) [D-LA]

```
     CELEBRATING THE RETIREMENT OF THE HONORABLE ETHEL SIMMS JULIEN

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. TROY A. CARTER

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, November 9, 2026

  Mr. CARTER of Louisiana. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor The 
Honorable Ethel S. Julien as we celebrate her thirty-year tenure on the 
bench. Judge Julien is dear to the citizens of New Orleans and 
Louisiana's 2nd Congressional District.
  Judge Julien was born to Mrs. Lucille Simms and Mr. Alfred Simms. Her 
mother, an educator, instilled in Judge Julien and her brothers. 
Seymour and Stewart, a profound reverence for God and an insatiable 
thirst for knowledge. She pursued her education at Valena C. Jones 
Elementary School, Blessed Sacrament School, and Xavier Prep High 
School, ultimately graduating from Southern University Law Center. 
Through divine providence and unwavering dedication, she achieved the 
distinction of being the first of her class to graduate magna cum 
laude. Throughout her illustrious legal career, Judge Julien has been 
consistently blessed by divine favor, enabling her to become the 
longest-serving African American judge on the Civil, District Court.
  Judge Julien first worked as an associate at Jefferson, Bryan, Gray 
and Jupiter Attorneys and Counselors at Law from 1983 to 1988. She then 
founded the law firm at Julien and Julien in 1989, alongside her late 
husband, Henry Julien, Esq., where she worked as a partner until her 
judicial election in 1995. Following her tenure as judge ad hoc in the 
Orleans Parish Juvenile Court and judge pro tempore in Civil District 
Court. Division ``A,'' Judge Julien was elected in September 1995 as 
Judge of the Civil District Court. Division ``N,'' where she has held 
office continuously since. In 2000, the Louisiana Supreme Court 
appointed her ad hoc to the First Circuit Court of Appeal to assist in 
mitigating the court's docket.
  Judge Julien's tenure on the bench was both gratifying and arduous. 
In August 2005, while serving as Chief Judge of the Civil District 
Court, Judge Julien assumed the unprecedented responsibility of guiding 
the court through the aftermath of Hurricane Katrina. Despite personal 
property damage to her residence, she collaborated with displaced 
judges, court personnel, and the clerk of court to reopen court 
operations in Gonzales, Donaldsonville, and subsequently St. Tammany 
Parish. These endeavors afforded litigants the opportunity to have 
their cases adjudicated while the court was temporarily relocated. 
Accommodations were secured, and expedited process was implemented to 
expedite Katrina-related cases, an achievement made feasible solely 
through the collective efforts of the court. Notably, this court became 
the inaugural regional entity to reopen and reestablish jury trials. 
Witnessing prospective jurors lined up, eagerly anticipating a return 
to normalcy, was particularly poignant.
  Judge Julien has consistently demonstrated a commitment to community 
service. She spearheaded the establishment of scholarship programs at 
Austerlitz Street Baptist Church, the First District Missionary Baptist 
Association, and the Cornerstone Missionary Baptist General 
Association, thereby providing scholarships and book stipends to foster 
academic excellence and support students. Furthermore, she serves as a 
founding member and president of INSPIRE, RR, an organization dedicated 
to encouraging students to embark on their literary journeys through 
positive reinforcement and rewards.
  Judge Julien serves on the Board of the New Orleans Mission and has 
held the position of President of the A.P. Tureaud Inn of Court. She is 
also a member of numerous attorney and judicial associations. Her 
tenure on the bench has been a fulfilling experience, and she has been 
unanimously elected for six consecutive terms. Judge Julien envisions 
that this continued trust reflects her dedication to administering 
justice impartially and equitably. She acknowledges the confidence 
bestowed upon her by the bench, the legal community, and the citizens 
of New Orleans. She expresses profound gratitude that both attorneys 
and voters have recognized her commitment to this esteemed role, an 
honor that she holds with utmost reverence.
  On this day, we celebrate the outstanding life of service of Judge 
Ethel Simms Julien.
```
