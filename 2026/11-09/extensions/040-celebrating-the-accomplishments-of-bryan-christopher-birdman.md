# CELEBRATING THE ACCOMPLISHMENTS OF BRYAN CHRISTOPHER "BIRDMAN" WILLIAMS

**Speaking:** Carter, Troy A. (C001125) [D-LA]

```
   CELEBRATING THE ACCOMPLISHMENTS OF BRYAN CHRISTOPHER ``BIRDMAN'' 
                                WILLIAMS

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. TROY A. CARTER

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, November 9, 2026

  Mr. CARTER of Louisiana. Mr. Speaker, today, I rise to highlight the 
achievements of a Louisiana Great, Mr. Bryan Christopher Williams, best 
known as ``Birdman'' or ``Baby.'' I am truly honored by his 
contributions to Bounce Music. He has made a lasting impact on the 
music industry, as co-founder of Cash Money Records, a record 
executive, and artist, along with his brother Ronald ``Slim'' Williams.
  Born February 15, 1969, in my hometown of New Orleans, Louisiana to 
Johnnie Williams and Gladys Brooks, Williams did not sign the birth 
certificate, which led to Bryan initially taking his mother's surname. 
For nearly a month after his birth, he did not have a first name and 
was referred to as ``Baby,'' a nickname he has retained to this day. As 
a child, Bryan and his family lived atop a bar owned by his family. His 
mother was tragically murdered when he was two years old, and following 
her death, Bryan and his siblings were sent to live with a relative in 
Prince George, British Columbia, Canada. After spending two years in 
the foster care system, his father was granted legal custody of his 
children in the mid-1970s. Shortly thereafter, Bryan's surname was 
changed to Williams. He, along with his siblings, lived in the Magnolia 
Projects in Central City in the 3rd Ward of Uptown New Orleans. His 
father, Johnnie Williams, passed away in 1996.
  In the late 1980s, Bounce Music emerged on the scene. Bounce Music, 
like many other genres, is derived from hip-hop. This sound, like non-
other, captivated club goers throughout New Orleans. In 1992, Baby and 
Slim

[[Page E1176]]

formed Cash Money Records, and signed their first artist, a local 
rapper names ``Kilo G,'' who released their label's first project 
titled ``The Sleepwalker.'' The nightclubs in New Orleans became a hub 
for local talent. Baby and Slim would travel around the city looking 
for new talent to join the Cash Money Record Label. Bryan convinced his 
friend DJ Mannie Fresh to serve as the label's in-house producer.
  By the mid-1990s, Cash Money Records popularity greatly increased as 
an independent label in the South. In 1995, 12-year-old Dwayne Carter 
was discovered at a local block party by Cash Money artist Lil Slim. 
Shortly after, Dwayne was signed to the label, making him the youngest 
artist signed. Dwayne and 14-year-old rapper Christoper Dorsey formed 
the group ``The B.G.'z'' under the names Baby D and Lil Doogie. They 
eventually would rename themselves ``Lil Wayne and B.G.'' in 1997. 
Later that year, Baby and Slim recruited Turk and Juvenile, along with 
Lil Wayne and B.G. to form the Hot Boys. The Hot Boys elevated Cash 
Money Records to new heights and because of their success in 1998, the 
label signed a $30 million deal with Universal Records
  As their popularity grew, more people realized that the South indeed 
had something to say. Baby and Mannie Fresh joined forces in the studio 
to form ``Big Tymers.'' Big Tymers debuted in 1998 with the album How 
Ya Luv That?, I Got That Work, in 2000, and Hood Rich in 2002. ``Get 
Your Roll On'' and ``#1 Stunna'' are popular songs that appeared on I 
Got That Work. I Got That Work was certified platinum by the Recording 
Industry Association (RIAA) for selling over 1 million copies in the 
United States.
  I am happy to celebrate Bryan ``Baby/Birdman'' Williams for his 
amazing contributions to amplifying New Orleans Bounce Music 
Nationally, extending sincerest wishes that he continues to flourish in 
his future endeavors both in Louisiana and abroad.
```
