# HONORING THE VETERANS OF PUERTO RICO

**Speaking:** Hernandez, Pablo Jose (H001103) [D-PR]

```
                  HONORING THE VETERANS OF PUERTO RICO

                                 ______
                                 

                       HON. PABLO JOSE HERNANDEZ

                             of puerto rico

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, November 9, 2026

  Mr. HERNANDEZ. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize and honor the 
veterans of Puerto Rico, whose courage, sacrifice, and devotion to duty 
have strengthened the United States of America for well over a century. 
Puerto Ricans have served in the United States Armed Forces since 1899, 
when Congress authorized the creation of the Porto Rico Battalion of 
Volunteer Infantry, just months after the island became part of the 
United States. That unit

[[Page E1174]]

grew into the Porto Rico Regiment of Infantry and, in 1920, was 
redesignated as the 65th Infantry Regiment. When the Jones Act granted 
United States citizenship to the people of Puerto Rico in 1917, 
thousands of our young men answered the call of duty, stepping forward 
without hesitation to serve in World War I.
  Across every generation since then, the sons and daughters of Puerto 
Rico have stood shoulder to shoulder with their fellow Americans in 
defense of freedom. They served with distinction in World War II, the 
Korean War, the Vietnam War, the Persian Gulf War, and the conflicts in 
Iraq and Afghanistan, as well as in peacekeeping and humanitarian 
missions around the world. Among the most celebrated are the members of 
the 65th Infantry Regiment, the Borinqueneers, whose bravery in Korea 
earned them the Congressional Gold Medal in 2014. Puerto Rican service 
members have received the Medal of Honor and countless other 
decorations for valor, and Puerto Rican women have served with honor in 
every branch of our Armed Forces.
  Their contributions did not end when they took off the uniform. When 
they came home, they became teachers, doctors, public servants, 
business owners, and community leaders, bringing the discipline, 
leadership, and sense of duty they learned in service to every 
community on the island. They have raised families, mentored our young 
people, and been among the first to help their neighbors recover and 
rebuild after hurricanes and earthquakes. Like so many Puerto Ricans, I 
grew up hearing their stories, told with quiet pride by fathers, 
grandparents, and uncles who had worn the uniform. Those stories taught 
us what it means to serve something greater than ourselves.
  For more than 125 years, Puerto Rico's veterans have demonstrated 
extraordinary patriotism and selflessness. Their sacrifices have helped 
secure the liberties that Puerto Ricans and all Americans enjoy, and 
their legacy remains a source of pride for the people of Puerto Rico 
and for the entire country. As we approach Veterans Day, we have a 
solemn responsibility not only to thank them, but to ensure they 
receive the care, benefits, and recognition they have earned.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in recognizing the 
veterans of Puerto Rico and in expressing our deepest gratitude for 
their courage, their sacrifice, and their enduring contributions to our 
country and to society.
```
