# CELEBRATING THE LIFE OF CHIEF EARL WEAVER, JR.

**Speaking:** Carter, Troy A. (C001125) [D-LA]

```
             CELEBRATING THE LIFE OF CHIEF EARL WEAVER, JR.

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. TROY A. CARTER

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, November 9, 2026

  Mr. CARTER of Louisiana. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to celebrate the 
life of an outstanding gentleman and blessing to the City of New 
Orleans, retired Chief Earl Weaver, Jr., who was a native of New 
Orleans, born November 5, 1948. He was deeply committed to our beloved 
city and the Orleans Parish Sheriff's ffice. He led the initiative to 
preserve and restore New Orleans through his dedicated service for 49 
years. He was devoted to his family, the Sheriff's Office, his Church, 
and his dogs, all Rottweilers, especially Tiger. After living a life 
committed to serving others, Chief Weaver passed away on April 23, 
2024, at 75 years old.
  Chief Weaver began his journey in law enforcement as a recruit in 
December 1973 under former Sheriff Louis Hyde and retired as Chief of 
Security on May 1, 2022. A very popular officer, he served in various 
roles. While at the original Parish Prison Facility, he led the 
Criminal Sheriff's Athletic Division and was involved in the boxing 
program under Sheriff Charles Foti. He also served as head of the 
Reserve Division, overseeing all movies filmed at the jail facilities 
and appearing in the movie Concrete Cowboys under Chief Les Bonano.
  Other responsibilities included overseeing the Juvenile Division as 
Warden and in command of the Reserve Division. He ultimately served as 
Chief of Security and Chief of Courts and Community Services. His 
unwavering commitment to excellence within the Orleans Parish Sheriff's 
Office was evident in every aspect of his work. Whether he was ensuring 
the safety and security of the facilities, mentoring deputies, or 
upholding the highest standards of professionalism, Chief Weaver set a 
shining example for all who had the privilege of working alongside him, 
continuing to raise the bar of commitment as a Sheriff.
  His dedication to serving others extended beyond the walls of the 
prison property. He served under 7 Mayors (from Mayor Moon Landrieu to 
Mayor LaToya Cantrell) during the Mardi Gras Season at Gallier Hall, 
providing security for all paradegoers and guests. In 2022, Chief 
Weaver was awarded the Key to the City by Mayor LaToya Cantrell to 
commemorate his long service. Chief Weaver, a true leader and mentor, 
instilled in his colleagues the importance of never settling and always 
striving for improvement. In addition to those mentioned, he worked 
with Chief Nat Hall and Colonel Wilfred Washington. He and Maurice 
Deloach provided security for Rock and Roll and Blues Hall of Fame 
singer Bobby Blue Bland whenever he visited New Orleans. During and 
immediately after Hurricane Katrina, he was responsible for the 
Juvenile Division during and immediately after Hurricane Katrina and 
safely evacuated them with no loss of life or injuries.
  Chief Weaver's legacy will continue to live on through the spirit of 
this city, which he loved so dearly. It will live on through all the 
people, the long-lasting friendships he made and cultivated, and those 
he touched around the Nation. Most importantly, Chief Weaver's legacy 
will live on through his dear, loving family: children, Alexander, 
Earl, Ronald, Ella Weaver; his 21 grandchildren; and five great-
grandchildren. He was preceded in death by his loving wife, Dianne 
Batiste, who passed away in 2007.
  He wore his uniform proudly and dressed impeccably and immaculately, 
like a Marine. He was a fair man and a stickler for the rules, 
especially regarding tardiness. He believed in always arriving early 
and would say, ``When you are on time, you are late.'' He also stressed 
``Going Back to the Basics.'' He was ``Old School.'' Chief Weaver is an 
inspiration to all those who dedicate their lives to serving their 
community. I, too, hope to carry his legacy with me as I continue to 
serve the people of Louisiana.
```
