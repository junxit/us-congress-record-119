# NUCLEAR REFUEL (RECYCLING EFFICIENT FUELS UTILIZING EXPEDITED LICENSING) ACT

**Speaking:** Latta, Robert E. (L000566) [R-OH], Pallone, Frank (P000034) [D-NJ]

**Measures:** H.R. 3978

```
     NUCLEAR REFUEL (RECYCLING EFFICIENT FUELS UTILIZING EXPEDITED 
                             LICENSING) ACT

  Mr. LATTA. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and pass the bill 
(H.R. 3978) to amend the definition of ``production facility'' in the 
Atomic Energy Act of 1954 to exclude an equipment or device capable of 
reprocessing spent nuclear fuel in a manner that does not separate 
plutonium from other transuranic elements, and for other purposes, as 
amended.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 3978

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Nuclear REFUEL (Recycling 
     Efficient Fuels Utilizing Expedited Licensing) Act''.

     SEC. 2. EXCLUSION FROM DEFINITION OF PRODUCTION FACILITY.

       Section 11 v. of the Atomic Energy Act of 1954 (42 U.S.C. 
     2014(v)) is amended--
       (1) by inserting ``or reprocessing'' after ``uranium 
     enrichment''; and
       (2) by striking ``separating the isotopes of uranium or 
     enriching uranium in the isotope 235'' and inserting ``(A) 
     separating isotopes of uranium or enriching uranium in the 
     isotope 235; or (B) reprocessing spent nuclear fuel in a 
     manner that does not isolate plutonium''.

  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Pursuant to the rule, the gentleman from 
Ohio (Mr. Latta) and the gentleman from New Jersey (Mr. Pallone) each 
will control 20 minutes.
  The Chair recognizes the gentleman from Ohio.


                             General Leave

  Mr. LATTA. Mr. Speaker, I ask unanimous consent that all Members may 
have 5 legislative days to revise and extend their remarks on the 
legislation and to include extraneous material on H.R. 3978.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Is there objection to the request of the 
gentleman from Ohio?
  There was no objection.
  Mr. LATTA. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise to speak in support of H.R. 3978, the Nuclear 
REFUEL Act, which I introduced with the gentleman from California's 
50th Congressional District.
  A strong, robust, domestic nuclear fuel cycle is essential for 
maintaining growing nuclear capacity in this country. The deployment of 
advanced reactors and the operation of our existing fleet cannot be 
accomplished without access to an abundant secure fuel supply.
  To assist this important goal, H.R. 3978 advances a policy to 
maximize access to the additional energy available in spent nuclear 
fuel, which retains up to 90 percent of potential energy for future 
use.
  This legislation does this by clarifying that spent fuel reprocessing 
facilities would be licensed under the same one-step framework as other 
fuel cycle facilities rather than the longer two-step process more 
appropriate for nuclear reactors and facilities that produce special 
nuclear material for national security purposes.
  This bill was thoroughly vetted in the legislative process in the 
Energy and Commerce Committee and was reported, as amended, by 
unanimous bipartisan recorded vote.
  Allowing for a more efficient and predictable licensing pathway for 
reprocessing facilities supports the pursuit of this promising 
technology.
  H.R. 3978 maintains nonproliferation protection and all necessary 
requirements to assure adequate protection of the public. It also 
creates a more efficient path for extraction of certain important 
isotopes from spent fuel, which are critical to American space 
exploration and national security missions.
  If our Nation is going to maintain its lead on nuclear technologies, 
we must make maximum use of our nuclear fuel resources and provide an 
efficient path to license promising reprocessing technologies.
  I urge all my colleagues to support H.R. 3978, the Nuclear REFUEL 
Act. Mr. Speaker, I reserve the balance of my time.
  Mr. PALLONE. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may consume.
  Mr. Speaker, H.R. 3978, the Nuclear REFUEL Act, will potentially help 
us get more energy out of the spent nuclear fuel piled up at reactor 
sites across the Nation.
  The bill makes a simple tweak to the Atomic Energy Act, ensuring that 
future proposals to develop facilities to

[[Page H6097]]

recycle or reprocess spent nuclear fuel are licensed similarly to other 
fuel cycle facilities.
  This is not a shortcut for reprocessing facilities. It just puts them 
on a level playing field with typical uranium enrichment facilities, 
mirroring an amendment Congress made to the Atomic Energy Act back in 
1990. These facilities would still go through the Nuclear Regulatory 
Commission's licensing process and would be subject to NRC scrutiny.
  I also want to note two important features of this bill. The first is 
that the changes here do not apply to any facility that would isolate 
plutonium nor do they apply to export controls on reprocessing 
technologies.
  These important safeguards ensure that our current nonproliferation 
regime is kept safe and intact. I thank Representative Peters and 
Energy Subcommittee Chairman Latta for their bipartisan work on this 
bill, and I urge my colleagues to support it.
  I have no additional speakers. This is an important bill, and I would 
urge support on a bipartisan basis. I yield back the balance of my 
time, Mr. Speaker.
  Mr. LATTA. Mr. Speaker, I also would request the Members to pass H.R. 
3978, as amended, as this is very important for our nuclear technology 
that we are advancing in this country. I yield back the balance of my 
time.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore. The question is on the motion offered by the 
gentleman from Ohio (Mr. Latta) that the House suspend the rules and 
pass the bill, H.R. 3978, as amended.
  The question was taken.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore. In the opinion of the Chair, two-thirds 
being in the affirmative, the ayes have it.
  Mr. LATTA. Mr. Speaker, on that I demand the yeas and nays.
  The yeas and nays were ordered.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Pursuant to clause 8 of rule XX, further 
proceedings on this motion will be postponed.
```
