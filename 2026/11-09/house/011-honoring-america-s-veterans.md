# HONORING AMERICA'S VETERANS

**Speaking:** Baird, James R. (B001307) [R-IN]

```
                      HONORING AMERICA'S VETERANS

  (Mr. Baird of Indiana was recognized to address the House for 5 
minutes.)
  Mr. BAIRD. Mr. Speaker, as my colleague General Bergman mentioned, it 
was a pleasure to be in the parade yesterday and to have a parade 
marshal who was a former Vietnam helicopter pilot, as was General 
Bergman.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor our veterans, especially those who 
served in Vietnam.
  Yesterday, I rode down Constitution Avenue in the National Veterans 
Parade aboard a replica of the Vietnam-era gun truck, Brutus.
  Roger Blink, a driver of the original Brutus, built the replica that 
participated in yesterday's parade. This work preserves the stories of 
the men who defended our convoys and honors those who never returned.
  For me, riding in the parade carried a deep personal meaning. In 
Vietnam, I served with the 523rd Transportation Company. Our convoys 
carried vital supplies along roads prone to ambush. Soldiers 
transformed ordinary Army trucks into gun trucks, adding machine guns, 
scrounged-up armor, and layers of sandbags, and we made do with what we 
had.
  The men operating those trucks saved countless lives, including my 
own.
  I was aboard the Proud American, formerly the Ace of Spades, that was 
made by Sammy Seay. It was driven by Robert Thorne when two rockets 
struck in the rear of the truck. Robert steered us into a mountain 
rather than over the embankment.
  In saving others' lives, Robert lost his own. Other gun truck crews 
who had already reached safety turned around, risking their lives to 
come back to save us. Those trucks were Satan's Lil' Angel, NCOIC 
Chester Israel, and King Kong, NCOIC Danny Cochran. You never forget 
courage like that.
  Mr. Speaker, as we mark Veterans Day during America's 250th 
anniversary, I ask Americans to remember all of our veterans who have 
given so much for this country. Those who I served alongside and all of 
the veterans deserve sincere gratitude when they return home. They 
deserve it today and every day.
  Mr. Speaker, I thank every veteran and servicemember for defending 
our freedoms. Honoring our service remains one of the greatest 
privileges of my life.


            Honoring the Life and Legacy of Roger T. Coffin

  Mr. BAIRD. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life and legacy of 
Robert T. Coffin.
  Born in Marion County, Indiana, Roger devoted his life to helping 
others. As a young lifeguard, he saved numerous lives during the 1963 
Indianapolis Coliseum explosion.
  Saving people was something Roger would continue to do throughout his 
life. Following his parents' footsteps, Roger completed ROTC at Indiana 
University and volunteered for military service in Vietnam, where he 
managed the field hospital as an Army second lieutenant.
  After returning home, Roger married his wife, Patricia, and graduated 
from IU Law School. He served as Morgan County deputy prosecutor, city 
attorney, and later as commander of the American Legion Post 230.
  Roger's greatest pride throughout his life was his family--his wife, 
two daughters, and a grandson--who he loved with unwavering devotion.
  Mr. Speaker, I thank Roger for a life honorably lived.


                        Recognizing Rhonda Beck

  Mr. BAIRD. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Rhonda Beck of 
Hendricks County for her dedication to honoring America's veterans and 
preserving their stories.
  Through her leadership in the Daughters of the American Revolution 
and the Indiana POW/MIA Council, Rhonda has ensured that those who 
served our country are remembered honorably.
  Rhonda established a military banner program with more than 2,000 
banners seen across 15 Indiana communities, featuring pictures of local 
veterans. She also led efforts to honor Indiana's missing 
servicemembers with memorial bricks on Monument Circle in Indianapolis.
  These are but a few of Rhonda Beck's accomplishments in serving 
Hoosier veterans. From organizing veteran breakfasts to displaying 
photographs of Hendricks County's fallen heroes, Rhonda brings 
neighbors together in gratitude and remembrance.
  Mr. Speaker, on behalf of myself, Indiana's Fourth Congressional 
District, and all of the veterans she served, I

[[Page H6086]]

thank Rhonda for keeping the faith with our veterans and our families.
```
