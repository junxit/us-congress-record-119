             DEPARTMENT OF ENERGY NUCLEAR TRANSPARENCY ACT

  Mr. LATTA. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and pass the bill

  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 9084

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Department of Energy Nuclear 
     Transparency Act''.

     SEC. 2. PUBLIC ANNOUNCEMENT REQUIREMENTS FOR DEPARTMENT OF 
                   ENERGY ACTIONS RELATING TO CERTAIN NUCLEAR 
                   FACILITIES.

       (a) Alterations to Directives and Safety Standards.--
       (1) Public announcement.--Not later than 72 hours after 
     altering a directive or safety standard relating to covered 
     DOE nuclear facilities, the Secretary of Energy shall post, 
     on a publicly accessible website of the Department of Energy, 
     an announcement regarding such alteration.
       (2) Inclusion.--Each announcement under paragraph (1) shall 
     include a summary of how the relevant directive or safety 
     standard was altered.
       (b) Documented Safety Analysis.--
       (1) Public announcement.--Not later than 72 hours after 
     issuing any preliminary documented safety analysis or 
     documented safety analysis with respect to a covered DOE 
     nuclear facility, the Secretary of Energy shall post, on a 
     publicly accessible website of the Department of Energy, an 
     announcement regarding the issuance of such preliminary 
     documented safety analysis or documented safety analysis.
       (2) Inclusion.--
       (A) Analysis.--Subject to subparagraph (B), each 
     announcement under paragraph (1) shall include the relevant 
     preliminary documented safety analysis or documented safety 
     analysis.
       (B) Commercially sensitive or controlled information.--The 
     Secretary of Energy may modify a preliminary documented 
     safety analysis or documented safety analysis included in an 
     announcement under paragraph (1) only to ensure that such 
     preliminary documented safety analysis or documented safety 
     analysis does not include any commercially sensitive 
     information, classified information, unclassified controlled 
     nuclear information, or controlled unclassified information.
       (c) Authorizations.--Not later than 72 hours after entering 
     into an agreement to authorize a covered DOE nuclear 
     facility, the Secretary of Energy shall post, on a publicly 
     accessible website of the Department of Energy, an 
     announcement regarding such agreement, including information 
     regarding any conditions applying to such agreement.
       (d) Report.--Not later than January 31 of each year, the 
     Secretary of Energy shall submit to the Committee on Energy 
     and Commerce of the House of Representatives and the 
     Committee on Energy and Natural Resources of the Senate a 
     report detailing any activities the Secretary of Energy took 
     to authorize covered DOE nuclear facilities during the 
     previous calendar year.
       (e) Definitions.--In this section:
       (1) Covered doe nuclear facility.--The term ``covered DOE 
     nuclear facility'' means any of the Hazard Category 1, 2, and 
     3 DOE nuclear facilities (as such term is defined in section 
     830.3(a) of title 10, Code of Federal Regulations (as in 
     effect on the date of enactment of this section)) that the 
     Secretary of Energy holds authority over pursuant to section 
     110 a. of the Atomic Energy Act of 1954 (42 U.S.C. 2140(a)).
       (2) Documented safety analysis; preliminary documented 
     safety analysis.--The terms ``documented safety analysis'' 
     and ``preliminary documented safety analysis'' have the 
     meanings given such terms, respectively, in section 830.3(a) 
     of title 10, Code of Federal Regulations (as in effect on the 
     date of enactment of this section).

  Mr. LATTA. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise in support of H.R. 9084, the Department of Energy 
Nuclear Transparency Act, as sponsored by the gentlewoman from 
Florida's 14th Congressional District and the gentlewoman from Iowa's 
First Congressional District.
  The legislation is a product of bipartisan deliberations by the 
Energy and Commerce Committee that resulted in a unanimous recorded 
vote.
  H.R. 9084 directs the Department of Energy, DOE, to publicly disclose 
actions and decisions involving authorization of nuclear facilities 
regulated by the Department. These actions would include changes to 
safety directives, standards, analyses, and any license or 
authorization to construct and operate a nuclear reactor under DOE 
jurisdiction.
  Transparency is important for public trust and nuclear safety 
regulation. The NRC currently exercises a high degree of transparency 
in its regulatory work, safety regulations, and license decisions, 
which are consistent with its statutory obligations.
  As DOE increases its regulatory activity over certain nuclear 
reactors and facilities, we should expect similar levels of public 
information, which is essential to public support and confidence.
  While the Department has been taking steps to show its work, H.R. 
9084 represents practical requirements to guide the Department as it 
advances its nuclear program.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support this legislation, and I 
reserve the balance of my time.
  Mr. PALLONE. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may consume.
  Mr. Speaker, the vast majority of nuclear reactors built in the 
United States are licensed by the Nuclear Regulatory Commission, and 
that has been the case for over 50 years, ever since Congress decided 
that it was vital for transparency and public confidence in the nuclear 
industry that we separate the government agency responsible for 
regulating the safety of our Nation's nuclear fleet, the NRC, from the 
part of the government responsible for promoting nuclear energy, and 
that is the Department of Energy.
  Both of these agencies have a vital role to play. The Department of 
Energy is making investments in the reactor designs of the future and 
helping the fundamental basic research that will be vital for our 
Nation to stay competitive in the coming decades. Meanwhile, the NRC is 
responsible for ensuring that our Nation's operating reactors are safe 
and secure.
  As a part of DOE's mission to promote nuclear energy, Congress 
granted it a very limited authority to permit its own nuclear reactors. 
These reactors cannot be commercial reactors that sell electricity. 
They are typically novel reactor designs that are thousands of times 
smaller than the NRC's regulated reactors that power our communities 
and our homes.
  Unfortunately, while NRC has taken a very robust stance toward 
transparency around the licenses it issues and its rules, DOE has 
doubled down on secrecy. It changed its nuclear safety rules without 
alerting Congress, and it took far too long to be transparent about the 
reactor pilot program, which kicked off last year.
  The only way to build public confidence and trust that these DOE-
authorized reactors are safe is through transparency, making 
authorizations, safety documents, and alterations to safety rules 
public.
  This bill, H.R. 9084, the Department of Energy Nuclear Transparency 
Act, brings a reasonable solution to the table. It would require DOE to 
make public announcements every time it changes its nuclear safety 
rules, issues a safety analysis for a proposed nuclear facility, or 
grants a proposed nuclear facility a formal authorization. It would 
also require the DOE to develop a public report detailing all of its 
authorization activities each year.
  These are vital transparency guardrails that can help ensure the 
success

of DOE's nuclear programs, including its new Launch Pad initiative. 
However, if the Department and the national laboratories running the 
program are not fully dedicated to transparency, then their efforts are

thank our Energy Subcommittee ranking member, Ms. Castor, for her
