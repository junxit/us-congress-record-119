# VETERANS' COMPENSATION COST-OF-LIVING ADJUSTMENT ACT OF 2026

**Speaking:** Hamadeh, Abraham J. (H001098) [R-AZ], McGarvey, Morgan (M001220) [D-KY], Luttrell, Morgan (L000603) [R-TX], Conaway, Herbert C. (C001136) [D-NJ]

**Measures:** H.R. 8552

```
                              {time}  1630
      VETERANS' COMPENSATION COST-OF-LIVING ADJUSTMENT ACT OF 2026

  Mr. HAMADEH of Arizona. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and 
pass the bill (H.R. 8552) to increase, effective as of December 1, 
2026, the rates of compensation for veterans with service-connected 
disabilities and the rates of dependency and indemnity compensation for 
the survivors of certain disabled veterans, and for other purposes.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 8552

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Veterans' Compensation Cost-
     of-Living Adjustment Act of 2026''.

     SEC. 2. INCREASE IN RATES OF DISABILITY COMPENSATION AND 
                   DEPENDENCY AND INDEMNITY COMPENSATION.

       (a) Rate Adjustment.--Effective on December 1, 2026, the 
     Secretary of Veterans Affairs shall increase, in accordance 
     with subsection (c), the dollar amounts in effect on November 
     30, 2026, for the payment of disability compensation and 
     dependency and indemnity compensation under the provisions 
     specified in subsection (b).
       (b) Amounts To Be Increased.--The dollar amounts to be 
     increased pursuant to subsection (a) are the following:

[[Page H6106]]

       (1) Wartime disability compensation.--Each of the dollar 
     amounts under section 1114 of title 38, United States Code.
       (2) Additional compensation for dependents.--Each of the 
     dollar amounts under section 1115(1) of such title.
       (3) Clothing allowance.--The dollar amount under section 
     1162 of such title.
       (4) Dependency and indemnity compensation to surviving 
     spouse.--Each of the dollar amounts under subsections (a) 
     through (d) of section 1311 of such title.
       (5) Dependency and indemnity compensation to children.--
     Each of the dollar amounts under sections 1313(a) and 1314 of 
     such title.
       (c) Determination of Increase.--Each dollar amount 
     described in subsection (b) shall be increased by the same 
     percentage as the percentage by which benefit amounts payable 
     under title II of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 401 et 
     seq.) are increased effective December 1, 2026, as a result 
     of a determination under section 215(i) of such Act (42 
     U.S.C. 415(i)).
       (d) Special Rule.--The Secretary of Veterans Affairs may 
     adjust administratively, consistent with the increases made 
     under subsection (a), the rates of disability compensation 
     payable to persons under section 10 of Public Law 85-857 (72 
     Stat. 1263) who have not received compensation under chapter 
     11 of title 38, United States Code.

     SEC. 3. PUBLICATION OF ADJUSTED RATES.

       The Secretary of Veterans Affairs shall publish in the 
     Federal Register the amounts specified in section 2(b), as 
     increased under that section, not later than the date on 
     which the matters specified in section 215(i)(2)(D) of the 
     Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 415(i)(2)(D)) are required to 
     be published by reason of a determination made under section 
     215(i) of such Act during fiscal year 2027.

  Mr. HAMADEH of Arizona. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I 
may consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise today in support of H.R. 8552.
  Each year, Congress passes the Veterans' Compensation Cost-of-Living 
Adjustment Act. This legislation ensures veterans VA benefits keep pace 
with inflation. Millions of veterans and their loved ones depend on 
these benefits to pay for daily living expenses.
  This bill would help them meet that need by increasing certain VA 
compensation at the same rate as the annual Social Security cost-of-
living adjustment.
  I thank my colleagues, especially Representatives Luttrell and 
McGarvey, for their bipartisan leadership and hard work on this 
important legislation to ensure our veterans receive the benefits they 
have earned and deserve.
  I also thank my colleague and friend, Congressman Luttrell, for his 
years of dedicated service to our great country, both in the United 
States Navy and here in Congress, as he prepares to retire from the 
House at the end of this year. I have had the pleasure of serving with 
him on both the Veterans' Affairs Committee and the Armed Services 
Committee, and he is a class act.
  As the Disability Assistance and Memorial Affairs Subcommittee 
chairman for the past 4 years, my colleague, Representative Morgan 
Luttrell, has done important work ensuring our veterans, their 
families, and their survivors are cared for on behalf of a grateful 
nation.
  It has been a true pleasure and honor to serve alongside Morgan on 
the committee. I am grateful for his friendship, leadership, and 
unwavering dedication to those who have served our country.
  I have no doubt that wherever Representative Luttrell's next mission 
takes him, he will continue to do good work for the country and his 
proud home State of Texas. Veterans and their families are better off 
today because of his bipartisan work.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge all my colleagues to support H.R. 8552, and I 
reserve the balance of my time.
  Mr. McGARVEY. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise today in strong support of H.R. 8552, the 
Veterans' Compensation Cost-of-Living Adjustment Act of 2026. I am 
proud to be the Democratic lead on this bill along with the gentleman 
from Texas (Mr. Luttrell), my good friend from the great State of 
Texas. This is a commonsense bill. It is a commonsense bill that will 
ensure veterans' compensation keeps pace with the rising costs of 
everyday needs.
  Mr. Speaker, we find ourselves today here less than 1 week from an 
election in which people from all across this country, literally from 
Maine to Alaska, from Texas to Montana, told us that things cost too 
much. The rising cost of the things we need and use every day, from gas 
to groceries, are just taking too much of the budget here at home. They 
are not leaving enough left over for the things we need. The gap 
between the ultrawealthy and the working class just continues to grow 
wider and wider.
  However, we can do a little something about that here today, and I am 
pleased that we are able to address the affordability crisis at least 
in part for our Nation's veterans.
  I don't think enough people know that our veterans don't get an 
automatic cost-of-living adjustment for their pay like somebody on 
Social Security. Congress must proactively pass this legislation each 
year to make it happen, and it is important to note we have never 
failed to do so. We have done it every year on a bipartisan, bicameral 
basis.
  This year, we are cutting it awfully close to the deadline to get 
this passed through the House, through the Senate, and on to the 
President for his signature. I hope for the sake of our veterans there 
are no further delays. This annual cost-of-living adjustment is the 
very least we can do to deliver relief for our veterans on a meaningful 
basis.
  I am not naive to think that there is not more work that needs to be 
done. We must do more to lift our veterans out of poverty, off the 
streets, and into a VA system where they can receive the care and the 
benefits not just that they deserve, but they have earned.
  Our veterans put on the uniform and put that flag on their shoulder. 
They are willing to serve and sacrifice everything for our freedom. In 
exchange for that, we make them a promise--it is both a legal and a 
moral promise--that we will be there for them after the uniform comes 
off.
  They held up their end of the deal. Today, we must hold up ours. Our 
veterans deserve it. Americans demand it, and we are the fortunate few 
who have the honor to pass the legislation that ensures it.

  Many disabled veterans rely on their benefits to help keep their

  Reducing the financial burden too many veterans, survivors, and their 
families face helps alleviate some of the stress and anxiety within the 
veteran community, and passing this cost-of-living adjustment provides 
veterans with the peace of mind they deserve and access to the benefits 
they are owed.
  With Veterans Day this Wednesday, I urge all my colleagues to support 
this bill honoring our commitment to serving those who served us.
  I thank my colleagues for the compliments and kind words. I have to 
tell Mr. McGarvey, if nobody was paying attention, he and I absolutely 
proved that if you sit down and take the time to learn each other, you 
can absolutely work hand in hand to get things accomplished in this

  Mr. CONAWAY. Mr. Speaker, I rise today in strong support of the
```
