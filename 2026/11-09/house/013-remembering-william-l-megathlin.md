# REMEMBERING WILLIAM L. MEGATHLIN

**Speaking:** Carter, Earl L. "Buddy" (C001103) [R-GA]

```
                    REMEMBERING WILLIAM L. MEGATHLIN

  (Mr. Carter of Georgia was recognized to address the House for 5 
minutes.)
  Mr. CARTER of Georgia. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life 
and memory of Bill Megathlin, a respected educator and college 
administrator, who passed away on September 7, 2026, at the age of 83.
  Bill dedicated his life to education, public service, and 
strengthening the communities he called home.
  During his distinguished career at Armstrong State College in 
Savannah, Bill served as a professor, a department chairman, and dean, 
helping guide the institution through a period of tremendous growth. 
His leadership was instrumental in expanding academic opportunities, 
strengthening enrollment, and securing support for new campus 
facilities.
  Beyond the classroom, Bill was a dedicated advocate for the 
preservation of Ossabaw Island and spent 40 years teaching Sunday 
school at Isle of Hope Methodist Church. Above all, Bill was a devoted 
husband to his wife of 58 years, Carol, and a loving father to their 
son, William Jr.
  My prayers are with Carol, William Jr., and the entire Megathlin 
family as they mourn the passing and celebrate a life dedicated to 
serving others. May Bill's legacy live on through the countless 
students, colleagues, and communities whose lives he touched.


                     Recognizing Downtown Brunswick

  Mr. CARTER of Georgia. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize 
downtown Brunswick, Georgia, on being named the Downtown of the Year by 
the Georgia Downtown Association.
  This grand achievement was presented at the GDA annual conference in 
Rome. The award celebrates communities that demonstrate outstanding 
achievement in planning, historic preservation, economic development, 
placemaking, organization, and community engagement.
  Over the past two decades, downtown Brunswick has transformed into 
one of Georgia's premier downtown destinations by restoring historic 
buildings, opening thriving businesses, and welcoming more than 1.3 
million visitors annually.
  Brunswick exemplifies the very best of downtown revitalization, 
having created a downtown atmosphere that is economically resilient, 
culturally vibrant, and has been positioned for future success. Its 
achievements serve as an inspiration for communities across Georgia.
  It is a great honor to represent the city of Brunswick, Georgia, and 
I could not be more proud of the economic growth we have had in this 
part of the district.


                    Recognizing Leopold's Ice Cream

  Mr. CARTER of Georgia. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize 
Leopold's Ice Cream and congratulate them on their 107th birthday.
  Leopold's Ice Cream was founded in Savannah, Georgia, in 1919 by 
three Greek immigrant brothers.
  The famous local ice cream spot threw quite the birthday party, 
consisting of $2 ice cream scoops, live music, face painting, and a 
special presentation of a restored World War I 48-star American flag 
belonging to the owner Stratton Leopold's family.
  Whether it is a hot August day or a cold February evening, you can 
always expect Leopold's to have the longest line in town. The 
incredible ice cream is not only favorites to its residents but a must-
have to the millions of tourists who visit the city every year.
  As part of a longstanding tradition, children 12 and under who recite 
the Pledge of Allegiance receive a free child's scoop of ice cream.

[[Page H6087]]

  We thank Leopold's for always having the best ice cream around, 
especially on the hot summer days when we need it the most.


                      Recognizing Tricia Pridemore

  Mr. CARTER of Georgia. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Tricia 
Pridemore, who resigned on September 4 from the Public Service 
Commission.
  Pridemore was appointed to the commission in February 2018 to fulfill 
the remainder of Commissioner Stan Wise's term. She then won an 
election later that year to serve a 6-year term.
  Tricia opted not to run for reelection, as she launched a run for 
U.S. Congress out of the 11th District. On the commission, she 
represented District 5, which covers a large portion of west Georgia, 
from the Tennessee border down to LaGrange and Thomaston.
  Pridemore has expressed how much of an honor it has been to serve 
Georgia in her position. I thank Tricia for her service to the great 
State of Georgia, and we wish her the best of luck in her future 
endeavors.


                         Honoring Hinson Mosley

  Mr. CARTER of Georgia. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life of 
Hinson Mosley, who passed away on September 1, 2026.
  The Jeff Davis native was the youngest of five children born to Nan 
Lou Mosley and Robert Mosley. Mr. Mosley was married to his wife, Linda 
Lawson Mosley, for 51 years and leaves behind a large family of 
siblings, children, and grandchildren that will continue to honor his 
legacy.
  In his early life, he worked 3 years with Atlantic Coast Railroad, 
and then he served 4 years in the United States Air Force during the 
Korean conflict. Upon returning from the military, he went to work with 
Rayonier, where he worked for 37 years and retired in 1994.
  Mr. Mosley was an active member of Odum Baptist Church and a devout 
member of his church.
  In 1992, he was elected to the Georgia House of Representatives and 
served for 14 years on several committees, but primarily the 
Appropriations Committee, where he chaired the Game, Fish, and Parks 
Committee and the Transportation Committee, where he was vice chairman. 
I had the honor and privilege of serving with Mr. Mosley. He was a fine 
representative.
  In referring to Mr. Mosley's time in State government, a friend once 
called him, truly, the people's representative.
```
