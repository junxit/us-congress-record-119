                EFFICIENT NUCLEAR LICENSING HEARINGS ACT

  Mr. LATTA. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and pass the bill 
(H.R. 5549) to amend the Atomic Energy Act of 1954 to provide for more 
efficient hearings on nuclear facility construction applications, and 
for other purposes.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 5549

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Efficient Nuclear Licensing 
     Hearings Act''.

     SEC. 2. UPDATING HEARING PROCEDURES.

       (a) Hearings and Judicial Review.--Section 189 a. of the 
     Atomic Energy Act of 1954 (42 U.S.C. 2239(a)) is amended--
       (1) in paragraph (1)(A)--
       (A) by striking ``In any proceeding under this Act'' and 
     inserting the following:
       ``(i) In any proceeding under this Act''; and
       (B) in clause (i) (as so designated), by striking ``The 
     Commission shall hold a hearing'' and all that follows 
     through ``upon a determination by the Commission that the 
     amendment involves no significant hazards consideration.'' 
     and inserting the following:
       ``(ii) The Commission may, in the absence of a request for 
     a hearing by any person whose interest may be affected, issue 
     a construction permit, an operating license, a combined 
     construction permit and operating license, an amendment to a 
     construction permit, an amendment to an operating license, or 
     an amendment to a combined construction permit and operating 
     license under section 103, 104 b., 104 c., or 185 b. for a 
     facility or a testing facility, without a hearing, but upon 
     thirty days notice and publication once in the Federal 
     Register of its intent to do so. The Commission may dispense 
     with such thirty days notice and publication with respect to 
     any application for an amendment to a construction permit, an 
     amendment to an operating license, or an amendment to a 
     combined construction permit and operating license upon a 
     determination by the Commission that the amendment involves 
     no significant hazards consideration.
       ``(iii) The Commission shall use informal adjudicatory 
     procedures for any hearing held by the Commission pursuant to 
     this subparagraph.'';
       (2) in paragraph (1)(B)(iv)--
       (A) by inserting ``informal'' before ``hearing 
     procedures''; and
       (B) by striking ``, whether informal or formal 
     adjudicatory,''; and
       (3) in the second sentence of paragraph (2)(A), by striking 
     ``required hearing'' and inserting ``hearing held by the 
     Commission under this section''.
       (b) Construction Permits and Operating Licenses.--Section 
     185 b. of the Atomic Energy Act of 1954 (42 U.S.C. 2235(b)) 
     is amended by striking ``After holding a public hearing under 
     section 189 a. (1)(A),'' and inserting ``After the thirty 
     days notice and publication period or holding a hearing, as 
     applicable, under section 189 a. (1)(A),''.
       (c) Licensing of Uranium Enrichment Facilities.--Section 
     193(b) of the Atomic Energy Act of 1954 (42 U.S.C. 2243(b)) 
     is amended by--
       (1) in paragraph (1)--
       (A) by striking ``on the record''; and
       (B) by inserting ``if a person whose interest may be 
     affected by such construction and operation has requested a 
     hearing regarding the licensing of the construction and 
     operation of the facility'' after ``and 63''; and
       (2) in paragraph (2), by striking ``Such hearing'' and 
     inserting ``If a hearing is held under paragraph (1), the 
     hearing''.
       (d) Applicability.--The amendments made by this section 
     shall apply to all applications and proceedings pending 
     before the Nuclear Regulatory Commission on or after the date 
     of enactment of this section.

  Mr. LATTA. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise in support of H.R. 5549, the Efficient Nuclear 
Licensing Hearings Act, sponsored by the gentleman from Virginia's 
Ninth Congressional District, the gentlewoman from Washington's Eighth 
Congressional District, and the gentleman from Texas' 33rd 
Congressional District.
  This bipartisan legislation advanced out of the Energy and Commerce 
Committee unanimously. Since Congress enacted the ADVANCE Act 2 years 
ago, the Nuclear Regulatory Commission, the NRC, has been hard at work 
to ensure an efficient, timely, and predictable licensing process.
  H.R. 5549 builds on this effort by repealing a 70-year-old provision 
of the Atomic Energy Act that is no longer necessary. This provision 
requires the NRC to hold a hearing on licensing applications, 
regardless of whether a person requests a hearing in the first place. 
This original requirement forces NRC and its staff to spend time and 
resources preparing for and conducting uncontested hearings on 
applications, basically hearings that nobody requests.
  The provision made sense when the nuclear industry was in its infancy 
and the licensing processes were not as transparent as today. Under 
current practice, there are numerous opportunities to participate in 
the licensing process and to request hearings.
  The NRC staff and commissioners have for over a decade called for 
elimination of this provision. By removing this requirement for an 
uncontested hearing, this bill significantly reduces the time and cost 
to deploy nuclear energy without impacting safety or public engagement.
  Importantly, the bill preserves the right of any person whose 
interest may be affected by a license proceeding to a hearing.
  This legislation, in combination with the suite of nuclear bills 
being considered today, will help enable the efficient and safe 
deployment of nuclear energy to benefit our society.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to join me in supporting H.R. 5549, 
and I reserve the balance of my time.
  Mr. PALLONE. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may consume.
  Mr. Speaker, H.R. 5549, the Efficient Nuclear Licensing Hearings Act, 
would cut down on red tape by eliminating the need for the Nuclear 
Regulatory Commission to hold a hearing on an application for a license 
when no one asked for one, and these so-called mandatory hearings often 
cost applicants up to $4 million, costs that are often eventually 
passed on to American families across the Nation in their electric 
bills.

[[Page H6098]]

  Mandatory hearings, Mr. Speaker, date back to an era when we had not 
separated out the safety regulation responsibilities that now belong to 
the NRC from the policy of promoting nuclear energy that now belongs to 
the Department of Energy, and the mandatory hearing, was a tool that 
Congress thought necessary to maintain public confidence and trust in 
nuclear safety regulations, especially in an era before the Sunshine 
Act and environmental reviews.
  Thankfully, we live in a very different and much more transparent 
world today, so these mandatory hearings are not hearings in the sense 
that you might think, Mr. Speaker. Instead, they are purely paper 
proceedings that take months to complete happening between NRC staff 
and reactor developers, not the public. No public scrutiny or 
transparency is lost by getting rid of them. Instead, the NRC and its 
staff can focus on the issues in an application that really matters.
  The NRC recognized this and endorsed the idea 18 years ago. 
Importantly, this bill does not really touch another type of hearing at 
the NRC, contested hearings, where someone demands that the NRC 
investigate a specific component of a license application, and 
communities retain their rights to demand hearings and community 
meetings as part of the environmental and safety review processes, and 
that is completely untouched.
  Mr. Speaker, I thank Representative Schrier, the coauthor of this 
bill and the lead Democrat, for her work on this legislation. I urge my 
colleagues to support it, and I reserve the balance of my time.

  Mr. GRIFFITH. Mr. Speaker, I thank my colleague, Chairman Latta, for 
yielding.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise today to speak in favor of this bill, H.R. 5549, 
the Efficient Nuclear Licensing Hearings Act.
  I am proud to see this bipartisan bill that I introduced along with 
Congresswoman Schrier and Congressman Veasey make its way to the House 
floor.
  H.R. 5549 would amend the Atomic Energy Act to repeal the requirement 
for a public nuclear reactor licensing hearing even when no one in the 
public or an affected party requests a hearing.
  Essentially, the Nuclear Regulatory Commission staff is required by 
law to contest the license in an adversarial, court-like hearing, even 
if no member of the public has any objections to the license. In most 
cases, these mandatory hearings for operating licenses only occur 
because they are required by law, not because they are essential 
evidence gathering exercises for the NRC. They really are hearings that 
no one asked for or wants.
  Uncontested hearings are rarely necessary these days. Mandatory 
hearings are relics of the 1950s when the nuclear licensing processes 
were created and every reactor design was novel or different. These 
hearings were intended to stress test each reactor design and have 
technical experts debate the merits of each project.
  When the mandatory hearing was adopted in 1957, there were no civil 
nuclear energy reactors operating in the United States. Today there are 
94.
  The original nuclear regulator, the Atomic Energy Commission, was 
abolished in 1974, yet the mandatory hearings remain.
  Uncontested mandatory hearings are an archaic section of the statute 
that was put in because of the newness of nuclear energy and because of 
the then-prevalent procedure-based regulatory structure that emphasized 
contested hearings, such as those used in electric rate cases.
  Today, the need for uncontested hearings is not evident and the 
hearings are simply a waste of resources. We actually heard testimony 
at Energy and Commerce that witnesses said that the nuclear license 
applicant pays a fee over $300 an hour for each NRC staff member to 
prepare for the hearing. One witness said that there could be up to 12 
NRC staff at each hearing. As you can see, Mr. Speaker, that could add 
up fairly quickly.
  According to one NRC study, on average, a mandatory hearing takes up 
to 1,000 hours, which is charged to the applicant, and, as my colleague 
said, could be $4 million pretty easily.
  Each perfunctory hearing costs a licensing applicant that money. 
Numerous current and former NRC commissioners nominated by Presidents 
of both political parties have all agreed that mandatory hearings are 
not necessary. The staff have also expressed support for repealing the 
requirement, saying it is unnecessary. Eliminating the uncontested 
hearing requirement would in no way affect public scoping or siting 
meetings.
  Over the years, the NRC statutorily authorized Advisory Committee on 
Reactor Safeguards has taken on more reactor safety review 
responsibilities.
  Additionally, NRC staff independently assess technical data and 
publish voluminous safety and environment data on its public database. 
Repealing these hearings would not affect the right of any affected 
party to request a hearing on specific matters.
  My bill includes language that specifically leaves open the 
possibility of the affected party requesting an uncontested hearing. 
The bill also codifies current NRC practices that use informal, 
nonadversarial hearing procedures.
  Finally, the bill requires the NRC to publish a notice of its intent 
to issue a construction or operating license in the Federal Register at 
least 30 days before the license takes effect.
  Over the past two Congresses, the Energy and Commerce Committee has 
received testimony showing that repealing this hearing requirement will 
not jeopardize public safety.
  Mr. Speaker, I thank Dr. Schrier and Congressman Veasey for leading 
this bill with me, I thank Chairman Latta for his leadership, and I 
urge all of my colleagues to vote in favor of the passage of this bill.
