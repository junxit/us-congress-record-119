# NRC STAFF PAY ALIGNMENT ACT

**Speaking:** Latta, Robert E. (L000566) [R-OH], Pallone, Frank (P000034) [D-NJ], Menendez, Robert (M001226) [D-NJ]

**Measures:** H.R. 9614

```
                      NRC STAFF PAY ALIGNMENT ACT

  Mr. LATTA. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and pass the bill 
(H.R. 9614) to amend the Atomic Energy Act of 1954 to provide 
compensation authority for certain career appointees, and for other 
purposes.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 9614

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``NRC Staff Pay Alignment 
     Act''.

     SEC. 2. COMPENSATION AUTHORITY FOR CAREER APPOINTEES.

       (a) In General.--Section 161B of the Atomic Energy Act of 
     1954 (42 U.S.C. 2201b) is amended--
       (1) by redesignating subsections (d) through (h) as 
     subsections (e) through (i), respectively;
       (2) by inserting after subsection (c) the following:
       ``(d) Compensation Authority for Career Appointees.--
       ``(1) In general.--Notwithstanding any other provision of 
     law, the Chairman may fix the compensation of a career 
     appointee at an annual rate of basic pay that is not greater 
     than 110 percent of the maximum annual rate of basic pay for 
     Senior Executive Service positions within the Commission 
     established under section 5382 of title 5, United States 
     Code.
       ``(2) Applicability.--The authority to fix the compensation 
     of a career appointee under paragraph (1) shall apply 
     regardless of when the career appointee was appointed.
       ``(3) Effect on certain certification.--The exercise of the 
     authority to fix the compensation of a career appointee under 
     paragraph (1) may not affect the certification of any 
     performance appraisal system of the Commission under section 
     5307(d) of title 5, United States Code.
       ``(4) Career appointee defined.--In this subsection, the 
     term `career appointee' has the meaning given such term in 
     section 3132(a) of title 5, United States Code.'';
       (3) in subsection (g) (as so redesignated), in paragraph 
     (2)(C)--
       (A) in clause (ii)--
       (i) in each of subclauses (I) and (III), by striking 
     ``subsection (g)(1)'' each place it appears and inserting 
     ``subsection (h)(1)''; and
       (ii) in each of subclauses (II) and (IV), by striking 
     ``subsection (g)(2)'' each place it appears and inserting 
     ``subsection (h)(2)'';
       (B) in clause (iv), by striking ``and'' at the end;
       (C) by redesignating clause (v) as clause (vi); and
       (D) by inserting after clause (iv) the following:
       ``(v) the total number of career appointees that have their 
     compensation fixed pursuant to subsection (d); and''; and
       (4) in subsection (i) (as so redesignated)--
       (A) in each of paragraphs (1) and (3), by striking 
     ``subsections (a) and (b)'' each place it appears and 
     inserting ``subsections (a), (b), and (d)'';
       (B) in paragraph (2), by striking ``or both subsections'' 
     and inserting ``subsection (d), or any combination of those 
     subsections''; and
       (C) in paragraph (3)--
       (i) by inserting ``, or a career appointee,'' after 
     ``compensation of an employee or other personnel serving in a 
     covered position''; and
       (ii) by striking ``subsection (a) or (b)'' and inserting 
     ``subsection (a), (b), or (d)''.
       (b) Conforming Amendment Relating to Government 
     Accountability Office Report.--Section 502(b) of the ADVANCE 
     Act of 2024 is amended by striking ``subsections (a), (b), 
     and (c)'' each place it appears and inserting ``subsections 
     (a), (b), (c), and (d)''.

  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Pursuant to the rule, the gentleman from 
Ohio (Mr. Latta) and the gentleman from New Jersey (Mr. Pallone) each 
will control 20 minutes.
  The Chair recognizes the gentleman from Ohio.


                             General Leave

  Mr. LATTA. Mr. Speaker, I ask unanimous consent that all Members may 
have 5 legislative days to revise and extend their remarks on the 
legislation and to insert extraneous materials on H.R. 9614.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Is there objection to the request of the 
gentleman from Ohio?
  There was no objection.
  Mr. LATTA. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise to speak in support of H.R. 9614, the NRC Staff 
Pay Alignment Act, sponsored by the gentleman from New Jersey's Eighth 
Congressional District.
  This legislation was developed through bipartisan committee work, 
examined in legislative hearings, and reported favorably by the House 
Committee on Energy and Commerce in a unanimous recorded vote.
  The level of expertise demanded by the positions at the Nuclear 
Regulatory Commission, NRC, requires highly qualified candidates, 
candidates which are also desirable for the private sector, creating 
retention pressures.

                              {time}  1620

  The special workforce and pay authority Congress established for the 
NRC in the bipartisan ADVANCE Act just over 2 years ago sought to help 
attract and preserve exceptional employees at the NRC.
  H.R. 9614 fixes an issue which was inadvertently created with those 
incentive pay provisions in the ADVANCE Act. Those provisions left out 
career Senior Executive Service, or SES, employees, who typically are 
managers. This created potential issues of pay alignment for 
exceptional managers that may undermine effective management and 
retention.
  It is important for quality personnel at the NRC to ensure efficient 
licensing and oversight, especially during this time of industry 
growth. This requires good management.
  H.R. 9614 authorizes the Chairman of the NRC to modestly increase the 
compensation rate of selected Senior Executive Service employees at the 
NRC. This authority to increase the rate of compensation by not more 
than 10 percent for certain employees will help the NRC maintain a 
strong, specialized workforce, as well as the quality management 
necessary to carry out the NRC's mission.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support the legislation, and I 
reserve the balance of my time.
  Mr. PALLONE. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may consume.
  Mr. Speaker, nuclear energy plays a vital role in delivering carbon-
free

[[Page H6105]]

power to our Nation's growing power grid. In an era of increasing 
demand for electricity, new nuclear reactors can provide clean energy 
around the clock, but only if they are safe.
  The guarantee of safety comes from the hardworking employees at the 
Nuclear Regulatory Commission, our Nation's nuclear safety regulator. 
They ensure that new proposed reactors will be safe, while also 
confirming that existing reactors are abiding by safety standards and 
not taking shortcuts. They are the safety cops on the beat, and they 
are extremely important.
  Unfortunately, the NRC's workforce has dwindled over the past few 
years. Attacks by DOGE and the rest of the Trump administration, 
combined with the reorganization of the Commission, have resulted in 
the NRC losing over 10 percent of its workforce, many from senior 
roles. That is on top of an agency workforce that was already 
shrinking, despite a growing need.
  The reality is if the Trump administration wants to accomplish its 
goal of moving nuclear power forward, it is going to need more safety 
regulators, not fewer. In a new wave of reactor or fuel cycle facility 
application, if that occurs in the future, the NRC will need more staff 
to analyze those applications and to ensure that those facilities stay 
safe once they are built.
  Mr. Speaker, 2 years ago, when we passed the ADVANCE Act into law, we 
recognized this problem. Then-Ranking Member DeGette of the Energy 
Subcommittee authored language that would give the NRC immense 
flexibility in its hiring and compensation authorities to attract and 
retain staff.
  Unfortunately, the NRC has been slow to implement the new authority. 
Furthermore, the attacks on the Commission over the past 2 years have 
disproportionately impacted the most senior civil servants who were 
excluded from the ADVANCE Act's reforms.
  Mr. Speaker, H.R. 9614 fixes this problem. It enhances the ADVANCE 
Act's hiring authorities by allowing the NRC to grant a 10-percent pay 
increase to Senior Executive Service employees. Those are the highest 
ranking, nonpolitical civil servants. These are the employees whose 
expertise and leadership will be the most important if the NRC sees an 
uptick in applications and facilities that it oversees.
  That fixes one-half of the problem, but the other issue is that the 
NRC has been slow to implement the ADVANCE Act's existing authorities 
due to some questions around congressional intent. We can't legislate 
that problem away, but I note for the record that it was never 
Congress' intention that the ADVANCE Act authorities could only be 
applied to a broad class of employee or to every single employee in a 
specific role. Those authorities and the authorities in this bill were 
designed to give the NRC maximum flexibility and not simply act as a 
blunt tool.
  Mr. Speaker, I thank Representative Menendez for his work in 
authoring this bill. I urge all of my colleagues to support it, and I 
reserve the balance of my time.
  Mr. LATTA. Mr. Speaker, I reserve the balance of my time.
  Mr. PALLONE. Mr. Speaker, I yield such time as he may consume to the 
gentleman from New Jersey (Mr. Menendez), the sponsor of the bill.
  Mr. MENENDEZ. Mr. Speaker, I thank my friend, Ranking Member Pallone, 
for yielding me time.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise in support of my bill, H.R. 9614, the NRC Staff 
Pay Alignment Act.
  This legislation would help the Nuclear Regulatory Commission recruit 
and retain experienced subject matter professionals by giving the 
agency greater flexibility to offer competitive pay. I especially thank 
Congresswoman DeGette, whose work on the ADVANCE Act laid the 
groundwork for this legislation.
  As our Nation's energy needs grow, nuclear power can deliver the 
reliable, clean electricity our communities need. In my home State of 
New Jersey, we are removing longstanding barriers to new nuclear 
development as part of our effort to expand energy supply and address 
rising utility bills.
  Mr. Speaker, we need to ensure there is a strong, independent nuclear 
regulator keeping our communities safe. The communities that host 
nuclear facilities deserve to know that decisions affecting their 
safety are guided by science, rigorous review, and independent 
judgment. That confidence depends on the people doing the work: the 
engineers, scientists, inspectors, and career leaders whose expertise 
protects the public every day.
  We cannot expect the NRC to take on greater responsibilities while 
losing the experienced professionals it needs to fulfill them. Yet, 
hundreds of employees have left the NRC since January 2025, raising 
serious concerns about its ability to sustain effective safety 
oversight.

  At the same time, reports of political interference have threatened 
public confidence in the Commission's independence. We have heard 
reports that a DOGE employee leading the charge at NRC said that the 
goal was to make the agency a ``rubberstamp'' for the Trump 
administration and dismiss safety concerns raised by NRC staff.
  We have seen career lawyers leave the Commission while working on 
consequential cases, and Commissioners have told Congress that they 
fear being fired for refusing to approve a reactor design favored by 
this administration.
  These concerns go directly to whether the NRC's experts can do their 
jobs and whether the public can trust that safety does, in fact, come 
first.
  The Commission must have the staff, resources, and independence to 
make decisions based on the evidence free from political pressure. My 
bill addresses one important part of that responsibility.
  Building on the ADVANCE Act, it would give the NRC additional pay 
authority for career Senior Executive Service employees, helping the 
Commission compete for experienced leaders and retain their expertise.
  Competitive compensation alone will not resolve every challenge 
facing the NRC, but it is a practical step Congress can take now to 
strengthen its workforce.
  If we want nuclear energy to help power our future, we must invest in 
the people responsible for keeping it safe.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support H.R. 9614.

  Mr. LATTA. Mr. Speaker, I urge support and a ``yea'' vote on H.R. 
9614. Again, this is another piece of legislation that advances our 
nuclear policy in the United States, and I yield back the balance of my 
time.
```
