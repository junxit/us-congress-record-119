# RECOGNIZING ALL VETERANS ON VETERANS DAY

**Speaking:** Courtney, Joe (C001069) [D-CT]

**Measures:** H.R. 2102

```
                RECOGNIZING ALL VETERANS ON VETERANS DAY

  (Mr. Courtney of Connecticut was recognized to address the House for 
5 minutes.)
  Mr. COURTNEY. Mr. Speaker, in a couple of days, our country will 
pause on one of the 12 national public holidays, Veterans Day, to 
recognize the sacrifice and service of veterans, both living and 
deceased, which is the mandate of Veterans Day.
  Veterans Day first appeared in this country on November 11, 1919, 
when Woodrow Wilson issued an executive order decreeing that Veterans 
Day would be celebrated on that day, and in 1938, Congress moved and 
actually made it a national holiday.
  Again, there are a lot of ways people can participate in Veterans 
Day. They can attend veterans ceremonies. They can attend veterans 
parades. They can express vocally their support and appreciation for 
veterans in their communities.
  But all of us have a duty to do more, particularly here in Congress, 
to provide support for veterans. As Abraham Lincoln in his second 
inaugural address said: We have a duty to care for the veteran that has 
borne the battle; in other words, provide real, tangible support, both 
in terms of healthcare and financial services.
  In a couple of days, Congress is going to have an opportunity to 
follow through on that mission that Abraham Lincoln announced so many 
years ago when Congress finally takes up the Richard Star Act for 
passage here in the House of Representatives. Again, this is a bill 
which will end one of the gravest injustices in the Veterans 
Administration's system of benefits where veterans, who have earned a 
pension and have earned a disability benefit because of a service-
connected injury, are no longer going to be subject to an offset which 
reduces those benefits which they earn.
  Again, H.R. 2102 will be up for consideration because of a discharge 
petition to finally end this injustice, which veteran service 
organizations of every stripe have come to Washington year in and year 
out to point out the blatant unfairness to people, who have worn the 
uniform of our country and actually suffered combat-related injuries, 
being denied the full scope of retirement benefits that they have 
earned as well as the disability benefits.
  Again, this is an issue that should unite both sides of the aisle 
when Congress takes it up pursuant to the discharge petition. Over 300 
Members have cosponsored this bill here in the House of 
Representatives. There was an attempt to sort of tweak it and mangle 
it, which failed earlier in this Congress, and on Wednesday it will be 
time to finally pass a clean Richard Star Act, which veterans all over 
this country are going to be watching closely to make sure that their 
Members have listened to them and moved forward in terms of making sure 
those veterans get their full support and benefits, as Abraham Lincoln 
said, having borne the battle.
  Again, I implore all of my colleagues on Wednesday when this measure 
comes up to vote ``yes,'' send it to the Senate, get it to the White 
House, and end again this unjust concurrent benefit offset.
```
