# HONORING CONSTANCE "CONNIE" YOUNG

**Speaking:** Mejia, Analilia (M001246) [D-NJ]

```
                              {time}  1215
                  HONORING CONSTANCE ``CONNIE'' YOUNG

  (Ms. Mejia of New Jersey was recognized to address the House for 5 
minutes.)
  Ms. MEJIA. Mr. Speaker, I rise this Veterans Day to honor Constance 
``Connie'' Young of West Caldwell.
  A proud World War II Navy veteran, Connie enlisted as a Woman 
Accepted for Volunteer Emergency Service, otherwise known as WAVES, in 
the United States Navy. She served our Nation well as both a captain's 
yeoman at Hunter College and on an antisubmarine missile project with 
the Navy's Bureau of Ordnance.
  Following her service, Connie built a life rooted in family, 
education, and community. She earned her degree in economics from Saint 
Elizabeth University, raised four children with her husband, Roger, and 
devoted decades to advancing educational opportunities for women and 
serving her community.
  At 105, Connie continues to inspire those around her. Her curiosity, 
creativity, and unwavering commitment defines a woman who is no 
stranger to sacrifice, service, and commitment to others. On Veterans 
Day, it is my privilege to honor her and the extraordinary example she 
sets.
  I wish Connie a happy 105th birthday and thank her for her service.


                     Recognizing Miriam Rosenstein

  Ms. MEJIA. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Miriam Rosenstein, a 
beloved member of the Maplewood community, as she celebrates her 102nd 
birthday on Veterans Day.
  Born on Armistice Day in 1924, Miriam spent more than a century 
demonstrating what it means to stand for peace, justice, and dignity 
for all people. For nearly 60 years, she has called Maplewood home, 
deeply committed to the community she loves.
  Miriam has never sat on the sidelines. Even at 101 years old, she 
walked up and down her neighborhood collecting signatures and 
encouraging her neighbors to make their voices heard. Her commitment to 
civic engagement and strengthening our democracy is an example for us 
all.
  Miriam's commitment to these values extends across generations. She 
continues to inspire her family, including her children, grandchildren, 
and great-grandchildren, to stand up for their communities and work 
toward a more just future.
  Miriam Rosenstein is a testament to a life lived with purpose and the 
power that ordinary people have to make a difference in this world. I 
wish Miriam a happy 102nd birthday.


              Recognizing National Family Caregivers Month

  Ms. MEJIA. Mr. Speaker, I rise for National Family Caregivers Month, 
honoring the millions of family members, friends, and neighbors who 
provide hours of unpaid care and labor.
  As a caregiver and a member of the sandwich generation myself, I know 
the challenges of balancing family, career, and caring for loved ones.
  The cost of caregiving goes far beyond time and energy. It is the 
time off from work, often unpaid, to ensure that loved ones attend 
doctors' appointments, apply for services, or simply make it through 
the day. It is in the countless hours struggling with health insurance 
companies that second guess doctors and question care when it is needed 
most, and it is in the emotional cost of worrying about what will 
happen to children, parents, and our other loved ones when we are gone.
  Caregivers deserve the same care that they offer our communities, and 
it is on those within this Chamber to make it so. While billionaires 
receive permanent tax breaks from Republicans' big, ugly bill, 
caregivers spend an average of over $11,000 a year to support a family 
member or friend.
  This silent struggle deserves a voice and a champion. I stand here 
more than willing to be exactly that.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to put all of their effort into 
expanding housing, healthcare, and respite services, as well as 
enacting a caregiver tax credit and expanding paid family leave. If we 
are lucky, we will all reach a stage in which this care truly matters 
personally.
```
