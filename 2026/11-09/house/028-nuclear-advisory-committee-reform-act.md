                 NUCLEAR ADVISORY COMMITTEE REFORM ACT

  Mr. LATTA. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and pass the bill 
(H.R. 9613) to update the role of the Advisory Committee on Reactor

  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 9613

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Nuclear Advisory Committee 
     Reform Act''.

     SEC. 2. ADVISORY COMMITTEE ON REACTOR SAFEGUARDS.

       (a) In General.--Section 29 of the Atomic Energy Act of 
     1954 (42 U.S.C. 2039) is amended--
       (1) by striking ``There is'' and inserting the following:
       ``(a) Establishment.--There is'';
       (2) by inserting ``(referred to in this section as the 
     `Committee').'' after ``Reactor Safeguards'';
       (3) by striking ``consisting of'' and all that follows; and
       (4) by adding at the end the following:
       ``(b) Membership.--The Committee shall consist of a maximum 
     of 15 members, to be appointed by the Commission, who shall 
     represent a diverse background of technical expertise 
     relevant to the mission of the Commission.
       ``(c) Terms.--
       ``(1) In general.--Except as provided in paragraph (2), the 
     Commission may appoint a member of the Committee for not more 
     than 2 terms of 4 years each.
       ``(2) Exception.--The Commission may appoint a member of 
     the Committee for more than 2 terms if the Commission 
     determines that there is a compelling need for the member's 
     continued service.
       ``(3) Applicability.--This subsection shall apply with 
     respect to members appointed after the date of enactment of 
     the Nuclear Advisory Committee Reform Act.
       ``(d) Committee Actions.--The Committee shall--
       ``(1) review safety studies and facility license 
     applications referred to it and make reports thereon;
       ``(2) advise the Commission with regard to--
       ``(A) the hazards of proposed or existing reactor 
     facilities; and
       ``(B) the adequacy of proposed reactor safety standards; 
     and
       ``(3) perform such other duties as the Commission may 
     request.
       ``(e) Focus of Committee.--In taking an action pursuant to 
     paragraph (1), (2), or (3) of subsection (d), the Committee 
     shall, to the extent practicable, focus on issues that--
       ``(1) directly relate to facility design;
       ``(2) are safety significant;
       ``(3) are unique, novel, or noteworthy; and
       ``(4) have not been previously acted on by the Committee.
       ``(f) Policies for Efficient and Timely Action.--The 
     Commission shall maintain and, if necessary, modify policies 
     to ensure efficient and timely action by the Committee, 
     including efficient and timely coordination between the 
     Committee and staff of the Commission.
       ``(g) Chairperson.--The Commission shall designate 1 member 
     of the Committee as the Chairperson of the Committee.
       ``(h) Per Diem.--The members of the Committee shall 
     receive--
       ``(1) a per diem compensation for each day spent in 
     meetings, conferences, or other work of the Committee; and
       ``(2) compensation for necessary traveling or other 
     expenses while engaged in the work of the Committee.

       ``(i) Applicability.--Section 163 shall apply to the 
     Committee.''.
       (b) License Application Review.--Section 182 b. of the 
     Atomic Energy Act of 1954 (42 U.S.C. 2232(b)) is amended--
       (1) by striking ``The Advisory Committee on Reactor 
     Safeguards'' and inserting ``(1) Except as provided in 
     paragraph (2), the Advisory Committee on Reactor 
     Safeguards''; and
       (2) by adding at the end the following:
       ``(2) The Advisory Committee on Reactor Safeguards may 
     decline to review an application referenced in paragraph (1)

       ``(A) contain issues that are unique, novel, or noteworthy;

       ``(B) raise issues that warrant review by the Advisory

       (1) Advisory committees.--Section 163 of the Atomic Energy

       (2) Temporary operating license.--Section 192 a. of the 
     Atomic Energy Act of 1954 (42 U.S.C. 2242(a)) is amended--
       (A) by striking ``Safeguards required by section 182 b.;''

     prepared in response to the report of the Advisory Committee

  Mr. LATTA. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise in support of H.R. 9613, the Nuclear Advisory 
Committee Reform Act, sponsored by the gentlewoman from Tennessee's 
First Congressional District and the gentleman from Texas' 12th 
Congressional District.
  This legislation reflects constructive, bipartisan collaboration 
through the Energy and Commerce legislative process and was reported 
favorably as amended by unanimous vote.
  The Advisory Committee on Reactor Safeguards, or ACRS, serves as an 
important independent adviser to the Nuclear Regulatory Commission, 
NRC, for reactor licensing. The committee assists NRC decisions on 
unique and important safety and technical issues, issues that arise and 
particularly with new designs and technologies. Congress established 
ACRS in the 1950s when the commission was licensing the first 
generation of nuclear reactors.
  Today, NRC staff are well-equipped to deal with licensing issues from 
many well-characterized and understood reactors and technologies. 
However, a new wave of technologies, expected to enter the license 
process, would very much benefit from the Advisory Committee's 
independent review and advice.

  H.R. 9613 serves to update membership requirements in terms of 
service and to focus the important work of ACRS where it will provide 
greatest value to the NRC and the public.
  The bill directs ACRS to focus on issues that directly relate to 
reactor facility design or safety significant or noteworthy or novel 
and have not been previously acted upon by the committee.
  The bill also provides ACRS flexibility to decline and review issues 
in applications that do not merit attention, allowing ACRS to follow a 
more efficient risk-informed approach to work.
  H.R. 9613 will help ACRS to more efficiently and effectively serve 
the commission as it reviews nuclear license applications.
  I urge my colleagues to join me in supporting this bipartisan 
legislation to advance our work to ensure the benefits of nuclear 
energy for our society.

  Mr. PALLONE. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may consume.
  Mr. Speaker, H.R. 9613, the Nuclear Advisory Committee Reform Act, 
alters the scope and organization of the Nuclear Regulatory 
Commission's Advisory Committee on Reactor Safeguards, the body that 
provides independent technical expertise on nuclear licensing.
  Now, 73 years ago, the Atomic Energy Commission created the ACRS, an 
expert body meant to enhance the quality of safety and environmental 
reviews of applications for nuclear licenses. Soon after its 
establishment, Congress passed a law enshrining the ACRS in statute and 
requiring that it review all applications for commercial nuclear power 
plants to ensure the safety of what was at the time a completely new 
technology.
  Seven decades later, the landscape has significantly changed. Nuclear 
energy is no longer novel. More importantly, there have been 
generations of nuclear experts that now staff the NRC, in contrast to 
the small handful of experts who could inform the work of the old 
Atomic Energy Commission in the aftermath of World War II. But the 
requirement that the ACRS review every single reactor has remained the 
same.
  But at the same time, getting rid of the ACRS altogether, as some 
have suggested, would be wildly irresponsible. We are seeing a new 
generation of nuclear reactors, many with new design elements or 
aspects that have never been tested. Independent technical review is 
vital for public confidence in the safety of these reactors, and that 
is increasingly important at a time when the NRC itself is under attack 
and becoming more political.
  Any alterations to the ACRS must balance a need for efficiency with a 
need not to disrupt an organization that has helped keep us safe for 
nearly 75 years. I think this version of H.R. 9613 before us strikes 
that balance. It removes the requirement that the ACRS review each 
application, while requiring that the ACRS review the subset of 
applications that raise unique, novel, or noteworthy issues. 
Critically, it keeps the ACRS itself in charge of making the decision 
about what is unique, novel, or noteworthy, not the NRC. The ACRS 
retains its ability to investigate issues other than reactor 
applications that deserve its attention as well.
  So the idea is to focus the ACRS on what matters most--issues that 
have not come up before that are tricky and can benefit from a unique 
technical viewpoint, and this viewpoint has been useful in the recent 
past. The ACRS has contributed to recent reviews of the first-ever 
small modular reactor and the first-ever restart of a nuclear power 
plant.
  This bill will help the ACRS continue its important work into a new 
generation of reactors, letting its experts focus on the highest 
important issues and ensuring that those experts are heard in the 
licensing process.
  So I thank Chairman Guthrie and Representative Harshbarger for their 
willingness to compromise and find a path forward for the ACRS that 
works for everyone. I intend to support the bill, and I reserve the 
balance of my time.

  Mrs. HARSHBARGER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today in support of the Nuclear 
Advisory Committee Reform Act, legislation that I introduced with my

  The next generation of American nuclear energy is here, with advanced 
reactors entering the pipeline amid skyrocketing demand for reliable, 
affordable energy. That is why preparing a regulatory framework to meet 
the moment is more important than ever.
  When it comes to nuclear energy, safety must always come first.
  My bill ensures our Federal nuclear regulatory system prioritizes its 
time and resources where they are needed most, and that is for novel 
reactor designs that warrant additional scrutiny.
  Since 1957, the Advisory Committee on Reactor Safeguards, ACRS, has 
provided an additional layer of review for nuclear license 
applications,

supplementing the Nuclear Regulatory Commission's own work.
  This was critical when nuclear energy was untested and Federal 
regulators had limited in-house technical expertise.
  However, today, the engineers and regulatory experts at the NRC 
possess the highest levels of institutional knowledge and technical 
capabilities worldwide.
  Yet the ACRS is often tasked with reviews that duplicate the NRC's 
existing work, creating unnecessary delays in the licensing process.
  Without a change to current law, novel reactor technologies that 
warrant additional scrutiny will continue competing for ACRS resources 
with conventional reactor designs that may not require the same level 
of additional review.
  More repetitious procedures do not automatically guarantee greater 
safety.
  My bill, which I am proud to have deployed through bipartisan 
collaboration, puts ACRS in the driver's seat when determining where 
its expertise provides the greatest value.

  Additionally, it directs ACRS to focus its reviews on aspects of 
reactor designs that are safety significant or novel and that the 
committee has not previously evaluated.
  So, Mr. Speaker, my bill ensures safety is never compromised and 
makes certain that the advanced reactors of tomorrow receive the 
appropriate level of oversight.
  This is how we strengthen our energy grid while maintaining the high 
standards of nuclear safety.
  I thank Chairman Guthrie and Ranking Member Pallone for their 
leadership, and I urge my colleagues to support this bill.
