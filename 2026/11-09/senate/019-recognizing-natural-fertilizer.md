# RECOGNIZING NATURAL FERTILIZER

**Speaking:** Ernst, Joni (E000295) [R-IA]

```
                     RECOGNIZING NATURAL FERTILIZER

● Ms. ERNST. Mr. President, as Chair of the U.S. Senate 
Committee on Small

[[Page S5347]]

Business and Entrepreneurship, each week I recognize an outstanding 
Iowa small business that exemplifies the American entrepreneurial 
spirit. This week, it is my privilege to recognize Natural Fertilizer 
of Harrison County, IA, as the Senate Small Business of the Week.
  Abe Sandquist came up with the idea of Natural Fertilizer after 
spending several years developing nutrient management plans for the 
USDA. His time there revealed that many agricultural producers did not 
realize the value of organic nutrients until they received professional 
guidance on how to effectively use them. Seeing the strong demand for 
assistance, Abe established Natural Fertilizer in 2007 to provide 
nutrient management consulting services. Since then, Natural Fertilizer 
has continued to grow. In 2009, Abe recognized the potential in 
brokering and selling excess manure to neighboring farms. Abe's 
decision to enter this new field allowed Natural Fertilizer to partner 
with local manure haulers, transforming the business into a full-
service operation that incorporated transporting, marketing, and 
fertilizing. In 2021, Natural Fertilizer acquired Soil Solutions in 
Onawa, IA. This allowed the business to recycle industrial byproducts 
into high-value fertilizers and expand its reach to farmers in other 
States.
  Today, Natural Fertilizer mainly focuses on the nutrient cycle of 
organic residuals in addition to its consulting services. Abe's goal is 
for everyone to win: the community, the processors, the feedlots, and 
the farmers. Abe and his team of 30 employees and 30 contractors have 
expanded operations to include two recycling centers in western Iowa. 
At these processing plants, heat and microbes break down thousands of 
tons of organic matter, transforming the waste into nutrient-rich, 
sustainable, and affordable fertilizer. Once fully processed and tested 
for quality, the business distributes its fertilizer to over 500 
farmers across Iowa. In 2025, Abe was awarded the USDA Fertilizer 
Production Expansion Program, FPEP, grant to begin constructing the 
Waste Into Nutrient Recovery, WINR Center. Since then, construction of 
a second WINR Center has begun, and a site for a third has been 
purchased. Abe hopes that Natural Fertilizer will one day be the 
national standard.
  Natural Fertilizer's impressive growth has been recognized by several 
organizations in the community. The Harrison County Farm Bureau honored 
Abe with the 2026 Distinguished Service to Agriculture Award. In 2025, 
Iowa's Small Business Development Center awarded him the Neal Smith 
Entrepreneur of the Year Award for Sustainable Innovation in 
Agricultural Waste. That same year, Abe was named Iowa Entrepreneur of 
the Year. Beyond these accolades, Abe remains an active member of 
Woodbine Iowa's IGNITE Pathways Program, which provides high school 
students with opportunities and career paths post-graduation. Abe also 
previously served as vice chair and advocacy chair of the Iowa 
Composting Council, where he remains a member and continues to advocate 
for nutrient cycling across the agricultural industry. Abe and his team 
at Natural Fertilizer continuously demonstrate their commitment to 
improving the lives of fellow Iowans through their work.
  I am pleased to recognize Abe Sandquist and the employees of Natural 
Fertilizer for their commitment to building and serving the Harrison 
County community. I look forward to their continued success and wish 
them the very best in the years ahead.●
```
