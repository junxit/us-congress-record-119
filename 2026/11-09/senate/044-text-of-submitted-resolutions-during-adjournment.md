            TEXT OF SUBMITTED RESOLUTIONS DURING ADJOURNMENT

  SENATE RESOLUTION 950--DESIGNATING THE WEEK OF SEPTEMBER 14 THROUGH 
         SEPTEMBER 18, 2026, AS ``NATIONAL CLEAN ENERGY WEEK''

  Ms. COLLINS (for herself, Ms. Cantwell, Mr. Warnock, Mr. 
Hickenlooper, Mrs. Shaheen, Mr. Curtis, Mr. Whitehouse, Mrs. Capito, 
Mr. King, and Mr. Sullivan) submitted the following resolution; which 
was referred to the Committee on the Judiciary:

                              S. Res. 950

       Whereas, across the United States, clean and readily 
     abundant forms of energy are powering more homes and 
     businesses than ever before;
       Whereas clean energy generation is readily available from 
     zero- and low-emissions sources;
       Whereas the clean energy sector is a growing part of the 
     economy and in recent years has been a key driver of economic 
     growth in the United States;
       Whereas technological innovation can further reduce costs, 
     enhance reliability, and increase deployment of clean energy 
     sources;
       Whereas the report of the Department of Energy entitled 
     ``2025 U.S. Energy & Employment Report'' found that, at the 
     end of 2024, the energy and energy efficiency sectors in the 
     United States employed approximately 8,500,000 individuals;
       Whereas the scaling of affordable and exportable clean 
     energy is essential to reducing global emissions;
       Whereas clean energy jobs contribute to the growth of local 
     economies; and
       Whereas innovative clean energy solutions and clean energy 
     jobs are part of the energy future of the United States: Now, 
     therefore, be it
       Resolved, That the Senate--
       (1) designates the week of September 14 through September 
     18, 2026, as ``National Clean Energy Week'';
       (2) encourages individuals and organizations across the 
     United States to support commonsense solutions that address 
     the economic, environmental, and energy needs of the United 
     States in the 21st century;
       (3) encourages the Federal Government, States, 
     municipalities, and individuals to invest in affordable, 
     clean, and low-emitting energy technologies;
       (4) supports reliable and affordable energy for the people 
     of the United States; and
       (5) recognizes the role of entrepreneurs and small 
     businesses in ensuring the leadership of the United States in 
     the global energy marketplace and in supporting low-cost, 
     clean, and reliable energy in the United States.

SENATE RESOLUTION 951--DESIGNATING SEPTEMBER 2026 AS ``NATIONAL INFANT 
MORTALITY AWARENESS MONTH'', RAISING AWARENESS OF INFANT MORTALITY, AND 
             INCREASING EFFORTS TO REDUCE INFANT MORTALITY

  Mrs. HYDE-SMITH (for herself and Mr. Ossoff) submitted the following 
resolution; which was referred to the Committee on the Judiciary:

                              S. Res. 951

       Whereas the term ``infant mortality'' refers to the death 
     of a baby before the first birthday of the baby;
       Whereas, each year, approximately 20,000 infants in the 
     United States die in the first year of life;
       Whereas the United States ranks 32nd out of the 38 
     countries in the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and 
     Development (referred to in this preamble as ``OECD'') for 
     infant mortality rate, with 5.5 deaths per 1,000 live births;
       Whereas high rates of infant mortality are especially 
     prevalent in minority and underserved communities, including 
     communities experiencing high rates of poverty and 
     unemployment and those with limited access to health care 
     providers;
       Whereas more than 35 percent of counties are considered 
     maternity care deserts, where there is no access to birthing 
     hospitals, birth centers offering obstetric care, or 
     obstetric providers, affecting more than 2,300,000 women of 
     reproductive age and impacting 150,000 births in 2022;
       Whereas approximately half of rural communities lack a 
     birthing facility or obstetrician;
       Whereas the leading causes of infant mortality are--
       (1) birth defects;
       (2) low birth weight;
       (3) sudden infant death syndrome;
       (4) unintentional injuries;
       (5) maternal complications;
       (6) bacterial sepsis of newborn; and
       (7) cord and placental complications;
       Whereas infant mortality can be substantially reduced 
     through--
       (1) community-based services, such as outreach, home 
     visitation, care coordination, health education, and 
     interconceptional care;
       (2) early prenatal care; and
       (3) paternal involvement;
       Whereas support for community-based programs to reduce 
     infant mortality may result in lower spending on medical 
     interventions, special education, and other social services 
     required by infants and children born with a low birth 
     weight;
       Whereas the Department of Health and Human Services has 
     implemented the Newborn Supply Kit program;
       Whereas newborn screening is important to detecting 
     potentially fatal or disabling conditions as early as 
     possible, often before the infant displays any signs or 
     symptoms of a disease or condition, and early detection 
     allows treatment to begin immediately, which may lead to 
     eliminate or reduce the effects of the condition;
       Whereas, if left untreated, these conditions can have 
     serious effects, such as lifelong nervous system damage, 
     intellectual, developmental, and physical disabilities, and 
     even death;
       Whereas untreated maternal mental health conditions during 
     pregnancy have been linked to adverse birth outcomes, 
     including preterm birth and low birth weight babies, which 
     can impact infant mortality;
       
       Whereas the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention Safe 
     Motherhood Portfolio, maternal mortality review committees, 
     and perinatal quality collaboratives, provides information 
     that is important to addressing preterm birth rates and 
     conditions that put babies at risk;
       Whereas the Maternal and Child Health Bureau Healthy Start 
     program has supported communities facing high rates of infant 
     mortality and maternal illness to improve

[[Page S5359]]

     health outcomes before, during, and after pregnancy;
       Whereas Congress continues to support adequate staffing and 
     resources to ensure infant mortality programs are 
     administered in a timely and effective manner and that funds 
     are distributed without unnecessary delay;
       Whereas coordination programs related to preterm birth, 
     infant mortality, and other adverse birth outcomes can 
     contribute to improved maternal and infant health;
       Whereas partnerships among educational institutions, 
     States, and other stakeholders play a role in maternal and 
     infant health initiatives; and
       Whereas public awareness and education campaigns on infant 
     mortality are held during the month of September each year: 
     Now, therefore, be it
       Resolved, That the Senate--
       (1) designates September 2026 as ``National Infant 
     Mortality Awareness Month'';
       (2) supports efforts--
       (A) to educate people in the United States about infant 
     mortality and the factors that contribute to infant 
     mortality;
       (B) to reduce infant deaths, low birth weight, preterm 
     births, and disparities in perinatal outcomes; and
       (C) to increase access to and encourage early prenatal 
     care;
       (3) recognizes the critical importance of including efforts 
     to reduce infant mortality and the factors that contribute to 
     infant mortality as part of prevention and wellness 
     strategies; and
       (4) calls on the people of the United States to observe 
     National Infant Mortality Awareness Month with appropriate 
     programs and activities.
