# TRIBUTE TO VAN INGRAM

**Speaking:** McConnell, Mitch (M000355) [R-KY]

```
                         TRIBUTE TO VAN INGRAM

● Mr. McCONNELL. Mr. President, I would like to take a moment to 
honor Van Ingram, the executive director of the Kentucky Office of Drug 
Control Policy, ODCP, who is retiring at the end of this year after 22 
years as one of the Commonwealth's leading drug enforcement officers. I 
have worked closely with Van to combat the issues of substance abuse, 
addiction, and the opioid crisis that has plagued our state. It is my 
privilege to look back at Van's lengthy career in public service 
fighting for the betterment of the people of the great State of 
Kentucky.
  Prior to joining the Kentucky Office of Drug Control Policy, Van 
spent more than 23 years in the Maysville Police Department, including 
his final 6 as chief. In 2001, Van was named Kentucky Chief of the Year 
and received the Governor's Award for Outstanding Contribution to Law 
Enforcement in 2004, the same year that the ODCP was created. The 
ODCP's office's main mission since its inception when it was formed has 
been to help communities throughout the Commonwealth understand 
substance abuse and reduce the risk of addiction, while coordinating 
efforts with law enforcement and other agencies to help those receive 
the treatment and help they need. Van was at the forefront of these 
efforts and became executive director of the office in 2009. Van led 
drug-control policy in Kentucky during a time when the State was at in 
the height of its struggle with the opioid crisis. In 2012, Van helped 
Kentucky strengthen its prescription-monitoring efforts by requiring 
many prescribers to check the Kentucky All Schedule Prescription 
Electronic Reporting system, known as KASPER, before prescribing 
controlled substances. Thanks to Van's innovation and leadership, 
almost all States in the years since have established similar systems.
  During my time as Senate leader, I was proud to leverage my 
leadership role and work alongside Van in fighting the substance abuse 
epidemic.

[[Page S5348]]

From 2000-2012, Kentucky saw hospitalizations for drug-dependent 
newborns drastically increase from 28 in 2000 to 824 in 2012. By 2018, 
the crisis had worsened, with Kentucky ranking fifth for the most 
overdose deaths in the Nation. Van and I both shared a commitment to 
finding solutions to this crisis that was plaguing the Commonwealth. 
Together, we supported new legislative efforts and created new programs 
to help combat the epidemic, including the bipartisan SUPPORT for 
Patients and Communities Act in 2018, which provided Federal funding to 
establish the State's first official comprehensive opioid recovery 
center. As part of these efforts, Kentucky saw overdose deaths fall by 
nearly 15 percent in 2018, the largest drop in more than a decade. That 
same year, the Nation also recorded its first decline in drug overdose 
deaths since 1990.
  Van was truly a critical member in the nationwide fight against the 
opioid epidemic, and it is thanks to his leadership and commitment to 
the people of the Commonwealth that Kentucky is a healthier and safer 
State today. I ask my Senate colleagues to join me in celebrating the 
work and career of Van Ingram and wish him well during his 
retirement.●
```
