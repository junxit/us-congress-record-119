# RECOGNIZING NATIONAL CONCUSSION AWARENESS DAY AND THE UNIVERSITY OF MICHIGAN CONCUSSION CENTER

**Speaking:** Dingell, Debbie (D000624) [D-MI]

```
  RECOGNIZING NATIONAL CONCUSSION AWARENESS DAY AND THE UNIVERSITY OF 
                       MICHIGAN CONCUSSION CENTER

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. DEBBIE DINGELL

                              of michigan

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 21, 2026

  Mrs. DINGELL. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize National 
Concussion Awareness Day, observed on September 18, 2026, and to draw 
attention to the millions of Americans, civilian and military, affected 
by concussions each year.
  Today, I am proud to highlight the University of Michigan Concussion 
Center, the national leader in advancing our understanding of these 
injuries. Since its founding, the Center has generated more than $100 
million in research funding and produced more than 200 publications 
spanning injury prevention, identification and clinical care, and most 
importantly its long-term effects. Michigan Concussion Center 
reseachers connect scientific discovery with clinical expertise and 
real-world needs, working alongside civilian and military health 
professionals, educators, and community partners to improve prevention, 
treatment and recovery. The Center's impact extends well beyond 
Michigan and the United States, with members collaborating on research 
and public education in more than 25 countries.
  As a leader of the NCAA-DoD-supported Concussion Assessment, 
Research, and Education (CARE) Consortium, researchers at the Michigan 
Concussion Center are conducting the largest concussion study in the 
world, tracking more than 53,000 civilians and military officers to 
understand the acute and long-term effects of concussions. This public-
private partnership has been instrumental in understanding and 
mitigating the effects of concussions on brain health, military 
readiness, and warfighter lethality.
  Their collaborations have also produced practical innovations. In 
partnership with the University's Center for Academic Innovation, the 
Center developed a virtual-reality training tool that allows athletic 
trainers and other health providers to practice recognizing and 
responding to concussions before stepping onto the sidelines. The 
Michigan Concussion Center also helped develop the recently released 
Concussion Navigator with the National Association of State Head Injury 
Administrators and the Brain lnjury Association of Michigan. This 
resource gives Michigan families trusted guidance on symptom 
monitoring, locating specialist care, and ensuring safe return to 
school, work, and play.
  The University of Michigan Concussion Center recognizes that research 
has its greatest impact when reliable information reaches the public. 
Through outreach, community engagement, and education, the Center 
equips students, athletes, parents, coaches, health professionals, and 
others to recognize concussion symptoms, respond appropriately, and 
support a safe recovery.
  On National Concussion Awareness Day, I encourage my colleagues and 
all Americans to learn the signs and symptoms of concussions, support 
safety measures, and seek timely medical care when a head injury 
occurs. I commend the University of Michigan Concussion Center as a 
national leader in advancing concussion science and for working to 
protect everyone, in every field, every day. Their dedication reflects 
the University of Michigan's enduring commitment to research and 
service for the common good.
```
