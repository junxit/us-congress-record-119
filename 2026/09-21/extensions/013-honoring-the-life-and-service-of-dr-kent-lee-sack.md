# HONORING THE LIFE AND SERVICE OF DR. KENT LEE SACK

**Speaking:** DeSaulnier, Mark (D000623) [D-CA]

```
           HONORING THE LIFE AND SERVICE OF DR. KENT LEE SACK

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. MARK DeSAULNIER

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 21, 2026

  Mr. DeSAULNIER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize the life and 
service of Dr. Kent Lee Sack.
  Kent was born in Hettinger, North Dakota, on January 13, 1935. He 
earned his degree with honor from North Dakota State University where 
he studied pharmacology. Kent returned to his alma mater later to help 
build the university's master's program before he went on to medical 
school at Northwestern University. After medical school, Kent relocated 
to California where he completed an internship at Orange County General 
Hospital and later, a family medicine residency at Contra Costa 
Regional Medical Center.
  Kent was then drafted into the Navy where he accepted a Marine Corps 
assignment and served with the First Marine Division in Vietnam. During 
this time, Kent served as an anesthesiologist, surgeon, and medical 
troubleshooter, reactivating a Marine Corps MASH unit. For his service 
in the Marine Corps, Kent was awarded the Bronze Star and Purple Heart.
  Following his military service, Kent continued to demonstrate his 
commitment to promoting public health and quality care. He returned to 
California where he completed a pediatric residency at Los Angeles 
County USC Medical Center and later returned to Contra Costa Regional 
Medical Center. Kent was a firm believer that everyone deserved to be

[[Page E959]]

treated with dignity and compassion. He led the way as an early 
provider of HIV care in the East Bay during the AIDS epidemic, helping 
to establish social service agencies for patients. Additionally, he 
served two terms as chair of Bay Area Physicians for Human Rights. He 
later went on to be a founding member of the Rainbow Community Center 
in Concord, providing critical resources and health services to Contra 
Costa's LGBTQIA+ community. Kent helped to shape the Rainbow Center 
into the organization it is today, guiding the organization through its 
early stages of development and leading the Rainbow Center through 
challenging financial periods. Throughout his career, Kent remained a 
tireless advocate for equitable, compassionate care.
  Kent sadly passed away in June of 2026, after greatly serving his 
country and his community throughout his entire life. Please join me in 
honoring him for his remarkable impact in our community and his 
incredible leadership and advocacy.
```
