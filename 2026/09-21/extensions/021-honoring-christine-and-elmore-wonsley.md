# HONORING CHRISTINE AND ELMORE WONSLEY

**Speaking:** Wilson, Frederica S. (W000808) [D-FL]

```
                 HONORING CHRISTINE AND ELMORE WONSLEY

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. FREDERICA S. WILSON

                               of florida

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 21, 2026

  Ms. WILSON of Florida. Mr. Speaker, I rise today, as Chair of the 
U.S. Commission on the Social Status of Black Men and Boys, on the 
occasion of the Congressional Black Caucus Foundation's Annual 
Legislative Conference, where we gathered to officially cut the ribbon 
for the Commission and to honor the memory of young Black boys who were 
murdered, and the parents who continue to carry their sons' legacies 
forward. It is in that spirit that I pay tribute to Christine and 
Elmore Wonsley, the parents of Nolan Xavier Wells, an 18-year-old 
student-athlete whose death this past July has left a family grieving 
and a community demanding answers.
  Nolan played football at Ocean Springs High School and went on to 
play at Southwest Mississippi Community College, known by teammates and 
friends as a steady, well-liked presence. On July 4, he traveled by 
boat with friends to Horn Island off the Mississippi Gulf Coast; he did 
not return with the group that evening, and his body was recovered two 
days later. Since that day, Christine and Elmore have transformed their 
grief into a public and unrelenting call for a full, transparent 
investigation, asking for nothing more than the same thoroughness any 
family would expect when a child does not come home.
  Rather than grieve in silence, Christine and Elmore have stepped into 
the national spotlight. They stood alongside attorney Ben Crump and 
Rev. Al Sharpton at a press conference in Harlem demanding answers, and 
they have continued to speak publicly at national gatherings, including 
alongside civil rights leaders at the National Association of Black 
Journalists conference in Atlanta, to insist that Nolan's case receive 
the scrutiny it deserves. Their willingness to speak out, even as the 
investigation remains active and unresolved, has kept Nolan's story 
before the American people and ensured that his life, and the questions 
surrounding his death, are not forgotten. Their advocacy reflects 
something larger than one family's search for answers. It reflects a 
growing chorus of families across this country who have refused to let 
their loved ones become statistics, who have turned personal tragedy 
into public accountability, and who have reminded this Nation that 
justice delayed too often becomes denied. Christine's and Elmore's 
strength in the face of unimaginable loss, and their determination to 
see their son's case pursued to its full conclusion, embodies the same 
spirit that brought so many families to Washington this week to share 
their stories.
  As they look toward the future, Elmore and Christine have spoken of 
building a foundation in Nolan's name, ensuring that his legacy endures 
beyond the headlines and beyond the investigation itself. It is a 
testament to the kind

[[Page E961]]

of parents they are that even in grief, they are already thinking of 
how to help others. Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in 
extending our deepest condolences to Christine and Elmore, and in 
recognizing their courage in speaking out, their persistence in seeking 
the truth, and their commitment to ensuring that Nolan Xavier Wells is 
remembered not only for how he died, but for the life he lived and the 
people he touched.
```
