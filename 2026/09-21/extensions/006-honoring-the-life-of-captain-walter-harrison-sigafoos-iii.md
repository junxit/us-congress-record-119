# HONORING THE LIFE OF CAPTAIN WALTER HARRISON SIGAFOOS, III

**Speaking:** Fitzpatrick, Brian K. (F000466) [R-PA]

```
       HONORING THE LIFE OF CAPTAIN WALTER HARRISON SIGAFOOS, III

                                 ______
                                 

                       HON. BRIAN K. FITZPATRICK

                            of pennsylvania

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 21, 2026

  Mr. FITZPATRICK. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life and 
service of a remarkable American, Captain Walter Harrison Sigafoos, 
III, whose courage and sacrifice have left an enduring mark on our 
Nation.
  Born on August 29, 1946, in Richboro, Pennsylvania, Walter Sigafoos 
answered the call to serve his country at a young age. He attended the 
United States Air Force Academy, where he graduated with the Class of 
1968 with a degree in Astronautics. During his time at the Academy, he 
was named to the Superintendent's Merit List, served as president of 
the Ski Club, and participated in the Dance Committee. His classmates 
remember him as a young man who embraced both the discipline of 
military service and the fellowship of Academy life.
  After graduation, Lieutenant Sigafoos continued his training in 
preparation for service as an Air Force combat aviator. He 
distinguished himself during his training and went on to complete F-4 
Phantom combat crew training at George Air Force Base. His path 
ultimately took him to Southeast Asia, where he served with the 421st 
Tactical Fighter Squadron, 366th Tactical Fighter Wing, based at Da 
Nang Air Base, South Vietnam.
  On April 25, 1971, Captain Sigafoos was serving as the weapons and 
systems officer aboard an F-4D Phantom II during a nighttime 
reconnaissance and strike mission over Laos. Then came a transmission, 
ultimately becoming the last known radio contact with the aircraft.
  The aircraft was subsequently presumed lost over Laos. Captain 
Sigafoos and his fellow crew member, Captain Jeffrey C. Lemon, were 
reported missing. More than five decades later, Captain Sigafoos 
remains unaccounted for. The Department of Defense continues to 
maintain his case among those of American service members lost in 
Southeast Asia.
  Mr. Speaker, we will never forget the sacrifice of Captain Walter 
Harrison Sigafoos, III. Though decades have passed since he was lost in 
service to our Nation, his memory lives on through the loved ones who 
have carried his name, his story, and his legacy forward. Their 
remembrance ensures that his courage and devotion will not fade with 
time but will continue to inspire future generations of Americans to 
understand the meaning of service, sacrifice, and duty to country. We 
honor Captain Sigafoos by remembering the life he lived, the service he 
gave, and the enduring example he leaves for all who come after him.
```
