# HONORING DICK TAYLOR

**Speaking:** Fong, Vince (F000480) [R-CA]

```
                          HONORING DICK TAYLOR

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. VINCE FONG

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 21, 2026

  Mr. FONG. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Mr. Dick Taylor, whose 
lifelong residency in Bakersfield has made him an unofficial ambassador 
of Kern County and a bastion of the Local community.
  Born September 5, 1955, to Harris and Suzanne Taylor, Dick spent the 
entirety of his adolescence in the Central Valley, graduating from East 
High School in 1973. Shortly thereafter, he proudly joined the United 
States Marine Corps in 1975, attaining the rank of Sergeant before 
being honorably discharged in

[[Page E956]]

1979. Following his years of service in the military, Dick returned to 
Bakersfield where he dedicated 27 years to Taylor Tire & Brake, his 
family's local business. During this time, he was named Director of the 
California State Tire Dealers Association, as well as both President 
and General Manager of Taylor Tire & Brake.
  After his time with Taylor Tire & Brake, he continued his service to 
Kern County, working six years as a field representative for Kern 
County District Supervisor Mike Maggard and, in 2012, becoming director 
of the Kern County Veterans Service Department. In the years following, 
he served in several leadership positions with the California 
Association of County Veterans Service Officers, served briefly as an 
Honorary Commander at Edwards Air Force Base, and retried in 2018 as 
the county veterans' services officer.
  Dick's lifelong commitment to Kern County is also visible through his 
work with local groups, serving as a liaison representative for the 
Devil Pups Youth Program, a board member for the Bakersfield National 
Cemetery Support Committee, and a tour leader for Tejon Ranch 
Conservancy along with numerous other volunteer and community service 
efforts. Notably, he and his wife Cheryl volunteered their time 
escorting World War II, Korean War, and Vietnam veterans to Washington, 
DC on seven `Honor Flight Kern County' flights, and helped establish 
the ``Kern County Line, Where We Honor Veterans'' signs installed in 14 
locations along federal and state highways.
  Though much of their time is dedicated to others, Dick and Cheryl 
have built a life in the Central Valley centered on family, raising 
their children Maxwell and Shellie, and celebrating four grandchildren 
Jared, Kamryn, Brody, and Kellan.
  Mr. Speaker, please join me in recognizing and honoring Dick Taylor 
on his 71st birthday, in appreciation for his lifelong service, 
leadership, and dedication to the people of Kern County.
```
