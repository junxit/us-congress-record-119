# HONORING LUCY McBATH

**Speaking:** Wilson, Frederica S. (W000808) [D-FL]

```
                          HONORING LUCY McBATH

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. FREDERICA S. WILSON

                               of florida

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 21, 2026

  Ms. WILSON of Florida. Mr. Speaker, I rise today, as Chair of the 
U.S. Commission on the Social Status of Black Men and Boys, on the 
occasion of the Congressional Black Caucus Foundation's Annual 
Legislative Conference, where we gathered to officially cut the ribbon 
for the Commission and to honor the memory of young Black boys who were 
murdered, and the parents who continue to carry their sons' legacies 
forward. It is in that spirit that I pay tribute to my colleague, 
Congresswoman Lucy McBath of Georgia's 6th district whose journey from 
a grieving mother to one of this Nation's most persistent voices 
against gun violence continues to shape the work we do in this 
Congress.
  In 2012, Lucy's son, Jordan Davis, was shot and killed at a 
Jacksonville gas station after an argument over the volume of his 
music. He was seventeen years old. In the years that followed, Lucy 
refused to let his death become another statistic. She became the 
national spokeswoman for Everytown for Gun Safety and Moms Demand 
Action, carrying Jordan's story into communities and statehouses across 
the country, and in 2018 she carried it all the way to the floor of 
this House.
  Since coming to Congress, Lucy has turned personal loss into 
legislative action. She has been instrumental in passing the Bipartisan 
Safer Communities Law, helped secure funding to study gun violence as 
the public health crisis it is, after decades in which that research 
had gone unfunded, and now serves as Vice Chair of the Gun Violence 
Prevention Task Force. She has stood on this floor to mark the 
anniversary of Sandy Hook and has authored legislation to fund 
community-based violence intervention, understanding that the fight 
against gun violence must be waged in neighborhoods as much as in this 
chamber.
  Today, Lucy continues to bring that same commitment to this panel, 
sharing Jordan's story once more, this time alongside other families 
who understand exactly what she has carried for over a decade. Her 
willingness to keep telling that story, even after years in public 
life, reflects a truth she has lived every day since 2012: that the 
pain of losing a child to gun violence never truly ends, but it can be 
transformed into the work of protecting other families from that same 
loss.
  Lucy's path from a mother on a mission to a leader on this floor 
stands as a testament to what grief can become when it is met with 
purpose. She has shown her colleagues, and the country, that advocacy 
borne of unimaginable loss can still change laws, change funding 
priorities, and change how this Nation talks about gun violence.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring Congresswoman 
Lucy McBath for her tireless advocacy against gun violence, and for 
continuing to carry Jordan's memory into every fight she takes on for 
the families of this country.
```
