# HONORING SANTONIO AND MONICA CARTER

**Speaking:** Wilson, Frederica S. (W000808) [D-FL]

```
                  HONORING SANTONIO AND MONICA CARTER

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. FREDERICA S. WILSON

                               of florida

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 21, 2026

  Ms. WILSON of Florida. Mr. Speaker, I rise today, as the Chair of the 
U.S. Commission on the Social Status of Black Men and Boys on the 
occasion of the Congressional Black Caucus Foundation's Annual 
Legislative Conference, where we gathered to officially cut the ribbon 
for the U.S. Commission on the Social Status of Black Men and Boys and 
to honor the memory of young Black boys who were murdered, and the 
parents who continue to carry their sons' legacies forward. It is in 
that spirit that I pay tribute to Santonio and Monica Carter, the 
parents of King Carter, a six-year-old boy from Miami whose life was 
taken by a stray bullet as he walked to a nearby store. In the years 
since, Mr. and Mrs. Carter refused to let his death be reduced to a 
headline.
  King was walking to buy candy near his home in Liberty City when he 
was struck and killed in the crossfire of a dispute that had nothing to 
do with him. In the aftermath, his parents did what no parent should 
ever have to do: they buried their son, and then they turned their 
grief into a demand that the community around them do better by its 
children.
  Mr. and Mrs. Carter have since become fixtures in their community, 
not as bystanders to its violence but as people actively working to 
interrupt it. They founded ``Save Our Kings and Queens'' in their son's 
honor, offering young people in their neighborhood an alternative to 
the streets and a reminder that someone is watching out for them. They 
have stood at rallies, spoken to reporters, and shown up year after 
year at prayer vigils and balloon releases marking the day their son 
was taken, insisting each time that King's name not be allowed to fade.
  The street where King was killed now bears his name, a small but 
permanent reminder to everyone who passes that a child's life was lost 
there and that his family refused to let the moment pass quietly. 
Santonio has said plainly that he cannot get much out of life right now 
except helping somebody else, and that spirit has carried him and 
Monica through a grief no family should have to bear.
  The Carters carry with them not only the memory of their son but 
years of hard-won experience turning private loss into public purpose. 
Their presence is a reminder that the fight against gun violence is not 
an abstraction debated in hearing rooms alone--it is carried, every 
day, by parents like them who have chosen to keep showing up.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring Santonio and 
Monica Carter for their courage, their persistence, and their 
unwavering commitment to ensuring that their son, King Carter's life 
continues to mean something for the children who come after him.
```
