# HONORING BENJAMIN CRUMP

**Speaking:** Wilson, Frederica S. (W000808) [D-FL]

```
                        HONORING BENJAMIN CRUMP

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. FREDERICA S. WILSON

                               of florida

                    in the house of representatives

                       Monday, September 21, 2026

  Ms. WILSON of Florida. Mr. Speaker, I rise today, as Chair of the 
U.S. Commission on the Social Status of Black Men and Boys, on the 
occasion of the Congressional Black Caucus Foundation's Annual 
Legislative Conference, where we gathered to officially cut the ribbon 
for the Commission and to honor the memory of young Black boys who were 
murdered, and the parents who continue to carry their sons' legacies 
forward. It is in that spirit that I pay tribute to Attorney Benjamin 
Crump, who spent his career confronting the disparities Black men and 
boys face in our criminal justice system, our schools, our hospitals, 
and our workplaces.
  For nearly two decades, families who had nowhere else to turn have 
turned to him. As founder and principal owner of Ben Crump Law, he has 
stood beside the families of Trayvon Martin, Michael Brown, Stephon 
Clark, Ahmaud Arbery, Breonna Taylor, and George Floyd, and he has 
fought for the residents of Flint, Michigan, in their long battle for 
clean water. He carried the case for reparations on behalf of the 
family of Henrietta Lacks. As this panel's participants share the pain 
of losing a child and the fight that grief has demanded of them, Mr. 
Crump is not hearing their stories for the first time--he has lived 
alongside families carrying that exact weight for most of his career.
  What sets his work apart is that it rarely ends with verdicts alone. 
Time and again, he has taken the outcome of a single case and pushed it 
further, turning accountability for one family into policy that 
protects others--legislation restricting excessive use of force and 
requiring implicit bias training within law enforcement agencies. He 
understands, in other words, how an individual family's tragedy becomes 
the basis for the very kind of systemic reform this Commission was 
created to recommend.
  That commitment to building something lasting extends beyond any 
single case. Mr. Crump founded and directs the Benjamin Crump Social 
Justice Institute, and in 2023, St Thomas University's College of Law 
in Miami was renamed the Benjamin L. Crump College of Law in his 
honor--the first law school in the country named for a practicing Black 
attorney. It is that same investment in the next generation, and in 
turning individual pain into lasting institutions, that he brings to 
this panel today: a chance to ensure the stories shared by our families 
are not simply heard, but translated into the policy recommendations 
this Commission is charged with delivering.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in recognizing Attorney 
Benjamin Crump for his extraordinary contributions to civil rights and 
social justice, and for lending his voice and his experience to this 
vital conversation on behalf of Black men and boys across America.
```
