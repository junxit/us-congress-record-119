# REMEMBERING TOM ROSE

```
                          REMEMBERING TOM ROSE

                                 ______
                                 

                         HON. MICHAEL R. TURNER

                                of ohio

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, September 10, 2026

  Mr. TURNER. Mr. Speaker, I rise to honor the memory of Tom Rose, from 
Springboro, Ohio, who passed away on August 15, 2026, surrounded by his 
loving family. Tom was a leader in the Delphi Salaried Retirees 
Association (DSRA), who spent the last 17 years of his life advocating 
for the restoration of pension benefits for these salaried retirees.
  Tom was one of more than 20,000 non-union Delphi salaried workers, 
including 5,000 in the state of Ohio, who saw their pensions unfairly 
slashed by as much as 70 percent in the aftermath of the federal 
government's bailout of General Motors in 2009--while the pensions of 
their hourly, union-represented coworkers were protected and made whole 
with taxpayer dollars.
  In addition to losing a significant portion of his pension, Tom also 
saw his healthcare costs triple after losing his company medical 
coverage, both of which were earned and promised as part of his 
employment. He spent the rest of his life fighting for justice--not 
just for himself, but for other non-union Delphi salaried workers whose 
pensions and healthcare benefits were taken away through no fault of 
their own.
  Thomas Stetson Rose was born in Oakland, California on July 31, 1946. 
He grew up in Nashville, Tennessee and earned a degree in Mechanical 
Engineering from Tennessee Tech. He honorably served his country as a 
Second Lieutenant in the U.S. Army, and spent a year in Vietnam during 
the war.
  On September 13, 1975, Tom married the love of his life, Jaci, who 
was from Dayton, Ohio. Together, they shared 50 years of marriage and 
produced a wonderful family that includes three children and six 
grandchildren.
  Tom spent 30 years working at General Motors plants in Dayton, Ohio 
and an additional nine years at Delphi Corporation, which GM spun off 
in 1999. His working career began as a young engineer at the local GM 
plant on Wisconsin Boulevard in Dayton and included many different 
management jobs, including Plant Manager at the Kettering Boulevard 
Plant.
  Tom led a team of talented salaried and union employees who delivered 
quality automotive parts to its customers on time, and whose hard work 
contributed greatly to the local economy. These men and women often 
worked more than the expected 40 hours per week, with the expectation 
of retiring with a full pension, health care and other benefits.
  Following the bankruptcy of Delphi, and the Obama Administration's 
taxpayer-funded bailout of General Motors, the Pension Benefit Guaranty 
Corporation unjustly terminated the earned pension plans of more than 
20,000 Delphi Salaried Retirees. This decision caused devastating 
financial losses to these families who depended upon their pensions, 
earned through decades of faithful service, to support themselves 
during retirement.
  There was no one at the table representing the Delphi Salaried 
Retirees when they saw their pensions severely slashed--but Tom stepped 
forward, and he used his powerful voice to speak out on behalf of those 
whose voices were not heard.
  Tom took on a leadership role in the DSRA and became a tireless and 
relentless advocate on behalf of Delphi's Salaried Retirees, whom he 
felt deserved the same opportunity to enjoy a comfortable retirement as 
other GM retirees. He traveled to Washington and testified before 
Congress on multiple occasions, detailing how the pension cuts impacted 
his retirement and

[[Page E897]]

threatened the financial security of other retirees.
  Tom Rose was the fiercest and most effective ally I have worked with 
in my decades-long fight to restore the pensions of the Delphi Salaried 
Retirees. This is an issue that hit close to home for both of us. I 
understood how the loss of these benefits impacted families because my 
father lost his healthcare during the GM bankruptcy after working for 
General Motors for over 40 years.
  Tom has stood at my side throughout this challenging fight, beginning 
with the initial Congressional investigations following the GM 
bankruptcy, through the ensuing court battles, through the House 
passage of the bipartisan Susan Muffley Act, and through this year's 
introduction of a legislative amendment, backed by the White House, 
funding the restoration of the pensions for the Delphi Salaried 
Retirees, which could finally bring closure to this difficult chapter 
in our history.
  It saddens me greatly to know that Tom did not live to see the final 
outcome of this long and often frustrating battle. One of the great 
privileges of my life was to be able to work with him, to get to know 
him, and to become his friend. I can think of no better way to pay 
tribute to Tom's memory than by carrying on this noble fight and seeing 
it through to a successful conclusion.
  Mr. Speaker, all of us who knew Tom Rose will not easily forget him, 
and his influence will certainly be missed. I ask my colleagues to join 
me in celebrating the life of this courageous and compassionate man, 
and I extend my heartfelt condolences to his friends and family.
```
