# HONORING ISRAELITE MISSIONARY BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
              HONORING ISRAELITE MISSIONARY BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, September 10, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise to honor Israelite Missionary Baptist 
Church, located at 1841 Thomas H. Delpit Drive in Baton Rouge, 
Louisiana. Established in May 1915 by Rev. Sidney W. Clark and a 
devoted band of Christians, the church began in a cottage house on 
Tennessee Street. Through the prayers and dedication of its early 
members, land was purchased from Robert Hart on Texas Street, now 
Thomas H. Delpit Drive, the site where the church stands today.
  Despite humble beginnings and many challenges, Rev. Clark and his 
congregation persevered, making numerous improvements and additions. In 
1936, plans were made for a brick building, but instead, funds were 
used to remodel the existing structure. Rev. Clark faithfully served 
until his health declined in 1940, appointing Rev. R. D. Green to carry 
on. After Rev. Clark's passing, Rev. Green became the church's second 
pastor in 1942 and led another remodeling. Under his guidance, new 
ground was purchased, and a brick building was constructed and 
completed on December 5, 1952. The church flourished under his 
leadership.
  When Rev. Green's health failed, Rev. Jesse Davis was brought in and 
became the third pastor in 1958. Rev. Davis oversaw a third remodeling, 
expanding the pulpit, choir stand, seating, kitchen, dining area, and 
adding choir rooms, a new office, and a larger pastor's study, as well 
as acquiring a Baby Grand Piano and a distinctive outdoor bulletin 
board. This renovation was completed in 1976. Rev. Davis served until 
1987, after which Rev. Louis Hardy and Rev. Ronald T. Williams led 
until Rev. Charles Lee Burris, Sr. was elected as the fourth pastor in 
1988.
  Under Pastor Burris, the church reached new heights, including the 
addition of a fellowship hall in 2001, property purchases for parking, 
and improvements to the sanctuary, culminating in a fourth remodeling 
in 2005. In

[[Page E893]]

2006, tragedy struck when the sanctuary burned, but through prayer, 
perseverance, and the support of the community, the church was restored 
and officially reoccupied in October 2007. Pastor Burris's vision was 
for Israelite to own most of its block on Thomas H. Delpit Drive.
  After Pastor Burris's passing in 2009, Rev. Donald R. Sterling, a 
``native son'' of Israelite Baptist Church, took up leadership in 2010. 
Under his guidance, the church acquired additional properties for 
parking and future expansion and officially amended its charter to 
Israelite Missionary Baptist Church of South Baton Rouge.
  Today, under Reverend Sterling's leadership, Israelite continues to 
serve as a beacon of faith, holding steadfast to the divine guidance 
and spirit of obedience that characterized its founders, and providing 
a spiritual home for worship and service to God in the Baton Rouge 
community.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring Israelite 
Missionary Baptist Church for over a century of faithful service, and 
in wishing Reverend Donald Sterling and the congregation continued 
blessings for generations to come.
```
