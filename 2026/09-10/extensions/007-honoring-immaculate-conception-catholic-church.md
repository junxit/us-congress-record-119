# HONORING IMMACULATE CONCEPTION CATHOLIC CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
             HONORING IMMACULATE CONCEPTION CATHOLIC CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, September 10, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Immaculate Conception 
Catholic Church in Scotlandville, Louisiana, a congregation whose deep 
roots and lasting legacy reflect decades of faith, service, and 
devotion within the Baton Rouge community.
  Immaculate Conception Parish began as a mission of St. Francis Xavier 
Parish in Baton Rouge, where the Josephite Fathers expanded their 
evangelization efforts into the Scotlandville community. Mass was first 
celebrated in Scotlandville in October 1946 at the Cook movie house. 
Archbishop Rummel later purchased land for a permanent church, which 
was built by parishioners of St. Francis Xavier Parish and dedicated in 
1953.
  The Scotlandville mission grew steadily and was officially elevated 
to the status of an independent parish on June 21, 1958, with Father P. 
Francis T. Carney, S.S.J., serving as its first pastor. From its 
earliest years, the parish became a place of worship, spiritual 
fellowship, and community strength for Baton Rouge's African American 
Catholic community.
  Throughout its history, Immaculate Conception Parish has continued to 
build ministries centered on worship, youth religious education, 
service, and fellowship. Its facilities include a church, rectory, 
activity center, and a parish ministry building that houses a food 
pantry--demonstrating the parish's sustained commitment to addressing 
community needs.
  The Josephite Fathers served Immaculate Conception Parish from its 
founding until 1983, when the Jesuits assumed pastoral responsibility. 
Under both orders, the parish has continued its long tradition of 
evangelization, community service, and spiritual leadership.
  Today, Immaculate Conception Parish remains a steadfast presence in 
Scotlandville--an enduring symbol of faith, service, and hope. The 
parish continues to uplift families, strengthen the community, and 
honor its historic legacy as a place of worship and outreach.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in recognizing Immaculate 
Conception Parish for its remarkable contributions to Baton Rouge, and 
for its unwavering dedication to faith, fellowship, and community 
service throughout its storied history.
```
