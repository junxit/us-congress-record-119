# HONORING GORDON-FELTUS-LAZARD CATHEDRAL CHURCH OF GOD IN CHRIST

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
    HONORING GORDON-FELTUS-LAZARD CATHEDRAL CHURCH OF GOD IN CHRIST

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, September 10, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to acknowledge Gordon-Feltus-
Lazard Cathedral Church of God in Christ, located at 8930 Plank Rd. in 
Baton Rouge, Louisiana 70811, East Baton Rouge Parish. Established in 
the summer of 1914 by the late Bishop Seaners Lazard, the church has 
been a beacon of faith and perseverance for more than a century. Bishop 
Lazard, a young unlicensed minister at the time, embraced the doctrine 
of holiness and sanctification preached by the growing African American 
Pentecostal denomination known as the Church of God in Christ. He was 
divinely inspired to organize the first Church of God in Christ in 
Baton Rouge, originally named Railroad Ave. Church of God in Christ and 
located at 1450 Railroad Avenue.
  Recognizing his need for maturity and experience, Bishop Lazard 
sought guidance from Bishop Charles Harrison Mason, Founder and Senior 
Bishop of the Church of God in Christ, Inc. Bishop Mason appointed 
Bishop Henry Feltus of Centerville, Mississippi, as the first Pastor of 
the Railroad Avenue Church of God in Christ, widely known as First 
Church of God in Christ of Baton Rouge. Bishop Feltus also served as 
the first Overseer of the Church of God in Christ in Louisiana. After 
Bishop Feltus' passing, his brother James Feltus was appointed Pastor 
and Overseer, serving faithfully from 1922 to 1946.
  Upon the passing of Bishop James Feltus, Minister Senears Lazard, now 
ordained and experienced, received the pastoral appointment and the 
Bishopric. Bishop Mason divided the state, appointing Bishop Lazard as 
Bishop of Eastern Louisiana, overseeing all congregations east of the 
Mississippi River. Under his leadership, the original church was 
replaced with a new brick building and renamed Lazard Temple Church of 
God in Christ. In 1967, following Bishop Lazard's death, Bishop Willie 
K. Gordon took over as pastor and Bishop of Eastern Louisiana, leading 
the congregation at 1450 Myrtle Street. He remodeled the church and 
renamed it Feltus-Lazard Memorial State Temple.
  In 1980, Bishop W.K. Gordon appointed his son, Elder James Earl 
Gordon, as the next Pastor. Under Elder Gordon's leadership, the church 
flourished, providing spiritual empowerment for South Baton Rouge and 
serving as the headquarters for churches of Eastern Louisiana. In 1987, 
Elder James Earl Gordon was consecrated as Bishop of the First 
Ecclesiastical Jurisdiction of Louisiana East. In 2004, the church 
relocated to North Baton Rouge after acquiring 30 acres at 8930 Plank 
Road, naming the campus Saintsville--the headquarters for Louisiana 
East and the home of Gordon-Feltus-Lazard Cathedral Church of God in 
Christ. Bishop Gordon served as Pastor for 44 years and Prelate for 27 
years.
  In 2015, Elder Kenneth G. McKnight was appointed as the current 
Pastor. Under his administration, the church has become a spiritual hub 
for North Baton Rouge, providing resources to citizens in the capital 
area and surrounding parishes. Today, Gordon-Feltus-Lazard Cathedral 
COGIC stands as a thriving instrument of the gospel, reaching the lost 
and despondent, supporting its parishioners, and partnering for social, 
political, and economic justice. With 112 years of ministry, the 
church's legacy continues to serve as a lasting testament to the Church 
of God in Christ's impact on Baton Rouge and beyond.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring Gordon-
Feltus-Lazard Cathedral Church of God in Christ as we celebrate more 
than a century of faithful service, and in wishing its pastor and 
congregation continued blessings for generations to come.
```
