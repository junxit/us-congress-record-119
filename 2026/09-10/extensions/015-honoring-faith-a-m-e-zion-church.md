# HONORING FAITH A.M.E. ZION CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                   HONORING FAITH A.M.E. ZION CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, September 10, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Faith A.M.E. Zion 
Church, a congregation whose history reflects vision, perseverance, and 
an unwavering commitment to spreading the Gospel in Baton Rouge, 
Louisiana.
  The origins of Faith A.M.E. Zion Church date to July 1995, when God 
inspired a new vision for a sanctuary in Louisiana's capital city--one 
that would usher in a renaissance within the A.M.E. Zion Church and 
reinvigorate the Louisiana Conference. During that year's Annual 
Conference, convened at Petty A.M.E. Zion Church in New Orleans, 
Presiding Bishop Joseph Johnson appointed Rev. Kenneth D. York to 
establish a new church in Baton Rouge. At that meeting, Bishop Marshall 
H. Strickland proposed the name that the congregation proudly holds 
today: Faith A.M.E. Zion Church.
  Rev. York, together with a small group of devoted believers, began 
meeting regularly for prayer and Bible Study to lay the foundation for 
this new ministry. On January 26, 1996, a pivotal meeting was held at 
the home of Ms. Abigail Richardson--now Mrs. Abigail York--to formally 
organize the church. Those present included Rev. York, Rev. Dr. Francis 
Williams, Ms. Richardson, Mrs. Imogene Williams, Mrs. Queen V. York, 
Mrs. Dorothy D. Addison, Mrs. Willie Mae Harrison, Ms. Charlene 
Harrison, Attorney Cassandra Simms, Mr. Dennis Braziel, and Mr. Eric 
Christian. A Board of Trustees was elected, Ms. Richardson became 
church secretary, and the first list of prospective members was 
submitted.
  On January 29, 1996, Rev. York presented to Bishop Johnson and the 
Louisiana Conference a detailed plan of action--including minutes, 
membership information, and financial and property acquisition 
assessments--and received full approval for the establishment of the 
new church.
  As Faith A.M.E. Zion Church continued its early development, prayer 
meetings and Bible Study were held at the home of Mrs. Abigail York. 
The Trustee Board was revised to meet the growing administrative needs 
of the local church. Attorney Stephone K. Addison became Chairman, 
Attorney Cassandra Simms Treasurer, and Rev. York and Mr. Derrick 
Vernon rounded out the board.
  To strengthen the church's foundation, Bishop Johnson requested a 
special worship

[[Page E892]]

service to raise support. Rev. York, Rev. Dr. Francis Williams, and 
Presiding Elders Rev. Willie G. Johnson and Rev. W.M. Bryson organized 
the service, and generous support arrived from across Zion--including 
the A.M.E. Zion Home Missions Department, the Women's Home and Overseas 
Missionary Society, Seeds of Faith offerings, Louisiana Conference 
churches, and family and friends. Their contributions nearly overnight 
cleared the path toward securing a physical church property.
  Following extensive discussions and prayer, the Trustee Board 
authorized Rev. York and Attorney Addison to purchase a historic church 
building located at 2364 Thomas Delpit Drive in South Baton Rouge. The 
purchase was completed on July 31, 1996. The building was renovated, 
incorporated as Faith A.M.E. Zion Church, Inc., and dedicatory services 
were led by Bishop Nathaniel Jarrett.
  Faith A.M.E. Zion Church soon blossomed as the first and only A.M.E. 
Zion congregation in Baton Rouge. Its ministries expanded through the 
faithful leadership of Rev. York, the dedication of members, and the 
talents of Mr. Reginald Stewman, the church's first full-time musician.
  Under Bishop Louis Hunter, Sr., the Faith congregation embraced a new 
vision--to find a larger house of worship to meet the needs of a 
growing membership. That vision became reality on February 1, 2010, 
with the acquisition of a 9,000-square-foot church building located at 
3919 Groom Road in Baker, Louisiana. Renovations occurred in phases, 
and despite construction still underway, the congregation entered its 
new church home on February 6, 2011, with Bishop Darryl B. Starnes, 
Sr., officiating the dedication.
  Faith continued to grow, hosting the 152nd Session of the Louisiana 
Annual Conference in 2016. Soon afterward, the 2016 Baton Rouge flood 
devastated the church building, bringing more than 6 feet of water 
through the sanctuary. The congregation united to rebuild, receiving 
support from across Zion and worshiping at Ebenezer Baptist Church 
until renovations were complete.
  During the COVID-19 pandemic, Faith adapted again--moving worship, 
Bible study, and meetings online from March 2020 through April 2021. 
Easter Sunday of 2021 marked its joyful return to in-person worship. 
Faith now worships both in-person and virtually.
  Faith's ministry continues to grow. In June 2025, the church fully 
paid off the mortgage on its Groom Road property. On January 25, 2026, 
Faith celebrated its 30th Church Anniversary and the burning of the 
mortgage in a service officiated by Bishop Anthony N. Witherspoon.
  Rev. York's leadership has nurtured many associate ministers over the 
years, including Rev. Charles Evans, Sr., Rev. Mark Green, Rev. 
Michelle L. Madison, Rev. Walter Harrison, Rev. Charles Magee, Minister 
Reginald Stewman, and the late Rev. Dr. William Simon. Today, the 
ministerial staff includes Rev. Patricia Johnson, Father Daniel 
Hopkins, Rev. R. Sheree Miller, Rev. Sonya Felders, and Sister 
Perphyria Kador.
  Mr. Speaker, Faith A.M.E. Zion Church proudly proclaims its identity 
as `A Radical Church for A Radical World.' Its family remains committed 
to expanding its ministry while keeping steadfast focus on carrying out 
God's mission--to spread the Gospel, make disciples, and provide love, 
knowledge, inspiration, and refuge to all who are in need.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring Faith A.M.E. 
Zion Church for its three decades of worship, service, sacrifice, 
resilience, and visionary leadership.
```
