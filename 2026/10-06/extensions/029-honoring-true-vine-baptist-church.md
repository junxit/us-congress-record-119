# HONORING TRUE VINE BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                   HONORING TRUE VINE BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                        Tuesday, October 6, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor True Vine Baptist 
Church of Alexandria, Louisiana, a congregation whose origins date back 
to August 1900 and whose long history of service has made it a 
cornerstone of the Alexandria faith community. After fervent prayer 
meetings in the old Peabody School frame building, a dedicated group of 
believers sought the guidance of Dr. C.L. Roberts of Cheneyville, who 
organized True Vine that same year. The church later moved to Duck 
Alley, where members constructed a small frame building for services. 
When Dr. Roberts was unable to continue leading because of the 
hardships of travel, he advised the members to seek a new pastor, and 
records reflect early pastoral leadership from Rev. Solomon Washington 
and Rev. Cephus Hayes.
  In 1910, Rev. John S. Smith became pastor of approximately 20 
members, and under his leadership True Vine nourished. He organized 
numerous auxiliaries, including the Sunday School, Women's Home Mission 
Society, Junior Mission, Y.W.A., Girl's Auxiliary, Sunshine Band, 
B.Y.P.U., and Royal Ambassadors, expanded the church facility, and 
purchased additional property on Carr Street. Membership grew from 20 
to 854 before his passing on March 15, 1940. On August 12, 1940, Rev. 
J.H. Hayes was elected pastor and led significant improvements, 
including adding a balcony, installing fans, building a library, and 
constructing an educational building. During his tenure, the church 
purchased three lots for a new site on Broadway Avenue, where a church 
was completed in 1954. Rev. Hayes passed away on October 26, 1960.
  Rev. Howard L. Williams was selected as pastor on January 18, 1961. 
His administration oversaw extensive renovations, including the 
annexation of buildings, new offices and rooms, paneling, tile 
flooring, telephones, and expanded facilities such as Hayes Dining 
Hall. He also organized the Truevineer Choir, the Young Adult Choir, 
and the Matrons. Rev. Williams served faithfully until his passing in

[[Page E1040]]

1977, leaving a membership of 972. Rev. Frank B. Whorton followed 
beginning in 1978, and Rev. Solomon Shorter, Sr., was installed on May 
14, 1982.
  Under Rev. Shorter's dynamic leadership, True Vine expanded its 
outreach dramatically. The church fed the hungry, supported charities, 
assisted the sick and needy, launched after-school programs, and 
distributed 5,000 pounds of food each month. It established the Student 
Referral Center through a $125,000 grant from the Louisiana Department 
of Education, acquired 100 computers, purchased new property, and 
supported evacuees during Hurricanes Katrina and Rita. The church 
continued to grow under Rev. Shorter's leadership until his passing in 
2022. During his tenure, it carried out a Debt Reduction Program, 
accumulated significant building funds, worshiped temporarily at 
Peabody Magnet High School, and in 2009 marched joyfully into its new 
19,500-square-foot sanctuary at 831 Broadway Avenue.
  In 2019, Rev. Wilfred Gallien, Jr., became Interim Pastor and guided 
the church through the COVID-19 pandemic, and he was installed as 
pastor on August 20, 2023. Under Pastor Gallien's leadership, True Vine 
has welcomed new trustees, deacons, and deaconesses, revised its 
bylaws, and made history by welcoming Rev. Loraine Holmes as the first 
woman to preach from its pulpit. The church has also renovated its Food 
Pantry with grant funding and established new ministries, including the 
Welcome Committee and the Outreach Ministry.
  The thread running through True Vine's 126-year history is service. 
From the early auxiliaries organized to serve women, girls, and young 
people, to the feeding of the hungry, the after-school programs, the 
Student Referral Center, the monthly distribution of food, and the 
support offered to families displaced by hurricanes, True Vine has 
shown, generation after generation, that faith is lived out in care for 
one's neighbors. Its Food Pantry, renovated and expanded under Pastor 
Gallien, carries that tradition forward today.
  The impact of True Vine extends well beyond its own membership. As 
one of the longstanding congregations of Alexandria, it contributes to 
the strength of the broader faith community, standing alongside other 
churches in the shared work of nurturing faith, supporting families, 
and serving those in need, and its support for families in times of 
crisis has made it a trusted partner for the community as a whole. In 
striving to be ``Alexandria's Community Church,'' True Vine has 
embraced a role that reaches far beyond Sunday worship.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to join me in recognizing True Vine 
Baptist Church, Pastor Wilfred Gallien, Jr., and the dedicated 
congregation for more than 125 years of faith and service. True Vine 
remains committed to its mission to Love, Serve, and Teach, building on 
its powerful foundation and striving to fulfill its vision of being 
``Alexandria's Community Church.'' May it continue to flourish for 
generations to come.
```
