# RECOGNIZING CAPTAIN STEPHEN M. YARGOSZ ON COMPLETION OF HIS COMMAND AT NAVAL STATION GREAT LAKES

**Speaking:** Schneider, Bradley Scott (S001190) [D-IL]

```
RECOGNIZING CAPTAIN STEPHEN M. YARGOSZ ON COMPLETION OF HIS COMMAND AT 
                       NAVAL STATION GREAT LAKES

                                 ______
                                 

                      HON. BRADLEY SCOTT SCHNEIDER

                              of illinois

                    in the house of representatives

                        Tuesday, October 6, 2026

  Mr. SCHNEIDER. Mr. Speaker, I am proud to rise today to recognize and 
honor Captain Stephen M. Yargosz as he concludes his tenure as 
Commanding Officer of Naval Station Great Lakes and prepares to retire, 
bringing a distinguished 27-year career in the United States Navy to a 
close.
  Naval Station Great Lakes, known as the Quarterdeck of the Navy, is 
the largest military installation in Illinois and home to the Navy's 
only enlisted basic training facility. Each year, tens of thousands of 
recruits are transformed into sailors as they begin their service to 
our nation. In addition, Naval Station Great Lakes hosts more than 50 
tenant commands and elements, with more than 20,000 people (active-duty 
personnel and civilian employees) working on the base.
  Born in North Webster, Indiana, Captain Yargosz graduated from Purdue 
University in 1998 and was commissioned as an ensign on September 24, 
1999. Initially assigned to an aviation maintenance unit, he later 
attended flight school, became a helicopter pilot, and rose to oversee 
aviation squadrons. After attending the Marine Command College, he 
served as executive officer in the Office of National Drug Control 
Policy, advising the President on strategies to confront illicit drugs.
  In addition to his leadership of Naval Station Great Lakes, Captain 
Yargosz made community engagement a hallmark of his tenure. He 
consistently prioritized building trust and expanding collaboration 
with local partners and community members.
  He relaunched the First Friday program that invited residents onto 
the base, building transparency, trust, and understanding across North 
Chicago and Lake County. He was instrumental in advancing solutions for 
Halsey Village--a dilapidated naval housing neighborhood long 
considered a blight on the community. And when the Department of 
Defense committed funding for the new Forrestal Elementary School, 
Captain Yargosz treated the project as a shared investment with the 
people of North Chicago, serving both Navy families and the broader 
community.
  Captain Yargosz has also been a great partner in my office's efforts 
on behalf of veterans across the region. He regularly joined our annual 
Veterans Day Breakfast. He joined me at several Vietnam Veteran Lapel 
Pinning Ceremonies, helping give long-overdue recognition to those who 
served during that era. Significantly, he was an anchor member of my 
annual Military Academy Nomination selection committee.
  Captain Stephen M. Yargosz leaves behind a wonderful legacy of 
leadership, community partnership, and deep devotion to the sailors, 
families, and neighbors of Naval Station Great Lakes. As he and his 
wife Amy start a new chapter in California, I extend my great 
appreciation to his service and his friendship. I have no doubt his 
impact will continue to shape this installation and the surrounding 
region for years to come.
```
