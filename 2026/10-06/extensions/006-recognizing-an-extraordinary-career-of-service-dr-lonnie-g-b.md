# RECOGNIZING AN EXTRAORDINARY CAREER OF SERVICE: DR. LONNIE G. BUNCH, III

**Speaking:** Mfume, Kweisi (M000687) [D-MD]

```
 RECOGNIZING AN EXTRAORDINARY CAREER OF SERVICE: DR. LONNIE G. BUNCH, 
                                  III

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. KWEISI MFUME

                              of maryland

                    in the house of representatives

                        Tuesday, October 6, 2026

  Mr. MFUME. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize the extraordinary 
service of Dr. Lonnie G. Bunch, III, the 14th Secretary of the 
Smithsonian Institution, as he prepares to conclude his service to one 
of our Nation's most precious cultural institutions.
  Dr. Bunch's legacy is inseparable from the National Museum of African 
American History and Culture. As its founding director, Dr. Bunch 
helped to transform a century-old dream into an American institution on 
the National Mall.
  The museum changed the landscape of Washington, but, more 
importantly, it changed how current and future generations understand 
our Nation and themselves.
  African American history is not a sidebar to American history--it is 
American history.
  As the first African American to serve as Secretary of the 
Smithsonian, Dr. Bunch broke an important barrier while continuing his 
lifelong commitment to scholarship, truth, and historical preservation. 
That commitment carries particular significance today.
  This year, the Trump Administration has continued efforts to reshape 
the presentation of American history at the Smithsonian and other 
federal cultural institutions.
  In July, and again this month, Congressman Jonathan Jackson and I 
stood outside the National Museum of African American History and 
Culture with faith and community leaders to reject any and all efforts 
to erase, distort, or diminish that history.
  Among those joining us were Bishop William J. Barber, II and Reverend 
Dr. Robert R.A. Turner of Empowerment Temple AME Church in Baltimore. 
Their presence reinforced an important truth: preserving history is not 
solely the responsibility of historians. It requires citizens, clergy, 
educators, lawmakers, students, and communities prepared to defend the 
integrity of our national story.
  As I stated during a House Oversight hearing in July regarding the 
history of Black people in America: ``If you erase it, we will replace 
it.'' That is more than a slogan. It is a commitment.
  That commitment is also reflected in my legislative work.
  Earlier this year. I introduced the African American History Act of 
2026, with Senator Cory Booker leading companion legislation in the 
Senate, and 92 original House co-sponsors. The bill would authorize $20 
million for the National Museum of African American History and Culture 
to expand African American history education for students, parents, and 
educators, and would strengthen the development and dissemination of 
historically accurate educational resources.
  Similarly, I also introduced the National Council on African American 
History and Culture Act of 2026, which would establish a national 
council within the National Endowment for the Humanities to preserve, 
promote, and strengthen the study of African American history and 
culture.
  These efforts rest on a simple principle: preserving history requires 
more than defending what already exists. It requires building the 
institutions, resources, and public understanding necessary to carry 
that history forward.
  Secretary Bunch has embodied this principle throughout his long and 
storied career. He understands that confronting the difficult chapters 
of American history does not weaken this Nation. It strengthens us, 
allowing future generations to see clearly both our failures and our 
progress.
  I salute Dr. Bunch for a fight well fought. Scholarship is more 
important than Presidential gamesmanship. Truth is stronger than 
political pressure. And history, faithfully preserved, will outlive 
those who attempt to rewrite it.

[[Page E1033]]

  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in thanking Dr. Lonnie G. 
Bunch, III for his extraordinary service to the Smithsonian 
Institution, the National Museum of African American History and 
Culture, and, most of all, the people of the United States.
  His service has enriched our Nation, deepened our understanding of 
ourselves, and helped to ensure that the full American story will be 
preserved for generations to come.
```
