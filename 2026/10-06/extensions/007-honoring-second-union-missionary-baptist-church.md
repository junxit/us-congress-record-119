# HONORING SECOND UNION MISSIONARY BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
            HONORING SECOND UNION MISSIONARY BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                        Tuesday, October 6, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Second Union 
Missionary Baptist Church, located at 6436 Old Baton Rouge Highway in 
Alexandria, Louisiana, on the historic Inglewood Plantation, a 
congregation that has traveled more than 130 years by faith. The church 
was founded and organized on March 12, 1894, under the leadership of 
Rev. George Washington. Rev. Matt Bryar served as its second pastor 
until early 1903, and Rev. Mack Williams became the third pastor in 
1903. During Rev. Mack Williams' illness, Rev. Isaac Williams, who had 
been baptized at the church and later ordained, carried on the 
services. Under the leadership of both men, the church held Sunday 
school and Baptist Youth Planning Unit services and purchased one-third 
of an acre of property.
  Rev. Lewis Jones became pastor in 1923 and served until his death on 
May 7, 1929, and Rev. J.S. Robinson served from July 1929 until 1935, 
during which time the church purchased an additional half acre of 
property. Rev. Dave Williams served faithfully from 1935 until 1938. 
That year, Dr. G.C. Jacobs was elected pastor, and under his leadership 
and guidance the church prospered in many ways, including through the 
time and patience it took to form the many auxiliaries and 
organizations in which the congregation takes pride today. The church 
building was torn down by Hurricane Audrey and later rebuilt, with 
classrooms and air conditioning added in later years. Dr. Jacobs 
resigned in January 1976 because of illness after serving as pastor for 
38 years, and Rev. Albert Robinson, an associate minister, carried on 
services until a pastor was elected.
  In April 1976, Rev. Wilbert Wilson, Jr., was elected pastor, and 
under his leadership the church prospered tremendously. He organized 
the Welfare Fund, through which the church helped many people in need, 
and the church supported charitable organizations including the 
Wolverine Foundation, the Sickle Cell Anemia Foundation, and the Doll 
and Toy Fund. The Youth Department was organized and membership 
increased greatly. In 1979, five deacons were ordained: Curtis 
Robinson, Sr., Johnnie Elie, Wesley Anderson, Charlie Gilmore, and 
Joseph Hudson, Jr., Deacon Gilmore was later called into the ministry 
and appointed as Youth Minister. In 1985, the church built a new 
cafeteria and fellowship hall, dedicated in the names of the late Rev. 
G.C. Jacobs and Deacon Dan Taylor. Rev. Wilson remained pastor until 
August 1985.
  On December 19, 1985, Rev. Theodore Simon was elected pastor, and he 
was installed on January 26, 1986, by the United Educational Missionary 
Baptist Association under the leadership of the late Dr. R.B. Bennett. 
During his tenure, the church held night services and Baptist Training 
Union classes on first and third Sunday nights, and ministers Charles 
Dearbone and Charles Simon were appointed to lead Wednesday night Bible 
study. Significant improvements were made to the church and fellowship 
hall in 1996 and 1997, and the congregation gathered for its first 
Watch Care Service to welcome the year 2000. Pastor Simon retired in 
June 2001 because of illness, preaching his last sermon on the fourth 
Sunday in June. During his leadership, the congregation was fed with 
the Word of God, and he encouraged the church to ``keep on keeping 
on.''
  On October 14, 2001, Rev. Joseph Martin, Jr., was elected pastor, and 
with his vision many updates were made to the church. He continues to 
serve today as Senior Pastor, supported by associate pastors Rev. 
Robert Phillips and Rev. Reginal Babin. In 2002, the congregation 
undertook a major restoration of the Inglewood Cemetery, including the 
purchase of crosses and a flagpole, and a dedication service was held 
on May 25, 2003, at which a bronze memorial donated by Mrs. Polly 
Keller Winters was unveiled. In 2005, Mrs. Patricia Fowler donated an 
additional 0.51 acres of land, appraised at $10,000, to the church.
  On Saturday, August 20, 2011, the church held a groundbreaking 
ceremony for its new sanctuary, a historic event attended by ministers, 
councilmembers, and other elected officials. The new sanctuary was 
completed in November 2012, and a ribbon-cutting ceremony was held on 
December 9, 2012, assisted by the Central Louisiana Chamber of 
Commerce. A passing of the torch ceremony took place that day, with the 
torch passed from an older member of the church down to its youngest, 
and the sanctuary was formally dedicated on December 16, 2012. The 
church lives by a creed built around five purposes: Worship, Reach, 
Teach, Grow, and Serve, ministering to the needs of the community. In 
the years that followed, the church established its website in 2015, 
laid the foundation for a parking lot, dedicated the Fowler and Davis 
Conference Room, and renamed the old sanctuary the Simon and Martin 
Family Center in 2017.
  In 2018, the church held an ordination service on September 16 for 
eight deacons: Roosevelt M. Anderson, Sr., Jerrell Antwine, Sr., James 
D. Hickman, Kevin U. Johnson, Gary W. Phillips, Sr., Tommy Robinson, 
Jr., Anthony Sewell, Sr., and James Woods, Sr. That same year, 
Inglewood Grace Community Outreach Ministries, Inc., the church's 
outreach ministry, accepted the donation of 0.312 acres of property 
from Moreland Farms, LLC.
  Through all of these years, the congregation has remembered the 
members who laid its early foundation, celebrated the present, and 
anticipated the future with great faith.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in celebrating Second 
Union Missionary Baptist Church, Senior Pastor Joseph Martin, Jr., and 
its dedicated congregation for more than 130 years of faith, 
resilience, and service to the Alexandria community. May Second Union 
continue to keep on keeping on for generations to come.
```
