# HONORING ROSE OF SHARON BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                 HONORING ROSE OF SHARON BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                        Tuesday, October 6, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Rose of Sharon Baptist 
Church of Alexandria, Louisiana, a congregation that has been dedicated 
to sharing the Word of God for more than a century and has long been an 
important part of the faith community of Alexandria. The church traces 
its origins to March 3, 1908, when about 65 people gathered for worship 
in Butler Hall on Lee Street in Alexandria. The group was so impressed 
by a young minister from Avoyelles Parish, Rev. James Wesley White, 
that on April 26, 1908, they invited him to preach, and on May 14, 
1908, they formally appointed him as their pastor, establishing the 
church with seven deacons, seven deaconesses, and nine trustees.
  In 1908, the congregation purchased a lot and building at the corner 
of 10th and St. James Streets, but the building was destroyed by fire 
in 1911. The church quickly rebuilt, and under Rev. J.W. White's 
leadership it grew to about 400 members and expanded into auxiliaries 
such as choirs, mission societies, youth groups, and educational 
boards, each deepening the spiritual life of the congregation and its 
service to the community.
  On March 20, 1947, following the passing of Rev. J.W. White, the 
church elected his son, Rev. Willie Augustus White, as pastor. Under 
his guidance, the church underwent major renovations, established more 
auxiliaries, and deepened its involvement in the Eighth District 
Missionary Baptist Association, the Louisiana Missionary Baptist State 
Convention, and the National Baptist Convention, USA.
  In 1959, a building committee planned a new church. Groundbreaking 
took place on July 12, 1959, and the current building was dedicated on 
June 12, 1960, at a cost of $175,000. The mortgage was paid off in 
1966. The church acquired additional property for parking, and its 
grounds came to extend from St. James Street to Winn Street. The later 
arrival of Interstate 49 transformed the surrounding area, but the 
church continued to grow in line with its vision.
  The story of Rose of Sharon is also a story of resilience. A 
congregation that rebuilt quickly after a devastating fire just 3 years 
after its founding, that dedicated a new sanctuary in 1960 and paid off 
its mortgage within six years, and that continued to grow even as 
Interstate 49 reshaped the neighborhood

[[Page E1038]]

around it, has shown the strength of a community that holds together 
through change. For generations, its choirs, mission societies, youth 
groups, and educational boards have given families a place to worship, 
learn, serve, and belong, and have helped shape the spiritual lives of 
countless Alexandria residents.
  The church's reach has extended beyond its own walls. Through its 
involvement in the Eighth District Missionary Baptist Association, the 
Louisiana Missionary Baptist State Convention, and the National Baptist 
Convention, USA, Rose of Sharon has stood alongside other congregations 
in the shared work of strengthening Baptist churches and carrying out 
the mission of the church at the district, state, and national levels. 
In this way, it has contributed to the strength and unity of the 
broader faith community of Alexandria and beyond.
  Today, Rose of Sharon Baptist Church, led by Rev. Carl Owens, remains 
a Baptist congregation dedicated to sharing the Word of God, 
evangelism, and holistic ministry through spiritual, educational, and 
recreational programs. Its history reflects a steadfast commitment to 
community service, growth, and enduring faith, and its spiritual, 
educational, and recreational programs continue to serve the needs of 
its members and neighbors alike.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in celebrating Rose of 
Sharon Baptist Church, Rev. Carl Owens, and the dedicated congregation 
for more than a century of faith and service to the Alexandria 
community and its faith community. May Rose of Sharon continue to be a 
source of strength, encouragement, and hope in Alexandria for 
generations to come.
```
