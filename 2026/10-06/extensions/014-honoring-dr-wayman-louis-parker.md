# HONORING DR. WAYMAN LOUIS PARKER

**Speaking:** Moore, Gwen (M001160) [D-WI]

```
                    HONORING DR. WAYMAN LOUIS PARKER

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. GWEN MOORE

                              of wisconsin

                    in the house of representatives

                        Tuesday, October 6, 2026

  Ms. MOORE of Wisconsin. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Dr. Wayman 
Louis Parker who recently passed away after a very well lived life. He 
was taken too soon but will not soon be forgotten. Nor will his impact 
in Milwaukee and everywhere else where his feet touched the ground and 
where he touched the hearts of so many.
  From the age of nine, Dr. Parker dreamed of becoming a doctor. He 
more than dreamed. And while there would be many valleys and obstacles 
on that road, he did not waiver nor did he dither.
  In 1966, while going to Wayne State University, he was drafted and 
found himself a combat soldier in Vietnam. Days after returning home, 
he was introduced to the woman, Emily, who would become his wife.
  After graduating from Wayne State, he was admitted to Michigan State 
University's College of Human Medicine. He graduated in 1974 and 
completed his residency in ob-gyn in 1978. Dr. Parker then moved to 
Milwaukee with his family, where he worked with Dr. William Finlayson, 
another groundbreaking ob-gyn doctor who served Milwaukee.
  He would remain in our great city for over 50 years, serving and 
impacting so many in my community, including my own family.
  In a state and country where high black maternal and infant death 
rates are alarmingly high, he provided the types of compassionate and 
high-quality care that can mean the difference between life and death. 
That too many black women often fail to receive.
  He delivered all three of my granddaughters. One of my grandbabies 
was born one pound and two ounces. That was such a difficult and trying 
time for our family, but Dr. Parker was such a rock. Now, she is 
thirty-three years old, wife, mother of two, with a Bachelor of Science 
degree and a career. She is one of his legacies. He has so many 
legacies that represent how much he meant to the broader community.
  He helped cofound the Black Health Coalition of Wisconsin--a group of 
local organizations and individuals working to address the health 
problems of African Americans--in 1988 with Dr. Patricia McManus and 
Claude Gilmore. He helped operate a free clinic, The C.H. Mason Clinic, 
on the campus of Holy Redeemer Institutional Church of God in Christ.
  Medicine was his profession but Dr. Parker's passion was caring for 
his patients. And during his time in Milwaukee and on this planet that 
the Good Lord blessed us with his presence, he did just about 
everything he could, worked with as many partners as he could, to 
advance that goal.
  My heartfelt condolences go out to his family.
  I sent my prayers to his wife, Emma, daughter, Cortney Washington, 
Son-in-law, Eric Washington, grandchildren Kyle, Jeffrey, Gabrielle, 
and Gabriele, great-grandchildren and a host of nephews, nieces, great-
nephews, great-nieces, extended family, and cherished friends who 
continue to honor his memory. He was preceded in death by his son, 
Jeffrey.
  Thank you to the family for sharing this gem with the rest of us.

                          ____________________
```
