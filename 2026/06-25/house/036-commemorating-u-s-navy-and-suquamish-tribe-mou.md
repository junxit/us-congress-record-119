# COMMEMORATING U.S. NAVY AND SUQUAMISH TRIBE MOU

**Speaking:** Randall, Emily (R000621) [D-WA]

```
            COMMEMORATING U.S. NAVY AND SUQUAMISH TRIBE MOU

  (Ms. RANDALL asked and was given permission to address the House for 
1 minute and to revise and extend her remarks.)
  Ms. RANDALL. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize the tremendous 
occasion that is happening in my district this weekend.
  A memorandum of understanding, the first of its kind, between the 
Suquamish Tribe and the U.S. Navy has finally been reached after years 
of good faith negotiations. This MOU sets a new standard for how our 
U.S. military collaborates with Tribal Governments to respect Tribal 
sovereignty and to preserve treaty rights.
  The Suquamish Tribe has stewarded the lands and waters of the Salish 
Sea or the Puget Sound since time immemorial and has been a gracious 
neighbor to the Puget Sound Naval Shipyard and the U.S. Navy's 
Intermediate Maintenance Facility in Bremerton.
  The shared waterways include Tribal fishing grounds and shellfish 
beds, and this MOU will compensate the Tribal members for the access 
that will be impacted during the construction of the Navy's proposed 
Multi-Mission Dry Dock.
  The Shipyard Infrastructure Optimization Program and its M2D2 project 
are a once-in-a-century investment to modernize four aging naval 
shipyards into new, modern facilities that will serve our Nation into 
the future.
  In my district alone, there are 12 federally recognized Tribes that 
often neighbor our four DOD installations, and this serves as a model 
not only for Washington State but the Nation.
  I commend the Navy and the Suquamish Tribe for their years of 
leadership and perseverance in reaching this historic agreement.

                          ____________________
```
