# Department of Homeland Security (Executive Session)

**Speaking:** Schumer, Charles E. (S000148) [D-NY]

```
                    Department of Homeland Security

  Mr. SCHUMER. Mr. President, the wounds of this Nation will not heal 
until the terrible abuses of ICE are reined in. The deaths of Renee 
Good and Alex Pretti have shocked people from one end of America to the 
other. The image of Federal law enforcement gunning down and murdering 
U.S. citizens in broad daylight is abominable.
  Now, a few moments ago, Tom Homan announced that there will be a 
drawdown of 700 law enforcement officers in Minneapolis.
  Let's be very clear. Homan's announcement is not close to enough. 
Seven hundred officers is going to be like a drop in the bucket to the 
people of Minneapolis, as over 2,000 agents--representing 70 percent of 
agents--will still be on the streets, brutalizing citizens, abusing 
citizens, abusing people. It is not close to enough.
  They all have to leave now. That is what the people of Minneapolis 
want, and, frankly, that is what the American people want, having seen 
these images.
  Seventy percent of the problem is still stationed in Minneapolis. The 
chaos is going to continue. The danger that someone gets killed--God 
forbid--continues. All of ICE needs to leave Minneapolis now. They are 
not needed. Residents don't want them patrolling the streets like a 
military occupation.
  To say, if 70 percent represents it, that about three-quarters of 
Minneapolis will still be militarily occupied ain't close to good 
enough. It is not what the people of Minneapolis want. It is not what 
the American people want. A full withdrawal of ICE is the only step--
the only right step--for the safety of the citizens and to allow 
Minneapolis to go function again.
  I have heard from our Senators. People can't go to schools. Stores 
are closed. Life is disrupted, and it is disrupted as much by 2,000 
agents as by 2,700 agents.
  Tom Homan is saying that a small group of agents will leave sometime 
down the line, and that is meaningless.
  And let's be honest, Tom Homan is no hero. Removing Bovino and 
replacing him with Homan is like switching from somebody who is really, 
really, really bad to someone who is just really, really bad. That is 
cold comfort to the people of Minneapolis.
  Tom Homan was the architect and the chief progenitor of many of the 
awful policies we see in effect right now that are hurting residents in 
Minneapolis and in so many other places in America.
  Today's announcement only affirms that the real solution to ICE is 
never going to come from the executive branch policing itself. ICE's 
abuse is not a problem that can be solved by executive fiat--and 
particularly when it is from Donald Trump. We all know that tomorrow 
Donald Trump could put the 700 troops back, acting like he didn't even 
ask for them to be withdrawn. That is how Trump operates.
  We need legislation, not executive fiat subject to the whims of 
Donald Trump. With Donald Trump, it is easy come, easy go. So we need 
legislation.
  Look, most Americans--certainly I--stand firmly behind law 
enforcement. They have a tough job. They put themselves at risk every 
day to keep our communities safe. But what Americans don't like is a 
subset of Federal agents going off the rails and acting with zero 
restraint, zero accountability, and acting like a squad of roving 
thugs--goons.
  So Democrats have been clear about what Congress needs to do to end 
ICE's violence and ensure public safety. ICE needs to end roving 
patrols, follow basic standards of accountability, and end the secret 
police who are masked and unidentified.
  These are reasonable demands. Reasonable demands include masks coming 
off and cameras staying on. Real reasonable demands include 
accountability so that someone is looking over what is going on here 
and stopping it and limiting it. Reasonable demands mean no roving 
patrols where these ICE agents just, at whim, pick up people going to 
schools and churches.
  What we are asking is common sense and what the American people want, 
and so it is troubling and perplexing that some Republicans, led by 
Speaker Johnson, are right now siding with the secret police.
  Speaker Johnson says having ICE agents take off their masks would 
somehow create further danger. Does he know anything about law 
enforcement? When he goes home to Louisiana, do the sheriffs or police 
officers wear masks? No.
  In every jurisdiction in America, police officers identify 
themselves. It is a good way of dealing with the handful of abusive 
officers that exist. It is a good way to make sure people know what is 
happening.
  And Johnson says taking masks off is a danger? A greater danger than 
people getting shot or beaten to a pulp on the streets by unidentified 
secret police?
  Meanwhile, the senior Senator from Texas called the idea of no masks 
ridiculous. Senator Cornyn says taking masks off is ridiculous.

[[Page S477]]

  But I will tell you what is actually ridiculous: defending secret 
police after they murder or maim or brutalize people in our streets. It 
is ridiculous to look at all the images of chaos to see what is 
happening in Minneapolis and other cities, to see the tear gas and 
bodies lying lifeless, people hurt and beaten, thrown into dark prisons 
with no understanding of why they were picked up and put in these 
prisons. That is the danger.
  It is ridiculous to think that ICE, a force of untrained people, 
should get special treatment, while every other law enforcement agency 
in the country is asked to follow reasonable codes of conduct.
  When you get pulled over, the traffic cop doesn't wear a mask. People 
don't see sheriffs and their deputies running around wearing masks. 
Security guards and State troopers and even most FBI agents don't walk 
around wearing masks. But, all of a sudden, these ICE agents, who have 
one of the worst records of law enforcement we have seen in America in 
decades, should be allowed to wear masks.

  It is truly ridiculous for Republicans to argue that at this very 
delicate moment, when people fear excessive force from Federal agents, 
the right answer is to defend ICE's ability to wear masks and enter 
homes with zero guardrails.
  Now, we have just a few days until DHS funding runs out. Democrats 
have been clear on what needs to happen. Our proposals are designed to 
ensure public safety and instill the kind of guardrails that the vast 
majority of Americans support.
  Republicans need to realize that the single worst thing they can do 
right now, in the eyes of the Nation, is to double down on the status 
quo of ICE. They must negotiate. They must sit down and work out a real 
solution.
  The Nation is tired of the chaos ICE is unleashing on our streets and 
does not want Republicans in the Senate and House to sit on their hands 
and allow it to continue.
```
