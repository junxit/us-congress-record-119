# REMEMBERING LARRY MAZZOLA, SR.

**Speaking:** Padilla, Alex (P000145) [D-CA]

```
                     REMEMBERING LARRY MAZZOLA, SR.

● Mr. PADILLA. Madam President, I rise today to honor the life 
and legacy of Larry Mazzola, Sr., a towering figure in California's 
labor movement, a proud San Franciscan, and a relentless advocate for 
working people who passed away in early May at the age of 82.
  Larry leaves behind a powerful legacy that helped shape the Bay Area 
labor movement, as well as the lives of countless workers and families 
across California.
  For decades, Larry Mazzola, Sr., stood as one of San Francisco's most 
respected and formidable voices for organized labor, a legacy deeply 
rooted in family tradition.
  His father Joseph Mazzola led San Francisco's plumbers union for more 
than four decades, instilling in Larry a deep commitment to solidarity, 
dignity, and the belief that every worker deserves fair wages, safe 
working conditions, and the opportunity to provide for their family.
  Larry carried that mission forward throughout his life and career, 
and passed it on to his own son Larry Mazzola, Jr., who continues the 
family tradition as the head of U.A. Local 38 and president of the San 
Francisco Building and Construction Trades Council.
  A native son of San Francisco, Larry dedicated nearly 50 years of his 
life to U.A. Local 38 and the United Association, serving in wide-
ranging roles including assistant training director, organizer, 
business agent, and ultimately, 24 years as business manager and 
financial secretary-treasurer.
  Through his leadership, he helped guide generations of workers into 
the middle class and fought tirelessly to secure family-supporting 
wages, quality healthcare, strong retirement benefits, and safe 
workplaces for workers across the Bay Area.
  Larry's leadership helped establish some of the strongest labor 
standards in the Nation and ensured that thousands of workers could 
retire with dignity after a lifetime of hard work. His impact on the 
labor movement was so profound that U.A. Local 38's union hall is named 
in his honor.
  Larry also served the people of San Francisco with distinction as a 
member of the San Francisco Airport Commission for 26 years, including 
nearly 18 years as its president. Even in public office, Larry never 
lost sight of where he came from or whom he represented. He brought the 
same unwavering advocacy for working people into government service, 
protecting the rights, wages, and benefits of union workers at San 
Francisco International Airport and setting an example for labor 
representation in public institutions nationwide.
  In recognition of Larry's extraordinary contributions to the city, 
San Francisco declared a ``Larry Mazzola Sr. Day'' when he retired from 
the airport commission in 2022, a fitting honor for someone whose 
life's work left an indelible mark on the city he loved.
  Larry Mazzola, Sr., devoted his life to fighting for working people 
and ensuring that future generations would have greater opportunity and 
security than the generations before them. His impact can still be felt 
in the strength of organized labor throughout the Bay Area and in the 
countless workers and families whose lives were improved through his 
advocacy and leadership.
  Angela and I send our deepest condolences to the entire Mazzola 
family, to his brothers and sisters in labor, and to all those whose 
lives he touched through his decades of service and leadership.●
```
