      Recognizing The Pennsylvania State University Wrestling Team

  Mr. McCORMICK. Mr. President, Pennsylvania is wrestling country. The 
sport fits the character of our Commonwealth. From smalltown school 
gymnasiums to the biggest stage in college athletics, generations of 
Pennsylvanians have learned on the mat that nothing is given and 
everything is earned.
  Our proud tradition is embodied by a team that has once again set the 
standard for intercollegiate wrestling and is here with us in the 
Gallery today. So I rise to honor the 2026 NCAA national championship 
Penn State Nittany Lions and submit a resolution congratulating them on 
their fifth consecutive national championship and their 13th title in 
the last 15 NCAA championships. This recognizes the

[[Page S4420]]

dedication, the teamwork, and the perseverance that made this historic 
achievement possible.
  When I watch wrestling, I see the best of Pennsylvania and the best 
of America: hard work, courage, resilience, humility, and respect. In 
this remarkable group of young men, those virtues are on full display.
  I grew up wrestling across Pennsylvania--from Shikellamy to 
Shickshinny, from Selinsgrove to Schuylkill Haven--and years later, I 
had the privilege of serving as the cocaptain of the Army wrestling 
team at West Point. Last year, I sat in the front row in Philadelphia 
at the NCAA national championships, alongside President Trump, as Penn 
State defended its title.
  I have spent much of my life around wrestling mats, and what I 
learned there is simple: Wrestling reveals character. For 7 minutes, 
there is nowhere to hide. No teammate can take your place. No excuse 
can score a takedown. The work you did or didn't do when nobody was 
watching shows up under the lights. That is why wrestling is more than 
a sport. It teaches the virtues that have always sustained America, and 
no program--no program--in the country embodies those virtues more 
consistently than Penn State wrestling.
  This season, the Nittany Lions went 15 to 0. They completed their 
sixth straight undefeated season and extended dual-meet winning track 
record to 86. Then they went to the national championships in Cleveland 
and raised that standard again. The team scored 181.5 points, breaking 
the NCAA record that Penn State had set just the year before.
  This is not just a good weekend; this is sustained excellence. Six 
Nittany Lions reached the national finals, tying the school record. 
Luke Lilledahl, Josh Barr, Levi Haines, and Mitchell Mesenbrink 
returned as champions, and Mitchell delivered one of the tournament's 
most defining performances, winning in the finals by a technical fault 
and earning the second straight national title for him and the 
tournament's ``Outstanding Wrestler.''
  But the most compelling statistic is that every one of Penn State's 
10 NCAA qualifiers scored points toward the record total, and 8 became 
All-Americans. That is teamwork. That is excellence.
  There is so much discussion these days about how boys become good 
men, and Penn State wrestling has found a winning formula: Demand 
excellence, teach discipline and humility, and bind individual ambition 
to a purpose larger than oneself.
  That is why Coach Cael Sanderson's legacy is bigger than 13 national 
titles. He has built more than a championship program; he has built a 
culture that forms young men into the kinds of leaders our communities 
and our country needs.
  Cael often points to the Old Testament story of Nehemiah, who was 
rebuilding Jerusalem's wall when the enemies repeatedly tried to lure 
him away. Nehemiah's response was simple:

       I am doing a great work, so I cannot come down.

  Coach Cael and his fellow coaches and the Penn State wrestling team 
are doing great work. As America celebrates its 250th anniversary, we 
in this body are striving to do great work too: preserving the liberty 
we inherited, renewing the character that sustains it, and proving that 
a free people can accomplish hard things together. But to be a 
champion, as this group knows, each of us needs to renew that great 
work every single day. The Penn State wrestling team understands that, 
and America sometimes needs to be reminded of it.
  I ask my colleagues in this body to join me in this resolution 
recognizing what this remarkable team has accomplished.
  To the Penn State Nittany Lions, congratulations on another historic 
championship. Pennsylvania is proud of you. The Senate honors you.
  We are Penn State.

                           Election Integrity

  Madam President, No. 2, let me talk about election integrity.
  Let me tell you what I think of when I think of the SAVE Act. I think 
of integrity in American elections. Now, you don't have to be a senior 
at CalTech to know that the American people, no matter what your 
political philosophy is--Democrat, Republican, Independent; socialist, 
communist, whatever--you wonder about elections now. You wonder about 
the integrity.
  I mean, I don't know how widespread, if at all, this is, but in New 
Jersey, we just discovered that the entire population of Bolivia is 
registered to vote in New Jersey. That is a bit of an overstatement. 
But after saying there is no problem with our voter rolls, we discover 
stuff like this.
  And we just witnessed elections in California that take months to 
resolve. When it takes a month to declare a winner for the good people 
of California, what do you think they are going to think--particularly, 
the loser?
  Look, if America is going to survive, in terms of your party, you 
have got to be willing to lose and accept that you have lost. And in 
order to do that, you have to trust in the election.
  So when I think of the SAVE Act, I am thinking of election integrity. 
And, really, what the SAVE Act means to me is two things: It means you 
have to prove you are who you say you are in order to vote and in order 
to register to vote. It is very simple. Rule No. 1, you have to prove 
you are who you say you are in order to register to vote and to vote.
  And, No. 2, we need to go back to having an election day and not an 
election month. We need to know who the winner is on election night. 
And if it is a really close race, I can understand it bleeding over 
until the next day.
  Frankly, I don't care how you vote. That is up to the States. The 
President does not want voting by mail. He is entitled to his opinion. 
I just disagree with him.
  If the people of Alaska or the people of New York or the people of 
Florida want to vote by mail, that is up to them and their State 
legislatures. So within reason, I don't care how you vote. I just want 
to require those votes to be tallied by election night--on election 
night.
  Now, this is all really easy for me to say, but the States run 
elections, and they run Federal elections. And it is really easy for me

should take it up.
  I am on the Budget Committee. Senator Ron Johnson is our chair. He is

say you are in order to vote, and you have to get those votes counted

  The States are going to need resources, and that is the whole purpose 
of the so-called SAVE Act in the budget resolution. And we need to take

                   Unanimous Consent Request--S. 1306

  Mr. JOHNSON. Mr. President, my fine State of Wisconsin has a gray 
wolf population problem, but that was not always the case. Back in the 
eighties and nineties, we had the opposite problem. We had an 
endangered wolf population, as probably most of the lower 48 States 
did. So back in 1978, under the Endangered Species Act, the gray wolf 
was listed as one of those endangered species.
  The good news is, it worked. You can see from the chart here--that 
starts in 1980 to about the year 1999--our wolf population slowly 
started coming back, to the point that in 1999, the Wisconsin DNR 
agreed that once we hit 250 gray wolves in Wisconsin, that would be the 
point at which we could delist the gray wolf as no longer an endangered 
species.
  We didn't delist them. The population continued to grow and grow, and 
unfortunately, as much as we all value a healthy and sustainable gray 
wolf population, there is a point at which it gets too large and it 
starts threatening livestock, starts threatening people.
  I think the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service recognized that fact, and 
so in 2007, the gray wolf was actually delisted by the U.S. Fish and 
Wildlife Service. The problem is, in 2008, it was relisted by a Federal 
court here in DC. So in 2009, we took action once again. The U.S. Fish 
and Wildlife Service delisted the gray wolf. That same year, that same 
court relisted it. Three years later--again, looking at the science, 
looking at the growing population--in 2012, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife 
Service delisted it. Three years later, the same DC court relisted it. 
In 2021, again, you can start seeing the explosion, quite honestly, in 
population. Our Wisconsin DNA states that about 1,250 is really the 
most population of gray wolf that the State can sustain. We had already 
hit that level, so U.S. Fish and Wildlife delisted it again, only to be 
overturned in 2022 by a Federal court decision in California this time, 
not DC.
  Why is this a problem? We get report after report of families, 
farmers in Wisconsin who are bearing the burden of this back-and-forth, 
of the fact that the gray wolf is protected and the populations are 
really at the maximum level right now.
  It is threatening livestock. I mean, throughout Wisconsin, wolves are 
killing cows, horses, goats, sheep, cats, dogs. We have a sheep farm in 
Wisconsin, and a multigeneration herd of sheep was all wiped out except 
for one sheep, in one night. In Price County, a dairy farmer had to 
sell his entire herd because the wolves kept killing his cows. 
Wisconsinites have to carry firearms just to walk their dogs. A Wood 
County resident was forced to drive their children to school because 
the bus route changed because wolf tracks were there at the bus stop.
  So, Mr. President, you know, I am just asking what makes sense when 
the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service has--one, two, three, four, five--
four times taken the gray wolves off the endangered species list, 
which, again, we all agreed was required. We should have all agreed it 
was successful. We are way past the delisting baseline established by 
the Wisconsin DNR. We are at the maximum sustainable limit here.
  I am asking consent to pass a bill that would simply delist them so 
that Wisconsin farmers, Wisconsin families, and Wisconsin children can 
feel safe in their residences, in walking about their properties.
  So, Mr. President, notwithstanding rule XXII, I ask unanimous consent 
that the Committee on Environment and Public Works be discharged from 
further consideration of S. 1306 and the Senate proceed to its 
immediate consideration; further, that the bill be considered read a 
third time and passed and the motion to reconsider be considered made 
and laid upon the table

                 Unanimous Consent Request--S. Res. 558

  Mr. WHITEHOUSE. Mr. President, simple fact: Climate change is real. 
It is actually pretty widely known. I think it is a true statement that 
every person knowledgeable about climate science who is not on the 
payroll of the fossil fuel industry or its denial operation agrees with 
that. Actually, a lot of people knowledgeable about climate science who 
are on the payroll of the fossil fuel industry agree with that. In 
fact, scientists from Exxon, from Chevron, in a report for API 30, 40 
years ago, acknowledged climate science, predicted the harms, and 
indeed quite accurately predicted the harms we are seeing now.
  Given that, I came to the floor to ask my colleagues if they could 
simply agree on that simple scientific fact--climate change is actually 
real. Well, unfortunately, to no one's surprise, Republican colleagues 
could not. So I came back to the floor and broke down the big 
question--the scientific fact that climate change is real--into some of 
its component truths, the smaller truths that make up the big truth 
that climate change is real. Each time faced with simple, well-
documented truths, such as sea level rise and economic fallout from 
climate change, Republicans objected.

  Objectors have protested that they are not climate change deniers. 
Well, you would never know that from their willingness to consider 
solutions. Indeed, the objectors are stridently opposed to any--any--
climate solutions, and they have made their opposition clear. The 
problem is that that comes with consequences.
  So I will try another simple truth today. It is actually a pair. One, 
climate change-driven extreme weather events are increasing, and at the 
same time, the government is dismantling weather monitoring and alert 
systems. I will ask you to accept those two truths together. I don't 
think either one is subject to much debate, so I hope we can all agree 
on that.
  As I have explained repeatedly and as anybody knowledgeable in this 
space pretty well knows, climate change exacerbates extreme weather and 
disaster events. You can see on this chart the billion-dollar disasters 
from 1980 to today. It gets worse and worse. It includes winter storms, 
wildfires, hurricanes, and other severe storms; freezes, flooding, and 
drought. But to make the chart, it has got to be a billion-dollar 
disaster. Climate change increases both the frequency and the intensity 
of these events. Scientists have known this to be true for decades now. 
It has actually developed to the point where the National Academies 
could release a report just this month making clear that the 
attribution science--linking extreme weather to climate change to 
emissions--is advancing. It is now quite well established.
  So what do we know?
  We know that the fossil fuel industry is making extreme weather 
worse. Now we have an even better sense of how much worse; how it is 
attributable to those companies.
  I mentioned flooding. Climate change drives flooding in several ways: 
wetter hurricanes, for one; sea-level rise for another. And I say this

hydrologist, a staff forecaster, and a chief meteorologist. They are 
just not there anymore. The San Antonio office was missing a science 
officer and a warning coordination meteorologist. What could possibly 
go wrong? Well, they found out in Kerr County, TX.
  In April of this year, East-Central Kansas got a Weather Service 
tornado warning out with just minutes to spare. Weather forecasters can 
usually issue tornado warnings hours in advance and then update. They 
can do those early warnings because, when the government is properly 
staffed and hasn't had Elon Musk's little minions wrecking it, it can 
release daily weather balloons in the predawn hours to build the 
dataset of what is going on. Well, due to staffing cuts, several 
Weather Service offices now don't have the people to do the predawn 
launches, so the balloons don't go up until regular office hours. By 
then, when those balloons went up on April 13, the storms were already 
on the move. This time, Kansans were warned to seek shelter just in the 
nick of time, but that is not an acceptable gamble for the U.S.

                          Washington Wildfires

  Mrs. MURRAY. Mr. President, I rise today with a very heavy heart 
because, right now, huge swaths of my State are being confronted by 
some of the worst fires that we have ever seen. This weekend, three 
fast-moving wildfires exploded across Spokane County.
  In a matter of hours, entire neighborhoods were told: Leave now. This 
may be your only notice. Over 65,000 people I represent have been 
forced to evacuate their homes. At least 700 homes, businesses, and 
buildings have been destroyed already.
  Families are sleeping in the Spokane Convention Center tonight, not 
knowing whether they even still have a home to go back to. And as of 
this morning, these fires are still not contained.
  The photos and videos tell the story: Houses reduced to ash, families 
embracing outside the burned remains of what they once called home. It 
is heartbreaking. Smoke so thick over Spokane that the air itself has 
now become a health hazard.
  To every family in Spokane and across eastern Washington who has lost 
a home or a business or a lifetime of memories, hear me: I promise you 
I am with you today, tomorrow, and for as long as it takes to rebuild.
  And amid this devastation, I also want this Chamber to hear about the 
extraordinary courage on display in my State right now. Within hours of 
the evacuation orders, neighbors were opening their doors to strangers. 
Families who had room to spare simply said: Come stay with us. 
Volunteers worked through the night at the convention center, handing 
out blankets, serving hot meals, sitting with frightened kids. Local 
restaurants showed up with food for evacuees and for the fire crews who 
are coming off the line. Churches and community groups collected 
clothes and diapers, pet food. Whatever people needed, someone in 
Spokane found a way to provide it.
  Firefighters have been on the lines around the clock in brutal heat 
against dangerous winds. Local fire crews, crews from across the State, 
the Washington National Guard--all mobilized within hours. Incident 
management teams and firefighters who actually came from out of State 
all the way from Florida to Alaska--there are even firefighters from 
Australia and New Zealand--because of them, because of the sheriff's 
deputies and police officers who went door to door to get people out, 
because of the emergency managers and dispatchers and shelter 
volunteers, tens of thousands of people got to safety. These are real-
life heroes, and, on behalf of the people of Washington State, ``Thank 
you.''
  But gratitude is not enough. The people of Spokane need action, and 
they deserve to know their government has their back.
  So here is where things stand as I see them: Our firefighters must 
have every resource they need to contain these fires--every aircraft, 
every bit of equipment, every dollar. So far our State has gotten the 
resources we need from the Federal Government, and I deeply appreciate 
that. That is how it should be.
  Yesterday, I led our congressional delegation in urging the President 
to approve the Governor's request for an expedited emergency 
declaration. The President has approved part of that request; that is 
good, and I will be watching closely to ensure the full request is 
approved.
  The next few days will no doubt be difficult as local fire crews do 
their best to try and contain these fires.
  Looking ahead, it is important to say: Spokane's recovery will not be 
measured in days or weeks; it will be measured in months and years. 
Rebuilding homes, rebuilding businesses--it will take a long time, and 
it will not be easy.
  But as vice chair of the Senate Appropriations Committee, I want the 
people of eastern Washington to know they have a voice in the U.S. 
Senate who will speak up for Spokane. I am not someone who will simply 
write letters to this administration asking for support. I help write 
the bills that fund the government across every Department and every 
Federal program, and I will make sure Spokane's recovery is a priority 
at every opportunity. There is not a single dollar I won't pursue if it 
means helping Spokane and communities across the State as they rebuild 
from these devastating fires. Whatever it takes, for as long as it 
takes.
  Spokane has been hurt and is being badly hurt by these fires, but 
Spokane is not broken. A community that takes care of each other in the 
way we have seen cannot be broken. Spokane will rebuild; Eastern 
Washington will recover; and I will be standing with them every single 
step of the way.
