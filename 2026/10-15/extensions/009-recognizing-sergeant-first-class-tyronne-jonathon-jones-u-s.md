# RECOGNIZING SERGEANT FIRST CLASS TYRONNE JONATHON JONES, U.S. ARMY (RET.), WITH A CONGRESSIONAL VETERAN COMMENDATION

**Speaking:** Sessions, Pete (S000250) [R-TX]

```
  RECOGNIZING SERGEANT FIRST CLASS TYRONNE JONATHON JONES, U.S. ARMY 
           (RET.), WITH A CONGRESSIONAL VETERAN COMMENDATION

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. PETE SESSIONS

                                of texas

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 15, 2026

  Mr. SESSIONS. Mr. Speaker, I rise to present retired U.S. Army 
Sergeant First Class Tyronne Jonathon Jones of Crockett, Texas, with a 
Congressional Veteran Commendation in recognition of his impressive 
service to his community and country. For more than 20 years, Tyronne 
served our Nation in the United States Army, retiring as a Sergeant 
First Class in 2023.
  His military career included service with the 1st Special Forces 
Group, the 3rd Cavalry Regiment, and other aviation and cavalry units, 
where he developed expertise in aircraft armament, avionics, and 
unmanned aircraft systems. He deployed in support of Operation Iraqi 
Freedom, Operation Enduring Freedom, Operation Spartan Shield, and 
Operation Inherent Resolve, serving in locations all across the Europe 
and the Middle East.
  But behind those deployments and decorations is a story of 
extraordinary courage and sacrifice. In January 2014, while deployed to 
Afghanistan with the 2nd Cavalry Regiment, Staff Sgt. Jones was caught 
in a deadly insurgent attack on his forward operating base. On January 
20, 2014, insurgents penetrated the compound following a massive 
explosion and opened fire. Jones was struck by a gunshot during the 
firefight. Beside him was his Chief Warrant Officer, who was killed in 
the attack. Jones and his Chief Warrant Officer provided covering fire 
while junior troops worked to get civilians to safety. The Officer paid 
the ultimate price for his devotion to his fellow Soldiers. Jones 
survived the attack, but he was seriously wounded and ultimately 
evacuated through Kandahar and Bagram before arriving at Landstuhl 
Regional Medical Center in Germany. That wound earned him the Purple 
Heart, one of our Nation's oldest and most respected military 
decorations.
  The Purple Heart represents more than an award on a uniform. It is a 
reminder that Sergeant First Class Jones was willing to put his own 
life in danger for his fellow Soldiers and for the mission of the 
United States Army. What happened next tells us even more about the 
character of this remarkable American. Tyronne had planned to reenlist 
while deployed in Afghanistan. His reenlistment ceremony was supposed 
to be conducted by his Chief Warrant Officer. Instead, Jones was forced 
to recover from his injuries at Landstuhl. Yet while recovering from 
his combat wounds, Jones still chose to reenlist.
  On February 19, 2014, he took the oath of enlistment again at 
Landstuhl Regional Medical Center, surrounded by his family and fellow 
Soldiers. His decision demonstrated a profound commitment to his 
country. He refused to allow an enemy attack to determine when his 
service would end. That is the kind of courage that deserves 
recognition. What makes Tyronne particularly deserving of this 
Congressional Veteran Commendation is that his service did not end when 
his military career ended.
  After retiring from the Army in 2023, Tyronne continued serving the 
people of Crockett. Just one week before his military retirement, he 
began a new chapter of public service as a Code Enforcement Officer for 
the City of Crockett. In this position, he works to help maintain the 
safety, appearance, and quality of life of his community. The City of 
Crockett has recognized Jones and other veterans employed by the city 
for their commitment not only to our Nation, but to their continued 
service to the local community. He also continued his commitment to his 
fellow veterans by joining American Legion Post 134, where he serves as 
Post Adjutant.
  In that role, Tyronne helps veterans and their families navigate 
difficult circumstances and provides assistance when they need it most. 
He has helped support veterans with necessities such as household 
expenses, utilities, automobiles, home repairs, and accessibility 
needs. He also helps organize meals and fellowship opportunities for 
veterans and first responders--creating the kind of community where 
those who have served our country know that their service has not been 
forgotten.
  His commitment to Crockett extends into the broader community as 
well. Tyronne serves on the Board of Directors of the Crockett Area 
Chamber of Commerce, helping promote local businesses and strengthen 
the community he now calls home. He has also volunteered with the 
Houston County Search and Rescue team, putting his training, 
discipline, and experience to work when his neighbors need assistance. 
And he has continued finding ways to serve through his volunteer work 
with the American Red Cross. That is the meaning of a true citizen-
soldier.
  The same qualities that made Tyronne a great Soldier--discipline, 
courage, leadership, loyalty, and commitment to others--continue to 
make him an outstanding member of the East Texas community. I am proud 
to recognize Sergeant First Class Tyronne J. Jones as a recipient of 
the 2026 Congressional Veteran Commendation. On behalf of the people of 
Texas's 17th Congressional District, I thank Tyronne for more than two 
decades of honorable military service, for the sacrifices represented 
by his Purple Heart, and for everything he continues to do for his 
fellow veterans and his community.
```
