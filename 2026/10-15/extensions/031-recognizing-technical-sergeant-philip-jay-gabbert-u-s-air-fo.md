# RECOGNIZING TECHNICAL SERGEANT PHILIP JAY GABBERT, U.S. AIR FORCE (RET.), WITH A CONGRESSIONAL VETERAN COMMENDATION

**Speaking:** Sessions, Pete (S000250) [R-TX]

```
   RECOGNIZING TECHNICAL SERGEANT PHILIP JAY GABBERT, U.S. AIR FORCE 
           (RET.), WITH A CONGRESSIONAL VETERAN COMMENDATION

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. PETE SESSIONS

                                of texas

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 15, 2026

  Mr. SESSIONS. Mr. Speaker, I rise to present Dr. Philip Jay Gabbert 
of Lufkin, Texas, with a Congressional Veteran Commendation in 
recognition of his distinguished military career, his service to our 
Nation, and his extraordinary commitment to veterans and the community.
  Dr. Gabbert's life reflects the very best traditions of military 
service: integrity, dedication, sacrifice, and a commitment to serving 
others. His service began more than five decades ago when he entered 
the United States Air Force in 1972. He would go on to serve 
approximately 20 years on active duty, attaining the rank of Technical 
Sergeant and serving as an Electrical and Switching Systems Technician. 
During his Air Force career, Dr. Gabbert served his country across the 
United States and around the world. His assignments included Barksdale 
Air Force Base, Andersen Air Force Base in Guam, NORAD in Colorado 
Springs, Misawa Air Base in Japan, Lindsey Air Station in Germany, and 
what became Air Force Space Command in Colorado Springs. His 
responsibilities included telecommunications, combat communications, 
equipment accountability, safety, quality control, and communications 
systems. He was entrusted with important responsibilities because of 
his technical expertise, attention to detail, and dedication to 
excellence.
  While stationed at Barksdale Air Force Base, Dr. Gabbert served with 
the Honor Guard, participating in military funeral services and 
community parades. During his service, he also demonstrated exceptional 
weapons proficiency, earning expert marksmanship qualifications. His 
military service included overseas assignments and support of Operation 
Desert Shield/Desert Storm. From August 1990 through February 1991, he 
served during a critical period in our Nation's history, answering the 
call when American forces were called upon to defend freedom and 
confront aggression in the Middle East.
  His awards include the Air Force Commendation Medal, Air Force 
Outstanding Unit Award with Oak Leaf Cluster, Air Force Good Conduct 
Medal, National Defense Service Medal, Air Force Overseas Short Tour 
Ribbon, Air Force Overseas Long Tour Ribbon, Air Force Longevity 
Service Award, Air Force NCO Professional Military Education Ribbon, 
Air Force Training Ribbon, Small Arms Expert Marksmanship Ribbon, and 
Combat Communications Group recognition.
  Yet Dr. Gabble's service was never limited to the responsibilities of 
his military occupation. While stationed overseas, he volunteered with 
the Boy Scouts of America and assisted young people participating in an 
international World Jamboree. He also became involved with the Girl 
Scouts, helping support successful cookie-sale activities, and 
volunteered with the American Red Cross as an instructor. His 
dedication was so extraordinary that he was recognized for contributing 
more than 1,000 volunteer hours annually for three consecutive years. 
He also became involved with the Japanese-American Friendship Club 
while stationed at Misawa Air Base, helping strengthen relationships 
between American service members and the Japanese community.
  In 1992, Dr. Gabbert honorably retired from the United States Air 
Force. But retirement from military service did not mean retirement 
from service. Following his military career, Dr. Gabbert continued his 
education and professional development, ultimately earning a Ph.D. in 
Information Technology. He worked in technology, telecommunications, 
education, and technical support, using his knowledge and experience to 
help others. Perhaps most importantly, however, Dr. Gabbert found a new 
mission in serving his fellow veterans. Through his involvement with 
the Veterans of Foreign Wars, American Legion, Disabled American 
Veterans, and Air & Space Forces Association, he has devoted countless 
hours to helping veterans obtain the benefits they earned through their 
military service.
  Dr. Gabbert is a Life Member of the Disabled American Veterans, Air & 
Space Forces Association, American Legion, and Veterans of Foreign 
Wars. He serves as a Service Officer with both the VFW and American 
Legion and serves as a VFW Chaplain. He has personally assisted many 
veterans in navigating the Department of Veterans Affairs disability 
claims process. He teaches them how to understand their claims, gather 
supporting documentation, prepare for Compensation and Pension 
examinations, monitor their cases, and advocate for themselves, Dr. 
Gabbert also gives approximately 120 hours each month to veterans' 
organizations and other community-service activities.
  Through Hospice in the Pines, he participates in veteran recognition 
and pinning ceremonies honoring veterans near the end of their lives. 
He helps ensure that these veterans and their families know that their 
sacrifices are remembered and appreciated. He has participated in 
numerous such ceremonies, providing dignity and recognition to veterans 
and comfort to their families. He also participates annually in Wreaths 
Across America, helping honor and remember deceased American service 
members. Through the American Legion, he assists with the annual Four 
Chaplains Ceremony, commemorating four Army chaplains who sacrificed 
their own lives to save others during World War II.
  Dr. Gabbert understands that serving veterans also means investing in 
the next generation. He serves as a coordinator connecting the Lufkin 
High School JROTC program with the Army National Guard unit in Lufkin, 
helping strengthen the relationship between young people, military 
organizations, and the veteran community. His commitment to his 
community extends even beyond veterans and military organizations. On 
numerous occasions, Dr. Gabbert has volunteered in character as Uncle

[[Page E1091]]

Sam and Santa Claus at community events. He has brought joy and 
fellowship to senior citizens, veterans at VFW posts and veterans 
centers, patients and visitors at VA facilities, and members of the 
broader community. These acts demonstrate something important about Dr. 
Gabbert's character. He understands that service is not always about 
recognition. Sometimes service means simply showing up, giving your 
time, bringing encouragement to someone who needs it, or making another 
person feel remembered.
  Dr. Gabbert's story demonstrates that military service does not end 
when a veteran hangs up the uniform. More than five decades after first 
answering the call to serve, he continues to dedicate his time and 
talents to others. His legacy can be seen in the veterans he has helped 
navigate the VA system, the families who have received comfort during 
hospice recognition ceremonies, the fallen service members remembered 
through Wreaths Across America, the young people encouraged through 
JROTC and Scouting, and the countless neighbors who have benefited from 
his willingness to volunteer.
  It is my privilege to recognize Dr. Philip Jay Gabbert for his 
distinguished military service, his extraordinary volunteerism, and his 
lifelong dedication to veterans, military families, and his community.
```
