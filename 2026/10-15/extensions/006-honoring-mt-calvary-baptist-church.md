# HONORING MT. CALVARY BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                  HONORING MT. CALVARY BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 15, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Mount Calvary Baptist 
Church of Alexandria, Louisiana, a congregation whose history of faith, 
sacrifice, and service spans roughly eight decades. The church traces 
its roots to Good Faith Baptist Church, founded by the late Reverend N. 
H. Ailsworth, who served as its pastor for 3 years. On July 21, 1946, 
Reverend J. S. Lawson, a member of Good Faith who was also pastor of 
Bright Morning Star on Jet Hill in Cloverdale, Louisiana, was 
unanimously elected pastor. Several members of Bright Morning Star 
followed him, contributing to the growth of the church, and a few 
months later it was renamed Mount Calvary Baptist Church. Reverend 
Lawson appointed the church's first officers, including Brother Henry 
Clark as Senior Deacon, Sister Velma Anderson as Secretary, and Sister 
Jane Hampton as Clerk, along with its first deaconesses and choir 
leaders.
  For approximately 27 years, the congregation worshipped in a small 
frame building at 3109 Houston Street in Alexandria. Because of the 
church's growth and the poor condition of the building, it was torn 
down on May 19, 1970. The 40-by-80-foot lot did not meet the city's 
minimum lot area requirements for a new structure, so the search began 
for another site. In the meantime, services were held at New Kingdom 
Baptist Church, then under the leadership of Reverend Matthew 
Slaughter, an example of the cooperation among Alexandria's 
congregations.
  The answer came when Sister Lillie Lawson spotted a church for sale 
at 320 Daspit Street and contacted Deacon Huey Lawson, a real estate 
salesman, who brought the information to the membership. The price was 
$60,000, and the church had only $1,300 in the bank. Some members felt 
the price was too steep, but others believed that with God all things 
are possible. After many prayers and much bargaining, Southside Baptist 
Church lowered the price to $33,000, and through sacrifices and 
generous donations Mount Calvary bought the church. Dedication services 
were held the first week of December 1970 under the auspices of the 
Educational Missionary Baptist Association, then led by Reverend J.J. 
Rax.
  Mount Calvary thrived under Reverend Lawson, whose soul-stirring 
sermons and witty personality were matched by the work of loyal 
members. They included Brother Willie Anderson, who chaired the Deacon 
Board for more than 30 years; Brother R.L. Nash, Sunday School 
Superintendent; Sister Rosa Wells White, Sunday School Secretary; 
Sister Thelma Elie and Sister Delores Ashner of the Food Ministry; and 
Sister Ashner, President of the Deaconess Board, who helped start the 
Graduating High School Seniors Program. After the move, Cenla Community 
Action Committee located a Head Start Center in the building, and the 
church grew so quickly that the sanctuary had to be enlarged. Members 
of the disbanded Mount Pleasant Baptist Church in Magda, Louisiana, 
also pastored by Reverend Lawson, later joined the congregation.
  Reverend Lawson served for a little over 40 years before failing 
health limited his time in the pulpit, and the congregation remained 
strong under three assistant pastors, Reverend Joseph Humphrey, 
Reverend Wilson King, and Reverend Huey Lawson. On December 22, 1986, 
fire caused extensive damage to the sanctuary and the rear of the 
church, and services were held at Smyrna Seventh Day Adventist Church, 
with Pastor Alfred Booker presiding, during the restoration. As 
renovations neared completion, Reverend Lawson passed, and the first 
service in the restored church was his funeral.
  In April 1987, Reverend Huey P. Lawson was elected pastor, and under 
his direction the church grew spiritually and physically. His tenure 
brought a Wednesday Night Prayer Meeting and Bible Study, the laying of 
the cornerstone, a steeple, renovated restrooms, a new pastor's study, 
and the purchase of several houses and of 3.7-acre and 15-acre tracts 
of land with plans for a new church facility. The church also 
established the J. S. Lawson Foundation, an Outreach Mission Ministry, 
and a Health Care Ministry, extending its service into the community. 
In July 2005, Reverend Ronnie Humphrey and the Saint Paul Baptist 
Church merged with the Mount Calvary family.
  Reverend Huey P. Lawson retired as pastor on January 6, 2008, and 
Reverend Ronnie Humphrey was elected pastor on January 13, 2008, and 
installed on February 14, 2008. Under his leadership, six new deacons 
were ordained in 2009, a church organ was purchased, a Youth Bible 
Study began, and the land for future expansion was paid off. Extensive 
renovations began in 2021, and in 2022 the sanctuary and women's 
classroom received state-of-the-art audio and visual enhancements and 
the kitchen was remodeled.
  Today, Mount Calvary Baptist Church continues its ministry in 
Alexandria, and many descendants of the ``Old Mount Calvary'' families, 
including the Anderson, Batiste, Humphrey, Lawson, Nash, and Obey 
families, still promote the growth and welfare of the church. Mr. 
Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring Mount Calvary 
Baptist Church and its congregation for its faith, perseverance, and 
service to the people of Alexandria. May Mount Calvary continue to grow 
in faith and in service for generations to come.
```
