# RECOGNIZING THOMAS OLEINIK WITH A CONGRESSIONAL VETERAN COMMENDATION

**Speaking:** Sessions, Pete (S000250) [R-TX]

```
  RECOGNIZING THOMAS OLEINIK WITH A CONGRESSIONAL VETERAN COMMENDATION

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. PETE SESSIONS

                                of texas

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 15, 2026

  Mr. SESSIONS. Mr. Speaker, I rise to present Thomas Oleinik, of 
Huntsville, Texas, with a 2026 Congressional Veteran Commendation. His 
life of service reflects the hard work, dedication, and devotion that 
exemplify the very best of our East Texas values.
  Thomas Oleinik entered active duty in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, in 
February 1961 at the age of nineteen. He trained with the 5th and 13th 
Infantry at Fort Riley, Kansas, and the 1st Engineering Brigade at Fort 
Leonard Wood, Missouri. Thomas went on to serve honorably overseas in 
Korea in the aftermath of the Korean War. Although he was trained as a 
combat engineer and prepared to serve in a still-hostile Korean 
theater, the Army recognized his abilities and entrusted him with an 
important administrative role. He was assigned to the 15th 
Administration Company and placed in charge of managing the Army Post 
Office for the 24th Infantry Division at Panmunjom for 15 months, 
serving as a vital link between soldiers and their families. Thomas 
took great pride in the trust his superiors placed in him.
  Thomas returned stateside following his overseas tour and transferred 
to the 20th Engineering Brigade at Fort Devens, Massachusetts, where he 
continued serving in the U.S. Army Reserves through 1967, completing 
six years of military service. During his service, Thomas qualified as 
an Expert Rifle Marksman and completed training in Basic Combat 
Training, the Military Justice Code of Conduct, Chemical, Biological, 
and Radiological Defense, and the Geneva Conventions.
  Following his military service, Thomas began an extraordinary 39-year 
career in law enforcement in 1964. He served as a Wisconsin State 
Trooper from 1964 to 1967 before becoming a Texas State Trooper in 
1968. He continued serving with the Texas Department of Public Safety 
until his retirement in 1993 at the rank of Corporal.
  Thomas' retirement from law enforcement was brief. In 1996, former 
County Judge and past Congressional Veteran Commendation recipient 
Charles Wagamon and County Commissioner Buddy Reynolds launched an 
effort to establish Walker County's first Environmental Enforcement 
Officer. Thomas applied for the position, was selected, and quickly 
made it his own. He enforced county ordinances addressing illegal 
dumping, burning violations, water and air pollution, and also handled 
septic inspections, 911 files, building codes, and permitting. His work 
helped make Walker County a safer, cleaner, and more orderly community 
while inspiring volunteers to take an active role in improving their 
surroundings. In recognition of his efforts, Thomas received third 
place in the Keep Texas Beautiful Ed Davis Litter Law Enforcement Award 
in 2002. He retired from the position in 2008 and devoted even more of 
his time to serving his community.
  Thomas became a Finance Officer and long-time board member of the 
newly established H.E.A.R.T.S. Veterans Museum of Texas, where he 
worked alongside its co-founder, his beloved wife Charlotte, to help 
build the museum into the institution it is today, serving thousands of 
veterans throughout the region. He has also served in numerous 
volunteer and leadership roles with VFW Post 5871, his church, and 
other community organizations, consistently giving his time to 
veterans, families, and neighbors in need.
  Thomas' devotion to his country extended into civic service as well. 
Because of his deep sense of duty and love of country, he served for 
many years as a Precinct Chair for the Republican Party of Walker 
County, where he worked to strengthen the integrity of local elections 
and remained committed to serving his commnunity.
  Thomas' greatest legacy of service, however, began at home with 
Charlotte. Built upon a strong foundation of faith, their marriage 
included fostering more than 50 young children over the course of their 
life together. Through his military service, law enforcement career, 
veteran advocacy, civic involvement, and dedication to his family, 
Thomas has exemplified what it means to be a great American, 
exceptional veteran, father, leader, and mentor.
  It is this lifetime of hard work, service, leadership, and devotion 
to his community and family that I proudly commend Thomas Oleinik of 
Huntsville, Texas, to his friends, family, community, and country. It 
is my honor to recognize him with the 2026 Congressional Veteran 
Commendation.
```
