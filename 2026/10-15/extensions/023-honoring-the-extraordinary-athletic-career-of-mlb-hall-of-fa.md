# HONORING THE EXTRAORDINARY ATHLETIC CAREER OF MLB HALL OF FAME PITCHER BILLY WAGNER

**Speaking:** Griffith, H. Morgan (G000568) [R-VA]

```
HONORING THE EXTRAORDINARY ATHLETIC CAREER OF MLB HALL OF FAME PITCHER 
                              BILLY WAGNER

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. H. MORGAN GRIFFITH

                              of virginia

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 15, 2026

  Mr. GRIFFITH. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the extraordinary 
athletic career of Major League Baseball (MLB) Hall of Fame pitcher and 
Southwest Virginia's very own, Mr. Billy Wagner.
  Mr. Wagner was born on July 25, 1971, in Marion, Virginia. He spent 
most of his upbringing in neighboring Tazewell County, where he would 
attend high school. At Tazewell High School, Mr. Wagner's athletic 
career took off. As a dual-sport competitor in baseball and football, 
he was part of the school football championship in 1986.
  But for Mr. Wagner, his heart was in baseball. Following his high 
school graduation, Mr. Wagner stayed in the region and pursued 
collegiate baseball at Ferrum College in Franklin County, Virginia. 
While there, Mr. Wagner set a National Collegiate Athletic Association 
(NCAA) Division III record by averaging 19.1 strikeouts per nine 
innings and secured All-American honors.
  After a dominant collegiate career, MLB scouts took notice. The 
Houston Astros selected Mr. Wagner with the 12th overall pick in the 
1993 MLB Draft. He would make his MLB debut on September 13, 1995.
  Over the course of 16 MLB seasons, Mr. Wagner pitched for five MLB 
franchises. He compiled 1,196 strikeouts, a 2.31 ERA, 422 saves and 
seven all-star selections. The successful reliever boasted 11.9 
strikeouts per nine innings, which remains the highest rate for any MLB 
pitcher with at least 900 innings since 1900.
  These accolades distinguished Mr. Wagner, also known as ``Billy the 
Kid,'' from most relief pitchers in MLB history. Accordingly, in 2025, 
Mr. Wagner was inducted into the National Baseball Hall of Fame. He is 
the first NCAA Division III baseball player to make the Baseball Hall 
of Fame. Those in his inductee class are MLB legends Ichiro Suzuki and 
CC Sabathia.
  Although these accomplishments warrant praise, Mr. Wagner is also 
recognized as a devoted husband, a father of four, a mentor and a 
charity leader. Through the Wagner Family Foundation, Mr. Wagner has 
supported literacy initiatives, homeless outreach programs and 
organizations serving at-risk youth.
  Additionally, Mr. Wagner never lost his identity nor forgot his 
roots. In 2005, he founded the Second Chance Learning Center in 
Bluefield, Virginia, to help provide tutoring and counseling services 
for local underserved children. A 2008 New York Post story detailed 
that despite his career taking him far from home, Mr. Wagner was still 
the same country boy throwing heat against the side of the dairy. As 
part of his Hall of Fame induction remarks, Mr. Wagner thanked his 
coaches from Tazewell and Ferrum for their positive impact on his 
career and life. He also wore a Ferrum College pin and Tazewell County 
pin on his suit lapel during the ceremony. Most recently, Mr. Wagner 
joined Ferrum baseball's coaching staff.

  Anyone from Southwest Virginia who visits Cooperstown, New York, will 
now get to see one of their own in legendary company. Mr. Wagner defied 
the odds during a hard-fought journey to the Baseball Hall of Fame. He 
continues to inspire scores of young athletes and athletic programs in 
our region. Southwest Virginia is incredibly proud of Mr. Billy Wagner.
```
