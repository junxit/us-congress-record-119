# HONORING THE 30TH ANNIVERSARY OF FRIENDS OF CANCER RESEARCH

**Speaking:** DeGette, Diana (D000197) [D-CO]

```
      HONORING THE 30TH ANNIVERSARY OF FRIENDS OF CANCER RESEARCH

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. DIANA DeGETTE

                              of colorado

                    in the house of representatives

                       Thursday, October 15, 2026

  Ms. DeGETTE. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to congratulate Friends of 
Cancer Research (Friends) as the organization celebrates 30 years of 
scientific leadership and advocacy. Their unwavering dedication to 
advancing biomedical research and supporting cancer patients and their 
families has been vital in our shared goal to end this terrible 
disease.
  For three decades, Friends of Cancer Research has reshaped the 
landscape of oncology research and regulatory science by serving as a 
bridge between patients and policy makers.
  I have been fortunate to witness their incredible impact on our 
Nation's health care policy firsthand. For example, in 2012, Friends 
was critical to bipartisan legislation I led alongside Rep. Brian 
Bilbray (R-CA) to establish the Breakthrough Therapy designation at the 
Food and Drug Administration (FDA) which would expedite therapy 
development. Those provisions were signed into law as part of the Food 
and Drug Administration Safety and Innovation Act. Since then, the 
Breakthrough Therapy designation has helped bring more than 370 life-
changing therapies to patients, in some cases, in half the time.
  In 2016, Friends of Cancer Research served as a crucial stakeholder 
when Rep. Fred Upton (R-MI) and I wrote the 21st Century Cures Act. 
Their expertise and advocacy were instrumental in passing this 
transformative bipartisan legislation that modernized the FDA, as well 
as delivered significant funding to the National Institutes of Health. 
Friends continued to be a collaborative partner as we later expanded on 
21st Century Cures, by establishing the Advanced Research Projects 
Agency for Health (ARPA-H), an independent research organization within 
HHS tasked with finding cures for the world's most serious illnesses, 
such as cancer, Alzheimer's, diabetes, and other diseases.
  Friends has been a phenomenal partner to me, Congress, and the 
American people, always ready to work side-by-side with lawmakers to 
streamline drug approvals, advance crucial regulatory frameworks and 
identify bottlenecks in medical research--all for the betterment of 
patients and their families.
  I also honor my dear friends Ellen Sigal and Marlene Malek, the 
organization's visionary founders, whose exceptional leadership 
realized the urgent need to unite Congress behind the bipartisan issue 
of tackling the indiscriminate scourge of cancer. I remain grateful to 
have been recognized by Friends of Cancer Research when I received the 
organization's Research Leadership Award in 2015 for my work on the 
21st Century Cures Act. Receiving that honor still carries so much 
weight for me as it reflects our shared commitment to ensuring that 
lifesaving treatments reach the patients who need them the most safely 
and efficiently.
  For 30 years, Friends has embodied the relentless pursuit of new 
cures and the bold collaboration needed across all sectors of society, 
to bring hope to millions. I can only imagine what they will accomplish 
in their next 30 years.
  I ask my colleagues to join me in celebrating Friends of Cancer 
Research's 30 years of extraordinary contributions to science, health 
policy, and the ongoing fight to make cancer a disease of the past.
```
