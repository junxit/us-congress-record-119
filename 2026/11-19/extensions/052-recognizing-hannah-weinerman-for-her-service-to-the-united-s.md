# RECOGNIZING HANNAH WEINERMAN FOR HER SERVICE TO THE UNITED STATES HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES

**Speaking:** Nadler, Jerrold (N000002) [D-NY]

```
RECOGNIZING HANNAH WEINERMAN FOR HER SERVICE TO THE UNITED STATES HOUSE 
                           OF REPRESENTATIVES

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. JERROLD NADLER

                              of new york

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, November 19, 2026

  Mr. NADLER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Hannah Weinerman 
for her eight years of outstanding service to my office and to the 
constituents of New York's 12th Congressional District.
  A native of Long Island, Hannah attended Cornell University, where 
she graduated with a degree in Industrial and Labor Relations and a 
minor in International Relations in 2011. Hannah returned downstate and 
moved to New York City, where she began her career as the Program 
Director of Queens Initiatives at the Jewish Community Relations 
Council of New York (JCRC-NY). In this role, she launched leadership 
fellowships and coalitions across the borough, strengthening JCRC-NY's 
intercommunal partnerships and becoming an anchor that Queens non-
profit, faith and community leaders came to know and rely upon.
  During this period, she attended the Silberman School of Social Work 
at Hunter College, where she earned a Master of Social Work with 
concentrations in Community Organizing, Planning and Development, and 
Policy and later obtained her license in the field. Building upon her 
experience at JCRC-NY, she honed her ability to lead collaborative 
responses to societal and policy issues. These skills gave her sound 
political judgment to see both the minute details and the bigger 
picture, a quality I relied upon daily.
  In 2018, Hannah became the Community Liaison in my District Office. 
She did an excellent job serving as the bridge between organizations, 
government agencies, and local community boards. Hannah is a team 
player, always willing to step up and take on more responsibilities.
  This was unmistakably clear during the reauthorization of the James 
Zadroga 9/11 Health and Compensation Act in 2019. Her compassion and 
determination ensured that the distinct needs of the Lower Manhattan 
community were recognized and valued throughout the legislative 
process. As a result of her work with constituents and advocacy groups, 
9/11 survivors, residents, students, and workers were not only properly 
informed of the health hazards they were exposed to near Ground Zero 
but also the resources available to them, including the medical care 
and financial compensation that they deserved.
  I also appreciate Hannah's efforts to ensure my national 
infrastructure and funding priorities supported the local efforts of 
community stakeholders in NY-12. During the reauthorizations of the 
Water Resources Development Act, she was instrumental in gaining 
community input on climate resiliency measures to address the threat of 
future flooding and storms across New York City. She also worked on 
outreach throughout several appropriations cycles, ensuring I was able 
to deliver federal dollars to support the range of wonderful 
organizations and their deserving projects across the district.

[[Page E1267]]

  Hannah also dutifully supported my role as the co-chair of the 
Congressional Jewish Caucus. As a fellow yeshiva day school graduate, 
her deep, complex understanding of the Jewish community was 
indispensable in informing our work on Jewish issues both locally and 
nationally. She built strong relationships with Jewish organizations 
and constituents and ensured their diversity of perspectives were heard 
in policy discussions. Hannah's ability to listen deeply and respond 
thoughtfully made her an exceptional aide, trusted by both colleagues 
and constituents alike.
  Hannah was invaluable to the office in ways that went beyond her 
defined role. Every staff member she worked with came to appreciate how 
much she contributed to the office culture, acting as a supportive 
sounding board or providing a midday sweet treat. She is a bright light 
who brings levity to every situation, coupling that warmth with grace, 
positivity, and humility.
  In her free time, Hannah leads historical walking tours around New 
York City, sharing stories and little-known facts about its 
neighborhoods. She also hosts a podcast, Who's Mad at the Mayor, where 
she dives into the city's history and politics with her curiosity and 
humor we have all come to know and love. Her passion for New York's 
people, food, and culture has made her a go-to source for 
recommendations or a good story. Hannah's enthusiasm is infectious, and 
her love of learning has inspired us all to see the city with fresh 
eyes.
  As Hannah moves on to the next professional chapter in her life, I 
commend her for her service and look forward to bumping into her at 
Zabar's on the Upper West Side soon.
```
