# RECOGNIZING AARON WEINBERG FOR HIS SERVICE TO THE UNITED STATES HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES

**Speaking:** Nadler, Jerrold (N000002) [D-NY]

```
 RECOGNIZING AARON WEINBERG FOR HIS SERVICE TO THE UNITED STATES HOUSE 
                           OF REPRESENTATIVES

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. JERROLD NADLER

                              of new york

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, November 19, 2026

  Mr. NADLER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Aaron Weinberg for 
his outstanding service to the constituents of New York's 12th 
Congressional District and to the United States House of 
Representatives.
  A native of Evanston, Illinois, Aaron attended Brandeis University, 
where he graduated cum laude. After earning his graduate degree at the 
Hornstein Jewish Professional Leadership Program at Brandeis, Aaron was 
named Director of Jewish Engagement at the Democratic National 
Committee for the 2016 presidential election cycle. In 2018, Aaron 
began his congressional career as a legislative assistant in the office 
of Representative Jan Schakowsky of Illinois, covering issues including 
foreign policy, defense, immigration, gun violence, and civil rights. 
Before joining my office, Aaron worked for a variety of nonprofits, 
providing insights and nuanced analysis to Members of Congress on 
issues including the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, Middle East foreign 
policy, antisemitism, global human rights, and hate crime prevention.
  I was fortunate to have Aaron join my Washington, D.C. office in May 
2024 to primarily work on foreign policy, antisemitism, and Jewish 
communal matters, and to assist me in my responsibilities as the most 
senior Jewish Member of the House of Representatives. I was immediately 
appreciative of Aaron's unique talents, background, and relationships, 
as shortly thereafter I charged him to enter into negotiations on my 
behalf to establish the first-ever Congressional Jewish Caucus. Over 
the course of the next several months, I witnessed how Aaron expertly 
navigated these delicate negotiations. He masterfully identified and 
analyzed key issues, solicited and keenly listened to feedback from my 
fellow Jewish members, and shaped proposals that threaded the 
incredibly fine needle to meet the vast diversity of needs of the 
Jewish members. As a skilled negotiator, Aaron was able to reach an 
agreement that was unanimously supported by all of the Jewish members. 
After the establishment of the Caucus, I was immensely proud to name 
Aaron its founding Executive Director. I am deeply appreciative of his 
skill in navigating the complex politics of the Caucus in its first 
Congress. I know my colleagues will join me in offering sincere thanks 
to Aaron for his hard work and in affirming that he leaves big shoes to 
fill.
  I am also grateful to have benefited from Aaron's talents as a 
sophisticated legislative drafter, shrewd coalition builder, and savvy 
whipper. One of the clearest examples of Aaron's legislative acumen is 
the West Bank Violence Prevention Act. When Aaron and I first discussed 
his idea for the bill, we imagined perhaps 50 or so of my colleagues 
might agree to cosponsor; yet the bill now enjoys over 175 cosponsors 
in the House and 44 of the 46 Senators who caucus with the Democrats. I 
am gratified that the entire House Democratic leadership team has 
cosponsored the bill and that Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries has 
publicly indicated his intention to bring the bill to the floor in the 
next Congress should he be elected Speaker of the House,
  I am also greatly appreciative of Aaron's efforts in drafting the 
Antisemitism Response and Prevention Act. Aaron understood that an 
effective congressional response to antisemitism must do more than 
condemn individual incidents or debate definitions; it must provide a 
comprehensive affirmative framework to combat antisemitism upstream and 
invest in preventing hatred before it takes root. I am thankful for 
Aaron's both principled and practical approach in drafting this 
legislation, and I know it will provide a blueprint for future 
legislation and discussion on this critical issue.
  Aaron's legislative acumen was also instrumental in our leadership on 
the Holocaust Expropriated Art Recovery (HEAR) Act. Having helped lead 
the original legislation in 2016, I was honored to serve as the lead 
House Democratic sponsor of the 2025 reauthorization. The HEAR Act 
strengthens the ability of Holocaust survivors and their families to 
pursue claims for artwork stolen by the Nazis, ensuring that their 
cases can be heard on their merits rather than dismissed on procedural 
grounds. At a time when more than 100,000 works of art stolen from 
Jewish families during the Holocaust remain unrecovered, this law 
represents a meaningful step toward justice. Aaron helped us navigate 
the legislative process and build support for this important measure, 
culminating in the bipartisan bill's unanimous passage in both the 
House and Senate and its being signed into law in April 2026.
  I am also profoundly grateful for the many floor remarks, rally 
speeches, press statements, and social media posts Aaron crafted for 
me. Most notable were my remarks during the Tom Lantos Human Rights 
Commission hearing on global antisemitism, calling out the hypocrisy 
and antisemitism with the Trump administration; my speech at the ``Say 
No to Ben-Gvir'' rally in New York opposing Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir's 
racism and visit to the United States; and my statement condemning 
Prime Minister Netanyahu's most recent speech to Congress.
  I am privileged to have benefited from Aaron's counsel as a gifted 
strategist and astute analyst. I am confident he will continue to work 
on the issues that are close to both of our hearts and will have a 
positive impact on the world. I wish him every success in doing so and 
thank him for his excellent service.
```
