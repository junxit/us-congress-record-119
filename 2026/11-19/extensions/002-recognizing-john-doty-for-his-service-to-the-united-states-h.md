# RECOGNIZING JOHN DOTY FOR HIS SERVICE TO THE UNITED STATES HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES

**Speaking:** Nadler, Jerrold (N000002) [D-NY]

```
  RECOGNIZING JOHN DOTY FOR HIS SERVICE TO THE UNITED STATES HOUSE OF 
                            REPRESENTATIVES

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. JERROLD NADLER

                              of new york

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, November 19, 2026

  Mr. NADLER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize John Doty for his 
more than 32 years of outstanding service to the people of New York and 
of the Nation.
  John's tenure on the Hill is truly impressive. He began working with 
me during my first full term in Washington and is here to the end of my 
last term. I appreciate his loyalty and friendship over these many 
years.
  To understand how truly exceptional John's service to the House is, 
consider the fact that I am now the 10th most senior Member of the 
House of Representatives, and John has now worked on the Hill longer 
than 416 of the current 433 Members of the House.
  Rising from intern to Chief of Staff, and holding nearly every job in 
between, John has played a critical role in virtually every issue that 
has come across my desk over these last three decades. John has served 
as my Legislative Correspondent, Legislative Assistant, Legislative 
Director, Washington Director, and the Chief of Staff in my personal 
office. When I led the Judiciary Committee, he also worked on the 
Committee as a Senior Advisor, Deputy Staff Director, and Chief of 
Staff. Along the way he covered nearly every legislative issue, built 
an impressive record of legislative achievement, and improved the lives 
of millions of people all over the country.
  John possesses all the skills that make him excel as a legislative 
staffer. He is a talented writer and communicator, he is a master of 
the legislative process, and he is an excellent manager of both junior 
and senior staff.
  John has written everything from dear colleague letters and press 
releases to op-eds, speeches, social media posts, congressional record 
tributes, and questions for congressional hearings, as well as 
testimony before such committees as the Appropriations Committee, the 
House Administration Committee, and even the September 11th Commission.
  He has also helped draft numerous bills on a wide variety of issues 
and has deftly navigated the legislative process to turn ideas into 
law.
  Among his most notable achievements, after ten years of hard work, he 
helped enact the James Zadroga 9/11 Health and Compensation Act into 
law, which has provided life-saving care to tens of thousands of people 
afflicted with 9/11-related illnesses.
  John also covered Judiciary issues for me during the impeachment of 
President Bill Clinton. At the time, we believed it might be the most 
consequential work of our Congressional careers. The national media 
attention was intense, the stakes were incredibly high, and the country 
was closely following our every word. John helped prepare me for media 
interviews and committee proceedings. His analysis and insights along 
with the work of David Lachmann were critical to my understanding of 
the issues and the nature of impeachment as the Constitution intended.
  As I look back now, that was just one brief moment in a career that 
spanned five presidents, two other impeachments, multiple wars in 
Afghanistan, Iraq and Iran, the September 11, 2001, attacks--which 
occurred in my district--the Covid pandemic, the January 6, 2021, 
assault on the Capitol, and the historic progress of the lesbian, gay, 
bisexual, transgender, and queer community.
  On many of the most important issues that I worked on throughout my 
career, John was by my side taking a leading role. These include 
efforts to repeal and reform the Patriot Act, to end the wars in Iraq 
and Afghanistan, to expand housing opportunities for people with AIDS 
and those eligible for Section 8 vouchers, to repeal Don't Ask, Don't 
Tell, and to prevent loose nuclear weapons from being used by 
terrorists. He also spent countless hours meeting with constituents and 
stakeholders and was instrumental in securing millions of dollars in 
federal appropriations for projects in my district.
  For more than 20 years, I have entrusted John with running my 
Washington, D.C. office. In this role, not only has he been a valued 
advisor, but he has also hired and smoothly managed a talented and 
dedicated staff that I rely on every day. This is no small feat. It is 
truly stunning the depth and breadth of John's work in my personal 
office. I cannot thank him enough.
  In 2017, as my most experienced D.C.-based staffer, John spearheaded 
my campaign to become Ranking Member, and later, the Chairman of the 
Judiciary Committee, He built and led a formidable Whip Team made up of 
talented Members and senior Hill staff. That team, with its vast 
network of contacts on and off the Hill, went on a full court press to 
reach out to every Democratic member multiple times, ask for their 
vote, and answer their questions. We reached out to every caucus--from 
the progressives to the blue dogs to the congressional black caucus--
customizing our campaign for each audience. John's organizational 
skills were masterful, and he created daily tasks for everyone that 
optimized our varying skill sets and focused our efforts where they 
were needed most. He even set up a game-day strategy with a Member-
buddy system to help pull out the vote. The result: I was able to win 
the race by a vote of 118-72.
  That was an intense, Member to Member campaign, where we fought for 
every vote, and used the power of the press and outside groups to 
secure victory. John always went the extra mile and when the campaign 
was over John made sure that we thanked our friends and supporters by 
delivering Zabar's chocolate babkas to show our appreciation. I want to 
thank him for his tremendous help in this crucial part of my political 
career. I really appreciate it.
  Once we had the opportunity to run the Judiciary Committee, with 
John's help, we seized hold of the operation right away, restructured 
the staff, prepared a vision for what we wanted to accomplish and set 
out to pass a bold legislative agenda while exercising intense 
oversight of the Trump Administration.
  We pushed a progressive agenda that included marriage equality, equal 
rights for women, voting protections, common sense gun violence 
prevention, consumer protection and antitrust enforcement, and strong 
protection of intellectual property rights, among many other important 
issues. We also exercised aggressive oversight of the Trump 
Administration, holding countless hearings exposing their cruel 
immigration policies, their corrupt self-dealing, and Trump's abuse of 
power. That oversight eventually led to the impeachment of Donald 
Trump.

[[Page E1252]]

  To help accomplish all this work, John oversaw the communications, 
Member Services, and administrative functions of the Judiciary 
Committee, first as Senior Advisor, then as Deputy Staff Director, and 
finally as Chief of Staff.
  John put together an excellent communications team that created 
engaging videos and social media posts, responded to press inquiries, 
and successfully worked with reporters to tell a cohesive story. 
Particularly during impeachment, we faced a voracious media and were 
able to ensure our key message of the day was in the chyrons on the 
bottom of the cable tv networks coverage. John and his team booked me 
on every cable news show from CNN to Fox to MSNBC to the weekly Sunday 
shows. John's leadership of the press team was remarkable, and thanks 
to their outstanding efforts, I was consistently featured in major 
media outlets like the New York Times, the Washington Post, and 
virtually every newspaper in the country.
  John also created our Member Services and outreach department in the 
Judiciary Committee and staffed it with the great Lisette Morton and 
the amazing Moh Sharma. Together, they made sure we never lost a vote 
in Committee, that our Members' concerns were heard, and that each 
Member's office staff was informed and prepared for all our hearings 
and markups. The team met individually with Members to seek their 
input, understand their motivations, and help them achieve their goals. 
They made sure Members came to the floor and spoke out on issues we 
were bringing before the American people. Again, I want to thank John 
and his team for everything they did for all the Members of the 
Committee, not just its Chairman.
  John also supervised the administrative functions and logistics of 
the Committee, preparing and managing the Committee's $18 million 
budget and drafting my testimony before the House Administration 
Committee to defend our budget request. He also rewrote the Committee's 
office manual, worked with our IT staff to ensure that the staffs 
technology needs were met, and approved the Committee's expenses. 
Working with my Staff Director and Chief Counsel, John managed the 
staff, set salaries, organized field hearings, and worked with the 
Capitol Police during high profile hearings. When Covid hit, he found a 
way for us to continue to hold hearings and marIcups remotely and then 
in a safe hybrid format so that the Committee never lost a step.
  In the four years I served as Chair, we held more than 165 hearings 
and passed 98 bills into law, many of which have made a meaningful 
difference in the lives of millions of Americans. These include bills 
that helped enshrine marriage equality into the law of the land, extend 
health care and compensation to the first responders and survivors of 
9/11 and to their families, curb gun violence, punish hate crimes, and 
combat violence against women.
  For every hearing that moved the issue forward, for every close vote 
that made the difference between success and failure, for every story 
in the press that enabled us to build public support for our agenda, 
John was there. And I thank him for his service.
  John truly cares about people. He cares about big issues that affect 
real people's lives. And he is smart enough and talented enough to be 
able to do something about it. One of the reasons why John has 
continued to work for me for so long and has done so without getting 
cynical and beaten down is because you can tell he is having fun. He 
enjoys working in the office and people enjoy working with him. He 
brings humor, enthusiasm, and caring into an environment that is often 
serious and stodgy. He loves to hear a good story and to tell one of 
his own. Whether it be adventures abroad to places like South Africa or 
Ireland, or about whatever book he is reading or video game he is 
playing, he is quick to make a detour from the serious to the silly and 
to tell a good story without losing sight of our ultimate goal.
  John and I have spent hours and hours telling each other stories, 
discussing biblical texts and ancient history, recalling district 
leader races of the 1960s and 1970s, discussing religion, war, civil 
rights, the Yankees--you name it. John has heard my stories so many 
times, at this point I think he may know them better than I do.
  I also want to thank John for supporting my role as the Dean of the 
New York Delegation, as founder and inaugural Co-Chair of the 
Congressional Jewish Caucus, and as Assistant Whip and Regional Whip. 
For every position of power I held in Congress, I owe a debt of 
gratitude to John and the staff he recruited and hired to help me carry 
out the work the position required.
  Prior to coming to work for me, John worked on peace and disarmament 
issues for the Friends Committee on National Legislation and as a 
paralegal in law firms in New York and Washington, D.C. He grew up in 
Park Slope, Brooklyn and graduated from Middlebury College, where he 
studied history and computer science.
  I would like to thank Cheryl Doty, John's wife, for supporting John 
so steadfastly over his decades of service. Thirty-two years of service 
translates into thousands of late nights, hundreds of missed family 
dinners, and time spent away from his family. I also want to thank 
John's daughter Emma Doty for bringing so much joy into his life. John 
is enormously proud of Cheryl and Emma and is quick to provide updates 
on their latest achievements--whether that is Cheryl helping people 
recover from a tsunami in Indonesia and helping improve nutrition for 
infants in Uganda or Emma studying at Haverford College, leading the 
Sneetches ultimate frisbee team, and working at the Quaker conference 
center at Pendle Hill.
  John has all of the qualities that make for the best staff on Capitol 
Hill: exceptional skills, solid judgment, and a great heart. As our 
career together comes to an end, I wish John the best of luck in his 
future endeavors and thank him for his service to me, to New Yorkers, 
and to the Nation.
```
