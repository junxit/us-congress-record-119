# RECOGNIZING MATTHEW STEINBAUM FOR HIS SERVICE TO THE UNITED STATES HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES

**Speaking:** Nadler, Jerrold (N000002) [D-NY]

**Measures:** H.R. 1

```
  RECOGNIZING MATTHEW STEINBAUM FOR HIS SERVICE TO THE UNITED STATES 
                        HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. JERROLD NADLER

                              of new york

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, November 19, 2026

  Mr. NADLER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Matthew Steinbaum 
for his outstanding service to my office and to the constituents of New 
York's 12th Congressional District.
  As the son of a Vietnamese immigrant and Jewish American family, 
Matthew's commitment to public service was formed early. He never 
forgot about the institutions that helped facilitate his mother's entry 
into the United States or that of his father's family, Jewish 
immigrants who passed through Ellis Island and originally settled in 
New York City generations ago.
  While originally from New Jersey, Matthew's musical journey began in 
New York City, in the heart of the 12th Congressional District. Trained 
as a violinist and later on as an opera singer, Matthew's love of music 
culminated in his receiving a degree in Voice and Opera Performance 
from Northwestern University, as well as a degree in Political Science.
  Having completed internships with my colleague, Senator Kirsten 
Gillibrand, and the Democratic Staff of the Senate Foreign Relations 
Committee, Matthew joined my office in the fall of 2024, as a 
Congressional Fellow with the Asian Pacific American Institute for 
Congressional Studies. During his fellowship, he introduced the 
Polluters Pay Climate Fund Act, the first federal superfund legislation 
of its kind to hold fossil fuel polluters accountable, as well as the 
Children's Health Protection Act, a bill which would ensure that the 
Environmental Protection Agency continued to fulfill its responsibility 
to safeguard children's environmental health. He also staffed me on tax 
issues, a crucial element during the continued fight over H.R. 1, the 
Big Ugly Bill.
  At the conclusion of his fellowship, Matthew began a new opportunity 
in New Jersey, as the Legislative Director to State Senator Andrew 
Zwicker. However, when my office needed a Legislative Assistant to 
cover health policy, Matthew enthusiastically returned to staff me. 
Shortly thereafter, he took on additional legislative responsibilities 
to help ensure a successful final year in Congress. Within his first 
months, Matthew helped me successfully include key provisions of the 9/
11 Responder and Survivor Health Funding Correction Act into a major 
funding package, which was recently signed into law. This work ensures 
that the World Trade Center Health Program is funded for decades to 
come, and that first responders and survivors can continue to receive 
the lifesaving care that the program provides.
  As a senior member of the House Committee on Transportation and 
Infrastructure, I am appreciative of Matthew's work staffing me on the 
Surface Transportation Act Reauthorization in May of 2026. After I 
opposed the bill in committee, Matthew worked with me to make my 
colleagues aware of the bill's shortcomings and the need to defend and 
expand funding and programs for mass transit and rail. Another critical 
transportation issue he covered is the renovation of New York Penn 
Station. Matthew worked with me to alert the city and country of my 
concerns over the politicization and potential impropriety in this 
process, both through letters with other elected officials and an 
eventual opinion piece in The New York Times.
  Matthew's work, which covers a wide range of issues, also includes 
one of my final legislative priorities--the push to pass the Living 
Donor Protection Act. He has worked tirelessly to pass this bill during 
the 119th Congress. This legislation would defend living organ donors 
from insurance and employment discrimination, and we are both intent on 
seeing it get over the finish line.
  Finally, he took on a large role this Congress, helping guide my 
office through the complicated appropriations process, and worked with 
staff and stakeholders to make sure that New York's 12th Congressional 
District received millions of federal dollars for important community 
projects. Additionally, on the policy side of the appropriations 
process, Matthew took part in my work to ensure that the Equal 
Employment Opportunity Commission is held accountable for politicized 
policy changes, and he helped me lead a public comment letter on the 
agency's actions.

[[Page E1266]]

  Matthew has been a hard worker and has dedicated himself to aiding me 
and my priorities in my final years in Congress. The relationships he 
has built across the Hill and with stakeholders has helped move 
legislation and guide his judgement. Whether he is accompanying me to 
the House floor or sitting behind me during committee activities, 
Matthew has shown great judgement and commitment to his role in my 
office.
  As a member of the final wave of young staffers in my office, Matthew 
always brought laughter and camaraderie, and his energy was always a 
highlight to fellow staff and members of the many constituent groups 
with which he met. I will miss working with him and wish him good luck 
as he continues his career in public service.
```
