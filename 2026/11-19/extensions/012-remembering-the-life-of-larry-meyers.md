# REMEMBERING THE LIFE OF LARRY MEYERS

**Speaking:** Arrington, Jodey C. (A000375) [R-TX]

```
                  REMEMBERING THE LIFE OF LARRY MEYERS

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. JODEY C. ARRINGTON

                                of texas

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, November 19, 2026

  Mr. ARRINGTON. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize the life of 
Larry Meyers, a Washington stalwart and longtime Alexandria community 
member. He was a devoted husband, a loving father, an adoring 
grandfather, and a strong friend to many, and it is my privilege to 
join his friends and family in celebrating Larry and include in the 
Record the following article recognizing his life and service to 
others, representing the very best of West Texas:

       A proud Texan, he championed a wide range of Texas state, 
     community, and military priorities--as well as broader 
     national agricultural issues and international development 
     efforts and was well known for his world-class skill at the 
     barbeque pit and overall Texas hospitality.
       For forty years he faithfully attended First Baptist Church 
     of Alexandria, where he served as a Deacon, led the men's 
     Bible study class for over 25 years, and held multiple church 
     leadership roles, as well as cooking thousands of steaks for 
     the quarterly men's breakfast.
       Larry was born in 1948 in Slaton, Texas to Jinks and Genie 
     Meyers. His family soon relocated to Muleshoe, Texas, where 
     he grew up helping on the family's farm with his brothers 
     Rick and Cliff. He graduated from Muleshoe High School and 
     remained a lifelong fan of Mules football. He then attended 
     Texas Tech University, where he was vice president of the 
     student body and president of the Kappa Alpha.
       In 1970, he was brought to Washington by recently elected 
     Senator Lloyd Bentsen and ultimately served as the senator's 
     legislative director before being appointed by President 
     Jimmy Carter as Director for Congressional Affairs in the 
     Department of Agriculture. When he left the USDA in 1981, he 
     opened his own government relations firm, Meyers and 
     Associates, and by 1989 Beacham's Guide to Key Lobbyists 
     listed him among the top lobbyists in the capital. He was 
     also the executive vice president of Agricultural Development 
     International, where he was instrumental in reconstruction 
     efforts and agricultural aid in both Russia (late 1990's) and 
     Iraq (2004). He was a long-time member of the Texas State 
     Society (to include serving a term as president); a proud 
     member of the Masons, receiving his 32nd degree as part of 
     the Alexandria Scottish Rite; and served on the board of 
     Washington First Bank, Leland Seminary, and the Texas State 
     Aquarium.
       Larry met Kris in 1979 and they married in 1981. For 45 
     years, they enjoyed taking on a wide range of endeavors 
     together, from running a catering company to the constant 
     project of renovating their (and eventually their children's) 
     homes, a labor of love they came to call the ``Midnight 
     Construction Company.'' But their best shared endeavor was 
     lovingly raising their children Max and Betsy. Larry 
     faithfully cheered on countless athletic events (never missed 
     a game), served as an assistant scoutmaster for Boy Scouts, 
     and patiently crafted a warm, loving home and as his family 
     expanded in recent years to include three grandchildren, he 
     treasured time with each of them.
       Larry built a remarkable life of service, and will be 
     remembered for his generosity,

[[Page E1256]]

     unwavering integrity, mentorship, and the many quiet acts of 
     kindness he was constantly doing for those around him.
       He is preceded in death by his parents, William Eugene 
     Meyers and Gertrude Elizabeth Sooter; and his younger brother 
     Clifton Meyers. He is survived by his beloved wife Kris, 
     children Max (wife Abby) and Betsy, and three grandchildren, 
     Liam, Tessa, and Nina. He is also survived by his younger 
     brother Richard Meyers and his wife Terry.
```
