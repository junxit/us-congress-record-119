# HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF RONALD E. "RON" ARMSTEAD

**Speaking:** Bishop, Sanford D. (B000490) [D-GA]

```
       HONORING THE LIFE AND LEGACY OF RONALD E. ``RON'' ARMSTEAD

                                 ______
                                 

                      HON. SANFORD D. BISHOP, JR.

                               of georgia

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, November 19, 2026

  Mr. BISHOP. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life and legacy of 
my dear friend and longtime partner in service, Mr. Ronald E. ``Ron'' 
Armstead, who passed away on November 1, 2026, at the age of 79. For 
nearly four decades, Ron was the heart, the conscience, and the steady 
hand of the Congressional Black Caucus Veterans Braintrust. His passing 
leaves a void in this institution, and in my life, that words alone 
cannot fill.
  Ron was born on April 12, 1947, in Boston, Massachusetts, and was 
raised by his devoted mother, Ruby Smith, and his stepfather, Leemon 
Smith. Even as a young man, he showed the gifts that would mark his 
whole life: a love of basketball, a sense of humor that could light up 
any room, and an artist's eye for painting and photography.
  When his country called, Ron answered. He enlisted in the United 
States Navy and served as a medic off the coast of Vietnam. He tended 
to the wounded and comforted the dying, and he carried those memories 
with him for the rest of his life. He came home a changed man, 
determined that the men and women who served beside him would never be 
forgotten.
  Ron used the GI Bill to attend Boston State College. In 1978, he and 
his fellow students founded the Veterans Club. That small gathering 
grew into the Veterans Benefits Clearinghouse, and then into the 
Congressional Black Caucus Veterans Braintrust here in Washington. Ron 
served as its Executive Director from its beginning in 1988, first 
under the leadership of our late colleague, Representative Charles B. 
Rangel of New York, a decorated Korean War combat veteran. It was the 
honor of my career to work beside Ron for many years as he carried that 
mission forward.
  Mr. Speaker, under Ron's leadership, the Braintrust grew from a small 
core group into one of the premier forums in the Nation where veterans 
of African descent could speak directly to the leaders of their 
government. Year after year, Ron filled hearing rooms with veterans who 
had been overlooked for too long. He made sure their stories were 
heard, their sacrifices were honored, and their needs were placed on 
the agenda of this Congress. Many of the improvements in care and 
benefits that Black veterans receive today bear his fingerprints, even 
where his name does not appear.
  Ron also served as a consultant to the Advisory Committee on Minority 
Veterans under the late Secretary of Veterans Affairs Jesse Brown. He 
earned a master's degree in city planning from the Massachusetts 
Institute of Technology, was a licensed social worker in Massachusetts, 
and pursued doctoral studies in social policy and planning at Howard 
University's Graduate School of Social Work. He was a scholar, but he 
never let his learning separate him from the people he served. He was 
as comfortable with a homeless veteran on a park bench as he was with a 
Cabinet Secretary in a conference room.
  Ron also understood that the story of Black veterans did not begin in 
his lifetime. He was an ardent supporter of the legacy of the 54th 
Massachusetts Volunteer Infantry Regiment, one of the first African 
American regiments to fight for the Union in the Civil War. In 1863, 
those brave men stormed Fort Wagner in South Carolina and proved to a 
doubting Nation that Black soldiers would fight and die for a country 
that had not yet granted them full freedom. Sergeant William H. Carney 
carried the flag that day and later received the Medal of Honor. Ron, a 
son of Boston, carried their memory forward with the same pride. He 
made sure that new generations knew that Black Americans have answered 
the call in every war this Nation has fought.
  He was a world traveler who never forgot where he came from, and a 
tireless advocate who never asked for credit. He did not seek the 
spotlight. He sought justice. And when the cameras were gone and the 
hearing rooms were empty, Ron was still working, still calling, still 
pushing, still believing that this Nation could keep its promise to 
every person who wore its uniform.
  I will miss his phone calls. I will miss his wisdom. I will miss his 
laughter. And I will

[[Page E1254]]

miss the quiet way he would remind all of us that the work was not 
finished.
  Ron is survived by his four children, Todd Williams, Kaili Y. Turner, 
Fari Nzinga, and Joseph Johnson; his grandchildren, Chiyanna Hinojosa, 
Tahir Williams, Angelina Williams, Dasan Turner, Eliyus Turner-Tillery, 
and Ilu Jaya Brooks McAfee; his great-grandchild, Zaiden Norman; and a 
grateful Nation of veterans whose lives are better because he lived.
  Family, friends, and fellow veterans will gather to celebrate Ron's 
life on Friday, November 20, 2026, at 12:00 p.m. at Floyd A. Williams 
Funeral Home in Boston, Massachusetts. It is fitting that Ron's final 
homecoming will be in the city where his journey began, where he first 
answered the call to serve, and where he first gathered veterans around 
a table and gave them a voice.
  The Apostle Paul wrote, ``I have fought a good fight, I have finished 
my course, I have kept the faith.'' Ron Armstead fought that good fight 
for his fellow veterans every day of his life. He finished his course 
with honor, and he kept faith with every man and woman who ever wore 
the uniform of this Nation.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join my wife, Vivian, and I, 
along with the more than 765,000 people of Georgia's 2nd Congressional 
District, in honoring the life of Ronald ``Ron'' Armstead. We extend 
our deepest sympathies to his family, to the Congressional Black 
Caucus, and to the entire veterans community. He was a sailor, a 
healer, a scholar, an advocate, and a friend. Ron's watch is ended. May 
he rest well. We have the watch from here.
```
