# SETTING CONSUMER STANDARDS FOR LITHIUM-ION BATTERIES ACT

**Speaking:** Gillibrand, Kirsten E. (G000555) [D-NY], Moran, Jerry (M000934) [R-KS]

**Measures:** H.R. 973

```
        SETTING CONSUMER STANDARDS FOR LITHIUM-ION BATTERIES ACT

  A bill (H.R. 973) to establish consumer standards for lithium-ion 
batteries.

[[Page S5794]]

  The bill (H.R. 973) was ordered to a third reading, was read the 
third time, and passed.
  Mrs. GILLIBRAND. Madam President, I rise now to support my bill, the 
Setting Consumer Standards for Lithium-Ion Batteries Act.
  This is a commonsense, bipartisan bill that could save American lives 
by preventing lithium-ion battery fires.
  Lithium-ion batteries are used to power electric micromobility 
devices like e-bikes and e-scooters. But when these batteries are 
faulty or poorly made, especially in China, they can turn deadly.
  Just 2 weeks ago, a fire tore through a Brooklyn row home killing six 
New Yorkers. Fire marshals are still investigating, but early reports 
link the fire to a lithium-ion battery.
  The week before that, an e-bike battery fire killed four more people, 
including children, in an apartment building in the Bronx. And it is 
not just in New York City. A grandmother and grandson were killed by an 
e-bike battery fire in Colonie in Upstate New York just a few days ago.
  This year alone, lithium-ion batteries have sparked over 400 fires in 
New York City. According to the fire department, lithium-ion batteries 
have caused 46 deaths and 628 injuries since they began reporting this 
statistic in 2019.
  These fires are entirely preventable. We have taken bipartisan action 
right now to save their lives.
  The Setting Consumer Standards for Lithium-Ion Batteries Act prevents 
fires by requiring the Consumer Product Safety Commission to implement 
more robust regulations for lithium-ion batteries used in micromobility 
devices.
  The bill has already passed the House with overwhelming bipartisan 
support. This body was the only thing standing in the way of 
regulations that will save lives today.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER (Mr. Husted). The Senator from Kansas.


                         Tribute to Donn Teske

  Mr. MORAN. Mr. President, I rise this afternoon to pay tribute, to 
recognize the service and the commitment of a man in my home State of 
Kansas. That man is Donn Teske, and he has been the Kansas Farmers 
Union president for much of my time, perhaps almost all of my time as a 
Member of this Congress. That indicates that Donn has dedicated 25 
years to this role as president of the Kansas Farmers Union. He will 
soon retire from a career that has been defined by strong leadership 
and a deep, deep commitment to Kansas agriculture.
  Donn is a fifth-generation farmer from Wheaton. Donn and his wife 
Kathy have spent their lives immersed in production agriculture. Today, 
the Teskes operate a cow-calf operation on the native Bluestem prairie 
of the Flint Hills, continuing a family legacy built on stewardship, 
resilience, and respect for the land.
  Donn embodies what it means to be a good neighbor, trusted community 
member, and a strong agricultural leader. His role with Farmers Union 
has allowed him to serve in a wide range of agricultural leadership 
positions, and each one has been shaped by his commitment to 
strengthening Kansas. I have had the opportunity to work closely with 
Donn over his years of leadership, and I value his dedication to the 
industry and to the farmers he represents.
  As Congress works to advance the farm bill--and I am hopeful that 
will be completed here in this year, before the end of December--I am 
reminded of not only the importance of this legislation but the value 
of engaging with a broad scope of industry stakeholders whose 
perspectives shape strong, lasting policy.
  Donn has advised me, has lobbied me, has encouraged me, has insisted 
with me on various provisions over a long period of time about what 
should be in a farm bill and what should not. Leaders like Donn make 
those efforts possible. We as leaders here in Washington, DC, are 
fortunate to be able to rely on his wisdom and advice.
  Thank you, Donn, for your dedication and the sacrifices you have made 
to be an advocate for our State and for its farmers and ranchers. Your 
willingness to show up, to speak up, and to bring producers to the 
table has strengthened Kansas agriculture and the national conversation 
around it. I am grateful for your leadership and passion, the passion 
that you have shown in working to support rural Kansas. While you may 
be stepping down from this role, I know you are far from being done, 
far from giving up involvement or advocating for agricultural producers 
in our State and across the country.
  I have enjoyed every encounter I have had with Donn Teske. You cannot 
meet this man without knowing he is a true, true Kansan, a true farmer, 
and committed to other people. He demonstrates in every conversation 
not only his knowledge but, even more, his importance, his love of the 
land and his love of Kansas people and bringing the two together to 
make certain good things happen on family farms and ranches across our 
State.
  Donn, I wish you all the best in this next chapter.
  I yield the floor.
```
