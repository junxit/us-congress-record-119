# REMEMBERING MARGARET GREEN SCRIVENS

**Speaking:** Blunt Rochester, Lisa (B001303) [D-DE]

```
                  REMEMBERING MARGARET GREEN SCRIVENS

● Ms. BLUNT ROCHESTER. Mr. President, I rise today to honor the 
life and legacy of Margaret Green Scrivens of Wilmington, DE, who 
passed away on

[[Page S5811]]

September 3, 2026, at 89. A graduate of Howard High School, Mrs. 
Scrivens devoted her life to public service. She overcame barriers and 
worked tirelessly to open doors for the Black women and public servants 
who followed her.
  Mrs. Scrivens spent 27 years at the Delaware Department of Labor, 
retiring as chief accountant in benefit accounting. Committed to 
workplace fairness, Mrs. Scrivens was a founding member of AFSCME Local 
2038 and was elected its vice president in 1973, helping give 
Delaware's State employees a stronger voice. She was also a founding 
member of Letty's Sisters in Session, an organization that addressed 
issues affecting African-American women in state government.
  Mrs. Scrivens' leadership extended well beyond her workplace. In 
1974, she was appointed to the Delaware Board of Parole and, in 1985, 
to Wilmington's Civil Rights Commission. In each role, she demonstrated 
a deep commitment to justice, civic engagement, and the economic 
empowerment of underserved communities.
  Mrs. Scrivens' example of service has carried forward through her 
family. Her daughter Shayne Williams devoted decades of service with 
the city of Wilmington. Her son-in-law, former mayor Dennis Williams, 
knew Mrs. Scrivens from a young age, and her example helped shape his 
path from the Wilmington Police Department to the Delaware House of 
Representatives and the mayor's office. Her granddaughter Lauren 
Williams has continued the family tradition through public service at 
the State and Federal levels. My late father Ted Blunt and Mayor 
Williams were friends and longtime colleagues, and I am grateful that 
our families have known each other for so many years. Across 
generations, she instilled in her loved ones the values that guided her 
own life: service, leadership, and a deep commitment to community.
  Today, I invite my colleagues to join the people of Delaware and me 
in honoring the life of Margaret Green Scrivens and her extraordinary 
legacy of public service.●

                          ____________________
```
