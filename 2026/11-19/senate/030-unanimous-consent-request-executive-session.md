# Unanimous Consent Request (Executive Session)

**Speaking:** Schatz, Brian (S001194) [D-HI], Merkley, Jeff (M001176) [D-OR], Lee, Mike (L000577) [R-UT], Murkowski, Lisa (M001153) [R-AK]

**Measures:** H.R. 2347

```
                       Unanimous Consent Request

  Mr. SCHATZ. Mr. President, every Senator has a perfect right to block 
a unanimous consent request, and at one time or another in our careers, 
we all do that. And we do that for leverage. We do that because we want 
to engage in the negotiation and try to enact something that is 
important to us.
  In this case, we had a long negotiation about a hotline, and the 
Senator from Oregon objected, as is his right. But here is the thing: 
The bill in question passed, and so the leverage is gone. And the 
question before this body is, Do we want to make this bill just a 
little bit better? Do we want to clarify the definition of 
``undertaking'' such that Native communities--Native Hawaiians, 
American Indian Tribes, Alaska Natives--are mandatorily consulted if 
there is an adverse effect from an Agency action on a cultural or 
historic site?
  The question at hand is, Do we want to provide $100 million more--
about a 50-percent increase--for the Historic Preservation Fund to 
actually help people--not at the regulatory level but to do actual 
restoration of historic and cultural sites?
  Do we want to take the expertise of environmental experts on the 
project certainty provision to ensure transparency for Federal permits 
so that this administration is not permitted to issue a permit without 
the public even knowing about it? Do we want that improvement in the 
bill?
  So the question is no longer ``Can we stop the passage of the bill 
out of the U.S. Senate?'' It is ``Do we want to make it a little bit 
better?''
  So as if in legislative session and notwithstanding rule XXII, I ask 
unanimous consent that, notwithstanding passage of H.R. 2347, the Lee-
Heinrich-Capito-Whitehouse amendment No. 7030 be considered and agreed 
to and the motion to reconsider be considered made and laid upon the 
table.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Is there objection?
  The Senator from Oregon.
  Mr. MERKLEY. Mr. President, reserving the right to object, I have in

feature continues to be insufficient in addressing our concerns.
  By the way, I ask unanimous consent to have this letter printed in 
the Record.

                                                November 19, 2026.

  A Public Statement From the Affiliated Tribes of Northwest Indians 
  (ATNI) on the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA)

       The 57 Tribal Nations ATNI represents have considerable 
     concerns regarding BAAJA's impacts on our water, lands, First 
     Foods, and especially our sacred places and sites. The second 
     manager's package continues to be insufficient in addressing 
     ATNI's concerns. We have called for the full Title III: 
     Historic Preservation provision to be struck from BAAJA.

     recognize or respect Tribal sovereignty.

     First Foods is not negotiable.

     sovereignty and upholding the federal government's trust and 
     treaty obligations. If enacted, the impacts of BAAJA are 
     likely to be the most significant reduction in Tribal 
     authority since the Termination Era.

now that it has passed, we are talking about what, if anything, to add 
to it as an enrollment correction before it leaves this Chamber to go 
to the House of Representatives.

sense to me, is to recognize that, look, there are a lot of amendments 
where there is a lot of cross-party disagreement, where the parties 
might be more or less irreconcilably at odds with one another, with one 
side saying X, not Y, and the other side saying Y, not X. But the 
managers' amendment that the Senator from Hawaii is trying to add to 
this bill as an enrollment correction before it leaves this Chamber en 
route to the House of Representatives is not one of those amendments. 
It was painstakingly negotiated as a bipartisan exercise.
  There is nothing in that managers' amendment that would do anything 
other than make the bill better. Some of it deals with Tribes, fixes 
issues important to Tribes. Some of it fixes some issues that are of 
concern to farmers in America in response to concerns raised by, among 
others, the Farm Bureau. Then there are other changes within that 
managers' amendment that are strictly technical--conforming amendments, 
making clear what the bill does.
  Now, the bill is fine without those things. It would be better with 
the bill. Adding the managers' amendment to this bill at this point 
would only improve it. It would make it neither more Republican nor 
Democratic; it would simply make it better.
  So that is why I am stunned and disappointed that we can't just 
accept what the Senator from Hawaii has wisely offered to make this 
better and to not put our thumb on the scale, either the right side or 
the left side of the scale.
  As to this amendment itself, as to his proposed modification, the 
proposed modification offered up by the Senator from Oregon in response 
to what the Senator from Hawaii has just offered, there are a couple 
things that are worth mentioning here.
  No. 1, the Senator from Oregon has made the baffling argument that 
somehow data centers are not subject to the Clean Air Act under this 
legislation. That is categorically false. That is utterly devoid of 
truth. I disagree with every single part of that sentence, including 
the words ``but,'' ``and,'' and ``the.'' That simply is not true. The 
Clean Air Act is still operative. It was operative before this 
happened. It is operative after this legislation takes place, even 
after it gets signed into law by the President following passage by the 
House of Representatives. The Clean Air Act is there. So do not 
be deceived into thinking that somehow data centers are immune, are 
exempt, from the Clean Air Act. That is not true.

  As to the rest of the idea of modifying this, here again, we offered 
up a whole bunch of amendments. Some of these could have been voted on 
in a package that we offered up that would have included about 20 
amendments. That was rejected. That is unfortunate. But, again, that 
ship has sailed, as has the ship of the passage of this bill. It is now 
passed. It is now on its way to the House of Representatives. The 
question before us is whether we take a step to make it better.
  No one here can reasonably dispute that the managers' amendment 
package would make this bill better--and better in a way that is 
neither Republican nor Democratic, neither liberal nor conservative, 
but that is simply American, simply conforming, simply technical. If we 
can't do that here, shame on us.
  I would respectfully request my friend and colleague the 
distinguished Senator from Oregon--ask him to reconsider.
  Will you modify your request to go back to the original request by 
the Senator from Hawaii and accept the managers' package in its 
entirety as an enrollment correction?
```
