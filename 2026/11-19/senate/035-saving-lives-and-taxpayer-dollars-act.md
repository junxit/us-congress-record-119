# SAVING LIVES AND TAXPAYER DOLLARS ACT

**Speaking:** Welch, Peter (W000800) [D-VT]

**Measures:** S. 2252

```
                 SAVING LIVES AND TAXPAYER DOLLARS ACT

  Mr. WELCH. Mr. President, according to press reports, the Trump 
administration plans to reprogram $49 million Congress appropriated for 
contraceptives for poor women in developing countries, purportedly to 
offset the spike in energy costs foreign countries are experiencing due 
to President Trump's reckless and unnecessary war against Iran.
  This is only the latest example of the Trump administration denying 
women and girls access to safe reproductive health care, contrary to 
the intent of Congress. This cruel practice has put countless lives at 
risk and wasted taxpayer dollars. It reflects what is all too common 
under this administration: prioritizing extremist ideology over what 
the world's most vulnerable want and need. In this instance, it 
involves a matter of life and death.
  Even before this decision to divert the $49 million, since January 
2025, the Trump administration has wasted almost half a million dollars 
by destroying contraceptives that would have been distributed to women 
and girls in the poorest countries, primarily in Sub-Saharan Africa. 
This funding could have provided contraceptives to an estimated 43,900 
women and couples, averted 15,700 unintended pregnancies and 4,800 
unsafe abortions, and saved the lives of 24 mothers.
  This unconscionable waste of taxpayer dollars began with President 
Trump's stop work order and foreign aid freeze, followed by DOGE's 
reckless dismantling of the U.S. Agency for International Development, 
USAID. I spoke out about this last October, when the State Department 
tried to destroy contraceptives that had already been purchased by 
USAID for distribution. After falsely labeling the contraceptives as 
abortifacients, either due to ignorance or as a pretext, they were 
withheld beyond their expiration date and could no longer be used.
  The destruction of contraceptives and the collapse of funding for 
foreign assistance is being felt around the world. In Tanzania, 
abortion is illegal except to save the mother's life. One in five 
maternal deaths is due to unsafe abortions. As a result of the Trump 
administration's actions, Tanzanian families are struggling to access 
contraception, especially in rural areas. Restoring funding for 
contraception would save lives, but the White House is motivated by 
politics, not women's health.
  I urge my colleagues to pass the Saving Lives and Taxpayer Dollars 
Act, S. 2252. Introduced by Senators Shaheen and Schatz, this bill 
would ensure that public health commodities are delivered to the 
intended recipients before their expiration date. The fact that such 
legislation is even needed illustrates the larger pattern within the 
Trump administration of refusing to comply with the intent of Congress. 
Congress should repeal the authority the administration has repeatedly 
abused to redirect appropriated funds for purposes other than what 
Congress specified in the law.
  In countries whose governments cannot feed their own people, 
providing families with the modern means to decide when to have 
children saves the lives of women and children. It reflects the best of 
this country by making available to the world's most vulnerable access 
to basic health care the American people take for granted. The lives of 
women and girls across the globe depend on it.

                          ____________________
```
