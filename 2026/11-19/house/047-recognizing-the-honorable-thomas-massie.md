# RECOGNIZING THE HONORABLE THOMAS MASSIE

**Speaking:** Green, Al (G000553) [D-TX]

```
                RECOGNIZING THE HONORABLE THOMAS MASSIE

  (Under the Speaker's announced policy of January 3, 2025, Mr. Green 
of Texas was recognized for 30 minutes.)
  Mr. GREEN of Texas. Mr. Speaker, I rise in the name of Carlyle, who 
proclaimed: ``No lie can live forever.''
  I rise in the name of William Cullen Bryant, who called to our 
attention: ``Truth crushed to earth shall rise again.''
  I rise in the name of the most honorable Dr. Martin Luther King, who 
said: Long though it may be, the arc of the moral universe bends toward 
justice.
  I also rise in the name of simply this: There are times when you have 
to take the ax of truth, slam it into the tree of circumstance, and let 
the chips fall wherever they may. I rise with the ax of truth.
  In so doing, Mr. Speaker, I rise to salute a person who is a Member 
of this House, who has the courage of King, who went to jail for 
speaking truth, literally, when he was in Birmingham, Alabama.
  I rise in the name of Rosa Parks, who had the courage to take a seat 
on a bus, in a racist southern town, when it was inappropriate and 
unlawful to do so by their standards.
  I rise to honor this American. In this American's honor, I had a flag 
flown over the Capitol of the United States of America. I hold in my 
hand the flag that was flown over the Capitol of the United States of 
America, up the same pole that a flag was flown for Kennedy, King, and 
many others.
  I rise to present this flag here on the floor and to the individual 
at a later time.
  Let me now, please, read the certificate of authenticity as it 
relates to the flag. It reads:

       The flag of the United States of America, commemorating the 
     Nation's 250th anniversary. This is to certify that the 
     accompanying flag was flown over the United States Capitol. 
     At the request of the Honorable Al Green, Member of Congress, 
     this flag was flown and presented to the Honorable 
     Congressman Thomas Massie, a courageous public servant who 
     stood alone and introduced, and forced a vote on, Articles of 
     Impeachment against Secretary of War Pete Hegseth, 
     demonstrating an unwavering commitment to the Constitution 
     and the rule of law and the wisdom to understand that on some 
     issues it is better to stand alone than not stand at all.

  Dear brother, I shall present it to you at an appropriate time, but I 
want you to know that I have great respect for your courage. You are 
not a Lilliputian politician. You are not pusillanimous. You will take 
the stand when the stand must be taken, for which I salute you.
  I rise today also to continue the countdown toward impeachment. 
Congressman Massie did a noble thing. This will be a follow-up, because 
I will use much of his language in the countdown, in the actual 
Articles of Impeachment.
  The countdown to impeachment continues. I will bring Articles of 
Impeachment. There will be a vote. I indicated when last I stood here 
that it would be done and that it would be done soon. I didn't say the 
word ``soon,'' but I wanted people to know it wouldn't be done that day 
or right away. But I said no earlier than the following week, soon. I 
still stand on this, the countdown to impeachment.
  Before we can have the actual articles presented, I think there are 
some concerns that have been called to the attention of the public by 
way of the media that have to be addressed. So I am going to today take 
as much time as I can to address the concerns. There are many more. 
Perhaps I won't finish all of them today, but I am going to address 
some of the major concerns.
  The first is whether to impeach a President that you believe has 
committed impeachable offenses. Now, if you don't think the President 
has committed impeachable offenses, then you would not vote to impeach. 
However, if you believe the President has committed impeachable 
offenses, the question is whether you should move to impeach or vote to 
impeach.
  Let's just say it is not your priority. It doesn't have to be your 
priority for a vote to be taken that does not protect the President. If 
the Articles of Impeachment are brought to the floor and it is not your 
priority, that doesn't mean that you have to now vote to protect the 
President. You can vote to impeach. You can give your vote to what you 
know is honorable and right, because you have said in your mind or 
publicly, as many have, that the President has committed impeachable 
offenses.
  For the life of me, I will not understand and I don't think posterity 
will understand people who say that the President should be impeached 
or believe that he should be, but when the vote is taken, they side 
with the President.
  Let me give you a reason why you should impeach, if you are short on 
reasons. If you don't impeach this President, in the future we will be 
electing kings, not Presidents. We will be electing kings because this 
President has already demonstrated that he has little respect for the 
Constitution. He doesn't see these as mandates, the rules of law 
enshrined in the Constitution by Madison. He sees them as advisory at 
best or maybe mandatory when they suit his needs, not the needs of the 
American people.
  We will be electing kings, people who will disrespect the 14th and 
the Fifth Amendments. They will disrespect this whole notion that you 
have to give people the opportunity to say you have the wrong person, 
something called due process. We will be electing people who are going 
to disrespect free speech, as has been done with this President, and 
disrespect the whole notion that you have a judiciary and you have a 
legislative branch and they are co-equal branches and that you don't 
dictate to the judiciary. You will be disrespecting the judiciary and 
the separation of powers. These are some of the things that we are 
passing on to the next President if we don't impeach this President.
  Of all of them, in my world, the thing that is most important is the 
whole notion of taking the country to war without conferring with 
Congress--taking the country to war without conferring with Congress.
  I just believe that a President who takes this country to war, who 
sends our military to some distant place--in this case, Iran--sends 
them to distant places, and he does so without conferring with 
Congress, I believe that that is reason, good reason, to impeach a 
President.
  I think that if we don't use what Mr. Massie used, which were 
Articles of Impeachment that dealt with that very issue, language that 
I will probably use to deal with that very same issue to impeach the 
President, I think we will be electing kings. Because if we can take 
the country to war, disrespect the Constitution--note that in the 
Constitution, we have all of these mandates that you can just sidestep, 
you can eschew--then surely you understand that we will be electing 
kings. I want you to know that I refuse to be a party to electing 
kings.
  I would also call another concern to the attention of the public, and 
that is that it may be a rush job. It wasn't a rush job when every 
Democrat that voted, voted to move impeachment forward when Mr. Massie, 
whom I have had the flag flown for, brought his Articles of Impeachment 
to the floor.

[[Page H6310]]

  There was no investigation. There was no need for an investigation 
because we know that the President took the country to war without 
consulting with Congress and continued to have our Army in foreign 
places to carry on this war.
  We know this. We did the right thing. We, I was among them, we voted 
to move impeachment forward. If I could vote on that a thousand times, 
I would do the same thing a thousand times, and many others would as 
well.
  There is no rush job when the facts are known, when it is intuitively 
obvious to even the most casual observer. That is no rush job. That is 
the right job. That is called doing your job. To do anything less is 
not doing our job.
  Then there is another concern: no consensus as to what we should move 
forward on. I think I have addressed that partially, but I am going to 
accentuate this.
  The consensus is not something that we have to debate because the 
consensus is the President took the country to war. We don't have to 
debate that. We have had many persons who have said that the President 
should be impeached, some for this very thing, but who have some 
consternation when it comes to voting to impeach the President for this 
very thing.

  Don't do yourself a disservice. When posterity looks on this time 
through the vista of time and they see that there were people who 
actually said the President has committed this impeachable offense but 
voted to protect the President when they had the opportunity to vote to 
impeach, history will not be kind to you. Your record will be revealed.
  Another concern is a fear of the MAGA Republicans. There are people 
who seem to think that if you bring up impeachment, somehow it is going 
to cause the MAGA Republicans to become angry and very supportive of 
the President. Well, the MAGA Republicans support him already, and you 
are not going to be able to sneak up on the President to impeach him.
  The President has said, oh, if they impeach me, it will destroy the 
Democratic Party. Do you really believe that? Do you believe the 
President who said he won the election and who still contends that he 
won an election that he lost? This is the President who still proclaims 
victory after many judges have shown clearly, with the evidence that is 
beyond a reasonable doubt, more than clear and convincing, that the 
President didn't win the election.

  This is the President who has more than 30 felony convictions. Do you 
really believe him when he tells you you are going to destroy the 
Democratic Party or that this is going to cause the MAGA base to do 
something that would be harmful to the Democratic Party?
  This President has no credibility when it comes to speaking truth in 
my world. Now, if you believe the President when he says these things 
that are inane, the kind of inanity that borders on insanity, then you 
are welcome to do so. I choose not to do so, because he has a record of 
being an insidious prevaricator. He prevaricates routinely with a great 
degree of regularity.
  Why would we believe what he says as it relates to what will happen 
to the Democratic Party? Why would we fear a MAGA base because that 
base would become upset if we vote to impeach a President? We should do 
it. We must do it.
  By the way, for those who have some degree of concern, it will be 
done. It is just a matter of time. It will be done. My great regret is 
that I won't be here to cast one of the votes to impeach him at the 
time that we finally conclude that it is okay to do so.
  I have noted that there are many comments that are being made in the 
media about this whole question of impeachment. When these comments are 
made, they will poll people and ask them: Well, what do you think about 
impeachment? They give their commentary. Everybody has a right to give 
their commentary. There is no complaint.
  I do express a scintilla of concern because I notice that they never 
ask the person who has talked about filing the articles and who has 
filed the articles. They don't ask that person about the impeachment. 
They will ask other people--many of whom will give a negative 
commentary; you have every right to give a negative commentary--but 
they won't ask the person who is bringing the Articles of Impeachment. 
This is what I call fearing the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but 
the truth.
  I was a judge for some time, and I recall in court that we would have 
people raise their hands and swear to tell the truth, the whole truth, 
and nothing but the truth. Well, they fear hearing the truth, the whole 
truth, and nothing but the truth, so they don't ask the person who is 
bringing the articles: Why are you doing it? They won't pose all of 
these questions that can be answered to the person who is actually 
bringing the articles.
  I say without question, reservation, or hesitation that they fear the 
person who is bringing the articles because they fear that this person 
will speak a truth that has been crushed to earth. That person will 
reveal the fact that the lack is not going to live forever and that the 
arc of the moral universe can be bent toward justice. That person will 
take the axe of truth, slam it into the tree of circumstance, and let 
the chips fall wherever they may.
  They fear what that person will say. Yes. Yes. I am going to bring 
those articles in spite of the media--the status quo media--that wants 
to make sure that it is only done on their terms and their timeline. 
No, you don't dictate to me. I am Al Green, the unbought, unbossed, 
liberated Democrat. I might also add unelected, but I still speak truth 
to power. I still have no fear of the MAGA base, no fear of the 
President and his men. Maybe I should say the king's men because of the 
authority that we have accorded the President.
  I would now call another concern to your attention, and that is votes 
in the Senate. Well, we can't impeach because the Senate may not 
convict or some people would say won't convict.
  Dear friends, dear brothers and sisters, please understand that the 
Senate ought not dictate the actions of the House. The House of 
Representatives should do its job and then see if the Senate will do 
its job. Do not allow the Senate to become the hidden hand of power 
that controls the House of Representatives. The House should do its 
job--impeach.
  This President doesn't want to be impeached again. Don't let him kid 
you and clown with you about his welcoming impeachment. He doesn't like 
it. He tried to overturn the two impeachments that were already 
presented against him. If he enjoyed being impeached, would he have 
tried to get Congress to rescind the impeachments? Nobody wants to be 
impeached. That is a scarlet letter, the impeachment letter. It is a 
scarlet word, I suppose I should say. He doesn't want that, but he uses 
that as a tactic to say: Do me a favor.
  Well, I will do you the favor. I am going to bring the articles 
against you, Mr. President, because the vote in the Senate does not 
dictate my actions in the House.
  I would also add this: If we followed that line of logic, that we 
will only send things to the Senate that the Senate will approve, such 
that we can become victorious with whatever we send, then we won't send 
a lot of legislation to the Senate in this next Congress. It takes two-
thirds to convict the President in the Senate, and it will take that 
same number to override a President's veto.
  We can pass all of the legislation here in the House and Senate that 
we would like, and if the President, who has a vote in the process, 
uses his pen to veto that legislation, it will take the same number of 
votes to overturn that veto in the Senate that it would take to convict 
the President for his impeachable behavior, his impeachable actions, 
his impeachable offenses. It would take the same number. That line of 
logic is flawed. If you are going to use that line of logic, use it 
consistently.
  By the way, I don't think we should stop sending things to the 
Senate, that it won't pass or that it won't be able to override a veto 
if one is necessary. I don't think we should stop. I think we should do 
it. But I don't think we should have a double standard and decide, 
well, I won't send Articles of Impeachment because they, of course, 
might not pass muster in the Senate.
  Mr. Speaker, may I inquire as to how much time I have remaining.

  Mr. GREEN of Texas. Mr. Speaker, I want to remind people that 
impeachment is a political question.
  Mr. Speaker, I am going to take a moment of my time and deviate 
slightly from the program. I would like to present to Mr. Massie this 
flag that was flown over the Capitol of the United States of America 
for his bravery, courage, and willingness to stand alone, and for his 
proving that on some issues it is better to stand alone than not stand 
at all. I thank my dear brother.
  Now, back to our regularly scheduled program.
  Friends, I was about to explain to you that impeachment is a 
political issue. It is not an issue associated with a judiciary and 
with the legal process that we have when we are in court.
  The grand jury, as it were, would be the House of Representatives, if 
you are going to see it as akin to something, but it is not the same.
  You don't have to conclude that if the Senate won't impeach that you 
don't take up the impeachment in the House. If it is brought to your 
attention in the House, you don't conclude that, because I don't think 
the Senate is going to impeach, I think I will side with the President 
and vote against moving impeachment forward, which is what many people 
do. You are voting to avoid moving the impeachment forward when you 
decide that you are not going to vote to do that, to move the 
impeachment forward, you are siding with the President.
  How do you reconcile that? You think posterity is going to reconcile 
that and conclude that it was okay to say: I think he should be 
impeached, and now that I have the opportunity to express that with my 
vote, I won't do so.

  So because time is running short, I thank all of you who believe that 
we must move forward to do that which Dr. King would say is right 
because the time is always right to do what is right. Those are the 
words of Dr. King.
  Mr. Speaker, as you move close to the microphone to bring my time to 
closure, I appreciate you allowing me to have the time.
  I commend and congratulate Mr. Jeffries. It looks like he will be the 
next Speaker. I congratulate the person who is going to be the next 
minority leader. They are persons who are going to, I am sure, take 
their positions seriously and I take my position seriously. I take my 
job seriously, and I promise you, I am going to work till the very last 
day.
  Mr. Speaker, I yield back the balance of my time.
```
