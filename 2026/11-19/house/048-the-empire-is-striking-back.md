# THE EMPIRE IS STRIKING BACK

**Speaking:** Roy, Chip (R000614) [R-TX], Green, Al (G000553) [D-TX]

**Measures:** H.R. 2

```
                      THE EMPIRE IS STRIKING BACK

  (Under the Speaker's announced policy of January 3, 2025, Mr. Roy of 
Texas was recognized for 30 minutes.)
  Mr. ROY. Mr. Speaker, while I don't agree with my friend from Texas 
on a number of different things, I am always glad to see him come down 
to the House floor because I know he is always down here trying to make 
sure people understand where he is coming from. He uses his floor time 
unlike a lot of our colleagues who want to catch the plane as soon as 
they can or, frankly, in the case of a lot of my Republican colleagues, 
refusing to show up while we are sitting here in November, looking into 
December. We are here supposedly to do the work of the people in the 
people's House.
  Mr. GREEN of Texas. Will the gentleman yield?
  Mr. ROY. I yield to the gentleman from Texas.
  Mr. GREEN of Texas. Mr. Speaker, I will say to dear Mr. Roy that I 
greatly appreciate the commentary that he just gave. I want him to know 
that I am one of those who believes that we have every right to occupy 
this space and to call things to the attention of the public that 
others may not. I see him as being sincere in his efforts and that 
means a lot to me.
  He speaks from his heart as well as his head, but from his heart and 
head, and I thank him for it.
  Mr. Speaker, I thank him for his commentary.
  Mr. ROY. Mr. Speaker, I thank the gentleman and my friend from Texas 
for his words.
  Mr. Speaker, I note that some piece of advice that I give to every 
incoming freshman Member is that you have an election certificate that 
reflects the 750-odd thousand people that you represent and that 
election certificate belongs to those people.
  It does not belong to the Speaker. It does not belong to the leader, 
majority or minority leader. It does not belong to some committee 
chairman. It doesn't belong to some steering committee. It doesn't 
belong to any of the other long line of butt-kissing, D.C. swamp-
oriented nonsense when everybody comes to this town to turn over their 
election certificate to somebody else.
  You have a duty representing 750,000 people to be here on the floor 
of the House. Right now, a Republican majority House and a Republican 
majority Senate is ready to tuck tail and run and head home. Half of 
the people aren't showing up. What bills we are putting on the floor 
don't matter to the American people.
  We could be debating right now a bill to ban congressional stock 
trading among Members of Congress so they do not enrich themselves when 
they vote on the matters they were sent here to vote upon. We are not 
doing that. We are heading home. It is Thursday at noon. Thanksgiving 
is not for another week.
  Every other American today is at work. Every other American tomorrow 
is at work and over the weekend. Every other American on Monday, 
Tuesday, and Wednesday, and, in some cases, some people are going to be 
working through next weekend, but Congress is adjourning. We are going 
to take off. We are not going to come back until the first week of 
December, and then what is going to happen?
  I will be very clear for everybody observing what is about to happen 
in December. For a few years now, for all of our faults and inability 
to do the things the American people sent us here to do, and there are 
many failures, we have at least managed to hold discretionary spending 
basically flat. We have at least managed to try to cut back some of the 
Federal bureaucracy. We at least managed to try to reduce mandatory 
spending by about $1.7 trillion, to reduce subsidies of unreliable 
energy that is destroying our grid and raising the price of energy. We 
have done some good things.
  But guess what. If you are a ``Star Wars'' fan, the empire is about 
to strike back, and the empire is the swamp. The empire is that which 
gave us the $42 trillion of debt, the rampant growth of the 
bureaucracy, the regulatory state that crushes small business, the laws 
and regulations and rules that favor corporate America over small 
business Main Street, and the average American family, and everybody 
knows this is true.
  Every American knows this is true. Every American who is suffering 
right now, unable to afford food, housing, and healthcare, and be able 
to put their kids in a school that they can actually believe in, every 
American feels that right now. What is this body doing? Look at the 
empty Chamber.
  The people's House in response to the people sending a message in 
November, it is empty at noon on Thursday. That is embarrassing. The 
empire is going to strike back. We are already seeing rumors in the 
media, rumors in all of the message threads among Members here that a 
big omnibus bill awaits on the horizon, that the Senate and House and 
all the powers that be in this town are working to concoct in the name 
of national defense, a big omnibus spending bill to make sure that 
defense gets funded. But guess what, that probably means a gravy train 
of other things. That is what is coming in December.
  When we have heard from our leadership, we won't do a Christmas tree 
omnibus bill before Christmas. We don't do that, not in our majority. 
Well, the Senate doesn't seem poised to try to get in front of that. 
Republicans aren't in this Chamber. So what is going to stop a big, 
bloated bill that is going to be a Christmas tree to put a bunch of 
legislation on it and spend money on defense that is unpaid for 
without, by the way, a serious debate ever in this Chamber--as my 
friend from Kentucky often raises--about war powers.
  This Congress has never voted on what is going on in Iran. We have 
never

[[Page H6312]]

had an up-or-down vote on it. My friend from Kentucky would argue we 
have had some votes on War Powers Resolutions, and he would be correct.
  Some of us struggle with those votes because we don't like to vote in 
the negative because the war powers construct is, frankly, reversed. It 
is basically saying the Commander in Chief takes action, Congress then 
comes in and we say: Oh, no, we don't like it so pull back.
  Some of us don't want to vote against our men and women in uniform 
who are in harm's way, sending a message to the world while they are in 
theater, but it is a reasonable point.
  Article I is supposed to mean something. Congress is supposed to 
actually have a voice. To declare war is supposed to mean something, 
even in the 21st century, even if it is not blue coats and red coats.
  Congress is supposed to check the executive branch. And, frankly, we 
haven't. We are told: trust us. There is bad stuff going on, so you 
must vote this way or we are going to do it anyway.
  The intelligence community on this big, bloated omnibus bill, what do 
you think is going to happen? The Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act 
is going to ride along in the name of defense, in the name of AI or 
cyberattacks. They are going to try to ram through another FISA 
reauthorization without protecting the American people with the 
requirements of warrants or any other protection against the power of 
government being used to target American citizens. That is what is 
going to happen.
  The swamp empire is going to strike back. They are going to keep 
spending more money to keep having more government power to ignore the 
Constitution because that is what this town is wired to do.
  With all due respect to this administration, which has done a number 
of things of which I am proud and I support, it is a part of that 
empire striking back right now, wanting a massive plus-up in defense 
spending. Which, by the way, I might support if we would lay it all out 
there. Let's stop being engaged in an endless war without congressional 
authorization, walk me through what we need to do to have our munitions 
replaced, our drones modernized, our Navy modernized, our Space Force 
empowered, all the things we think we need to do to combat 
cyberattacks. I am all in in figuring out how to defend the people of 
the United States and to figure out what is needed to fund it.

  I am not in for a blank check to use, in the name of war, the power 
of government to target the American people, to rack up debt, and to be 
used as an excuse by my colleagues on the other side of the aisle to 
have an expansive welfare state in trade for defense spending, because 
that is what is coming. That is what is going to happen.
  The fact of the matter is that the people sent a loud-and-clear 
signal in November that they want their lives back. They want the 
American Dream back. They want to be able to ensure that their children 
and their grandchildren will be able to buy a home, get married, have 
children, put them in a school that is not corrupting them or leaving 
them behind, be protected, and be secure. They want to be able to have 
a community they live in that they can trust and enjoy and not just be 
slaves to a government-corporate crony welfare state that prohibits 
their very ability from achieving the American Dream.
  The election wasn't just about the Iran war and diesel and gas 
prices. It was. That was a piece of it, but that was proxy for an 
American people who are frustrated with the powers that be focusing on 
the powers that be. They are sick of it.
  Last week on this floor, I gave a speech about my concern about how 
we focus so much on ``economic growth,'' and all the eggheads in this 
town and all the think tanks in this town went apoplectic: Chip Roy is 
a populist who opposes economic growth. Yes, that is clearly what my 
voting record and clearly what my positions have always indicated.
  If I dare stand here and say perhaps there should be a party, some 
party somewhere, that is focused on the people, local and small 
businesses, and families before focusing on earnings per share or 
random economic growth measures--which, by the way, to all of you 
eggheads out there, if you know that most of our economic growth in 
recent years has been on the back of a bloated healthcare industry and 
on the back of government spending often, and not actually on the back 
of a true, genuine, community-based economic development from the 
grassroots up, from the people up, from communities up, if you know 
that to be true, then why don't you, like, shut your pie hole for just 
a second with your ivory tower, Washington, D.C., nonsense and focus on 
the people?
  Get out of the bubble. Go home somewhere. ``But my home is in D.C.'' 
I know. That is part of the problem.
  The richest city in the world is living off of the back of this God-
forsaken town, and the God-forsaken people in this body and in the 
other body that spend money that they don't have to rack up debt, to 
bankrupt our kids and grandkids, while the people in this town get rich 
off of the back of it, including the defense lobbyists who no doubt 
right now really want a massive defense spending bill, including all 
the insurance companies and all the hospitals that right now want to 
get rich off of whatever this body and the Senate and this 
administration are going to do in the name of healthcare.
  Do you think Medicare for All is about helping people? It is about 
continuing the enrichment of these massive corporations that are 
nothing more than crony government entities screwing the average 
American out of the ability to go to the doctor of his or her choice.
  ObamaCare was a complete and total failure. Did Republicans campaign 
on that for 1 second this year and talk about the plight of an American 
right now who cannot go to the doctor of his or her choice, who cannot 
go to MD Anderson if they get cancer like I did 15 years ago, who can't 
afford the insurance premiums which aren't really insurance but are 
managed care to subsidize a broken healthcare system that is 
bankrupting all of us?
  My kids' school can't afford insurance to pay for the teachers' 
healthcare, and everybody in this God-forsaken town keeps getting 
richer. We all act like it is not happening. We all turn a blind eye.
  I went home to Texas to run for attorney general because I saw what 
was happening in Texas. Austin is no better than Washington. The Austin 
swamp is every bit as corrupted and self-enriching of the very power 
brokers as is this place, after 30 years of Republican one-party rule.
  Republicans got sent home in Texas because they forgot the people. 
Republicans got sent home in Texas because they got fat and happy, felt 
it was perfectly okay to nominate somebody scandal-ridden for the 
United States Senate, and have people running for a fourth term to 
continue to perpetuate this notion that Texas was somehow free from the 
very grift that we point at Washington and complain about.
  This Nation is crumbling under the weight of people spending other 
people's money and ignoring that we are, in fact, a nation and a people 
worthy of defense. When I say that we shouldn't be a Republican Party 
or even a body that focuses on economic growth, I don't mean we don't 
want economic growth. Literally, that is just stupid. Of course we do.
  If you lose sight of who we are as people, if you lose sight of your 
community, if you lose sight of your churches, if you lose sight of the 
veteran down the street because you expect the VA to solve the problem, 
if you lose sight of caring for somebody, your family member, and 
instead ship them off to a nursing home, then you have lost sight of 
who we are as a people.
  America is the greatest country in the history of the world because 
we have empowered people. We are the greatest country in the history of 
the world when we make government the least consequential it can be in 
your life.
  You should be able to start a business without checking the codes. 
You should be able to start a business without competing against large 
corporations that have massive-scale benefits and can hire attorneys to 
beat back the regulatory state. You should be able to

[[Page H6313]]

go to any doctor you want to, and you should be able to go into the 
marketplace and get real insurance against risk, which we have outlawed 
in the supposedly freest country in the history of the world. We are 
not.
  You should be able to afford a home that isn't overregulated and the 
ability to build a home without it costing a fortune. You should be 
able to borrow money without it being inflated and having high interest 
rates because this body and the Senate and the President have spent 
money we don't have.

  You should be able to believe that your child will go to a school 
that isn't indoctrinating them to hate their country. You should be 
able to believe that this Nation will remember why ``In God We Trust'' 
is emblazoned above the head of the Speaker and above the American 
flag, and why Moses is looking out over the Chamber.
  It is who we are. We were built upon the principles of Western 
civilization. We were built upon Judeo-Christian values and principles. 
The bond of those principles, with those articulated in the Declaration 
of Independence, the Constitution, and the Bill of Rights, is what made 
this country so strong for almost a quarter of a millennium.
  It was those things that empowered and gave hope to the men who sat 
in the foxholes in Bastogne, stormed the beaches of Normandy, and bled 
the ground red at Gettysburg, Antietam, and all the battlefields across 
our country.
  Right now, Republicans, who were just sent home--do you know what the 
biggest fight raging right now among Republicans in the minority is? 
Anybody know? It is not about getting the stock trading bill on the 
floor. It is not about how to get a reconciliation package to the floor 
to figure out how, if you are going to fund defense, to actually pay 
for it. It is not about passing a term limits bill. I assure you, they 
are not going to want to do that. It is not about passing anything to 
balance the budget.

                              {time}  1240

  No. They are having a full-throated debate in the Republican 
Conference, your Republican Conference, about rules and how they need 
to punish Members of this body if they dare vote against a rule.
  Now, what does that mean for the average viewer out there? The rule 
is when the majority says: ``Hey, this is what we are going to put on 
the House floor,'' and they put it altogether, and they put it on the 
House floor.
  If you are a single Member of the House, you have no real voice in a 
majoritarian body. In the Senate, you have some voice. However, if you 
have enough people who believe that the rule is not the business that 
you think we should be proceeding to, then you can vote ``no.'' 
Because, by definition, you have the right to use the election 
certificate that I described before--that does not belong to the 
Speaker and does not belong to the party; it belongs to the people--to 
say no, we should not proceed to that matter. We should proceed to this 
other matter.
  Or, as my friend from Kentucky and I, serving on the Rules Committee, 
tried to do--succeeded for a while and failed over time--to say that 
maybe we should put something on the floor, but only if the people and 
the Members of this body can amend it and can have a voice.
  So in this Congress, there were, by my count, four times that a rule 
was delayed--three times it was delayed; one it went away--because some 
of us raised a concern.
  Well, let me give you an example. We were going to proceed to a bill 
this last July, probably a bill that I wasn't overly excited about to 
be honest with you, but you normally just go along with the team. We 
had stipulated for the entirety of this Congress that we should pass a 
codification of President Trump's border security policies that were 
reflected in H.R. 2 in the previous Congress: That is, stop the abuse 
of parole, stop the abuse of asylum, stop catch and release, stop the 
abuse of unaccompanied alien children. We have legislation to do those 
things that are overwhelmingly popular. We had not even brought it 
through the committee and to the floor of the House.
  So myself and some of my colleagues objected to the rule. Guess what? 
We forced that bill to get voted out of the Judiciary Committee.
  Now, I am willing to wager what little congressional salary I have 
left for the remainder of this year with any of my colleagues if they 
want to come down to the floor of the House--I don't think they will, 
but I will debate anybody on the floor of the House anytime your 
position of why we shouldn't do that and why I shouldn't have stood up 
to say we are not going to pass this rule if we are not going to do the 
things we told the American people we were going to do.
  I have news for my colleagues. I am going to leave the Chamber, for 
now, but I am not leaving the body politic. I might be unleashed to say 
what I really think in January.
  I have colleagues in this Chamber who campaign on border security, go 
home to their districts and talk about border security, but refused to 
do the thing we told them we would do, which is to codify and change 
the law to secure the border of the United States, and they failed. And 
they got their butts handed to them and sent out of the majority.
  And, now, they want to complain about rules. They want to complain 
about the ability of a Member of this body to work with other Members 
to say: You know what, we have a voice in what should be brought to the 
floor of the House.
  My election certificate belongs to the people in the 21st District of 
Texas. It does not belong to the Speaker. It does not belong to the 
leader. It does not belong to a chairman. It belongs to the people.
  I can assure you, for my remaining 45 days, I am going to use it that 
way. I can assure you that, in the future, we are going to have eyes on 
this institution, because it is our House.
  I am sick and tired of us mortgaging the future of our kids and 
grandkids, turning over our country to 53 million foreign-born people 
that are fundamentally changing the entirety of our body, and then 
going out and raising money to campaign that they are going to do 
something different.
  We spent $500 million to get our butts kicked in Texas, $250 million 
trying to prop up a U.S. Senate candidate, which was nakedly, 
obviously, highly flawed, and then wonder why.
  This town reeks of corruption. This town reeks of self-dealing.
  You want to know why your MAHA agenda hasn't gotten fully 
implemented? Because of money.
  I don't know whether I will be able to come back to the House floor 
again before this body closes down. We can't get a quorum, so Lord 
knows whether we are even going to be back here. And if we are back 
here, Lord knows whether they will give me the floor again.
  I thank profusely the people who work hard to make this body work.
  I thank the floor staff. I thank the Office of the Parliamentarian. 
There are no finer people than the people who are down here on a 
nonpartisan basis serving the people here and serving the House floor.
  I thank the Capitol Police. I thank the people who keep us safe.
  I thank all the people who make this institution work as well as it 
can despite the fecklessness of the people elected who are supposed to 
be on this floor and are too interested in being on their planes to go 
home.
  I will be here every day until January 3 if the body is open.
  I am deeply appreciative of all the staff who have ever worked for 
me. I could start naming them, but I am going to leave them out. I have 
had the best staff on Capitol Hill. We all think that, but I would 
match cards with anybody.
  We all depend on a whole lot of other people, and we depend on our 
families. I am deeply thankful to my wife and to my children. Part of 
the reason I am going home is so that they can have a husband and a 
father at home rather than being on the road.
  I am thankful for the trust that the people of the 21st District 
placed in me to represent them.
  I don't possibly believe I got every vote right, every position 
right, every speech right, or every statement right; but I have thrown 
everything I know into trying to make this body work for them. We made 
a lot of progress, but we are nowhere near where we need to be.
  We are entrusted with carrying this institution and this country 
forward. We are entrusted with preserving and

protecting the Constitution, and I hope that my colleagues will 
remember that. I hope that the House, both Republicans and Democrats, 
will remember that. It is your election certificate on behalf of your

to preserve and protect the Constitution and fight for the people who 
sent you here. Do it. Fight for them.
  I thank my friend from Kentucky, who I have been proud to serve with. 
He is a good friend and a good man. And Texas bourbon is still good.
  Mr. Speaker, I yield back the balance of my time.
```
