# CELEBRATING WESLEY UNITED METHODIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
               CELEBRATING WESLEY UNITED METHODIST CHURCH

                                  _____
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, September 3, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to celebrate a historic 
congregation in my District, Wesley United Methodist Church in Baton 
Rouge, Louisiana, as it commemorates 160 years of faith, freedom, and 
service to our community. John Wesley, founder of the Methodist 
movement, was deeply troubled by the cruelty of slavery, calling it 
``the vilest that ever saw the sun,'' and vowed that no Methodist 
church would be established in a region that condoned such an inhumane 
practice. His conviction drew many Black believers to Methodism and its 
core belief that every person is created equal in the eyes of God.
  From those ideals of freedom, equality, and service, Wesley United 
Methodist Church, originally known as Wesley Chapel, was born. After 
the Emancipation Proclamation, newly freed African Americans in Baton 
Rouge sought to establish sacred spaces of their own, and in May 1866, 
William D. Mann purchased land in Beauregard Town, one of the city's 
oldest and most historic neighborhoods. With saws and axes in hand, 
Wesley's founders cut trees, floated logs down the Mississippi River to 
the Harbor Sawmill, and built the first structure of Wesley Chapel 
Methodist Episcopal Church. Even before that building rose, the people 
of Wesley worshipped on the same ground the present structure stands 
upon, a reminder that the Holy Spirit has made a people to worship God, 
not a building.
  The original church stood strong for more than eighty years through 
fires, storms, and time itself, and as the congregation flourished, it 
became Wesley Methodist Church in 1939 and constructed a new sanctuary 
in 1966 to accommodate its growing membership. The church has been 
blessed with over fifty ministers and a congregation that includes more 
than fifty-fIve members who have each served faithfully for over fifty 
years. Wesley also holds a unique distinction as the only African 
American church in Baton Rouge to house a pipe organ, and its stained-
glass window, designed for full visibility from top to bottom, serves 
as the radiant heart of a sanctuary furnished largely by gifts lovingly 
donated by members.
  On Founders' Day this year, the congregation honors the pioneers who 
built Wesley on a foundation of faith, love, and resilience, a legacy 
that lives on in every hymn, every prayer, and every act of service 
that continues to bless the community. It remains inspired by the 
timeless words of John Wesley, which continue to guide its mission 
today: Do all the good you can, by all the means you can, in all the 
ways you can, in all the places you can, at all the times you can, to 
all the people you can, as long as ever you can.
  Mr. Speaker, Wesley United Methodist Church is led today by Reverend 
Dr. Jonathan C. Richardson, and I ask my colleagues to join me in 
celebrating the church on the occasion of its 160th anniversary, and in 
wishing its pastor and congregation continued blessings in the years to 
come.
```
