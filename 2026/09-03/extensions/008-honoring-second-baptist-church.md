# HONORING SECOND BAPTIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Fields, Cleo (F000110) [D-LA]

```
                     HONORING SECOND BAPTIST CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. CLEO FIELDS

                              of louisiana

                    in the house of representatives

                      Thursday, September 3, 2026

  Mr. FIELDS. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Second Baptist Church, 
located on North Acadian Thruway West, Baton Rouge, Louisiana, for its 
faith and community strength since its founding in 1920. The origin of 
Second Baptist Church began under the leadership of the late Reverend 
William Hall. The church's original name was Hall's Chapel Baptist 
Church and was subsequently changed to Hall's Second Baptist Church, 
with its first location in the 600 block of Eureka Street, now known as 
North Acadian Thruway West. Though membership initially consisted of 
about thirty members and the church building was small, it quickly 
became known as ``the church with a lot of fire.''
  After Reverend Hall was called home to glory in 1922, Reverend Edward 
Doyle (E.D.) Billoups was chosen as Pastor. Under his outstanding 
leadership, the church was remodeled at a cost of $3,030.00, which 
included land and building, and the debt was liquidated over nine 
years. Two more lots were purchased, and Hall's Second Baptist Church 
was built, along with an educational building

[[Page E856]]

now serving as the Food Pantry & Clothing Distribution Center adjacent 
to the church.
  In 1961, Reverend Leo E. Billoups was named Assistant Pastor, and 
Reverend E.D. Billoups became Pastor Emeritus. The church moved to a 
new location to build a one-story building for greater accessibility. 
After Reverend Leo E. Billoups's passing, Reverend W. Marshall Myles 
served as Assistant Pastor.
  From 1983 to 1986, following Reverend E.D. Billoups's retirement, 
Reverend Leo D. Cyrus, Sr. was chosen as Pastor. During his tenure, a 
Fellowship Hall was added to the Sanctuary, and the SBC Christian 
Acaderny was formed, initially using one room in Fellowship Hall. Due 
to substantial enrollment, the Academy eventually moved to the SBC 
Unity Center, a two-story multi-purpose facility. Second Baptist Church 
was one of the original sites for the Fourth District Missionary 
Baptist Congress of Christian Education Institute, hosting leadership 
training and Christian Education classes.
  Between 1991 and 1995, the church implemented the SBC Christian 
Education Institute, the Gertrude S. Hayes Memorial Scholarship Fund, 
the Evangelistic and Street Witnessing Ministry, and the Prison 
Ministry. In 1995, the church's name was formally changed to ``Second 
Baptist Church,'' retaining its slogan: ``You're in a Church Where 
You're Never A Stranger, and It's Always a Pleasure to Have You Come.''
  From 2001 to 2009, the Deaconess Ministries were combined, and 
members adopted a Mission and Vision Statement to be recited at 
services and church activities. In August 2005, Second Baptist Church 
assisted families affected by Hurricane Katrina, hosting mid-week and 
Sunday Worship Services for First Emmanuel Baptist Church of New 
Orleans for over a year at no charge. In 2008, the church partnered 
with local, federal, and national organizations to provide supplies for 
those affected by Hurricane Gustav. In June 2009, Second Baptist Church 
became part of a historic cooperative with the Southern Baptists and 
New Orleans Baptist Theological Seminary, serving as a host site for 
weekly classes and theological education opportunities in Baton Rouge, 
bridging racial and denominational lines.
  Second Baptist Church's ministries and affiliations are extensive and 
impactful. They include Adult, Young Adult, and Youth Ushers, Pastor's 
Support Group, Mission, Flower Circle, Youth and Media Ministry, 
Brotherhood and Women's Ministry, Health & Wellness Ministry, Food 
Distribution, Mass Choir, Male Chorus, Women's & Youth Choir, Hunts 
Correctional Facility Worship, and Foreign Mission Ministry in places 
such as St. Kitts and St. Dominique. The church supports institutions 
of higher learning and nonprofits including Southern University, Sickle 
Cell Anemia Foundation, American Heart Association, United Way, 
Interfaith Federation of Churches and Synagogues, and the American 
Cancer Society. These ministries and partnerships reflect the church's 
mission to ``minister to the needs and hopes of the total community by 
encouraging and exalting one another in Christian love.''
  From 2010 to 2016, Pastor Cyrus and other leaders organized ``The 
Greater Louisiana Baptist State Convention,'' operating under the 
guidance of the National Baptist Convention of the U.S.A., Inc. 
Reverend Cyrus served as Convention President, and Second Baptist 
Church, along with New Hope Baptist Church, was selected as an official 
FEMA site to assist residents impacted by the 2016 flood. In 2019, 
Reverend Cyrus added Reverend Samuel Calvin Lofton from Lafayette, 
Louisiana, to the Ministerial Staff as an Associate Minister, 
faithfully conducting and teaching weekly Prayer Services, Bible Study, 
and other church functions.
  During the COVID-19 pandemic from March to December 2020, Second 
Baptist Church suspended in-person services but established live 
streaming via Zoom, Facebook, and other media outlets. The church also 
distributed food and supplies to residents of Lake Charles affected by 
Hurricane Laura. By mid-June 2021, in-person worship resumed, with 
continued online streaming. After Hurricane Ida, the church partnered 
with New Hope and Southern University Athletics Department to provide 
disaster relief for residents of New Orleans and Laplace, Louisiana, 
and volunteered to serve hot meals at St. Peter Catholic Church in 
Reserve, Louisiana.
  In early 2025, after much prayer and God's guidance, Reverend Cyrus 
announced his retirement, concluding more than 41 years of service at 
Second Baptist and over 48 years at New Hope. On March 21, 2025, both 
churches hosted a retirement celebration in his honor, with reflections 
from area Pastors, Convention leaders, and community figures. On April 
13, 2025, Reverend Samuel Calvin Lofton accepted the call to Pastoral 
Leadership and was officially installed as Pastor-Teacher on June 22, 
2025. Pastor Lofton is recognized for his engaging, practical teaching 
and preaching style, presenting God's Word with clarity, simplicity, 
and conviction, making it accessible and applicable to all.
  Today, Second Baptist Church continues its mission as a mighty 
fortress and guiding light to all mankind, striving to ensure 
preaching, teaching, and worship remain heartfelt and genuine. The 
church's Holy Spirit motivates sincere devotion, touching the hearts of 
those who seek Christ. Second Baptist Church remains steadfast and 
unmovable, faithful to Christ, reaching the community, and relevant to 
the culture, as inspired by Acts 2:42-47.
  Mr. Speaker, I encourage my colleagues to join me in celebrating 
Second Baptist Church, Pastor Samuel Calvin Lofton, and the devoted 
congregation for over a century of enduring legacy and ongoing impact. 
Their unwavering commitment to service and leadership continues to 
inspire and uplift families across Baton Rouge and East Baton Rouge 
Parish, leaving a legacy for generations to come.
```
