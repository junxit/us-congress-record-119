# RECOGNIZING FELICIA PARK-ROGERS FOR HER SERVICE TO THE UNITED STATES HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES

**Speaking:** Nadler, Jerrold (N000002) [D-NY]

```
 RECOGNIZING FELICIA PARK-ROGERS FOR HER SERVICE TO THE UNITED STATES 
                        HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. JERROLD NADLER

                              of new york

                    in the house of representatives

                       Friday, November 20, 2026

  Mr. NADLER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Felicia Park-
Rogers for her years of outstanding service to my office and to the 
constituents of New York's 12th Congressional District.
  A native of San Francisco, Felicia was raised in an atmosphere full 
of social activism, which set her off on a career path that would blend 
her deep caring of people, passion for advocacy, and ability to procure 
strategic wins and results. She attended Macalester College in Saint 
Paul, Minnesota, where she pursued a degree in Women's Studies.
  Early in her career she served as Executive Director of COLAGE, the 
only national organization dedicated to supporting people with one or 
more LGBTQ+ parents or caregivers, at a time where the community 
experienced high levels of discrimination. Under her leadership COLAGE 
expanded and strengthened the national network for LGBTQ+ individuals 
and families, shifting the narrative and empowering the young people to 
become self-confident and leaders for equal rights in their communities 
creating many laws protecting LGBTQ+ families and the right to same 
gender marriage.
  Felicia brought this love of community, skill for building 
empowerment, and implementing a long-term vision to Los Angeles when 
she became the Executive Director of Beth Chayim Chadashim, the world's 
first LGBTQ+ synagogue. While there she became an integral part of Los 
Angeles's progressive Jewish community.
  Felicia first entered government work as the Senior Field Deputy for 
Sheila James Kuehl, a member of the Los Angeles County Board of 
Supervisors. In addition to honing her deep relationships with the 
Jewish and LGBTQ+ constituents, Felicia's work on the Los Angeles light 
rail expansion projects sparked her passion for transportation. She saw 
firsthand how a single transit line could reshape neighborhoods, 
connect people to opportunity, reduce climate change, and change how a 
city moves.
  Following that calling, Felicia moved with her family to New York 
City in 2015 and earned a master's degree in urban planning from Hunter 
College, with an emphasis on transportation and infrastructure policy 
advocacy. Her studies gave her the technical grounding to match her 
field experience, and she soon put both to work in one of the country's 
most complex transportation systems serving at the Tri-State 
Transportation Campaign.
  As the Regional Director, she oversaw campaigns supporting congestion 
pricing, the Gateway Hudson Tunnel Project (the most important 
infrastructure project in the Northeast), Penn Station redesign, and 
MTA funding and improvement projects, among other priorities. Notably 
she was one of the leaders that helped secure $100 billion dollars for 
public transit funding in the 2021 Infrastructure Investment and Jobs 
Act, bringing mass transit back from the brink of disaster in the wake 
of the COVID-19 epidemic.
  In 2024, Felicia became the District Director for my office, bringing 
every chapter of her career to the role. Arriving at the outset of the 
second Trump administration, Felicia spearheaded our office's 
community-centered response initiatives such as Know Your Rights 
Workshops, Town Halls, and Mobile Constituent Services Sessions to 
ensure that our constituents' concerns could be directly heard and 
addressed by myself and staff. She was able to mobilize our team 
quickly to consistently respond to the rapidly changing realities on 
the ground, keeping a cool-headed demeanor and centering our work on 
improving the quality of life for the residents of New York's 12th 
Congressional District.
  Felicia and I share the deep belief that transportation and 
infrastructure shapes daily life for New York City residents. She 
understands the long, arduous task of taking the region's biggest 
projects from idea to reality. She was indispensable in my office's 
efforts to support and advocate for local community transportation 
legislation, including Sammy's Law that will make New York's streets 
safer for pedestrians and cyclists, as well as the implementation of 
congestion pricing that delivers cleaner air, transit funding, and less 
gridlock for millions of New Yorkers.
  Felicia's invaluable contributions to my office went well beyond her 
title. Her public service is grounded in her ability to recognize 
humanity in everyone she interacts with and constantly looks for 
innovative ways to support their needs. Constituents who sought help 
from our office found in Felicia someone who would readily use every 
tool available to her to assist while staff found a supportive manager 
who was always approachable, fair, and invested in their professional 
growth and success. Her affable, warm nature is infectious, becoming a 
beloved colleague among staff, other Congressional aides, and community 
leaders. She embodies all the characteristics of an ideal public 
servant, and I was lucky to have her lead my district team in the 
waning days of my tenure in public service.
  In her free time, Felicia enjoys spending time with her kids, Eitan 
and Benji, and her dog, Bo. She knits to relax, is a voracious reader, 
New York City infrastructure nerd, and is also an avid moviegoer who 
watches every award-nominated film. Even away from the job, she enjoyed 
keeping up with the latest transportation developments in the city and 
around the country.

exemplary service and commend her for a job well done.
```
