# Tribute to Amy France (Executive Calendar)

**Speaking:** Moran, Jerry (M000934) [R-KS], Whitehouse, Sheldon (W000802) [D-RI]

```
                         Tribute to Amy France

  Mr. MORAN. Madam President, I rise this afternoon here in the Senate 
to recognize an individual and express my gratitude. That individual is 
Amy France, who recently concluded her time as the chair of the 
National Grain Sorghum Producers.
  I had the opportunity to work closely with Amy during her time as 
chair. Amy served in this role for 3 years and displayed a tremendously 
strong commitment to advocating for the men and women who help drive 
American agriculture.
  Before becoming the organization's first woman chair, she spent years 
advancing its mission through her service as a member of its national 
board beginning in 2018.
  Amy's leadership strengthened the organization, elevated the voice of 
sorghum producers, and helped make certain Kansas agriculture was well 
represented at the national level.
  Amy and her husband Clint are third-generation farmers and ranchers 
in western Kansas who exemplify what it means to be hard-working 
stewards of the land. While she did not grow up in an agricultural 
background, she was fully dedicated to the daily operations of her farm 
and the broader mission of agricultural advocacy.
  As someone who joined the industry of agriculture, despite not being 
raised in it, she has a unique ability to connect with those unfamiliar 
with agriculture, with farmers, with rural America. This perspective, 
like her many other skills, helps to make her a better champion for the 
producers she represents.
  We have had the opportunity to work together on issues that affect 
grain sorghum growers such as the agricultural issues that are 
particular to that crop, including the farm bill's year-round E15 and 
my legislation to permanently transfer Food for Peace to the U.S. 
Department of Agriculture. Recently, Amy was on site in Hugoton when--
as part of the Food for Peace Program--400,000 bushels of grain sorghum 
were loaded on train cars bound for the coast and, ultimately, for 
Africa.
  She has been a bug in my ear numerous times, on numerous occasions, 
to make sure that, as we negotiate trade agreements--particularly with 
China--that grain sorghum, along with soybeans, is at the forefront of 
those negotiations.
  Our markets are hugely important to all of agriculture. Our world 
markets are hugely important to all of agriculture. But grain sorghum 
and China are significant--as they work together, as they come 
together--to make sure there are markets for what we produce in Kansas.
  We have had a long history of fighting hunger in our State, and grain 
sorghum has been a part of that. I am grateful for her efforts in this 
support of Food for Peace.
  Her example embodies what American farmers do so well: They feed a 
hungry world.
  It is a noble profession, and she exemplifies that. Amy's leadership, 
voice, and advocacy continue to make a meaningful difference for 
farmers and ranchers in Kansas and across the Nation. I am confident 
that her efforts will continue to shape the industry and that Kansas 
and American producers will rely upon her advice for years to come.
  She is a wonderful person. She has a great family. And it is an honor 
to be here on the Senate floor to recognize her support for our 
neighboring farmers and for the United States of America as we compete 
in a world of agriculture and feeding that hungry world.
  I yield the floor.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from Ohio.
```
