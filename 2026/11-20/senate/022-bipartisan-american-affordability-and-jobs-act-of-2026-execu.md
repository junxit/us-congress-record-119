# Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026 (Executive Calendar)

**Speaking:** Capito, Shelley Moore (C001047) [R-WV], Whitehouse, Sheldon (W000802) [D-RI]

```
         Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026

  Mrs. CAPITO. Mr. President, last night, the Senate passed the 
Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026, historic 
legislation that makes generational reforms to our energy, 
environmental review, and permitting statutes that will benefit our 
Nation for years to come.
  It took tremendous effort from many people to put together true 
bipartisan legislation of this magnitude that could pass the body. And 
as you know, and certainly Senator Whitehouse in the Chamber would 
know, this was a labor of more than a year.
  So I wanted to take this opportunity to recognize and thank those who 
played such an important role in the development and passage of this 
legislation.
  First, I want to thank all of my colleagues, including you, Mr. 
President, who voted for this bill.
  I want to thank the President for his leadership and support during 
this effort.

[[Page S5833]]

  I want to thank Secretary Doug Burgum and Secretary Chris Wright who 
played critical roles in the passage of this bill. I thank them both 
for their work and their staff. They were critical. I wouldn't say it 
was smooth sailing all the way through, but they were an important 
part.
  In the Trump administration, I would like to thank Jarrod Agen, 
Director of the National Energy Dominance Council. Jarrod was there 
with us every step of the way, as well as Blake Deeley, Nick Elliott, 
and Nick Gallagher of the NEDC staff.
  Former Council on Environmental Quality Chairman Katherine Scarlett, 
who is an alum of my staff, and Justin Schwab and Katie Devlin at CEQ 
were instrumental in the development of this bill and EPA Assistant 
Administrator for Water Jess Kramer, another one of my alum. I also 
thank Jess for her efforts and her expertise that helped us get here.
  I appreciate former White House Director of Legislative Affairs James 
Braid, current OLA Director Katie Bailey and Chad Yelinski and Will 
Mascaro of the OLA team for their hard work and support for this 
effort.
  I think by the time I get through all of these thank-yous, you can 
see what a massive effort this really was.
  I appreciate the strong partnership and support of the House Natural 
Resources Committee Chairman Bruce Westerman--we have already been in 
communication this morning--and his staff led by staff director Vivian 
Moeglein for their work in passing the SPEED Act last year and for 
working with us on a number of provisions that are included in this 
bill.
  Similarly, I would like to thank my House classmate Chairman Sam 
Graves and his staff led by Nick Christensen for their work on the 
PERMIT Act that served as a basis for the Clean Water Act permitting 
provisions in this bill.
  I also appreciate my friend Chairman Brett Guthrie's contributions to 
this legislation. There were many House Democrats that weighed in on 
this as well. I would like, in particular because of the many 
conversations that we had, to thank my fellow Duke alum Scott Peters 
for his help, and I am counting on his future help.
  In the Senate, several of my Senate Republican colleagues played 
important roles in the multiyear development of the bill's EPW 
division. Senators Kevin Cramer and Dan Sullivan have been champions of 
permitting reform legislation with me on the Environment and Public 
Works Committee for years. Their fingerprints are all over the bill.
  Our package was strengthened by legislation from  John Curtis and 
Cynthia Lummis. I also appreciate certainly Senator Alan Armstrong and 
Dave McCormick for their efforts.
  I want to thank the talented attorneys in the Senate legislative 
counsel's office who drafted and redrafted and redrafted sections of 
this legislation, often late at night under very tight deadlines.
  I appreciate the work of SLC attorneys Karson Katz, Mark Mazzone, 
Chris Patterson, Heather Burnham, Taylor Newman, and Heather Lowell.
  I appreciate the support of majority leader John Thune and his 
staff--certainly his chief Geoff Antell, David Adkins, Tyler Owens, and 
David Cole for putting this legislation on the floor and for also 
giving us space. I know he talked to everybody involved with this to 
try to work out our differences.
  I would like to thank the whip John Barrasso. He began this journey 
over on ENR, and he was very helpful in getting us across the finish 
line.

  I also want to thank the Democrat leader Chuck Schumer for his 
support on this bill.
  I want to thank the Republican floor staff here, especially Robert 
Duncan and Chris Tuck for their tremendous help in managing this 
process.
  I also want to thank the Energy and Natural Resources Committee staff 
and the Environment and Public Works Committee, which I chair, on both 
sides of the aisle for their tireless effort that went into producing 
this bill.
  I want to thank my entire Environment and Public Works Committee 
staff, and I think most of them are in the room: Murphie Barrett, 
Pierce Wiegard, Andy Zach, Maggie Ayrea, Maddie Blalock, Libby 
Callaway, Jack Cline, Will Dixon, Rebecca Hattar, Jared Jones, Brett 
Jortland, Elle Krolicki, Karly McQuitty, Caryn Moore Lund, Kaitlyn 
Peterson, Dom Rupp, and Brent Scott for their outstanding--
outstanding--work every day.
  Thanks as well to my chief of staff, who was chief of staff of the 
Senate of the year last year, J.T. Jezierski. He is an incredible West 
Virginian and an incredible leader in the Senate, and James Mann, my 
legislative director, who was with us all last night as he has been in 
many of the late-night sessions. And I thank both of them.
  I also would like to thank Chris Prandoni, Rob MacGregor, Jake 
McCurdy, and James Bartholomew on Chairman Lee's staff, and Kara Allen 
and Bill Dauster on Senator Whitehouse's staff, and Maya Hermann and 
Michael Kellermann on Senator Heinrich's staff for their expertise and 
hard work on this bill.
  I also realized that I want to thank the four staff directors. Now, I 
cannot explain to you--I suppose if we had put a timer on them to see 
how many hours and hours they spent--late nights, early mornings--you 
know, we get the glory or the pain, but they are incredibly hard 
workers. They spend an incredible amount of time negotiating this 
legislation, managing the ups and downs--and there were quite a few of 
both of this legislative process. I would like to thank Wendy Baig who 
is Chairman Lee's staff director. I would like to thank Dan Dudis, who 
I know is really the best because he is Ranking Member Whitehouse's 
staff director. Thank you, Dan. Jasmine Hunt, fantastic, Ranking Member 
Heinrich's staff director.
  And I cannot even begin to thank my staff director Adam Tomlinson who 
is an incredible talent that I have been privileged to work with for 
over 20 years. Now, I am getting emotional here, but Adam and I have 
grown up together here in the House and in the Senate. He is 
phenomenal. I told him yesterday was his Super Bowl, and he came out 
the winner. Adam, thank you for everything.
  Finally and most importantly, I want to thank my negotiating partners 
Senators Whitehouse, Lee, and Heinrich. I remarked yesterday to the 
paper or to some of the media, really four different people--very 
different people here. Permitting reform was not given much chance when 
we began our negotiations over a year ago, and people could be forgiven 
for betting against us because we were an unlikely team.
  Senator Whitehouse, the Senate's foremost champion of action to 
address climate change; Senator Lee is one of the body's most committed 
conservatives; Senator Heinrich is fiercely committed to protecting our 
Nation's public lands; and I am a West Virginian who supports 
wholeheartedly my State's coal and natural gas industry. Wow. That is 
quite a spread.
  But despite those differences, the four of us were able to find 
common ground in a few core principles that are reflected throughout 
this bill.
  First, America must be able to build faster to meet our energy and 
infrastructure needs while keeping our air and water clean. Second, 
projects of all types deserve a fair permitting process that ends with 
a timely and clear answer, not with lengthy bureaucratic process and 
endless litigation. And third, projects should have the certainty that 
permits and investments won't be lost simply because the political 
winds change.
  It is never easy to cut bipartisan deals, especially on a topic as 
politically charged as the permitting and environmental review process 
has become. None of us got everything we wanted in this bill.
  I want to acknowledge and commend the political courage that my 
counterparts Senator Whitehouse and Senator Heinrich demonstrated in 
their willingness to work on this legislation. And I thoroughly enjoyed 
working with my colleague Senator Lee whom I never really worked a 
piece of legislation for and with. Not only is he a brilliant scholar, 
but he is a lot of fun to keep things going and keep the discussion 
going. So without their efforts, there would have been no chance to 
pass this.
  None of this would have been possible had we not had the combined 
efforts of the Energy and Natural Resources Committee and the 
Environment and Public Works Committees to build support by addressing 
issues within the jurisdiction of each committee.

[[Page S5834]]

  Chairman Lee, as I said, has been an incredible partner in 
negotiating that, and I certainly appreciate his efforts.
  So I hope our colleagues in the House will act quickly. I said last 
night--and I believe this at the very heart of me--that people send us 
here to do things, to make their lives better. This is an affordability 
bill. This is jobs for the future. This is building America 
infrastructure quicker and faster and more efficiently. That is what 
the American people want us to do. They don't want to see us fight and 
bicker and not try to work out our differences.
  This is a great example of a difficult climb where we were able to 
work out our differences because we put the principle of doing what we 
think is right and good for the American people first.
  So I hope our House counterparts, when they return, will act quickly 
and send this bill to President Trump's desk without delay.
  I yield the floor.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The Senator from Rhode Island.
  Mr. WHITEHOUSE. Mr. President, I wasn't planning to speak, but I do 
want to take a moment to thank the senior Senator from West Virginia 
for her remarks and for the manner in which she conducted these 
investigations.
  In this body, there are a lot of different things that need to happen 
for major bipartisan legislation to come together. One essential thing 
is process integrity. However we may disagree about substantive matter, 
you have to trust that when the person you are negotiating with says 
something, you can rely on that, and that has to be mutual. There has 
to be an honorable trust between parties to make this work, and Senator 
Capito held steadfast to that standard of honor through all of our 
disagreements.
  I would like to echo her appreciation for all of the folks around the 
Senate who helped make this happen. For me, Dan Dudis--my staff 
director--and his deputy Kara Allen, who is a master of grid knowledge, 
have made enormous contributions to this bill at considerable personal 
cost to their free time, and I am indebted to both of them.
  I don't believe Senator Schatz has been mentioned yet, but his work 
advising us on how to solve problems the Tribe Nations identified and 
his work helping with the process through this floor, as our incoming 
Senate Democratic whip, were very important and valuable.
  Martin could not have been a better partner on the Democratic side, 
and one of the things that gives me even more confidence in this bill 
than its written text is that Martin will stay on and will become 
chairman of the Energy Committee with oversight over the implementation 
of this bill, with oversight over the Interior Department.
  And so understandings have been reached about better behavior from 
the Department of Defense and from the Interior Department with respect 
to permitting. We actually have standing to enforce, and we intend to 
do that.
  On that point, when the administration's behavior has lapsed from 
what I thought was appropriate, one of the pleasant things about this 
process has been the extent to which my Republican colleagues rallied 
to the cause to get that behavior corrected.
  We saw that with respect to the offshore wind stop work orders. We 
have seen that with respect to the release of wind projects by the 
Department of Defense, so-called Department of War. And we are seeing 
that begin to turn into actual practice at the Department of the 
Interior, with a very clear opening statement by the Secretary that all 
energy sources will henceforth be treated fairly.
  That behavioral piece was completely beside the actual substantive 
piece that we worked on. Both together are really vital to getting us 
where we are.
  There is considerable dispute about this bill on our side of the 
aisle and about what it means. I am very confident that we have written 
a bill that will do what we say it will do. There are various 
hypotheticals that things could go badly wrong with it.
  One of the things that I take confidence in is that events will soon 
prove who was right. And with our new majorities, we will be in a very 
good position to make sure that if things go wrong, we will be able to 
put that back right again so that this bill is implemented in the way 
that we intended.
  But I will close by saying that we were an odd assembly of Senators 
to make this work, and one of the key elements in making it work was 
the commitment of the Senator from West Virginia to an extremely 
honorable process and an extremely honorable relationship, even across 
some pretty broad disagreements. So I thank her.
  I yield the floor.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER (Mrs. Moody). The Senator from Kansas.
```
