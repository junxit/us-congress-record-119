# Military Family Appreciation Month (Executive Session)

**Speaking:** Thune, John (T000250) [R-SD], Schumer, Charles E. (S000148) [D-NY]

```
                   Military Family Appreciation Month

  Mr. THUNE. Mr. President, one of the privileges of my job is being 
invited to attend welcome home or deployment ceremonies for our 
military men and women.
  I attended two ceremonies this fall for the South Dakota National 
Guard: an activation ceremony for members of the 155th Engineer Company 
and a welcome home ceremony for the 665th Support Maintenance Company.
  I also attended the U.S. Army Reserve mobilization ceremony for the 
452nd Ordnance Company, 2nd Platoon.
  And earlier this year, I had the privilege of meeting with the 28th 
expeditionary bomb wing when our Ellsworth B-1 bombers were forward 
deployed.
  Whenever I go to one of these ceremonies or meet with our military 
men and women in other contexts, like at Ellsworth Air Force Base, I 
always leave filled with both admiration and gratitude. Admiration for 
the character and courage of these men and women and gratitude for 
their willingness to change a life of convenience for one of challenge 
and sacrifice.
  Military life is tough. It is challenging. It asks a lot of them 
physically, mentally, emotionally. In modern life, we often place a 
high value on comfort and convenience. Military life is neither 
comfortable nor convenient.
  There is nothing convenient for a Guard member about having to step 
away from his or her civilian job and deploy for a year. There is 
nothing comfortable about boot camp: Training, a high operational 
tempo, deployment.
  The men and women of the U.S. military choose this life anyway. They 
sacrifice personal comfort, convenience, in service of a cause greater 
than themselves: The safety and security of the United States, the 
preservation of our freedoms. And it is a privilege to be invited to 
spend time with them. They inspire me always.
  There is another thing that always strikes me when I attend events 
like the recent welcome home and deployment ceremonies, and that is the 
families gathered there to support their loved one in uniform. They 
also serve.
  Being in the military is a tough job--so is being married to the 
military. Having a husband or a wife, a dad or a mom, a son or a 
daughter in uniform asks for sacrifice.
  If your spouse or parent is Active Duty, you have to pull up stakes 
on a regular basis and move to a new State or country and a new 
community, leave friends and family behind, enroll in a new school, 
learn a new city or town, only to leave it again a couple of years 
later.
  And then, of course, there are deployments. All of a sudden, the 
spouse left at home has to shoulder all of the business of life, from 
home maintenance, to car upkeep, to bill payments. If you are the 
parent left behind, you have to be both mom and dad for months, 
sometimes many months at a time. You miss time with your loved one. 
There are birthdays and Christmases and Thanksgivings and Easters where 
husband or wife, mom or dad isn't there.
  On top of this, there is always the fear in the back of your mind--
the fear that your husband or wife, your dad or mom, son or daughter, 
will never come home. And this isn't a theoretical fear. Military 
families have seen other families go through injury or loss. They know 
intimately what could happen to their loved one, but they serve anyway, 
shouldering the burden of that knowledge along with all the practical 
work that needs to be done to support their individual in uniform.
  It is Military Family Appreciation Month and Thanksgiving next week. 
As we count our blessings, as we reflect on the free country we live in 
and the liberty we enjoy, at the top of our list--at the top of our 
list--should be those who secure those freedoms for us--those who 
secure these freedoms for us--and their families.
  I want to wish a very happy Thanksgiving to our men and women in 
uniform and their families.
  Thank you, all of you, for your service. And may God bless you.
  I yield the floor.
```
