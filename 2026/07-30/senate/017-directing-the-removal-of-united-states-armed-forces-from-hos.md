# DIRECTING THE REMOVAL OF UNITED STATES ARMED FORCES FROM HOSTILITIES WITHIN OR AGAINST THE ISLAMIC REPUBLIC OF IRAN THAT HAVE NOT BEEN AUTHORIZED BY CONGRESS--Motion to Discharge

**Speaking:** Gillibrand, Kirsten E. (G000555) [D-NY], Barrasso, John (B001261) [R-WY], Schumer, Charles E. (S000148) [D-NY]

**Measures:** S.J.Res. 181, S.J.Res. 199

```
 DIRECTING THE REMOVAL OF UNITED STATES ARMED FORCES FROM HOSTILITIES

  Mrs. GILLIBRAND. Mr. President, pursuant to 50 U.S.C. 1546(a) and in 
accordance with section 601(b) of the International Security Assistance 
and Arms Export Control Act, I move to discharge the Committee on 
Foreign Relations from further consideration of S.J. Res. 181.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The clerk will report.
  The legislative clerk read as follows:

       Motion to discharge from the Committee on Foreign 
     Relations, S.J. Res. 181, a joint resolution to direct the 
     removal of United States Armed Forces from hostilities within 
     or against the Islamic Republic of Iran that have not been 
     authorized by Congress.

  Mrs. GILLIBRAND. I ask for the yeas and nays.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Is there a sufficient second?
  There appears to be a sufficient second.
  The yeas and nays are ordered.
  Mrs. GILLIBRAND. Mr. President, I ask unanimous consent to speak for 
up to 10 minutes.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Without objection, it is so ordered.


                             S.J. Res. 181

  Mrs. GILLIBRAND. Mr. President, I rise in support of my legislation 
to end President Trump's unauthorized, illegal war with Iran. This war 
will go down in history as one of the greatest strategic failures in 
modern American history.
  It was launched without a clear plan, executed without a coherent 
strategy, and has become a costly, self-inflicted disaster for the 
American people. Every justification that we were given for this 
conflict has proven hollow.
  We were told that Iran's nuclear program would be eliminated. 
Instead, Iran still holds nuclear material, and its leaders may be more 
determined than ever to build a weapon.
  We were told that the Iranian regime would crumble; instead, it now 
holds new leverage of the world's energy supply in the Strait of 
Hormuz.
  We were told this President would bring peace and lower prices; 
instead, he has given us war, pain, and higher prices.
  New York families are struggling to afford groceries and keep up with 
the rent, and what is President Trump doing? Pouring billions of tax 
dollars into an open-ended war in Iran. This reckless war is driving up 
monthly bills and making everyday life harder for working families.
  This administration demands billions for wars abroad, then claims we 
can't afford to improve our schools or repair our roads or lower the 
cost of healthcare for millions of Americans.
  President Trump has made it clear that he doesn't factor in how 
Americans are doing financially, but the families I represent feel it 
every single day. Every time they fill the gas tank and watch the 
numbers climb, New Yorkers are being forced to pay a premium for this 
President's instability.
  According to data from the Joint Economic Committee, the average 
family has to pay an additional $432 on gas alone since this war began. 
Statewide, that is an extra $2 billion at the pump. Let me repeat that. 
That is an extra $2 billion at the pump.
  Nationwide, Americans have paid $60 billion more on gas. Every time I 
go home, I hear from constituents about their concerns: the cost of 
healthcare, the cost of gas, the cost to keep lights on, and put food 
on the table.
  Those are the conversations happening in kitchens across America. 
That is what we should be focused on here in Washington, not dropping 
bombs around the world. And let us not forget there is a human cost to 
this war that no dollar figure could ever capture.
  Our servicemembers did not volunteer to be pawns in a chaotic, 
unauthorized war with no plan and no end in sight. Eighteen American 
heroes have already paid the ultimate sacrifice. Eighteen families have 
been shattered.
  Just last week, New York lost one of our own: SGT Angel Rampersad of 
Queens.
  Sergeant Rampersad was a true patriot who begged her mother to let 
her enlist the moment she was old enough. She served this country 
courageously and honorably for more than 6 years before she was killed 
in an Iranian attack in Jordan.
  Our servicemembers take one of the most dangerous jobs there is. In 
return, we owe them a sacred obligation. We do not send them into 
harm's way without a clear mission, defined objectives, and the support 
of the American people. The President's war with Iran is a betrayal of 
that obligation. New Yorkers and all Americans deserve more. They 
deserve better.
  So I ask my colleagues: How many more servicemembers will we lose? 
How many more billions of dollars must families pay at the pump? How 
many more empty promises must be made from an out-of-control 
administration before this body finally acts?
  Under the Constitution, we have the power to end this war today. 
Let's use it. I urge my colleagues to vote yes on this resolution.
  I yield the floor.
  I suggest the absence of a quorum.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER (Ms. Lummis). The clerk will call the roll.
  The legislative clerk proceeded to call the roll.
  Mrs. GILLIBRAND. Madam President, I ask unanimous consent that the 
order for the quorum call be rescinded.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Without objection, it is so ordered.

                             S.J. Res. 181

  Mr. SCHUMER. Mr. President, I will be brief.
  Nearly half a year into Trump's ruinous war with Iran, the conflict 
is widening instead of winding down. More countries get dragged into 
the mix, more fronts open up, and more Americans get killed and 
injured.
  The American people have made it clear: No one wants this war to go 
on.
  A new poll, this morning, found only a fifth--a fifth--of the 
American people, including less than half of the Republicans, want the 
war to continue.
  Are our Senate Republicans listening?
  The country is clamoring for you to end this war and to bring our 
troops home--something you can do right now by voting for Senator 
Gillibrand's War Powers Resolution.
  Is Trump so intimidating, so untouchable that Republicans will keep 
siding with him instead of the 80 percent of Americans calling for the 
fighting to end?

[[Page S4357]]

  The biggest thing standing between our troops and home is a 
Republican Party that is too afraid to cross the President.
  Congress can break this vicious cycle of escalation Trump started in 
Iran, and this War Powers Resolution is how we do it.
  I urge--I plead--with my Republican colleagues to listen to the 
American people. Vote to end this war. We are going to keep forcing 
Republicans to vote until you do.
  I yield the floor.
```
