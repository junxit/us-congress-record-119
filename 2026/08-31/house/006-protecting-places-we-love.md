# PROTECTING PLACES WE LOVE

**Speaking:** Vasquez, Gabe (V000136) [D-NM]

```
                       PROTECTING PLACES WE LOVE

  (Mr. Vasquez of New Mexico was recognized to address the House for 5 
minutes.)
  Mr. VASQUEZ. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to speak about patriotism and 
what it means to be a patriot in this great country.
  The American experience is one that we all share. It bonds us 
together under one Constitution, and as lawmakers serving in these 
hallowed halls, we have the privilege of carrying forward

[[Page H5262]]

one of the world's greatest democratic ideas through one of its most 
deliberative bodies.
  Fortunately for the American public, that experience includes over a 
century of bipartisan support for another one of America's greatest and 
best ideas: the creation, shared stewardship, and enjoyment of our 
Nation's public lands, so that we may continue to hunt, to fish, to 
take our families camping, to discover America's history, and to 
unravel the great mysteries and gifts that can only be found in our 
Nation's national forests, national parks, BLM lands, and wildlife 
refuges.
  Long after the creation of the U.S. Forest Service in 1905 by 
Republican President and conservationist Teddy Roosevelt, Americans of 
all political stripes have continued the legacy of conserving our 
public lands for all Americans, for multiple uses--both for our 
enjoyment and for the domestic production of natural resources, a 
careful balance that requires bipartisan and local support.
  Let me remind this body of the tremendous work of U.S. Senator Frank 
Church. The Senator's impact on this Nation's wilderness, wild and 
scenic rivers, and land and water conservation is to be celebrated. In 
particular, he should be lauded for the way in which he went about 
passing many of our Nation's bedrock public land laws that stand until 
this day.
  You see, Senator Church was a quiet, careful workman who worked 
across the aisle to strike compromise and pass overwhelmingly 
bipartisan legislation such as the Wilderness Act of 1964, the Land and 
Water Conservation Fund Act of 1965, and the Wild and Scenic Rivers Act 
of 1968.
  This was an unprecedented period of patriotism and public lands where 
bipartisanship flourished, and Congress worked as a unified body to 
protect our Nation's most precious resources.
  In 1964, the Wilderness Act passed the House by a whopping 373-1 
majority. That same year, the Land and Water Conservation Fund Act 
passed by a voice vote in the House and a 92-1 vote in the Senate. In 
1973, the House passed the Endangered Species Act in a landslide 355-4 
vote. Even Nixon, not known as an environmentalist, in 1972, proclaimed 
that what began as an environmental awakening must mature finally into 
a new and higher environmental way of life.
  Believe it or not, there was a time which in this body we crafted 
win-win models of public land legislation. That is why I helped 
establish the first bipartisan Public Lands Caucus with former Interior 
Secretary and Montana Representative Ryan Zinke.
  Today, we have the potential to piece together the remnants of 
bipartisan political consensus and provide a powerful framework for 
modern conservation and public lands initiatives.
  Working together under this patriotic caucus to achieve wider 
bipartisan agreement, I believe that we can mediate conflicts between 
natural resource users and protection proponents and demonstrate that 
economic development and conservation are not mutually exclusive.
  Today, foreign companies have spread their tentacles and sprawled 
into our forests, rivers, and national monuments, all in search of a 
quick buck.
  Paired together with short sighted proposals from this administration 
that threaten to undo the Roadless Rule, to eliminate the Public Lands 
Rule, to shrink our national monuments, to open sensitive habitat in 
the Alaskan Arctic Refuge to drilling, to narrow regulations of the 
ESA, to shrink the U.S. Forest Service, to rob crucial maintenance 
funds from the National Parks budget, and just recently to sell off 
part of Yosemite National Park to a private developer, we are in deep, 
deep trouble of losing what both Democrats and Republicans have long 
fought to conserve and to protect.
  Public lands improve every American's quality of life. They provide 
abundance and liberty. We must fight to ensure access to the American 
Dream, and that includes access to public lands and a healthy 
environment.
  The truth is, Americans love nature. Hunters, anglers, and families 
alike agree that public lands are patriotic.
  Let us renew these concepts of bipartisan cooperation and protect our 
shared patriotism, even if it means standing up to our own party and 
this administration in the wake of the largest wave of attacks we have 
seen to our public lands in our Nation's history.
  Mr. Speaker, I know that I will be doing my damndest to protect the 
places we love in New Mexico, across the West, and this great Nation. I 
hope that my colleagues in this body can put partisanship aside and 
have the courage to do the same.
```
