retired Glynn County Fire Rescue Chief and former Glynn County

and dedicated over 33 years protecting coastal Georgians.
  Through hard work, integrity, and leadership, he rose through the 
ranks

His commitment to Glynn County did not end with retirement. He served 
as a member of the Glynn County Board of Commissioners from 2006 to

profession. It is a calling. Whether leading firefighters through 
emergencies or helping guide the future of Glynn County, he exemplified 
an unwavering dedication.
  As his family, friends, former colleagues, and the people of Glynn 
County gather to celebrate his remarkable life, we join them in 
remembering a man whose impact will be felt for generations.

               Recognizing Goshen United Methodist Church

  Mr. CARTER of Georgia. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Goshen 
United Methodist Church in Rincon, Georgia, as its congregation 
celebrates an incredible 275 years of faith, fellowship, and service.
  Dating back to around 1751, Goshen Church has witnessed generations 
of Georgia history, first serving congregations along Abercorn Creek 
near the Savannah River.
  Around 1790, the church itself was remarkably cut into two sections 
and moved to its present location using logs, horses, and mules. In 
1820, the Methodist Church was organized at Goshen, beginning more than 
two centuries of Methodist ministry that continues today.
  Through changing times and a growing community, Goshen has remained 
rooted in its mission to love God, love others, and serve both.
  Today, under the leadership of the Reverend Chance Ward, Goshen 
continues building on that extraordinary legacy by sharing the way of 
Jesus and serving as a light to the community.
  Mr. Speaker, I congratulate the entire Goshen United Methodist Church 
family on 275 years of ministry and pray its legacy of faith and 
service continues for generations to come.


                      Honoring John ``Jack'' Welsh

  Mr. CARTER of Georgia. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor John 
``Jack'' Welsh, a World War II combat veteran and soon to be 
centenarian. In fact, on September 4, he will be 100.
  Upon graduating high school on the same day as D-day in June 1944, 
Jack asked his parents if he could enlist.
  He served in the Philippines during General MacArthur's campaign, 
carrying a Browning Automatic Rifle and later served in the occupation 
force in Japan.
  He was awarded the Asiatic-Pacific Theater Campaign Ribbon with two 
Bronze Stars, the Philippine Liberation Ribbon, and the Army of 
Occupation Medal, among other honors.
  After his service, Jack earned two degrees from Duke University's 
Forestry School and spent 35 years with the U.S. Forest Service. He and 
his wife, Mary Ann, were married 62 years and raised four sons 
together. Jack is the proud grandfather of 8 grandchildren and 10 
great-grandchildren.
  His service of lifelong dedication to family and community reflects 
his great character and service.
  Mr. Speaker, I thank him for his service and wish him a happy 100th 
birthday.

                   Honoring the Life of Derrick Moore

  Mr. CARTER of Georgia. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor the life 
and the sacrifice of Derrick Moore, who died rescuing a young girl from 
a rip current in Myrtle Beach, South Carolina.
  On a late Friday evening, Mr. Moore, who was just 47, saw the young 
girl struggling in the ocean and immediately rushed into the water to 
help her. He successfully pulled the child from the rip current and 
brought her to safety, but was then pulled under water himself.
  First responders from the Myrtle Beach Police Department and Myrtle 
Beach Fire Department launched an intensive search that extended into 
Saturday morning. Unfortunately, ocean currents washed Moore's body 
ashore the next morning where officials later recovered him.
  Mr. Speaker, I am honoring Derrick for his courageous and selfless 
acts, and our thoughts and prayers are with him and with his family.
  Mr. Speaker, I yield back the balance of my time.
