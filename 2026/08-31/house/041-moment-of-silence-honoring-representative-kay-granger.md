# MOMENT OF SILENCE HONORING REPRESENTATIVE KAY GRANGER

**Speaking:** Goldman, Craig A. (G000601) [R-TX], Veasey, Marc A. (V000131) [D-TX]

```
         MOMENT OF SILENCE HONORING REPRESENTATIVE KAY GRANGER

  (Mr. GOLDMAN of Texas asked and was given permission to address the 
House for 1 minute.)
  Mr. GOLDMAN of Texas. Mr. Speaker, on August 2 we lost Kay Granger. 
All of you knew her. She was a legend in this Chamber. She was a legend 
in Fort Worth, Texas. She was a legend in Texas. She was a legend in 
America.
  At her funeral a week ago Saturday, her son spoke so eloquently about 
her. He didn't talk about her accomplishments here. He talked about her 
being just an ordinary mom, just an ordinary person who happened to do 
extraordinary things.
  She was the first Republican woman mayor of the city of Fort Worth. 
She was the first Republican woman ever to be elected to Congress from 
Texas. As many of you know and so many of her fellow appropriators who 
are up here know, she was the first woman to be chair of the Defense 
Subcommittee on Appropriations and then the first woman ever in the 
U.S. Congress to be chair of the Appropriations Committee.
  She broke down barriers everywhere she went. When people said, ``You 
can't do that,'' she said, ``Tell me why. I am going to anyway,'' as 
all of you know, especially when she rose to be the Appropriations 
Committee chair.
  She didn't get there because it was easy. She got there because it 
was tough, and she always took the tough route.
  She was an extraordinary woman. Fort Worth has lost a legend. We will 
never forget her, and we will remember her forever just as every single 
one of you who served with her will as well.
  Mr. Speaker, I yield to the gentleman from Texas (Mr. Veasey), my 
Democratic colleague.
  Mr. VEASEY. Mr. Speaker, I thank Mr. Goldman for yielding me time.
  The ceremony for Kay was absolutely beautiful. I have to let everyone 
know that. I want to reiterate what Craig just talked about. Her son 
J.D. gave such an amazing testimony about her life.
  I want everyone here to know that Kay was a Congresswoman here, and 
she did remarkable things during her tenure representing Fort Worth. 
Fort Worth is a better place because of Kay's service in the U.S. 
House.
  But so many people had long-term relationships with Kay Granger. She 
was a teacher in the Birdville ISD, and people knew her from that. So 
many people have stories about her time teaching them, people that are 
still with us today. She was a businesswoman. Kay had a group of 
diverse friends that she dined with, that she vacationed with, that she 
did things with. She was someone who was, as I say, a woman before her 
time really, and she touched so many different people's lives.
  When I would go home, people would always ask me: Marc, how do you 
and Kay get along? I see Democrats and Republicans are always at each 
other. I would tell people: Kay still is like our mayor. She is still 
just like the mayor of Fort Worth. We worked great together on things.
  Fort Worth will miss her. She was an amazing person to work with, and 
she always put Fort Worth first when she was here. As the gentleman 
from Texas (Mr. Goldman) just said, Kay is going to be sorely missed. 
She was an incredible and remarkable person.
  Mr. GOLDMAN. Mr. Speaker, I ask for a moment of silence in honor of 
Kay Granger.
```
