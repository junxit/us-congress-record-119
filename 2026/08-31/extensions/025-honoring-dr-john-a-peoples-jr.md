# HONORING DR. JOHN A. PEOPLES, JR.

**Speaking:** Thompson, Bennie G. (T000193) [D-MS]

```
                   HONORING DR. JOHN A. PEOPLES, JR.

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. BENNIE G. THOMPSON

                             of mississippi

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, August 31, 2026

  Mr. THOMPSON of Mississippi. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor Dr. 
John A. Peoples, Jr., a distinguished Mississippian, educator, veteran, 
and the sixth president of Jackson State University.
  A native of Starkville, Mississippi, Dr. Peoples served his country 
with distinction as a United States Marine during World War II. He 
served as a Drill Instructor at Montford Point at Camp Lejeune, where 
Black Marines were trained during segregation. In July 2012, Dr. 
Peoples was awarded the Congressional Gold Medal in recognition of his 
service as one of the African-American Marines who trained at Montford 
Point.
  After his military service, Dr. Peoples returned to Jackson State 
University, earning his bachelor's degree in mathematics in 1950. He 
later earned a master's degree and Ph.D. from the University of 
Chicago. In 1964, he returned to Jackson State as a professor of 
mathematics and vice president and became president in 1967.
  Dr. Peoples served as president from 1967 to 1984, overseeing an 
extraordinary period of growth and transformation. During his tenure, 
enrollment increased from approximately 2,200 to 7,800 students, 
academic programs expanded through the doctoral level, and the 
University's physical facilities grew substantially. He also founded 
the Army Reserve Officers' Training Corps (ROTC) unit at Jackson State 
University, establishing a lasting tradition of military leadership and 
service.
  Dr. Peoples led Jackson State through some of the most challenging 
years of the civil rights era, including the tragic May 14, 1970, 
shootings at Jackson State, when Phillip Gibbs and James Green were 
killed and other students were wounded.
  Beyond Jackson State, Dr. Peoples became a nationally recognized 
leader in higher education and was the first African-American to serve 
as Chairman of the Board of the American Council on Education. His 
contributions to education, civil rights, and public service have 
earned him numerous honors, including the Medgar Evers Award, the 
Mississippi Medal of Service, and induction into the Jackson State 
University and Southwestern Athletic Conference Halls of Fame.
  Dr. John A. Peoples, Jr., has devoted his life to service, education, 
leadership, and the advancement of opportunity for others. From the 
United States Marine Corps to the halls of Jackson State University, 
his legacy reflects courage, excellence, and an unwavering commitment 
to his community and his country.
  I am proud to honor Dr. John A. Peoples, Jr., for his extraordinary 
contributions to Mississippi, Jackson State University, the United 
States Marine Corps, and our Nation.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in recognizing this 
remarkable American and Mississippi trailblazer.
```
