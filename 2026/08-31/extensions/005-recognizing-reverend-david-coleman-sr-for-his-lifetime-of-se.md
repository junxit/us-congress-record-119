# RECOGNIZING REVEREND DAVID COLEMAN, SR. FOR HIS LIFETIME OF SERVICE

**Speaking:** Krishnamoorthi, Raja (K000391) [D-IL]

```
  RECOGNIZING REVEREND DAVID COLEMAN, SR. FOR HIS LIFETIME OF SERVICE

                                 ______
                                 

                        HON. RAJA KRISHNAMOORTHI

                              of illinois

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, August 31, 2026

  Mr. KRISHNAMOORTHI. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize Reverend 
David Coleman. Sr., a devoted pastor, community leader, and servant of 
Chicago's South Side, who passed away on July 21, 2026, following a 
lifetime of faith, hard work, and service to the Englewood community.
  Born in Parkdale, Arkansas, as the seventh of eleven children, 
Reverend Coleman learned the values of faith, family, and hard work 
from an early age. Following the passing of his father in 1967, he 
assumed the pastoral leadership of New Mt. Sinai Missionary Baptist 
Church, where he served faithfully for more than 50 years until his 
retirement in 2020. Through his ministry, Reverend Coleman created a 
sanctuary where generations of families found spiritual nourishment, 
youth programs, and community support.
  Beyond his pastoral work, Reverend Coleman demonstrated his 
commitment to service through hard work and civic engagement. For more 
than two decades, he served as a custodian, engineer, and manager at 
the Stone Terrace Apartments complex. A lifelong self-taught repairman 
and craftsman, he personally rehabilitated a former warehouse at West 
59th Street and transformed it into a spiritual sanctuary and community 
gathering place. He was also a dedicated member of the Rainbow PUSH 
Coalition, the Baptist Pastors Conference of Chicago and Vicinity, and 
the Baptist Ministers Conference of Chicago.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join me in honoring the life and 
legacy of Reverend David Coleman, Sr., whose more than five decades of 
pastoral leadership and service touched generations of Chicagoans and 
strengthened the community he called home. As we mourn his passing, we 
extend our deepest condolences to his family, including his wife, 
former Alderman, Bishop Shirley Coleman, daughter, Alderman Stephanie 
Coleman, and all those who knew and loved him.
```
