# HONORING JANE HEALY FOR A LIFETIME OF CONTRIBUTIONS TO CENTRAL FLORIDA

**Speaking:** Soto, Darren (S001200) [D-FL]

```
 HONORING JANE HEALY FOR A LIFETIME OF CONTRIBUTIONS TO CENTRAL FLORIDA

                                 ______
                                 

                            HON. DARREN SOTO

                               of florida

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, August 31, 2026

  Mr. SOTO. Mr. Speaker, I rise to honor Jane Healy, a D.C. native, 
former editorial page editor of the Orlando Sentinel, and the 
newspaper's first Pulitzer Prize winner, who died of a blood disorder 
on May 15, 2026, at the age of 77.
  She was a devoted mother to her two sons, Randall and Kevin; mother-
in-law to Kevin's wife, Emily; grandmother to their son, Teddy, and to 
Randall's children, Elizabeth (plus husband Alex and great-
grandchildren, Zayden and Leighten), grandmother to Taylor (plus 
fiance, Spencer) and grandson, RJ; twin sister to Monica and sister to 
Kevin, Kathleen (all live in D.C.), and the late Julie; and sister-in-
law to Monica's husband, George Hager; and ultimately, close friends 
with her ex-husband, Jim Clark.
  After attending Bethesda-Chevy Chase High School and graduating from 
the University of Maryland in 1971, she got a job at the Orlando 
Sentinel after her job as a part-time reporter for the New York Daily 
News' Washington bureau.
  Healy began as a metro reporter at the Sentinel and was promoted to 
the editorial board in 1981. She rose to editorial page editor in 1985, 
the same year she was a Pulitzer Prize finalist for her ``Florida's 
Shame'' editorial series, which exposed politicians who gave in to 
aggressive developers destroying the environment. The series continued, 
and in 1988 it won Healy the Pulitzer in editorial writing, the 
Sentinel's first Pulitzer. She was again a Pulitzer finalist for the 
ongoing series in 2007.
  In 1994, Healy became the Sentinel's managing editor, supervising a 
newsroom of nearly 400 staffers. In 2001, she returned to oversee the 
editorial pages and was named vice president of the Sentinel. In 2008, 
she retired from the Sentinel, but she continued to write a Sunday 
political column until 2010.
  Throughout much of her career, Healy was a significant force in 
journalism at the University of Maryland. She was a member and later 
chair of Merrill College's Board of Visitors and performed the same 
roles at Maryland's Knight Center for Specialized Journalism. She was 
named to the University of Maryland Distinguished Alumni in 2003, and 
to the university's Hall of Fame in 2022.
```
