# HONORING THE 30TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE GUAM WATERWORKS AUTHORITY AND PROCLAIMING GUAM WATERWORKS AUTHORITY YEAR

**Speaking:** Moylan, James C. (M001219) [R-GU]

```
  HONORING THE 30TH ANNIVERSARY OF THE GUAM WATERWORKS AUTHORITY AND 
               PROCLAIMING GUAM WATERWORKS AUTHORITY YEAR

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. JAMES C. MOYLAN

                                of guam

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, August 31, 2026

  Mr. MOYLAN. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to honor a remarkable milestone 
for the island of Guam and for one of the most strategically important 
pieces of infrastructure in the United States Indo-Pacific territory--
the Guam Waterworks Authority. This year marks the 30th Anniversary of 
GWA's establishment, and I am proud to bring this occasion to the 
attention of the United States House of Representatives.
  Guam is not just an American island--it is America's forward post in 
the Pacific. As the westernmost territory of the United States, Guam is 
home to some of the most significant military installations in the 
Indo-Pacific region: Andersen Air Force Base, Naval Base Guam, Marine 
Corps Base Camp Blaz, and the headquarters of Joint Region Marianas. 
The ongoing U.S. military buildup on Guam--one of the largest in the 
Pacific since World War II--is reshaping the island's strategic 
landscape and will bring thousands of additional military personnel, 
families, and civilian workers to our shores in the coming years.
  That buildup depends entirely on one thing: water. Clean, reliable, 
abundant water. And that is precisely what the Guam Waterworks 
Authority provides.
  GWA was established on July 31, 1996, by the Guam Legislature through 
Public Law 23-119, creating an autonomous, self-supporting public 
corporation to manage the island's water and wastewater infrastructure. 
Since commencing operations on February 1, 1997, GWA has served not 
only the 160,000 residents of Guam but also the United States military 
installations that depend on GWA's water systems for operational 
readiness.
  The federal government has recognized this reality--and invested 
accordingly. The Department of Defense provided $55.3 million in August 
2016 and an additional $117.9 million in November 2017 through the 
Office of Economic Adjustment, helping to finance the upgrade of the 
Northern District Wastewater Treatment Plant to full secondary 
treatment, completed in August 2022. That upgrade was completed ahead 
of the opening of Marine Corps Base Camp Blaz--the first new Marine 
Corps base established since 1952, activated in October 2020, and 
officially opened in January 2023, located in Dededo and named in honor 
of Brigadier General Vicente T. ``Ben'' Blaz, Guam's own son and the 
first person of an ethnic minority to reach general rank in the U.S. 
Marine Corps. Camp Blaz is home to approximately 1,300 permanently 
stationed Marines, with another 3,700 rotating through on temporary 
deployments--all of whom depend on GWA's water and wastewater 
infrastructure. This partnership--GWA and the United States government 
working together--is a model of what the One-Guam Initiative 
represents: the proposition that Guam's security, readiness, and 
sustainability cannot be separated.
  The One-Guam Initiative, formalized through a 2010 memorandum of 
understanding between GWA and U.S. Naval Facilities Engineering 
Command, expanded by the 2016 MOU, deepened by the 2022 Memorandum of 
Agreement for co-management and protection of the Northern Guam Lens 
Aquifer, and anchored by a 10-year Intergovernmental Support Agreement 
executed in July 2023 following Typhoon Mawar, is now a cornerstone of 
Guam's utility strategy and the military's long-term infrastructure 
planning. Protecting the Northern Guam Lens Aquifer--the primary source 
of drinking water for northern Guam and the military installations that 
rely upon it--is both an environmental imperative and a matter of 
national security.
  GWA has not waited for the federal government to solve its 
challenges. Beginning with an inherited crisis--more than $25 million 
in accumulated debt and federal lawsuits for water quality violations--
GWA undertook a sustained, twenty-year transformation. It filed its 
first rate petition in 2004, entered the bond markets in 2005, achieved 
debt service coverage compliance for the first time in Fiscal Year 
2012, and has since built a capital program of $898.8 million for the 
period 2025 through 2029. The 2025 Interim Water Resources Master Plan 
calls for approximately $1.892 billion in infrastructure investment 
through 2037--a commitment that will shape Guam's water security for 
decades.
  The scale of that investment reflects the scale of what Guam is 
becoming. Camp Blaz is receiving thousands of Marines relocating from 
Okinawa, Japan--a realignment that is decades in the making that makes 
Guam the centerpiece of America's forward-deployed Marine presence in 
the Indo-Pacific. The U.S. military buildup is not just a national 
security deployment--it is an economic and demographic transformation 
for our island. GWA is planning and investing now so that Guam's water 
and wastewater infrastructure will meet not only today's demands but 
the demands of a larger, more strategically vital Guam for the next 
generation.
  I also want to acknowledge something that often goes unsaid in this 
chamber: the federal government has an ongoing responsibility to the 
people of Guam. We are American citizens. We serve in the United States 
military at extraordinary rates--among the highest per capita of any 
state or territory. We pay federal taxes. We abide by federal laws and 
federal environmental mandates. GWA's 2024 Partial Consent Decree with 
the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency commits GWA to a 10-year 
wastewater collection system improvement program--a federal requirement 
that comes with a price tag. Congress can and should continue to 
support Guam's utility infrastructure as part of its broader commitment 
to the Indo-Pacific strategy and to the constitutional obligation owed 
to all American citizens, wherever they live.
  As GWA celebrates its 30th Anniversary, I include in the Record each 
of the General Managers who has led this Authority since inception:

       Richard A. Quintanilla, Herbert J. Johnston, Jr., Gil 
     Shinohara, David E.R. Craddick, John Benavente, Dr. Leonard 
     Olive, Martin L. Roush, Thomas F. Cruz, Mark G. Miller, Greg 
     P. Cruz, and current General Manager Miguel C. Bordallo.

  Their leadership--and the work of every GWA employee--has kept water 
flowing to the homes, schools, hospitals, and military installations of 
Guam through typhoons, drought,

[[Page E818]]

pandemic, and the rapid changes that come with being at the forefront 
of America's Pacific presence.
  Mr. Speaker, GWA Year runs from July 31, 2026, through July 31, 2027. 
February 1, 2027, will mark thirty years of continuous utility 
operations as a self-supporting public corporation. I ask my colleagues 
to join me in honoring this milestone, recognizing that a stronger GWA 
means a stronger Guam, and a stronger Guam means a stronger United 
States in the Pacific.
  God bless the people of Guam, and God bless the United States of 
America.
```
