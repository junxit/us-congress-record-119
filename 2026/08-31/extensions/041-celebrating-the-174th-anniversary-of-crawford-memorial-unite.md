# CELEBRATING THE 174TH ANNIVERSARY OF CRAWFORD MEMORIAL UNITED METHODIST CHURCH

**Speaking:** Torres, Ritchie (T000486) [D-NY]

```
CELEBRATING THE 174TH ANNIVERSARY OF CRAWFORD MEMORIAL UNITED METHODIST 
                                 CHURCH

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. RITCHIE TORRES

                              of new york

                    in the house of representatives

                        Monday, August 31, 2026

  Mr. TORRES of New York. I rise today to honor and celebrate 174 years 
of Crawford Memorial United Methodist Church--a remarkable institution 
that has stood as a beacon of faith, fellowship, service, and community 
in the Bronx for nearly two centuries.
  The church's roots reach deep into the history of the Bronx. Its 
story includes a congregation that moved to the neighborhood in the 
late nineteenth century, and a community that grew and evolved 
alongside it. The church was later named in honor of Francis Crawford, 
a dedicated layman who moved into the neighborhood in 1877 and served 
as superintendent of the Sunday School and president of the Board of 
Trustees. Following a devastating fire, the Crawford family 
demonstrated extraordinary kindness when they gave generously to 
rebuild the church.
  The church endured another devastating fire in the winter of 1974, 
when flames engulfed the front of the sanctuary and damaged the organ. 
Yet even in the face of destruction, the spirit of the congregation was 
not extinguished. The church rebuilt. It persevered. And it continued 
its mission.
  That spirit of perseverance is captured beautifully in the words that 
stood on the only remaining Church wall: ``GOD IS LOVE.''
  The church's history also reflects the changing face of the Bronx. As 
the surrounding community changed, so too did the congregation. The 
church experienced a significant

[[Page E826]]

transition from a predominantly white congregation to a predominantly 
black congregation, growing under the leadership of successive pastors 
and generations of committed members.
  During that transition, the women of Crawford Memorial played an 
especially important role in preserving and strengthening the life of 
the church. Women including Mrs. Manzelle Orange, Mrs. Gladys Brewster, 
Miss Harriet Brewster, Mrs. Marie Allen, Miss Ruth Womsley, and Ms. 
Roseann Chapman helped sustain cherished traditions and keep the doors 
of Crawford open. Through the Women's Auxiliary and other ministries, 
they organized events, supported the church's work, and helped ensure 
that Crawford remained a place where faith was lived through service.
  The church's commitment to service has continued across generations. 
Crawford Memorial has nurtured young people through youth fellowship 
and dance ministries. It has supported seniors. It has maintained a 
vibrant music ministry, including choirs and the Faith-Hope Ensemble. 
Their work extends outside of the Church's walls through their Service 
Guild, dramatics stage, and space for community meetings. It has 
welcomed people of different cultures and educational backgrounds, 
united by a shared commitment to God's work.
  Today, Crawford Memorial continues its journey under the leadership 
of its 50th pastor, Reverend Heidi N. Thomas.
  Fifty pastors. Generations of families. Countless prayers. Countless 
acts of service. Countless lives touched. That is the legacy we 
celebrate today.
```
