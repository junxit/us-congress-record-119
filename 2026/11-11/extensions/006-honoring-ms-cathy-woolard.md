# HONORING MS. CATHY WOOLARD

**Speaking:** Williams, Nikema (W000788) [D-GA]

```
                       HONORING MS. CATHY WOOLARD

                                 ______
                                 

                          HON. NIKEMA WILLIAMS

                               of georgia

                    in the house of representatives

                      Wednesday, November 11, 2026

  Ms. WILLIAMS of Georgia. Mr. Speaker, I rise today to recognize and 
honor Ms. Cathy Woolard for her decades of distinguished service to the 
Atlanta community as she moves to Western Noah Carolina. Cathy was a 
trailblazing elected official, an advocate for equality, and a 
visionary leader; she dedicated her career to making Atlanta inclusive 
and equitable but also a vibrant city for all who call it home.

[[Page E1194]]

  Throughout her career, Cathy has been a leader of many firsts. In 
1997, she made history as the first openly gay elected official in 
Georgia, winning a seat on the Atlanta City Council. Just four years 
later, she broke another barrier when she became the first woman 
elected President of the Atlanta City Council in 2001. Her historic 
leadership not only opened doors for women and members of the LGBTQ+ 
community in public office but also helped usher in a new era of 
representation and progress in Atlanta's city government.
  The daughter of a military veteran, Cathy was raised with a deep 
appreciation for public service and a commitment to serving her 
country. Although discriminatory policies prevented her from pursuing 
military service as an openly gay woman, her dedication to service 
never wavered. Instead, she joined the Peace Corps, where she further 
developed the values of compassion, civic responsibility, and community 
engagement that would eventually come to define her career.
  Recognizing that true equality required both representation and 
action, Cathy became deeply involved in the movement for LGBTQ+ rights. 
Her advocacy eventually led her to Washington, D.C., where she served 
as a lobbyist for the Human Rights Campaign, one of the Nation's 
leading LGBTQ+ civil rights organizations. Through this work, she 
gained invaluable experience, laying the foundation for her future 
career in elected office.
  In 1997, Cathy returned to Atlanta's political landscape with a 
determination to create meaningful change. Her election to the Atlanta 
City Council marked a turning point in the city's history, 
demonstrating that public service should be defined not by whom one 
loves, but by one's commitment to the people they represent. As City 
Council President, she served alongside another historic leader, Mayor 
Shirley Franklin, helping shape Atlanta's future during a 
transformative period in the city's development.
  Among Cathy's most enduring contributions to Atlanta is her early 
leadership in advancing what would become the Atlanta BeltLine. 
Recognizing the potential of a bold proposal to transform abandoned 
railway corridors into a connected network of trails, parks, 
neighborhoods, and public transit, Cathy helped elevate the concept 
from an ambitious vision into a citywide priority. Her advocacy laid 
critical groundwork for one of the most significant urban redevelopment 
initiatives in Atlanta's history.
  Today, the Atlanta BeltLine stands as a testament to Cathy's 
visionary leadership and commitment to building a more connected 
Atlanta. Her contributions have left a lasting mark on our city and 
will continue to benefit generations to come.
  Beyond her accomplishments in elected office, Cathy has remained a 
steadfast advocate for equality, particularly for the LGBTQ+ community. 
Throughout her career, she has used her platform to challenge 
discrimination and ensure that those who have historically been 
excluded have a voice in shaping their communities.
  Mr. Speaker, Cathy Woolard's legacy is one of courage, conviction, 
and an unwavering belief in the promise of a more inclusive Atlanta. 
She has shown us that breaking barriers is not simply about being the 
first, but about ensuring that those who follow have greater 
opportunities to lead and succeed.
  On behalf of the people of Georgia's 5th Congressional District, I 
extend my deepest gratitude to Cathy for her decades of dedicated 
service to our community.
  I ask my colleagues to join me in thanking Ms. Cathy Woolard and 
recognizing the lasting impact she has made on Atlanta and the 
countless lives she has touched.
```
