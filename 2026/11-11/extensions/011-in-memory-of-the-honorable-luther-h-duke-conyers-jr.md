# IN MEMORY OF THE HONORABLE LUTHER H. "DUKE" CONYERS, JR.

**Speaking:** Bishop, Sanford D. (B000490) [D-GA]

```
       IN MEMORY OF THE HONORABLE LUTHER H. ``DUKE'' CONYERS, JR.

                                 ______
                                 

                      HON. SANFORD D. BISHOP, JR.

                               of georgia

                    in the house of representatives

                      Wednesday, November 11, 2026

  Mr. BISHOP. Mr. Speaker, I rise today with a heavy heart to pay 
tribute to a soldier, a teacher, a statesman, and my dear friend, the 
Honorable Luther Harold ``Duke'' Conyers, Jr., of Bainbridge, Georgia, 
who passed from labor to reward on Saturday, November 7, 2026, at the 
age of 97. A celebration of his life will be held on Wednesday, 
November 11, 2026, at 1:00 p.m. at Saint Paul Missionary Baptist Church 
in Bainbridge. It is fitting that an old soldier will be laid to rest 
on Veterans Day.
  Luther Conyers was born on a plantation in Southwest Georgia to 
parents who had little formal schooling but a boundless faith in what 
education could do. They raised five sons and expected every one of 
them to learn. All five earned college degrees. Luther earned his 
Bachelor of Science in Agriculture in 1948 from Georgia State 
Industrial College, now Savannah State University, where he met the 
love of his life, Gwendolyn, who preceded him in death. He later earned 
a Master's in Education from Florida A&M University in 1961. What his 
parents planted in him, he spent the rest of his life planting in 
others.
  His first students were soldiers. As a young man he taught in the 
Institutional On-Farm Training Program for veterans returning from 
World War II. Then his own call came. He entered the United States Army 
in January 1951, trained at Fort Jackson, South Carolina, and Fort 
Bliss, Texas, and served 16 months and 27 days in Korea with a 
transportation crew. He came home in December 1952 with the Korean 
Service Medal and three Bronze Stars. Even in a war zone he could not 
stop teaching. He held education classes for his fellow soldiers 
between duties.
  Back in Georgia, he returned to the work he loved. He served as a 
substitute teacher in Bainbridge, taught at Randolph County High 
School, and in 1954 joined the faculty of Bainbridge Elementary School. 
In 1965 he moved to Hutto High School to teach social studies, and from 
1970 he taught eighth and ninth graders at Hutto Junior High School. He 
never left Savannah State behind. The Conyers family was named SSU 
Family of the Year in 2009, Mr. and Mrs. Conyers were inducted together 
into the Savannah State University Hall of Fame in 2015, and the 
Conyers-Kirbo Endowed Scholarship Fund they helped establish is still 
opening doors for students who will never know how far he had to walk 
to open his own.
  The people of Bainbridge first elected him to the City Council in 
1977, and he took the oath of office on January 4, 1978. He kept that 
oath for 37 years, retiring in December 2015 as the longest-serving 
councilman in the history of the city. He served as the Council's 
liaison to the Planning Commission, helped guide the Georgia Municipal 
Association's work on employee retirement benefits and leadership 
training, and each year taught the students of Bainbridge how their own 
government works. He was inducted into the Georgia Municipal 
Association Hall of Fame in 2008 and received the Georgia Municipal 
Training Institute's Certificate of Distinction in 2012. He was a 
faithful member of the Rotary Club, American Legion Post No. 502, the 
NAACP, and the Bainbridge-Decatur County Chamber of Commerce.
  When he retired, the people of Bainbridge gathered at the fountain 
behind City Hall and gave that place his name. Mr. Speaker, think on 
that. A child born on a plantation lived to see the city of his birth 
carve his name in its public square. The Luther H. Conyers, Jr. Plaza 
stands there today. Children who never met him will play beside that 
fountain, and they will be walking on ground he made better.
  I had the privilege of honoring Mr. Conyers in 2015, while he could 
still hear the words. I said then that he had done so much for so many 
for so long, and I recalled the teaching of Dr. Benjamin E. Mays that a 
living is made by what we get, but a life is made by what we give. By 
that measure, Luther Conyers lived one of the richest lives this 
Congress has ever recorded. I am grateful I did not wait to say so. On 
a personal note, he was my friend. He gave me his counsel when I needed 
it, his honesty when I did not want it, and his friendship through 
every season. Southwest Georgia has lost a giant, and I have lost a 
brother.
  The Apostle Paul wrote, ``I have fought a good fight, I have finished 
my course, I have kept the faith'' (2 Timothy 4:7). Luther Conyers 
fought for his country, finished his course, and kept faith with his 
family, his students, and his city for nearly a century. Now he is 
reunited with his beloved Gwendolyn, who went on ahead to wait for him.
  Mr. Speaker, I ask my colleagues to join my wife, Vivian, and me, 
along with the more than 765,000 people of Georgia's 2nd Congressional 
District, in extending our deepest sympathies to his daughters, Audrey 
and Luthenya; his granddaughter, Leslie; and the entire Conyers family. 
May they be comforted by an abiding faith and by the knowledge that the 
school bells, the council gavel, and the waters of that fountain will 
keep saying his name.
```
