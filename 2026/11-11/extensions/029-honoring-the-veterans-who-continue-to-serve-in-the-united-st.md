# HONORING THE VETERANS WHO CONTINUE TO SERVE IN THE UNITED STATES CONGRESS

**Speaking:** Issa, Darrell (I000056) [R-CA]

```
   HONORING THE VETERANS WHO CONTINUE TO SERVE IN THE UNITED STATES 
                                CONGRESS

                                 ______
                                 

                           HON. DARRELL ISSA

                             of california

                    in the house of representatives

                      Wednesday, November 11, 2026

  Mr. ISSA. Mr. Speaker, on this Veterans Day, I rise to recognize the 
extraordinary Americans who have worn our Nation's uniform, answered 
the call to serve, and embodied a lifelong commitment to something 
greater than themselves.
  Today, I also offer a special tribute to the veterans and military 
fellows who have served in my congressional office, and to the 
countless veterans who traded the battlefields of distant lands for the 
halls of Congress--continuing their service to our Nation in a 
different uniform.
  We ask young men and women to leave the comforts of home, stand watch 
in dangerous places, and endure hardship, separation, and sacrifice, 
and give the best years of their lives. When necessary, we also ask 
them for something else: to place their lives between this Nation and 
those who would do it harm. Our veterans unlock unimaginable potential 
in themselves. Knowing this, they don't flinch and instead reply ``send 
me.'' They do so without fully knowing what their service will demand 
of them--or what it will deliver to and for others. They know the 
freedoms we enjoy are not guaranteed. Fundamentally, they believe that 
America, imperfect as she may be, is worth defending.
  For many, military service becomes a defining chapter in their lives. 
For others, including those I am privileged to work alongside every 
day, it is only the beginning of a lifetime devoted to our country.
  In my office some veterans came to us through military fellowship 
programs, allowing them to leverage their experience and call upon 
their training to shape and impact the legislative process. Others 
completed their military careers, hung up their uniforms, and then 
served in a different way, in a different mission--as congressional 
staff. We are so fortunate they chose to bring the lessons and legacy 
of service to the People's House.
  Their battlefield is different now. Their tools are legislation, 
oversight, constituent service, and the power of the written word. 
Their missions are measured not in miles patrolled or objectives 
secured, but in laws strengthened, veterans assisted, communities 
protected, and opportunities created for the next generation of 
Americans. Yet the oath that brought them here remains unchanged.
  That is what makes their continued service so meaningful. Long after 
the deployments have ended, the formations have dismissed, and the 
uniforms have been folded away, the sense of duty remains. The uniform 
may come off, but the sense of duty we carry never leaves us. I know 
this because I, too, have worn our Nation's uniform. I know the pride 
of serving alongside fellow Americans from every walk of life. I know 
the bonds that military service creates, the sacrifices it asks of our 
families, and the profound responsibility that comes with being 
entrusted to defend the Constitution of the United States.
  I know that some of the finest Americans I have ever met are those 
who continue to ask, long after their military service has ended, 
``What more can I do for my country?'' That spirit lives within the 
walls of my congressional office.
  I see it in the veteran who stays late to ensure a fellow service 
member receives the benefits they have earned. I see it in the military 
fellow who brings years of operational experience to the difficult 
questions of national security. I see it in the staff whose military 
service taught them that leadership is not about rank or recognition, 
but about responsibility to the people who depend upon you. I see it in 
those who understand that behind every defense authorization, every 
veterans' benefits decision, and every vote to send American forces 
into harm's way are real people, real families, and consequences that 
may last for generations.
  Their experience reminds us that the decisions made in these halls 
reach far beyond Washington. They reach the young soldier standing 
watch thousands of miles from home. They reach the military spouse 
carrying the burdens of family life during another deployment. They 
reach the veteran navigating an uncertain transition to civilian life. 
They reach the families of those who never returned. These are not 
abstract policy questions to the veterans who serve as congressional 
staff. They are personal. They have faces, names, and memories attached 
to them. That perspective makes this institution stronger. The staff 
who have served are bound by a shared duty and privilege that 
transcends any political party. The veterans within these hallowed 
walls are no summer soldiers, they are true heroes who have made a 
choice to serve this great Nation not once, but twice.
  Mr. Speaker, on Veterans Day, we rightly honor courage in combat, 
sacrifice in service, and devotion to duty. We should also honor 
something quieter and no less profound: the decision to keep serving 
when their duty has been long fulfilled.
  There is no parade for the veteran who helps a constituent navigate 
the Department of Veterans Affairs. No medal for the staff member who 
works tirelessly to improve the lives of military families. No ceremony 
for the defense fellow whose expertise helps Congress better understand 
the burdens we place upon those in uniform. Much of this work happens 
far from public view, without applause or recognition. But it matters. 
It matters deeply. Because the true measure of service is not found 
solely in what we accomplished while wearing the uniform. It is also 
found in what we choose to do with the lives, opportunities, and 
freedoms that service helped preserve.
  The veterans in my office are living examples of that truth. There 
are many monuments honoring military service within a few square miles 
of where I stand now, but if we seek a monument to American military 
success--look around you. The veterans who walk these halls stand as 
living examples of what we are so proud of today.
  Each of them have my deepest gratitude. I thank them for the 
sacrifices they made in uniform. I thank the families who stood beside 
them and shared in those sacrifices. I thank them for the experiences, 
wisdom, humility, and integrity they bring to this institution. Above 
all, I thank them for believing that their country was worthy of their 
service not once, but again.
  I am proud to serve alongside them. I am uplifted by their 
dedication. I am reminded every day that America's greatest strength 
has never been its weapons, its wealth, but the stalwart character and 
indomitable courage of those who volunteer to her.
  On this Veterans Day, may we remember those who came before us, honor 
those who stand watch today, and recognize those who continue to serve 
in ways that do not require a uniform.
  To the veterans and military fellows of my office, and to the 
veterans serving throughout

[[Page E1200]]

the Congress: The Nation remains in their debt, their continued service 
is an example to us all, and their commitment to the American people is 
worthy of recognition in this House.
  They answered the country's call once--they raised their hands and 
ran towards danger when the Nation said ``send me.'' When that chapter 
ended, they answered it again.
  I include in the Record the names of the veterans who have answered 
the call in my tenure:

       Colin Aamot, John Cloninger, David Cook, David A. Foley, 
     Jr., Travis Galloway, Anthony ``Tony'' Garofano, Michael 
     Hileman, Damion Jacobs, Bryan Maass, Christopher Mika, 
     Michael O'Neil, Kirk Proctor, Todd Putnam, John Richards, 
     Glenn Sanders, Dre Stafford, David Tebbe, and Eric Tubbs.

  May God bless our veterans, may God bless their families, and may God 
continue to bless the United States of America.
```
