  It is far past time to remedy the injustice that is the Wounded 
Warrior tax. The Major Richard Star Act would do just that, finally 
allowing our combat-wounded veterans to receive the full, fair, and 
just compensation they have earned through their service and sacrifice.
  Before we get to that, I must say how shameful it is that last night 
on the eve of Veterans Day, the outgoing Speaker of the House has 
decided to ambush veterans by bringing to the floor the largest cut to 
veterans benefits in history. The Take Care of America's Veterans Act 
and the billions of dollars of cuts it contains fails to honor the 
promise we made to America's veterans. This is the parting gift of the 
current Republican majority, and Members should reject this harmful and 
hurtful bill outright.
  Mr. Speaker, this bill forces veterans to pay the tab for their own 
benefits and creates two classes of veterans by making those currently 
serving ineligible for the same benefits current veterans receive.
  Any perceived good that may be in TCAVA is more than outweighed by 
the harm inflicted by the way that Republicans are choosing to pay for 
them.
  As I said, this is not just me saying this. This bill has been 
fiercely debated in the veterans community as well.
  Let me read a few of the statements from some of the largest veteran 
service organizations in the country on TCAVA. This is from the VFW. 
The VFW has said in a press release last week: ``Section 108 [of TCAVA]

sleep apnea, resulting in lower disability compensation for some future 
veterans.
  ``These cuts would help pay for other provisions in the bill. But 
section 108 goes further by putting changes to disability ratings into 
law instead of allowing VA to make those changes through its normal 
process. That would make the changes harder to reverse, even if new 
evidence shows they should be reconsidered.
  ``It could also set a precedent for Congress to cut compensation for 
other service-connected disabilities in the future simply to save 
money.
  ``Veterans who already receive compensation would be protected from 
reductions under this provision, but future veterans could receive less 
for the same disabilities.''
  ``Congress should not improve benefits for some veterans by cutting 
benefits for others.''

Act was included in the Take Care of America's Veterans Act, a broader 
package of veterans legislation. That version would place limits on the

future disability compensation by $57 billion for up to 1.5 million
