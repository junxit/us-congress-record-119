# CYBERSECURITY FOR SMALL BUSINESSES ACT OF 2026

**Speaking:** Williams, Roger (W000816) [R-TX], Velazquez, Nydia M. (V000081) [D-NY], Wied, Tony (W000829) [R-WI]

**Measures:** H.R. 10238

```
             CYBERSECURITY FOR SMALL BUSINESSES ACT OF 2026

  Mr. WILLIAMS of Texas. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and 
pass the bill (H.R. 10238) to require the Administrator of the Small 
Business Administration to disseminate to small business concerns 
certain information and resources relating to cybersecurity matters, 
and for other purposes, as amended.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 10238

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Cybersecurity for Small 
     Businesses Act of 2026''.

     SEC. 2. INFORMATION AND RESOURCES RELATING TO CYBERSECURITY 
                   MATTERS FOR SMALL BUSINESS CONCERNS.

       The Administrator of the Small Business Administration, in 
     consultation with the Director of the Cybersecurity and 
     Infrastructure Security Agency and the Director of the 
     National Institute of Standards and Technology, shall develop 
     and disseminate information and resources to assist small 
     business concerns in strengthening the cybersecurity 
     infrastructure of such concerns and implementing 
     cybersecurity best practices.

     SEC. 3. CYBERSECURITY COMPLIANCE INFORMATION.

       (a) Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification Resources.--
     The Administrator of the Small Business Administration, in 
     coordination with the Secretary of Defense and other 
     appropriate Federal agencies, shall provide information and 
     resources to a small business concern seeking to enter into a 
     contract with the

[[Page H6158]]

     Federal Government, or seeking to be a subcontractor on a 
     Federal contract, regarding the Cybersecurity Maturity Model 
     Certification program (or successor program), including 
     information and resources produced and provided by--
       (1) the Director of Small and Disadvantaged Business 
     Utilization (established under section 15(k) of the Small 
     Business Act (15 U.S.C. 644(k))) for the Department of 
     Defense;
       (2) the program established under section 1807 of the 
     National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2026 (10 
     U.S.C. 144 note); or
       (3) the APEX Accelerator Program of the Department of 
     Defense (or a successor program).
       (b) Publication.--The Administrator shall include the 
     information described in subsection (a) in outreach and 
     communications of the Small Business Administration, 
     including through publication on a website of the 
     Administration.
       (c) Best Practices.--The Administrator shall annually 
     consult with the Chief Counsel for Advocacy of the Office of 
     Advocacy of the Administration to determine best practices 
     for the dissemination of other information relating to 
     cybersecurity matters to small business concerns.
       (d) Annual Report.--Not later than 90 days after the date 
     of the enactment of this Act, and annually thereafter, the 
     Administrator shall submit to Congress a report that includes 
     the number and a description of small business concerns that 
     contacted the Administrator during the year covered by the 
     report with matters relating to the cybersecurity of such 
     concerns.

  Mr. WILLIAMS of Texas. Mr. Speaker, I ask unanimous consent that all 
Members may have 5 legislative days in which to revise and extend their 
remarks and include extraneous material on the bill.

  Mr. WILLIAMS of Texas. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise today in strong support of H.R. 10238, the 
Cybersecurity for Small Businesses Act of 2026, as amended, introduced 
by Representatives Wied, Van Duyne, Latimer, and Delegate King-Hinds.
  Small businesses face a growing wave of cybersecurity threats and may 
lack the resources to defend themselves. In 2025 alone, 61 percent of 
small businesses reported being the victim of at least one successful 
cyberattack.
  Foreign adversaries use cyberattacks to steal American intellectual 
property and sensitive business information. A small business with a 
Federal contract can be their back door into government systems.
  H.R. 10238 helps close that gap. This bill directs the SBA to consult 
directly with the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency and 
the National Institute of Standards and Technology to deliver 
cybersecurity information and resources for small businesses.
  It also requires the SBA to provide a small business with information 
and resources on the Department of War's Cybersecurity Maturity Model 
Certification, or CMMC, program. It draws on existing resources rather 
than standing up a duplicative new program.
  This commonsense approach gives Main Street businesses easy access to 
cybersecurity resources and guidance without creating further Federal 
programs or new mandates.
  Mr. Speaker, I thank Representatives Wied, Van Duyne, Latimer, and 
Delegate King-Hinds for their hard work on this bipartisan effort, and 
I urge all of my colleagues to support H.R. 10238. Mr. Speaker, I 
reserve the balance of my time.
  Ms. VELAZQUEZ. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Cybersecurity is a significant threat for many small businesses. 
According to the U.S. Chamber of Commerce, 99 percent of small 
businesses use at least one platform, but only a quarter use 
cybersecurity malware detection tools.
  This disconnect is driven by the high cost and increasing complexity 
of cybersecurity measures and practices, especially with the advent of 
AI.
  These burdens are particularly pronounced for small government 
contractors who need to comply with government-unique cybersecurity and 
supply chain risk management requirements to enter or remain in the 
Federal market.
  The latest version of new cybersecurity regulation, known as the 
Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification, sets a standard designed to 
ensure contractors have adequate security in place to safeguard 
controlled information.
  Implementation of this standard has been far from smooth, and the 
cost to the industrial base and small contractor is staggering.
  H.R. 10238 is designed to bolster access to resources like assessment 
tools, counseling help, and education for small government contractors.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to support the bill, and I reserve 
the balance of my time.
  Mr. WILLIAMS of Texas. Mr. Speaker, I yield such time as he may 
consume to the gentleman from Wisconsin (Mr. Wied).
  Mr. WIED. Mr. Speaker, I rise today in support of my bill, H.R. 
10238, the Cybersecurity for Small Businesses Act.
  In recent years, American businesses have faced rising cybersecurity 
threats from both foreign and domestic actors. While large businesses 
often have experts dedicated to preventing these attacks, small 
businesses often do not. This lack of resources leaves our Nation's 
small businesses especially vulnerable.

  My bill requires the Small Business Administration to coordinate with 
other Federal agencies such as the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure 
Security Agency when disseminating recommendations to small businesses 
on how to secure their cyber infrastructure. This will ensure our 
Nation's small businesses receive the most accurate and up-to-date 
information.
  Additionally, my bill will require the SBA to provide small 
businesses with clear information on how to comply with the 
Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification program created last year by 
the Department of War.
  This will help small businesses better understand the cybersecurity 
requirements that they must meet to compete for Federal contracts and 
ensure that they are not forced to spend hundreds of thousands of 
dollars on cybersecurity measures that they simply don't need because 
Washington bureaucrats have failed to provide clear guidance.
  This commonsense bill will guarantee small businesses have access to 
high-quality, practical, and most up-to-date resources they need to 
protect themselves from cyberattacks.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge all my colleagues to support this important 
legislation and stand with the America's small businesses.
```
