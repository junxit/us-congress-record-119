  Mr. TAKANO. Mr. Speaker, I ask unanimous consent that all Members may

  Mr. TAKANO. Mr. Speaker, the subject of the Special Order hour today 
is about our veterans, the men and women who served our Nation and 
military.
  It is fitting that on the 11th of November, a day we call Veterans 
Day--a tradition that began in the wake of World War I, the 11th hour 
of the 11th day of the 11th month--we use this day to remember the 
sacrifices of the people who sacrificed for us.
  It is with a mixture of reverence for their service, but also, 
because of the events today, I am angry. I am sad. I am confused by 
what I feel is the unjust and unfair way that the majority has 
conducted themselves with regard to how this Chamber works.
  I am trying to contain and make sure that my anger does not explode 
to the extent that I will say things I will regret.
  But my deepest regret is that on this day, of all days, on Veterans 
Day, we would have seen, but for the distractions, the decoys that the 
majority used to distract and to put forward and move forward through a 
specious voice vote, the Taking Care of America's Veterans Act, which 
in reality is an ironic title.
  There is nothing really about this act that takes care of veterans. 
It is the largest cut to veterans' benefits in the history of the 
United States.
  The way that the majority contrived to push this bill through was to 
use decoys in a decoy way, the floor staff, to negotiate technical 
changes to the bill that we were supposed to agree to on unanimous 
consent, and while this was happening, they very quietly put forward a 
vote on TCAVA.
  Subsequently, there was no opportunity for the minority to ask for 
its right for a recorded vote. There was no opportunity later for the 
majority to grant that vote. As a result, there has been no 
accountability for the Members of this Chamber to take responsibility 
for what they would have voted on on TCAVA.
  For a bill that was supposed to be so good for veterans, I don't 
understand why there would have been an eagerness to be recorded as an 
``aye'' vote. But, alas, this was not their intention. Their intention 
was not to have an open vote, a roll call vote. Their intention was to 
sneak this through.
  Well, all I can say is I think it is a shallow and hollow victory 
because it will go nowhere in the Senate. Why? Because veterans service 
organizations across the country have made clear their opposition to 
the Taking Care of America's Veterans Act, TCAVA. They have made clear 
that the largest cut to veterans' benefits in history is simply not 
acceptable.
  It is disgraceful that they pushed through this bill in the way that 
they did on today, of all days.
  Today, of all days, Mr. Speaker, the majority has pushed through the 
largest cut to veterans' benefits in history, all the while our men and 
women in uniform are on bases around Iran, subject to drone attacks, 
and subject to the explosions and the blasts.

  What this majority did is push through a bill that would say that 
those men and women in uniform will not be able to make claims of 
hearing loss or another condition that is associated with traumatic 
brain injury and PTSD, which is sleep apnea. These are two of the most 
prevalent conditions that veterans make claims on.
  The Republican majority has said, in its infinite wisdom, that these 
servicemembers who are serving right now will not be able to make 
claims for those conditions so that they can ``save'' $57 billion.
  I say this is a hollow victory. It is very shallow. It is despicable 
that the floor tactics that were used today have resulted in this 
hollow victory.
  But what remains is the work of the whole Democratic Caucus and a few 
Republicans who were willing to sign the discharge petition. I will 
note that the discharge petition, the motion to discharge, the Major 
Richard Star Act, was debated this morning, with 33 Republicans joining 
all the Democrats in voting for that motion to discharge in order to 
bring the Richard Star Act to the floor.
  That was an indication to me that TCAVA was on a pathway this 
afternoon to sure and certain failure.
  This is, I think, very clear evidence of why the Republican majority 
was motivated to do what it did today, which is to use really 
treacherous and devious floor tactics to achieve their ends.
  I do not think that they are proud of TCAVA, otherwise they would 
have been eager to call for the floor vote themselves. That happens all 
the time.
  They should have been proud on Veterans Day to tout the centerpiece 
legislation on veterans. But they know that too many of their side, too 
many of their Members were not going to vote for this.

on another matter, the House moved to a voice vote on TCAVA, with the 
Chair quickly declaring that the yeas had prevailed without 
the opportunity for a roll call, while House Veterans' Affairs 
Committee Ranking Member Mark Takano was called off the floor to 
discuss amending the bill.

Congress were not required to go on the record on legislation that

to know where their elected Representatives stand. They deserve more 
than procedural maneuvering that leaves the public questioning whether 
their Representatives were willing to accept accountability for their 
votes.
