# DISASTER LOAN OUTREACH CONSISTENCY ACT

**Speaking:** Williams, Roger (W000816) [R-TX], Velazquez, Nydia M. (V000081) [D-NY], Mejia, Analilia (M001246) [D-NJ], King-Hinds, Kimberlyn (K000404) [R-MP]

**Measures:** H.R. 10355

```
                 DISASTER LOAN OUTREACH CONSISTENCY ACT

  Mr. WILLIAMS of Texas. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and 
pass the bill (H.R. 10355) to require the Administrator of the Small 
Business Administration to establish procedures for consistency of 
certain disaster loan outreach materials, and for other purposes.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 10355

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Disaster Loan Outreach 
     Consistency Act''.

     SEC. 2. PROCEDURES FOR CONSISTENCY OF DISASTER ASSISTANCE 
                   OUTREACH MATERIALS.

       (a) Outreach Materials.--Not later than 180 days after the 
     date of the enactment of this Act, the Administrator of the 
     Small Business Administration shall establish procedures to 
     ensure that outreach materials, including any press releases 
     and fact sheets, prepared by each disaster field operations 
     center of the Administration consistently include accurate 
     and current information regarding--
       (1) the terms of the disaster loan program carried out 
     under section 7(b) of the Small Business Act (15 U.S.C. 
     636(b));
       (2) any applicable changes to Federal disaster assistance 
     eligibility processes relevant to applicants for such 
     disaster loan program; and
       (3) such additional information, as determined by the 
     Administrator.
       (b) Procedures.--The procedures established under 
     subsection (a) shall include--
       (1) standardized templates for outreach materials;
       (2) a process to verify, before public release, that 
     outreach materials use the current templates and reflect 
     current terms and eligibility processes for the disaster loan 
     program;
       (3) a review process to ensure consistency in outreach 
     materials and templates across disaster field operations 
     centers, where such review occurs annually and immediately 
     after any significant changes to such procedures; and
       (4) such additional procedures, as determined by the 
     Administrator.
       (c) Notification.--Not later than 30 days after 
     establishing or making significant changes to the procedures 
     described in subsection (b), the Administrator shall submit 
     to the Committee on Small Business of the House of 
     Representatives and the Committee on Small Business and 
     Entrepreneurship of the Senate a notice of such 
     establishment.

  Mr. Speaker, I rise today in strong support of H.R. 10355, the 
Disaster Loan Outreach Consistency Act, introduced by Representative 
Mejia from the great State of New Jersey and Delegate King-Hinds from 
the great Northern Mariana Islands.
  When disaster strikes, small businesses need clear, accurate 
information to get back on their feet. A 2023 GAO report found that 
after the SBA updated materials under new rules, the information 
distributed by field operations centers did not always match, and this 
is a problem we can and should fix.
  This legislation requires the SBA to establish procedures to ensure 
disaster information is accurate, consistent, and up-to-date, and as a 
result, small business owners will receive clear information in the 
wake of a disaster.

  Ms. KING-HINDS. Mr. Speaker, H.R. 10355, the Disaster Loan Outreach 
Consistency Act, which I am proud to co-lead with Representative Mejia, 
addresses a straightforward but important issue: Disaster survivors 
deserve consistent, accurate, and up-to-date information about the 
assistance available to them regardless of which SBA field operations 
center handles their disaster.
  In the Northern Mariana Islands, we know firsthand how important 
timely disaster assistance is. Just this year, our communities were 
struck by two super typhoons, and families and businesses across the 
Commonwealth are still recovering.
  SBA's two disaster field operations centers play a critical role in 
communicating with survivors and explaining the disaster loan programs 
available to them. For many survivors, SBA's fact sheets, press 
releases, and other outreach materials are their primary source of 
information about these programs. That information needs to be accurate 
and up to date.
  Unfortunately, a May 2026 GAO report found inconsistencies in SBA's 
disaster loan outreach materials. Depending on which field operations 
center handled a disaster, some materials did not include updated 
information, including changes to mitigation loan limits and deadline 
extensions.
  Mr. Speaker, survivors should not receive different answers simply 
because their disaster is handled by a different SBA field office. H.R. 
10355 would require SBA to establish procedures to ensure its disaster 
outreach materials are consistent, accurate, and current. The bill 
requires standardized templates, review and verification before 
materials are released, and periodic reviews to ensure information 
remains up to date across both field operations centers.
  This is a commonsense fix to ensure that disaster survivors get the 
information they need when they need it the most.
  Mr. Speaker, I thank Chairman Williams and Ranking Member Velazquez,

support, and I urge my colleagues to adopt this bipartisan legislation.
  Ms. VELAZQUEZ. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself the balance of my time.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise today in support of H.R. 10355, the Disaster Loan 
Outreach Consistency Act.
  When disaster strikes--and it is striking more frequently than ever 
before--it is crucial that victims have access to high-quality, up-to-
date information about the resources available to them. As these 
programs evolve, the information and outreach related to those programs 
must be updated to reflect the realities of the world we live in.
  The SBA's disaster loan program, which provides long-term, low-
interest loans to individuals, homeowners, and businesses in the wake 
of a catastrophe, has seen several rule changes.
  For instance, in 2023, SBA updated the program to reflect cost-of-
living increases. The following year, FEMA updated their programs to 
remove the requirement that people apply for the disaster loan program 
as a precondition for some types of assistance. These may sound minor, 
but they make a material difference for disaster victims.
  Unfortunately, the GAO found that not all victims were provided with 
full information about the rule changes. Specifically, some press 
releases contained different information depending on which part of the 
country you lived. As a result, people in the Eastern U.S. often had 
more information about increased loan limits or the FEMA application 
process than people in the Western half of the United States.
  The Disaster Loan Outreach Consistency Act simply codifies GAO 
recommendations that SBA establish a process to ensure all disseminated 
information related to the disaster loan program is current and 
consistent no matter where you live.
  I thank Representative Mejia and Delegate King-Hinds for their 
bipartisan work in bringing this commonsense legislation forward.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge my colleagues to vote ``yes,'' and I yield back 
the balance of my time.

reliable information they need to access disaster assistance and 
rebuild their communities.
```
