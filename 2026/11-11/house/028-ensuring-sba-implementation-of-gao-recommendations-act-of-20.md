# ENSURING SBA IMPLEMENTATION OF GAO RECOMMENDATIONS ACT OF 2026

**Speaking:** Williams, Roger (W000816) [R-TX], Velazquez, Nydia M. (V000081) [D-NY], McIver, LaMonica (M001229) [D-NJ]

**Measures:** H.R. 10371

```
     ENSURING SBA IMPLEMENTATION OF GAO RECOMMENDATIONS ACT OF 2026

  Mr. WILLIAMS of Texas. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and 
pass the bill (H.R. 10371) to direct the Associate Administrator for 
Veterans Business Development of the Small Business Administration to 
ensure compliance with certain reporting requirements, and for other 
purposes.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 10371

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Ensuring SBA Implementation 
     of GAO Recommendations Act of 2026''.

     SEC. 2. COMPLIANCE WITH CERTAIN REPORTING REQUIREMENTS.

       Not later than 60 days after the date of enactment of this 
     Act, the Associate Administrator for Veterans Business 
     Development of the Small Business Administration shall 
     develop and implement policies and procedures to ensure 
     compliance with the requirements of section 603 of the 
     Veterans Entrepreneurship and Small Business Development Act 
     of 1999 (Public Law 106-50; 15 U.S.C. 657b note).

  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Pursuant to the rule, the gentleman from 
Texas (Mr. Williams) and the gentlewoman from New York (Ms. Velazquez) 
each will control 20 minutes.
  The Chair recognizes the gentleman from Texas.


                             General Leave

  Mr. WILLIAMS of Texas. Mr. Speaker, I ask unanimous consent that all 
Members may have 5 legislative days in which to revise and extend their 
remarks and include extraneous material on the bill.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Is there objection to the request of the 
gentleman from Texas?
  There was no objection.

  Mr. WILLIAMS of Texas. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise today in strong support of the bill H.R. 10371, 
the Ensuring SBA Implementation of GAO Recommendations Act of 2026 
introduced by Representative McIver from the great State of New Jersey 
and Ranking Member Velazquez from the great State of New York.
  Our Nation's veterans have served to protect and defend the United 
States, and we should ensure that they have the support they need to 
succeed when they transition to civilian life. This includes when they 
choose to start or grow a small business.
  Congress has long recognized the importance of supporting veteran 
entrepreneurs. In 1999, Congress directed the

[[Page H6156]]

SBA to do a report on how its programs serve veteran small business 
owners, including contracting opportunities and ways to strengthen 
support.
  The SBA already provides much of this information through existing 
reports, however, a 2023 GAO report found those reports did not fully 
capture the specific requirements Congress established back in 1999.
  This legislation makes the SBA's existing reporting requirements 
digestible and easier to follow. As a result, Congress will have a 
clearer picture of how the SBA is supporting veteran small business 
owners.
  Mr. Speaker, I reserve the balance of my time.
  Ms. VELAZQUEZ. Mr. Speaker, I yield such time as she may consume to 
the gentlewoman from New Jersey (Mrs. McIver).
  Mrs. McIVER. Mr. Speaker, I rise today in strong support of my bill, 
H.R. 10371, the Ensuring SBA Implementation of GAO Recommendations Act 
of 2026, a commonsense, bipartisan bill that I am proud to lead 
alongside my wonderful Ranking Member Velazquez of New York.
  In New Jersey's 10th Congressional District, I have the privilege of 
representing more than 15,000 veterans who answered the call to serve 
our country. These are our neighbors, our family members, and our 
community leaders, and we owe it to them to ensure that their service 
to our country is met with real support and opportunity.
  For many veterans, that service continues long after they return 
home. As they start small businesses, create jobs, and invest in the 
neighborhoods where they live, the Small Business Administration has an 
important responsibility to ensure that these veteran entrepreneurs 
have access to the Federal resources and assistance they need to 
succeed.
  Congress has a responsibility to ensure that these programs are 
actually delivering for the veterans they were designed to serve.
  That is why, more than 25 years ago, Congress required the SBA to 
submit an annual report on the needs of veteran-owned and service-
disabled-veteran-owned small businesses, their participation in SBA 
programs, and their access to Federal contracting opportunities.
  Unfortunately, the Government Accountability Office found that the 
SBA could not prove it was complying with this longstanding reporting 
requirement.
  Mr. Speaker, without complete and consistent reporting, Congress 
can't know whether veteran entrepreneurs are receiving the support they 
deserve. We also can't identify where improvements are needed.
  My bill offers a straightforward solution. It will require SBA to 
comply with reporting requirements on the needs of veteran-owned and 
service-disabled-veteran-owned small businesses, including 
recommendations to improve their access to Federal assistance, 
programs, and contracting opportunities.
  This is not about creating new burdens. In fact, it will make sure 
the system is less burdensome for our veterans. This bill would ensure 
accountability, improve transparency, and make certain that the Federal 
Government is fulfilling its duties to our Nation's veterans.
  Mr. Speaker, as we observe Veterans Day today, we are reminded of the 
tremendous sacrifices our veterans have made in service to our country.
  Honoring that service must go beyond words. It must also mean 
ensuring that our veterans have the resources, opportunities, and 
support they deserve when they return home.
  Our veterans have upheld their commitments to this country. The very 
least we can do is ensure that our government upholds its commitments 
to them.
  Mr. Speaker, I thank my ranking member for her commitment and her 
dedication. We love her and owe her a deep amount of gratitude for her 
commitment and work on the Small Business Committee.
  Mr. Speaker, I urge members to support my bill.

strengthen support for veteran-owned small businesses and ensure

  Ms. VELAZQUEZ. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself the balance of my time.
  I am pleased to support H.R. 10371, the Ensuring SBA Implementation 
of GAO Recommendations Act of 2026.
  I commend Mrs. McIver for her initiative requiring SBA to close out a 
priority GAO recommendation, and I am pleased to join her as an 
original cosponsor.
  The Veterans Entrepreneurship and Small Business Development Act of 
1999 requires the SBA to submit an annual report to the House and 
Senate Small Business and the Veterans' Affairs Committees on the needs 
of veteran-owned and service-disabled-veteran-owned businesses and 
offer ways to improve access to them.
  The GAO found that the SBA's existing reporting did not include the 
requisite information, and it issued a priority recommendation that SBA 
establish procedures to comply with the requirement. This is a 
straightforward recommendation for SBA to close out, and doing so will 
deliver an important benefit to veterans.
  Before SBA cuts staff and moves offices around, it should make sure 
it is getting the basics right. As we celebrate Veterans Day, it is 
even more imperative that SBA close out this recommendation in order to 
make a meaningful difference for programs that benefit veterans.
  Mr. Speaker, I am pleased to support this bipartisan bill, and I 
yield back the balance of my time.
  Mr. WILLIAMS of Texas. Mr. Speaker, as we celebrate America's 250th 
anniversary, let us honor those who defended our freedom by helping 
them pursue their American Dream.
  Our veterans have given so much to this country. Supporting their 
success and entrepreneurship is one meaningful way that we can give 
back.
  I urge my colleagues to vote in favor of H.R. 10371 and stand with 
the veteran small business owners who continue to strengthen our 
Nation.
  Mr. Speaker, I yield back the balance of my time.
```
