# BAR RUSSIAN OIL SALES

**Speaking:** Kaptur, Marcy (K000009) [D-OH]

```
                         BAR RUSSIAN OIL SALES

  (Under the Speaker's announced policy of January 3, 2025, Ms. Kaptur 
of Ohio was recognized for 30 minutes.)
  Ms. KAPTUR. Mr. Speaker, I rise today at a moment of great 
consequence for our allies in Ukraine and the cause of liberty across 
the continent of Europe, all our closest allies.

[[Page H6180]]

  The American people fully know that dictator Vladimir Putin is 
liberty's foe. They know that President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and the 
brave Ukrainian people are our allies in liberty.
  Shockingly, President Trump's advisers have chosen to ignore this 
difference thus far. Instead of pressuring Putin to end his war of 
aggression through the bipartisan sanctions this Congress passed, the 
Trump administration is taking sanctions off of Russia so that Russia 
can sell its Russian diesel here and make money to kill more 
Ukrainians, lots of innocent people--women, and children--in civilian 
areas.
  The same Russian blood money is also being used by Putin to buy 
Iranian missiles that are killing American troops in the Middle East. 
Congress passed legislation that any business or government entity that 
violates existing law and facilitates the purchase of Russian diesel 
will face real consequences.
  Liberty has won for now, as Russian diesel tankers have been turned 
back from America's shores by the Governors of New Jersey and New York.
  Going forward, Congress must formally kill illegitimate diesel deals 
and force our President to sanction Russia. President Trump, to date, 
has made it clear that he will not choose the defense of liberty.
  For the cause of liberty and peace, Congress must bar Russian oil 
sales in our Nation. It is the only logical choice.


                          Recognizing Veterans

  Ms. KAPTUR. Mr. Speaker, I rise to honor and recognize our veterans 
on this Veterans Day 2026. Let us salute and thank all veterans for 
their noble service to our Nation. I thank their loved ones for their 
sacrifice.
  Just in my region of the United States, I represent over 60,000 
veterans in northwestern Ohio, and I consider it the honor of a 
lifetime to serve veterans and military families across our region of 
northwestern Ohio, but also wherever they might be stationed in our 
Nation and across the world. America appreciates them. We see them. We 
love them.
  I come from a long line of beloved uncles, cousins, and family 
members who worked in our war industry. All of us were steeped in 
patriotism and military service. They instilled in me the need to serve 
in my own way and give back to our Nation that afforded my family 
freedom and opportunity.
  I have made support for America's veterans a major focus of my career 
in Congress. I want to put this on the record tonight because, for some 
of the younger Members coming in here from all over the Nation, if they 
work at it long enough, they can really do something here.
  The first committee assignment I received was to serve on the 
Veterans' Affairs Committee. I continue to serve our veterans today as 
a senior member of the Appropriations Subcommittee on Defense. I ended 
up, in that early service on the Veterans' Affairs Committee, as the 
only woman. I still remember that.
  Two of the most noble fights I have led in Congress were the long 
legislative battles--first, to build a World War II Memorial here in 
our Nation's Capital. It took us 16 years. Now, 100 million Americans 
and other visitors have gone to that very sacred place.
  It took 16 years to build the Veterans' Glass City Skyway Bridge 
across the largest river that flows into the Great Lakes, back home in 
Toledo, Ohio. Millions of vehicles have traversed that bridge now. The 
construction and manufacture of all the parts that went into that took 
an additional 16 years and probably about $400 million over several 
cycles of Congress.
  Along the way, I helped lead a community effort to save the 180th 
Fighter Wing when it was threatened by base closure. That wing has 
grown to be 1 of the 10 most strategically important wings in our 
country. We are proud of them and their achievements right in the heart 
of northern Ohio.
  We advocate every single day for the brave men and women who serve 
our Nation to defend the cause of liberty. One of our greatest 
achievements has been the construction of the veterans outpatient 
clinic in Toledo, Ohio.
  I also helped secure funding to open Defiance County's first VA-based 
outpatient clinic to serve our veterans on the western side of the 
State, and I continue to advocate for the Sandusky veterans home, which 
has served thousands of veterans in their elderly years.
  I continue to push for dual affiliation between our University of 
Toledo Medical Center and the Department of Veterans Affairs hospital 
system so that we can actually have the affiliation inside the State of 
Ohio rather than being one of the few States in the Union where you 
have to cross borders to get to a veterans hospital.
  I was able to authorize and secure funding for the largest expansion 
of VA care in American history through the passage of the Sergeant 
First Class Heath Robinson Honoring our Promise to Address 
Comprehensive Toxics Act of 2022, named after an Ohioan with family 
roots in Ohio's Ninth District.
  Burn pits and other toxic hazards have grievously wounded 
servicemembers from Vietnam, Iraq, and Afghanistan. Named in honor of 
Sergeant First Class Heath Robinson of Ohio, the PACT Act has expanded 
healthcare to 3.5 million veterans across our Nation. All Ohioans are 
proud of that achievement, and we have given it to the Nation and to 
those courageous veterans.
  I will continue to fight for this funding to stay accessible to 
veterans who served in Vietnam, the Gulf war, and the Middle East 
conflicts of Iraq and Afghanistan. They have served us, and now the 
Heath Robinson act will serve their health, well-being, and, believe 
me, that of their caring families.
  When I returned to Washington this week, I was reminded of the 
excellent bipartisan legislative efforts we have been proud to co-lead 
and even see them become law.

                              {time}  1850

  For example, earlier this year, I was glad to see the bipartisan Care 
for Military Kids Act, which I led with Representative Jen Kiggans of 
Virginia, signed into law as part of the crucial Federal budget 
legislation. It was a pleasure to work with Jen Kiggans, who herself is 
a veteran.
  I worked with Congressman Don Bacon of Nebraska to introduce a 
bipartisan bill recognizing the sacrifice of Gold Star spouses by 
expanding their healthcare benefits. I was thrilled to be a lead 
sponsor to create the National Medal of Honor Highway that goes through 
Ohio but is part of U.S. 20 from coast to coast, a 3,365-mile 
remembrance of those who gave us everything.
  We owe so much to those who perished in defense of our Nation. Caring 
for their surviving spouses is part of our commitment, and remembering 
them is part of our commitment.
  I hope we can see all bills signed into law one day, including the 
law that we hope will pass here tomorrow to award to veterans the 
health benefits that they have so deservedly earned.
  I encourage those who are listening to thank a veteran in your life 
today and reflect on the unimaginable sacrifice they made to keep us 
and our Nation safe. Their service can never be fully repaid and will 
never be forgotten. From a grateful nation, we thank them for their 
sacrifice.
  Mr. Speaker, before I yield, I wish to address an unprincipled action 
taken by the majority earlier today and I hope we can pick up the 
broken pieces tomorrow.
  After announcing a recorded vote on a benefits bill, which would only 
support some veterans by robbing others and taking money from other 
veteran programs, the majority began a conversation with the Democrats 
to discuss possible bipartisan changes. Then this majority used the 
absence of Democrats on the House floor to call-up the bill by voice 
vote and pass it.
  It was one of those legislative tricks that really isn't worthy of 
occurring on this floor. Shady actions like this don't happen without 
intention. This was a setup to pass partisan legislation over a 
stronger piece of bipartisan legislation. I think that is going to end 
soon, but the Senate should reject this trickery, and we should have 
passed the Major Richard Star Act as a clean stand-alone bill.
  On Veterans Day of all days, our veterans deserve better than that 
trickery. They deserve to be represented by people who share their 
morals and, frankly, their honor. I hope that in the new Congress, we 
can truly treat them with the honor and respect they have earned and 
deserve.
  Mr. Speaker, I yield back the balance of my time on this very 
memorable day of this year of 2026.
```
