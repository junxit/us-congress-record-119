# COMBATING FOREIGN THREATS TO MAIN STREET ACT OF 2026

**Speaking:** Williams, Roger (W000816) [R-TX], Velazquez, Nydia M. (V000081) [D-NY], Tran, Derek (T000491) [D-CA]

**Measures:** H.R. 10329

```
          COMBATING FOREIGN THREATS TO MAIN STREET ACT OF 2026

  Mr. WILLIAMS of Texas. Mr. Speaker, I move to suspend the rules and 
pass the bill (H.R. 10329) to require the Administrator of the Small 
Business Administration to disseminate information on foreign adversary 
conduct that poses material harm to small business concerns, and for 
other purposes.
  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 10329

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Combating Foreign Threats to 
     Main Street Act of 2026''.

     SEC. 2. INFORMATION DISSEMINATION ON FOREIGN ADVERSARY 
                   CONDUCT THAT POSES MATERIAL HARM TO SMALL 
                   BUSINESS CONCERNS.

       (a) Educational Materials.--The Administrator of the Small 
     Business Administration, in consultation with the appropriate 
     Federal agency heads, shall develop and disseminate 
     educational materials regarding foreign adversary conduct 
     that poses material harm to small business concerns.
       (b) Dissemination.--The Administrator shall make the 
     educational materials described in subsection (a) available 
     through district offices and other programs of the 
     Administration.
       (c) Contents.--The educational materials described in 
     subsection (a) shall, at a minimum--
       (1) describe the nature of the foreign adversary conduct 
     that poses material harm to small business concerns;
       (2) describe how such conduct materially harms small 
     business concerns;
       (3) identify best practices for small business concerns to 
     protect intellectual property, sensitive business 
     information, and supply chains;
       (4) provide guidance regarding cybersecurity and data 
     protection for small business concerns;
       (5) inform small business concerns of risks associated with 
     foreign investment, joint ventures, licensing arrangements, 
     and other commercial relationships involving entities owned 
     or controlled by a foreign adversary or an additional covered 
     entity;
       (6) describe resources available from the Federal 
     Government to help small business concerns identify, avoid, 
     prevent, and address such conduct; and
       (7) provide guidance on safeguarding business interests in 
     foreign markets without unnecessarily restricting lawful 
     commercial activity.
       (d) Annual Review and Updates.--Beginning October 1, 2027, 
     and annually thereafter, the Administrator, in consultation 
     with the appropriate Federal agency heads, shall--
       (1) review the educational materials described in 
     subsection (a) to ensure such materials reflect emerging 
     foreign adversary conduct that poses material harm to small 
     business concerns, evolving tactics used by foreign 
     adversaries, changes in applicable Federal law and policy, 
     and current best practices for protecting small business 
     concerns; and
       (2) update such materials as necessary to remain accurate 
     and up to date.
       (e) Notice.--If, under subparagraph (F) of subsection 
     (f)(6), the Administrator determines, in consultation with 
     the appropriate Federal agency heads, that an activity poses 
     a significant risk to the economic or national security 
     interests of the United States, the Administrator shall 
     notify the Committee on Small Business of the House of 
     Representatives and the Committee on Small Business and 
     Entrepreneurship of the Senate of the determination, what 
     specific activity has been determined to pose such a

[[Page H6153]]

     risk, and the justification for such determination of such 
     activity.
       (f) Definitions.--In this section:
       (1) Additional covered entity.--The term ``additional 
     covered entity'' means an entity, an affiliate of an entity, 
     or an individual included on a list described in section 
     9(g)(16)(D) of the Small Business Act (15 U.S.C. 
     638(g)(16)(D)).
       (2) Administration.--The term ``Administration'' means the 
     Small Business Administration.
       (3) Administrator.--The term ``Administrator'' means the 
     Administrator of the Small Business Administration.
       (4) Appropriate federal agency head.--The term 
     ``appropriate Federal agency head'' means the Secretary of 
     Homeland Security, the Secretary of State, the Director of 
     the Federal Bureau of Investigation, the Secretary of the 
     Treasury, the Secretary of Commerce, the United States Trade 
     Representative, the Director of the United States Patent and 
     Trademark Office, the Director of the United States Copyright 
     Office, and the head of any other relevant Federal agency, as 
     determined by the Administrator of the Small Business 
     Administration.
       (5) Foreign adversary.--The term ``foreign adversary'' 
     means a foreign adversary, as defined in section 791.4 of 
     title 15, Code of Federal Regulations (or any successor 
     regulation).
       (6) Foreign adversary conduct that poses material harm to 
     small business concerns.--The term ``foreign adversary 
     conduct that poses material harm to small business concerns'' 
     means an activity carried out by a foreign adversary, an 
     entity owned or controlled by a foreign adversary, or an 
     additional covered entity that poses risks to the economic or 
     national security of the United States or to small business 
     concerns, including--
       (A) theft of intellectual property, trade secrets, or 
     proprietary business information;
       (B) cyber intrusions and malicious cyber-enabled 
     activities;
       (C) forced labor and the importation of goods produced 
     wholly or in part with forced labor;
       (D) counterfeit goods, illicit trade, customs evasion, and 
     related fraud;
       (E) predatory or non-market investment practices; and
       (F) any other activity the Administrator determines, in 
     consultation with the appropriate Federal agency heads, poses 
     a significant risk to the economic or national security 
     interests of the United States.
       (7) Small business concern.--The term ``small business 
     concern'' has the meaning given under section 3 of the Small 
     Business Act (15 U.S.C. 632).

                             General Leave

  Mr. WILLIAMS of Texas. Mr. Speaker, I ask unanimous consent that all 
Members have 5 legislative days in which to revise and extend their 
remarks and include extraneous material on the bill.
  The SPEAKER pro tempore. Is there objection to the request of the 
gentleman from Texas?
  There was no objection.
  Mr. WILLIAMS of Texas. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise today in strong support of H.R. 10329, the 
Combating Foreign Threats to Main Street Act of 2026. I introduced this 
bill alongside the gentleman from California (Mr. Tran), my friend.
  Whether it is a Fortune 500 company or a small business on Main 
Street, no American business is beyond the reach of our foreign 
adversaries. China, Russia, and North Korea are working to undermine 
our economic and national security, and American businesses are 
increasingly caught in the crosshairs.
  The Chinese Communist Party, in particular, is an expert at stealing 
intellectual property, exploiting unfair trade practices, and using 
predatory investment to gain economic advantage at our expense.
  While these threats affect businesses of every size, small businesses 
are often the most vulnerable, and they simply do not have the 
resources or expertise to identify these threats and protect 
themselves.
  This bill closes that gap. It requires the SBA to develop and publish 
educational materials on foreign adversary conduct that poses material 
harm to small businesses and their concerns.
  These materials would also help small businesses protect their 
intellectual property, sensitive information, and supply chains. They 
will also help small businesses understand the risks of foreign 
investment, joint ventures, and licensing arrangements tied to foreign 
adversaries.
  The SBA must also review and update these materials annually to keep 
pace with evolving threats. Congress must be notified if the SBA 
identifies such activity that poses a significant risk to our economic 
and our national security.
  By strengthening awareness of foreign threats on Main Street, we can 
better safeguard American innovation, protect American jobs, and ensure 
that our economic future is protected from foreign adversaries.
  I thank Representative Tran for joining me on this bill. I urge all 
my colleagues to support my bill, H.R. 10329. Mr. Speaker, I reserve 
the balance of my time.

  Mr. Speaker, as we bring this Congress to a close, I would like to 
express my sincere gratitude to my colleagues for their support and 
confidence in my leadership of the Small Business Committee both as the 
chair and ranking member. It has been an honor to serve alongside them. 
Throughout my tenure I have worked in a bipartisan manner to advance 
critical measures to help small businesses weather challenges from the 
pandemic and disasters to expanding lending and Federal contracting

  Most of our Nation's adversaries, like Russia, are pariah states 
shunned by the world for their military aggression and dictatorships. 
Their state-run firms often employ heinous, unfair practices, like 
espionage, counterfeiting, and nonmarket investment.
  A number of U.S. small businesses have fallen prey to these tactics. 
Some have been ensnared in predatory arrangements, had intellectual 
property stolen, and unknowingly broken the law. H.R. 10329 is a bona 
fide attempt to address these issues. It will mandate the creation of 
an educational resource for small businesses on foreign adversary 
conduct harming them, and how to avoid and respond to such conduct.
  Let me be clear. This bill is not meant to target essential business 
partners of mainstream firms based on ethnicity or national origin. In 
this vein, let me commend Mr. Tran's leadership and strength on 
protecting both small businesses and Asian Americans in his district 
and nationwide.

consideration and scrutiny that it deserves.

global influence, steal American innovation, and harm American small
```
