than 38,000 veterans who call North Carolina's Eighth District home and 
the millions of brave men and women across our great Nation who have 
proudly worn the uniform of the United States Armed Forces.
  From the battlefields of the Revolutionary War to the conflicts of 
today, generations of Americans have answered the call to serve, 
putting their lives on the line for the freedoms that we all hold dear.
  As a son of a World War II veteran, I know that the strength of our 
Nation is rooted in the courage and character of those who have 
defended it. Our veterans come from every walk of life. Whether serving 
on the front lines in times of war or standing ready during times of 
peace, each one of them has shared a commitment to something greater 
than themselves.
  That commitment has often come at a tremendous personal cost. Some 
made the ultimate sacrifice and never returned home. Others returned 
carrying wounds, both seen and unseen.
  For countless families, military service meant years apart, resulting 
in missed birthdays, holidays, and precious moments that can never be 
replaced.
  Their service does not end when they take off the uniform. Our 
veterans continue making a difference as teachers, first responders, 
businessowners, and community leaders.
  Let us remember that Veterans Day is not simply a day of recognition. 
It is a reminder that the freedoms we enjoy were not freely given and 
that our gratitude must extend far beyond a single day.
  We owe our veterans more than words of thanks. We owe them the 
respect, care, and benefits they have earned through their service to 
our country.
  To every veteran across North Carolina's Eighth District and 
throughout our Nation, let me say: Thank you. Thank you for answering 
the call. Thank you for the sacrifices you have made. Thank you for the 
example that you have set for generations of Americans.

God bless the United States of America.

  As our Founders put pen to paper, a group of ordinary men with

honor to this very unique idea, an idea that all men are created equal 
and endowed by their creator with the inalienable right to life, 
liberty, and the pursuit of happiness.
  Every generation since has prepared its young men and women to love 
our country enough to raise their hands to say: ``Send me.''
  I am a proud son of a World War II veteran, Clarence Burgess Owens.

Depression and nationwide segregation. He and his two brothers proudly 
volunteered to serve in World War II.
  My dad, due to his rifle skills, was part of our nation's first 
integrated reconnaissance team. He was trained in Alabama at Fort 
McClellan's Infantry Replacement Training Center as part of an Army 
experiment, an experiment of leadership integration in which Black and 
White officers were training Black enlisted soldiers.
  I was blessed to have a dad who always was my hero. He was 
exceptional but not an exception for the men of that generation. He 
knew how to dream big, work hard, compete for respect, and when 
necessary, run toward his fears, as did millions of young American 
soldiers during past wars.
  Last year, I ran across notes from his journal that summarized his 
feelings as a 20-year-old upon returning home from the Philippines and 
Japan. He wrote: ``I have fulfilled my dream to serve my country.''
  After returning home from war, dad earned his Ph.D. at Ohio State in 
agronomy, taught 40 years at FAMU, was a successful entrepreneur and 
father of five. He joined an aggregate of his fellow veterans who built 
the greatest diverse middle class in our Nation's history. They 
simultaneously tore down the walls of our country's racial divide.
  This is what defines our history as a nation: brave, patriotic 
Americans who answer the call, serve our Nation in uniform, and then

our country. It is a proud history of dedication and sacrifice. It is 
the greatest legacy we can give to those who come after us.

Americans now, and millions in the future, will benefit from the faith-
filled veterans who sacrifice for our country through their blood, 
sweat, and tears.

  Mr. Speaker, I rise today to commemorate Veterans Day and honor the 
brave men and women who have served in the United States Armed Forces.
  Throughout our Nation's history, generations of Americans have 
answered the call to defend our country, often at great personal 
sacrifice. From the battlefields to the American Revolution to the 
conflicts of today, our veterans have demonstrated extraordinary 
courage and willingness to put the needs of the country above their 
own.
  Winston Churchill once said, it is not enough that we do our best; 
sometimes we must do what is required. Our veterans and those serving 
in our military forces today understand this. When their Nation called, 
they answered, putting service above themselves and placing the 
freedoms of their fellow Americans ahead of their own comfort and 
safety.
  There have been many times that Americans have sacrificed themselves 
for our country. In the 250 years of our history, over 1.3 million have 
laid down their lives for our benefit.
  One such occasion was June 6, 1944, when American and Allied Forces 
stormed the beaches of Normandy in what would become one of the most 
consequential military operations in world history. Operation Overlord 
ended up being the turning point in World War II, paving the way for 
the liberation of France and eventually the defeat of Nazi Germany.
  We know how impactful that day was, but then, in the moments before 
they hit the shore, those soldiers had no idea what that day had in 
store. They did not know whether they would survive, whether their 
mission would succeed, or whether they would ever see their families 
again. But when the landing craft reached the beach and the ramps came 
down, those men ran headlong into hell.

                 Honoring the Life of Coach Ron Riddle

  Mr. BEAN of Florida. Mr. Speaker, I rise to honor the life and legacy 
of Coach Ron Riddle, a beloved educator, mentor, and coach at Clay High 
School in Green Cove Springs, Florida.
  There are teachers who educate, coaches who inspire, and community 
leaders who make a difference. Every once in a while someone comes 
along and does all three. For generations of Clay High Blue Devils, 
that person was Coach Ron Riddle.
  Coach Riddle was more than a coach. He was a mentor and a role model. 
For countless young people, he was someone who believed in them before 
they believed in themselves.
  He was a trailblazer. As one of the first Black teachers at Clay High 
School during integration, Coach Riddle helped break down barriers and 
open doors for others. For 37 years, he dedicated himself to teaching 
and coaching, instilling lessons that went far beyond athletics, 
discipline, perseverance, self-respect, and the importance of looking 
out for one another.
  My wife, Abby, was fortunate enough to know Coach Riddle. Growing up 
in Green Cove Springs, she saw firsthand the tremendous influence he 
had on her and so many of her classmates. His encouragement stayed with 
them long after their days at Clay High School.
  One of those former players, Rick Wheeler, recently shared a 
beautiful tribute. Rick said Coach Riddle coached them for 10 years, 
from Pop Warner through High School. He said something that captured 
Coach's legacy perfectly: ``If you were privileged to be coached by 
him, you were a better player, but more importantly, you were a better 
person.'' That is what made Coach Riddle so special.
  Coach Riddle was part of Clay High School's championship football 
tradition. After retiring, he helped tirelessly to bring a new track to 
Clay High. That track was fittingly named in his honor. His legacy 
isn't about a championship or track, it is the generations of young 
people whose lives he changed.

  To his family, former students, former educators, and the entire Clay 
High School community, we extend our heartfelt sympathy and gratitude.
  Mr. Speaker, I will close with Rick Wheeler's beautiful words to his 
beloved coach: ``Rest well, Coach--you left it all on the field--now 
enjoy your heavenly overtime in the arms of Jesus.'' May God bless 
Coach Riddle and the countless lives he touched.

just lost Allison, and he truly was one of a kind.
  A seventh-generation Floridian, he loved his State, its people, its 
history, and its natural beauty, and he dedicated his life to 
protecting and serving all of them.

he brought to everything he did.

things better than he found them.

northeast Florida's treasured natural lands. He understood that 
protecting Florida wasn't just about saving trees and waterways, it was 
about preserving something precious for our children and grandchildren.
  He had a favorite saying: It is now or never. He lived on those 
words. When he saw something that needed to be done, he got to work.

man with Florida.

devoted to family and service.

DeFoor. May God bless his family, and may we honor his memory by
