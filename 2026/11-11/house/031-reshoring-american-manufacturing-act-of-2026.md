[[Page H6159]]

  The Clerk read the title of the bill.
  The text of the bill is as follows:

                               H.R. 9718

       Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of 
     the United States of America in Congress assembled,

     SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

       This Act may be cited as the ``Reshoring American 
     Manufacturing Act of 2026'' or the ``RAM Act''.

     SEC. 2. INFORMATION AND RESOURCES FOR SMALL BUSINESS CONCERNS 
                   RELATING TO ONSHORING MANUFACTURING CAPACITY.

       (a) Webpage Required.--Not later than 60 days after the 
     date of the enactment of this Act, the Administrator of the 
     Small Business Administration, acting through the Associate 
     Administrator for the Office of Manufacturing and Trade of 
     the Administration, shall establish and maintain a webpage 
     containing a list of resources and information for small 
     business concerns (as defined under section 3 of the Small 
     Business Act (15 U.S.C. 632)) relating to onshoring to the 
     United States part or all of the manufacturing capacity of 
     such concerns.
       (b) Contents.--The resources and information included on 
     the webpage required by subsection (a) shall include--
       (1) contact information for general inquiries, relevant 
     offices of the Small Business Administration, and any partner 
     organizations;
       (2) a free tool designed to help small business concerns 
     identify entities that could assist with onshoring to the 
     United States the manufacturing capacity of such concerns; 
     and
       (3) other information, as determined appropriate by the 
     Associate Administrator for the Office of Manufacturing and 
     Trade of the Administration.
       (c) Updates.--Until the date that is 4 years after the date 
     of the enactment of this Act, the Associate Administrator for 
     the Office of Manufacturing and Trade annually shall seek 
     input from the Chief Counsel for Advocacy of the Office of 
     Advocacy of the Small Business Administration for 
     improvements to the webpage and updates to the information 
     and resources included on such webpage.
       (d) Study and Report.--Not later than 2 years after the 
     date on which the webpage is established, the Administrator 
     of the Small Business Administration shall conduct a study 
     and submit to Congress a report on the effectiveness of the 
     webpage required under this Act. The Administrator shall 
     submit to Congress an updated report annually thereafter 
     until the date that is 4 years after the date of the 
     enactment of this Act.
       (e) Termination.--This Act and the requirements of this Act 
     shall terminate 4 years after the date of the enactment of 
     this Act.

  Mr. WILLIAMS of Texas. Mr. Speaker, I yield myself such time as I may 
consume.
  Mr. Speaker, I rise today in strong support of H.R. 9718, the 
Reshoring American Manufacturing Act of 2026, as amended, introduced by 
my colleagues Representatives Wied, Van Duyne, Ellzey, and Olszewski, 
or ``Big O,'' as I know him.
  For decades, America watched as manufacturing jobs, production 
facilities, and critical supply chains moved overseas. This shift cost 
American communities good-paying jobs, made our small businesses more 
dependent on foreign suppliers, and left our economy vulnerable to 
supply chain disruptions beyond our control.
  We cannot afford to continue down that path.
  America's small businesses create jobs, support local communities, 
and drive innovation. Too often, those looking to manufacture their 
products here at home face unnecessary challenges in identifying 
domestic suppliers, finding the right resources, and navigating the 
process of bringing production back to the United States.
  We should be making it easier, not harder, for American entrepreneurs 
to invest, produce, and grow right here at home.
  This is exactly what the legislation seeks to accomplish.
  In May 2025, the SBA launched its Make Onshoring Great Again portal, 
a tool designed to help small businesses identify American 
manufacturers and suppliers.
  With a searchable database of more than 1 million American suppliers, 
the portal gives small businesses an easy-to-use resource to find 
domestic partners, strengthen their supply chains, and keep more 
economic activity here in the U.S.
  This bill codifies the portal and ensures small businesses have 
access to information and resources to bring manufacturing operations 
back home, including connections to relevant agency offices, partner 
organizations, and a free tool to identify entities that can assist 
with onshoring manufacturing capacity.
  Mr. Speaker, this is a commonsense approach to supporting American 
manufacturing and strengthening our small businesses. I urge my 
colleagues to very strongly support H.R. 9718, the Reshoring American 
Manufacturing Act.
