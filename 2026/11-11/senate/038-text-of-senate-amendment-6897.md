# Text of Senate Amendment 6897

**Speaking:** Alsobrooks, Angela D. (A000382) [D-MD]

```
  SA 6897. Ms. ALSOBROOKS (for herself, Ms. Duckworth, Mr. Merkley, Mr. 
Lujan, Ms. Blunt Rochester, Mr. Blumenthal, Mr. Bennet, Mr. Kim, and 
Mr. Kelly) submitted an amendment intended to be proposed to amendment 
SA 6883 proposed by Mr. Thune (for Mrs. Capito (for herself, Mr. Lee, 
Mr. Whitehouse, and Mr. Heinrich)) to the bill H.R. 2347, to amend the 
Internal Revenue Code of 1986 to exclude from gross income any damages, 
other than punitive damages, received on account of any sexual acts or 
sexual contact; which was ordered to lie on the table; as follows:

        Beginning on page 74 of the amendment, strike line 20 and 
     all that follows through page 76, line 14, and insert the 
     following:
       ``(5) Remedy.--
       ``(A) In general.--Except as provided in subparagraphs (B) 
     and (D) and notwithstanding any other provision of law, if, 
     after review on the merits of a claim, a court determines 
     that the environmental review for an authorization was not 
     conducted in accordance with the requirements of this Act, 
     the only remedy the court may order to redress that violation 
     is to remand, without vacatur or issuance of an injunction, 
     the applicable authorization to the agency with--
       ``(i) specific instructions to correct errors or 
     deficiencies found by the court; and
       ``(ii) a reasonable schedule and deadline, which shall be 
     enforced by the court, for the agency to act on remand, which 
     shall not exceed 180 days after the date on which the order 
     of the court was entered, except that the court may extend 
     the deadline once, by not more than 90 days, for good cause 
     shown.
       ``(B) Injunctions pending remand.--
       ``(i) In general.--Notwithstanding subparagraph (A) and 
     subject to clause (ii), if a court makes a determination 
     under subparagraph (A), the court may enjoin the applicable 
     authorization, or the activity that is the subject of the 
     authorization, until the agency completes the actions 
     required on remand, if--

       ``(I) the court finds that--

       ``(aa) the authorization or the activity that is the 
     subject of the authorization will cause a reasonably 
     foreseeable adverse environmental effect or preclude the 
     consideration of reasonable alternatives; or
       ``(bb) the agency failed to prepare an environmental 
     document required under this Act for the proposed agency 
     action that the authorization implements;

       ``(II) the plaintiff will suffer irreparable harm in the 
     absence of an injunction;
       ``(III) the balance of equities favors the plaintiff; and
       ``(IV) an injunction is in the public interest.

       ``(ii) Duration of injunction.--An injunction issued by a 
     court under clause (i) shall last no longer than the time the 
     court finds is reasonably necessary--

       ``(I) for the completion of any environmental review 
     required under this Act; and
       ``(II) to prevent the irreparable harm described in clause 
     (i)(II).

       ``(iii) Scope of injunction.--

       ``(I) In general.--Except as provided in subclause (II), an 
     injunction issued by a court under clause (i) shall be 
     limited to the portion of the agency action that has not been 
     adequately addressed through an environmental review under 
     this Act.
       ``(II) Exception.--If a portion of an agency action 
     described in subclause (I) is not severable from the action 
     as a whole, a court may enjoin such agency action in its 
     entirety.

       ``(iv) Irreparable harm.--A finding by a court under 
     subclause (I)(aa) of clause (i) shall satisfy the requirement 
     under subclause (II) of that clause.
       ``(C) Violations of other law.--
       ``(i) In general.--Subparagraph (A) and paragraph (6) shall 
     apply only to a violation of the requirements of this Act.
       ``(ii) Limitation.--Nothing in subparagraph (A) or 
     paragraph (6) limits the remedies available under any other 
     Federal law for a violation of that Federal law that a court 
     finds with respect to a claim within its jurisdiction under 
     paragraph (2).

[[Page S5582]]

       ``(D) Failure to act on remand or inadequate remand.--If an 
     agency does not complete the actions required on remand by 
     the deadline established under subparagraph (A)(ii), 
     including any extension, or if the court finds that the 
     actions taken on remand do not correct the errors or 
     deficiencies found by the court--
       ``(i) paragraph (6) shall cease to apply to the 
     authorization;
       ``(ii) on motion of any party, the court may grant such 
     other relief as the court determines appropriate, until the 
     agency completes the actions required on remand;
       ``(iii) the court shall retain jurisdiction to ensure 
     compliance with its order; and
       ``(iv) if the court finds that the failure was willful, the 
     court may award reasonable attorney fees and costs to the 
     party that obtained the remand.
```
