                              Veterans Day

  Mr. GRASSLEY. Mr. President, today is Veterans Day. We were reminded 
of that by the prayer of the Senate chaplain. But I hope we don't have 
to be reminded of it because this is a special day of thanks and 
remembrance for those who served.
  Originally, what we call Veterans Day was called Armistice Day at the 
end of World War I. On the 11th hour of the 11th day of the 11th month 
of 1918, it was all quiet on the Western Front. And I believe there was 
a movie named that way.
  Anyway, from my hometown of New Hartford, IA, where I have lived my 
entire life--700 residents--they are reminded of this sacrifice that we 
honor today. The two names are Geyer and Bricker, two New Hartford 
residents who lost their lives in World War I. The American Legion post 
is named after them.
  Veterans Day, as I said before, is a time of remembrance for the 
sacrifices of brave men and women who have made that sacrifice to 
preserve freedom and liberty here at home and to broadcast liberty and 
freedom around the world.
  Veterans sacrifice for our country. So then it is our responsibility 
to take care of them when they come home.
  Now, everybody has that responsibility, but for those of us in public 
life--through lawmaking and oversight and case work, I work to ensure 
Iowa veterans have the resources they need, and I work to hold the 
Federal Government accountable to the promises it has made to those who 
served in uniform.
  Just one of the projects that I have for veterans is my office 
hosting a Veterans History Project event around Veterans Day to collect 
stories from Iowa veterans and submit those stories to the Library of 
Congress to honor their service for generations to come but also for 
any American that wants to know what it is to serve in the military and 
particularly in wartime. They can go and understand those same stories 
that I understand when I receive them from the veterans.
  So this past Friday, my office's Veteran History Project was held at 
the American Legion Post 403 in Van Meter, IA. So far, including last 
week, with our several years of interviews, I have submitted over 100 
stories from

[[Page S5568]]

Iowa veterans to the Library of Congress.
  So I say to all veterans: Thank you for your service.
  I yield the floor.

                         Republican Conference

  Mr. THUNE. Mr. President, yesterday the Republican conference held 
its first meeting with our new Members for the 120th Congress, and we 
had a robust discussion about our conference's future and how we can 
best serve the American people for the next 2 years. We also held our 
leadership elections for the coming Congress. I am grateful to my 
colleagues for again placing their trust in me to be the Republican 
leader.
  And I am looking forward to working with the other Members of our 
leadership team and with all of our colleagues, both old and new, over 
the next 2 years.
  In my first speech from this desk nearly 2 years ago, one of the 
things I talked about was restoring the Senate as a place of discussion 
and deliberation. And over these last 2 years, I have striven for that 
ideal, and I am proud of the legislative record that we have built. The 
Working Families Tax Cuts, which delivered permanent tax relief to 
hard-working Americans, the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, a 
bipartisan effort to make home ownership more affordable.
  Republicans and Democrats came together to craft the first Federal 
legislation dealing with digital assets. We passed the Laken Riley Act, 
the Protect College Sports Act, and we demonstrated what is possible 
when both parties come together for a more open appropriations process. 
On nominations, we faced historic obstruction from Democrats, and 
despite that, we got President Trump's Cabinet in place at the fastest 
pace in over two decades.
  We have confirmed 605 civilian nominees and put 56 article III judges 
on the Federal bench, and at the fastest pace since the Reagan 
administration. And we are not done.
  We could not have confirmed all of these individuals or crafted and 
passed these bills without the hard work of our Republican conference. 
And I want to thank them for all they have done in this Congress, and I 
have no doubt that we will continue to work just as hard through the 
end of this year and into the 120th Congress as well.
  In that first speech I gave as leader last January, I talked about my 
leadership style, specifically some of the lessons I learned from 
playing sports. In sports you need to learn from your losses and then 
move on to the next game. As I said, part of our meeting yesterday was 
a conversation about Republicans' future. I expect those conversations 
to continue, and for all Republican Senators to play a role in building 
up our party.
  We will see where we need to improve, identify our strengths, and use 
them to best serve the American people. Whether we are in the majority 
or the minority, we work for the American people.
  Last week, voters sent a clear message, and a large part of that 
message has to do with the cost of living. Hard-working people have 
faced cost of living challenges for more than 5 years now since the 
early days of the Biden administration. And while I am proud of 
accomplishments like the Working Families Tax Cuts, there is more to 
do.
  In the coming months and years, we will see legislation like the 
Working Families Tax Cuts and the ROAD to Housing Act continue to bear 
fruit, and affordability will continue to be at the top of our priority 
list here in the Senate and not just next year--right now.
  This week, the Senate has advanced bipartisan permitting reform 
legislation. A more efficient permitting system would reduce costly 
delays, get things built faster, and provide more affordable and 
accessible energy across the country. It would also encourage 
investment in America, create jobs, and boost made-in-America 
manufacturing.
  The need is real and the opportunity is ripe for us to get this done. 
We have a bipartisan agreement that has support from the President. We 
should waste no time delivering this win for the American people. And 
the sooner we finish permitting, the more time we will have for 
additional priorities.
  As I said, we will also be confirming more of the President's 
nominees before the end of the year, and I expect that we will continue 
to face obstruction from Democrats in our efforts. But we are going to 
put in the work to overcome that obstruction to get qualified 
individuals in place.
  Over the last 22 months we have been busy. And I hope everyone is 
ready to run through the tape because there is more work to be done in 
the 119th Congress, and we are going to leave it all on the field.


                              Veterans Day

  Mr. President, before I close, I want to say a few words about 
Veterans Day. Last month I had the opportunity to attend two deployment 
ceremonies back home in South Dakota. In this 250th year of our 
country's independence, these ceremonies held a special significance. 
Celebrating 250 years of American independence would be incomplete 
without acknowledging that this Nation endures because Americans have 
answered and continue to answer the call to serve.
  For many Americans, serving our country is a family tradition they 
trace back through generations. Others are the first in their family to 
put on the uniform. All--all--are ready to give their lives and their 
future for this country's future.
  I am grateful to all of the Americans who have served our country in 
uniform. To Chairman Moran and members of the Veterans Affairs 
Committee, thank you for all of your work this Congress to help improve 
the lives of all of America's veterans. And I want to wish all of those 
who have served a happy Veterans Day.
  I yield the floor.
