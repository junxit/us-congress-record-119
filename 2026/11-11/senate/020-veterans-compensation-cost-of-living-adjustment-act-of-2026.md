# VETERANS' COMPENSATION COST-OF-LIVING ADJUSTMENT ACT OF 2026

**Speaking:** Moran, Jerry (M000934) [R-KS]

**Measures:** H.R. 8552

```
      VETERANS' COMPENSATION COST-OF-LIVING ADJUSTMENT ACT OF 2026

  Mr. MORAN. Mr. President, just a few moments ago--a half hour ago--I 
was on the floor recognizing our veterans from across the country, 
recognizing the veteran Members of the U.S. Senate, and I indicated 
that I would be back on the Senate floor today to take concrete action 
to demonstrate our respect and gratitude to those who served our Nation 
by passing legislation that keeps our commitments, that makes certain 
that our veterans have a greater opportunity to a fuller life and that 
we reward them and pay tribute to them for their service in actions, 
not just words.
  I am here to do that. I am joined by a number of my colleagues in 
support of this legislation and certainly in support of the veterans 
that it will benefit.
  I also know that since my last remarks on the Senate floor--in those 
remarks, I mentioned the Take Care of America's Veterans Act, 
indicating it would deliver meaningful benefits to those who served our 
country. And since that time, the House of Representatives has 
unanimously, by voice vote, passed that legislation, what we call 
TCAVA. And it will provide millions of veterans, caregivers, survivors, 
and their families who have been waiting a long time for those 
benefits--it will give them the opportunity to receive those.
  I can think of no better way--I applaud the House of Representatives 
for the passage of that legislation. And I can think of no better way 
for our veterans to be honored today--this Veterans Day 2026--than us 
now taking up that legislation and seeing it to its final moment, 
signed by the President of the United States, enacted into law, to the 
benefit of many caregivers, veterans, and family members.
  And, importantly, that legislation includes the Richard Star Act to 
end this unjust way in which we treat those who are disabled and reduce 
their compensation related to their retirement benefits.
  So significant developments that I pay tribute to are occurring today 
on this specific occasion.
  But I rise now in support of legislation introduced by Ranking Member 
Senator Blumenthal and me and members of the Veterans' Affairs 
Committee in particular. The legislation is the Veterans' Compensation 
Cost-of-Living Adjustment, or COLA, Act. This is a nonpartisan, 
commonsense bill where Congress comes together.
  I indicated in my remarks earlier that, surely, veterans is a place 
where we can still set aside partisan differences and work on behalf of 
every veteran in this country. And here is an example of that 
happening--bipartisan effort, bipartisan legislation--to come together 
to pass this legislation.
  It makes certain the VA's disability compensation benefits for 
survivors and dependents and others covered by benefits keep pace with 
the Social Security adjustments. They are adjusted, as is Social 
Security, and now these veterans' benefits are adjusted to keep pace 
with the Consumer Price Index.
  Veterans and their families deserve peace of mind, knowing the 
benefits they earned will keep pace with the changing economy. Millions 
of veterans and survivors rely upon these benefits to help make ends 
meet, and this annual adjustment helps alleviate financial uncertainty 
that can come with rising costs.
  Today, we honor and recognize those who have served our Nation in 
uniform. We must also remain committed to making certain they receive 
the support and benefits they have earned and deserve.
  The Members of the Senate who are with me today are here on Veterans 
Day. I would guess they, too, like me, wish we were home among those 
who served our Nation and honoring them in person. But we are here 
doing work today that includes making certain we keep our commitments 
to those we wish we were at home to be with.
  In addition, the opportunity for us to come together to pass this 
legislation today is especially meaningful to us on Veterans Day.
  As chairman of the Senate Committee on Veterans' Affairs, I know that 
taking care of veterans and their families, caregivers, and their 
survivors is a responsibility that extends beyond any one person and 
any one member or any political party.
  This legislation is one of the important ways we can come together 
and fulfill that responsibility in this Chamber today.
  Mr. President, let me now utilize the magic words that will move this 
legislation forward by asking for this legislation to be passed by the 
U.S. Senate today.
  Therefore, as if in legislative session, I ask unanimous consent that 
the Senate proceed to the immediate consideration of H.R. 8552, which 
was received from the House and is at the desk.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The clerk will report the bill by title.
  The legislative clerk read as follows:

       A bill (H.R. 8552) to increase, effective as of December 1, 
     2026, the rates of compensation for veterans with service-
     connected disabilities and the rates of dependency and 
     indemnity compensation for the survivors of certain disabled 
     veterans, and for other purposes.

  There being no objection, the Senate proceeded to consider the bill.
  Mr. MORAN. I now ask unanimous consent that the bill be considered 
read a third time.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Without objection, it is so ordered.
  The bill was ordered to a third reading and was read the third time.
  Mr. MORAN. I know of no further debate on the bill.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. The bill having been read the third time, the 
question is, Shall the bill pass?
  The bill (H.R. 8552) was passed.
  Mr. MORAN. Mr. President, I ask unanimous consent that the motion to 
reconsider be considered made and laid upon the table.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER. Without objection, it is so ordered.
  The PRESIDING OFFICER (Mr. Moreno). The Senator from Kansas.

                          ____________________
```
