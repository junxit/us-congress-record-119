# TRIBUTE TO CRAIG WILLIAMS

**Speaking:** McConnell, Mitch (M000355) [R-KY]

```
                       TRIBUTE TO CRAIG WILLIAMS

  Mr. McCONNELL. Madam President, I rise today to congratulate my 
friend and fellow Kentuckian Craig Williams for receiving the Defense 
Community Champions Award. He is one of 18 recipients this year across 
the country honored for making a difference in the defense community. I 
ask my colleagues to please join me in recognizing his impressive 
achievement, which is well deserved.
  In his early years, Craig dutifully served our Nation as an Army 
translator solidifying his dedication to our country and to missions 
bigger than himself. This was only the tip of the iceberg when it came 
to his career of dedicated public service. After returning to the 
States, Craig refocused his attention on a pressing local issue that 
impacted thousands of his fellow neighbors in Richmond, KY, where over 
500 tons of chemical warfare agents were stored at the Bluegrass Army 
Depot (BGAD).
  President Ronald Reagan's call for a global prohibition on chemical 
weaponry spurred much needed attention to the disposal of our Nation's 
stockpile. The Army's initial plan to destroy these weapons through 
incineration was cause for concern due to potential poison leaks into 
the greater Madison County community. Craig jumped into action, 
pursuing the daunting task of taking on the bureaucracy and holding our 
government accountable for the promise it made to safely dispose of 
these chemical weapons. While Craig and I may have seemed like unlikely 
political allies, I believed his cause was just, and I was ready to 
help him push government in the right direction to keep Kentuckians 
safe.
  Our first victory together dates back to my first term in the U.S. 
Senate, when we put a stop to new munitions entering the Commonwealth. 
The success continued thanks to Craig's tireless advocacy work. In 
1991, he founded a coalition consisting of over 40 representatives from 
around the world known as the Chemical Weapons Working Group. Within 
just 2 years, Craig and his group successfully lobbied Congress to 
delay the incineration of the chemical weapons, forcing a further 
exploration of safer options. Thus began a 30-year march toward the 
final goal of total disposal.
  I am proud to have helped Craig by fighting to bring home over $7 
billion in Federal funding to support the responsible destruction of 
these munitions. This project was nothing short of a team effort. 
Through Craig's work on the Kentucky Chemical Demilitarization 
Citizens' Advisory Commission, he served as dependable eyes and ears on 
the ground, ensuring effective communication and transparency between 
government and the public. Our relationship was built on trust, and it 
proved invaluable as we worked together to meet the needs of this 
project. In July 2023, our hard work alongside community partners paid 
off as the last chemical weapons at BGAD were destroyed.
  Craig is well known for his work and was honored in 2006 with the 
Goldman Environmental Prize, an award inspired by the Nobel Prize. His 
story is one of true passion for the well-being of his neighbors and 
community. Our accomplishments together are proof that government 
functions best when working in tandem with the people it serves. I am 
confident Craig will continue to tackle the issues that matter most to 
him and will serve the public vigilantly in all of his future 
endeavors. Once again, I ask my Senate colleagues to join me in 
congratulating Craig on this distinguished award.

                          ____________________
```
