# Daily Digest/Senate Committee Meetings

**Measures:** S. 287, S. 2082, S. 2110, S. 2235, S. 2319, S. 2741, S. 2878, S. 3022

```
Committee Meetings
(Committees not listed did not meet)
NOMINATIONS
Committee on Agriculture, Nutrition, and Forestry: Committee concluded 
a hearing to examine the nominations of Mindy Brashears, of Texas, to 
be Under Secretary for Food Safety, who was introduced by 
Representative Arrington, Stella Herrell, of New Mexico, to be an 
Assistant Secretary, and John Walk, of Virginia, to be Inspector 
General, all of the Department of Agriculture, after the nominees 
testified and answered questions in their own behalf.
BIG TECH
Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation: Committee concluded 
a hearing to examine Big Tech and silencing Americans, part II, after 
receiving testimony from Markham Erickson, Google, Neil Potts, Meta 
Platforms Inc., and Harold Feld, Public Knowledge, all of Washington, 
D.C.; and Will Creeley, Foundation for Individual Rights and 
Expression, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania.
NATIONAL HISTORIC PRESERVATION ACT
Committee on Energy and Natural Resources: Committee concluded a 
hearing to examine the Section 106 consultation process under the 
National Historic Preservation Act, after receiving testimony from 
Christopher W. Merritt, Utah State Historic Preservation Officer, 
Millcreek; Andy McDonald, Montana-Dakota Utilities Co., Bismarck, North 
Dakota; and Steven Concho, National Association of Tribal Historic 
Preservation Officers, Acoma, New Mexico.
BUSINESS MEETING
Committee on Environment and Public Works: Committee ordered favorably 
reported the following business items:
  S. 2082, to amend the Atomic Energy Act of 1954 to modify the 
definition of ``production facility'' to exclude an equipment or device 
capable of reprocessing spent nuclear fuel in a manner that does not 
separate plutonium from other transuranic elements;
  S. 2235, to amend the Energy Policy Act of 2005 to reauthorize the 
diesel emissions reduction program;
  S. 2741, to establish within the Environmental Protection Agency the 
Office of Mountains, Deserts, and Plains, with an amendment;
  S. 3022, to amend the Save Our Seas 2.0 Act to reauthorize certain 
Environmental Protection Agency programs;
  S. 2110, to require the Administrator of the Environmental Protection 
Agency to prepare a report on reuse and refill systems;
  S. 2878, to reauthorize funding to monitor, assess, and research the 
Great Lakes Basin;
  S. 287, to designate the Federal building located at 225 South Pierre 
Street in Pierre, South Dakota, as the ``Marcella LeBeau Federal 
Building'';
  S. 2319, to designate the Federal building located at 300 West 
Congress Street in Tucson, Arizona, as the ``Raul M. Grijalva Federal 
Building'';
  3 General Services Administration resolutions; and
  The nominations of Jeffrey Hall, of Virginia, to be an Assistant 
Administrator, and Douglas Troutman, of Maryland, to be Assistant 
Administrator for Toxic Substances, both of the Environmental 
Protection

[[Page D1092]]

Agency, Ho Nieh, of Alabama, to be a Member of the Nuclear Regulatory 
Commission, Mitch Graves, and Jeff Hagood, both of Tennessee, Randall 
Jones, of Alabama, and Arthur Graham, of Florida, each to be a Member 
of the Board of Directors of the Tennessee Valley Authority.
NOMINATIONS
Committee on Finance: Committee concluded a hearing to examine the 
nominations of Arjun Mody, of New Jersey, to be Deputy Commissioner 
Social Security for the term expiring January 19, 2031, Jeffrey 
Goettman, of Virginia, to be a Deputy United States Trade 
Representative (Africa, Western Hemisphere, Europe, the Middle East, 
Environment, Labor, and Industrial Competitiveness), with the Rank of 
Ambassador, Julie Callahan, of the District of Columbia, to be Chief 
Agricultural Negotiator, Office of the United States Trade 
Representative, with the rank of Ambassador, and Thomas Bell, of 
Virginia, to be Inspector General, Department of Health and Human 
Services, after the nominees testified and answered questions in their 
own behalf.
FUTURE OF BIOTECH
Committee on Health, Education, Labor, and Pensions: Committee 
concluded a hearing to examine the future of biotech, focusing on 
maintaining U.S. competitiveness and lifesaving cures to patients, 
after receiving testimony from Lowell Schiller, University of Southern 
California Leonard D. Schaeffer Institute for Public Policy and 
Government Science, and John F. Crowley, Biotechnology Innovation 
Organization, both of Washington, D.C.; Josh Makower, Stanford 
University Schools of Medicine and Engineering, Stanford, California; 
Aaron S. Kesselheim, Brigham and Women's Hospital Division of 
Pharmacoepidemiology and Pharmacoeconomics, Boston, Massachusetts; and 
Reshma Ramachandran, Yale School of Medicine, New Haven, Connecticut.
IMPACTS OF GOVERNMENT SHUTDOWN
Committee on Indian Affairs: Committee concluded a hearing to examine 
impacts of government shutdowns and agency reductions in force on 
Native communities, after receiving testimony from Sarah Harris, United 
South and Eastern Tribes Sovereignty Protection Fund, Nashville, 
Tennessee; Ben Mallott, Alaska Federation of Natives, Anchorage; Kerry 
D. Bird, National Indian Education Association, and Anthony Locklear, 
II, National Indian Health Board, both of Washington, D.C.; and Pete 
Upton, Native CDFI Network, Grand Island, Nebraska.
VA DISABILITY SYSTEM
Committee on Veterans' Affairs: Committee concluded a hearing to 
examine putting veterans first, focusing on the current VA disability 
system, after receiving testimony from Cheryl L. Mason, Inspector 
General, Department of Veterans Affairs; Elizabeth H. Curda, Director, 
Education, Workforce, and Income Security Issues, Government 
Accountability Office; and Jeremy Villanueva, Paralyzed Veterans of 
America, Jon Retzer, Disabled American Veterans, Ryan Gallucci, 
Veterans of Foreign Wars of the United States, and Daniel M. Gade, 
Lieutenant Colonel, USA (Ret.), all of Washington, D.C.
```
